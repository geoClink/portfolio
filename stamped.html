Stamped! — A City Passport

A SwiftUI app that turns the world's great buildings into a passport to fill: more than 2,100 architectural landmarks across 166 cities and 122 university campuses. Fly through photoreal 3D cities, plan a whole trip day by day, walk the streets in Look Around, and earn a stamp for every place you visit.

The Problem

Architecture apps are either too passive (a static list of landmarks) or too heavy (full trip planners with accounts and subscriptions). I wanted something in between: tell me what to see, help me get there, and give me a reason to come back. Stamped! turns sightseeing into a collection game: visit every landmark in a city and you earn its stamp.

See It Work

Recorded on iPhone in New York: tap a pin to fly to it in 3D, then drop to street level in Look Around.

Design: A Passport, Not a Database

Under the hood it's a list of 2,100+ buildings. The design job was making it feel like something you collect, not something you scroll. I wrote a style guide for the app (editorial, travel-inspired, reward-driven) and hold every screen to it.

  • Every screen serves the loop: browse → visit → stamp. Weather, events, and dining stayed because they answer "what now that I'm here?"
  • Rewards feel ceremonial: a dashed border, a slight tilt, a typewriter date, a fact, and a haptic.
  • The map is the app. Stamp, Want to Go, Add to Trip, and Look Around all live on the map card.
  • Accessibility changes the skin, not the structure. High contrast swaps color for borders; Reduce Motion stills the stamp and pins.

Architecture

Stamped! is pure SwiftUI with no backend. Landmark, city and campus content ships with the app as bundled JSON (BuildingRegistry.json, CityDetails.json, CampusTraditions.json), so browsing and tracking work offline; weather, events, and dining layer on top when there's a connection. The one shared piece is building scans: 3D scans people capture with ARKit go to a CloudKit public database so everyone can see them, still with no server of my own.

One ObservableObject, GlobalProgressManager, owns visited IDs and photos. It writes to UserDefaults and NSUbiquitousKeyValueStore, so progress syncs through iCloud with no account. Rank and completion are computed from the visited set, so they can never drift out of sync.

The 3D map is SwiftUI's Map with realistic elevation and a MapCamera pitched 60–65°. Guided and trip tours are one cancellable Task that flies stop to stop; any tap cancels it, so the tour never fights you for the camera.

One codebase, four platforms. iPhone, iPad and Mac share the SwiftUI views; on the Mac, sheets become windows. A MacPanelWindows scene declares a Window for single panels (World Map, My Journey) and a WindowGroup(for:) for ones that take a value (one quiz window per city). An opensWindow(id:when:) modifier turns the existing showingX = true flags into openWindow calls on macOS, so none of the call sites changed and iOS keeps its sheets. The Vision Pro app is its own target that reuses the models, the bundled JSON and the iCloud key-value store.

Immersive panoramas on visionOS. A 360° photo is wrapped on the inside of a 1,000 m RealityKit sphere: a sphere's faces point outward, so its x scale is flipped to -1 to turn it inside out without mirroring the photo. It opens in a full-immersion ImmersiveSpace, and since visionOS allows one immersive space at a time, opening a panorama first dismisses any open one. The 26 panoramas were found by a Python script (Wikidata, Commons categories, per-city searches), every pick checked by eye, and each licence is credited on screen.

Partner links with tests. Hotel and tour buttons are built by small link builders (HotelLinks.swift, TourLinks.swift) across three styles of tracking: Partnerize click links for Expedia and Vrbo, CJ deep links for Booking.com, and plain query-parameter IDs for GetYourGuide and Viator. Each button hides itself when its ID isn't set, and unit tests pin down the tracking parameters, place names and trip dates in every link.

Offline cache. Save for Offline writes a manifest, one JSON file per city and shared photo files to Application Support, excluded from iCloud backup. The Wikipedia service reads memory first, then the saved copy, then the network, so a saved city works without a connection and an unsaved one behaves as before. Removing a city keeps photos another saved city still uses; that rule has its own tests.

What Was Hard

Scope. A travel app can grow forever. The rule: every feature answers "what do I see?", "how do I get there?", or "what now that I'm here?" Anything else got cut.

Camera framing. Centering on a landmark hid it under the card, so the camera aims slightly south. One far-off pin (JFK in New York) zoomed whole cities out, so city framing now uses the closest 80% of landmarks.

Bad data. Flying to a pin in 3D makes a wrong pin obvious. For 2.4 I audited every landmark: 181 pins moved to their real locations, 200+ photos fixed.

App Review. Apple pushed back on the camera usage string, Ticketmaster links, and the location flow. Getting all three right was the last hurdle to launch.

Aiming Look Around. On Vision Pro, asking Apple for the street view nearest a landmark's pin often faced the wrong thing: a café awning at the Colosseum, a concrete wall at Willis Tower. Now the app first runs an MKLocalSearch for the landmark by name around the pin, keeps the closest match within 300 m, and requests the scene for that map item, so Apple points the camera at the building. With no match it falls back to the old request.

Four platforms, one design. A layout that's right on iPhone can look like a stretched phone on iPad and like an iPad app on a Mac. For 2.8 I did a design pass on each platform and fixed what didn't fit: a solid top bar on iPhone, a wider sidebar and larger sheets on iPad, windows on Mac, and card buttons and translated building styles on Vision Pro.

Where the Idea Could Go

When MSU students visited the Apple Developer Academy, one of them said Stamped! would be perfect for learning your way around campus as a freshman, with dining halls as landmarks and orientation as stamps. I wrote it up as a pitch doc and deck. The first piece is now real: 2.4 added a Campus Architecture collection, 2.5 grew it to 101 campuses, every Big Ten, SEC, ACC and Big 12 school, Michigan State included, grouped by conference, and 2.7 gave each campus its own History, Game Day and events. With 2.8 it reaches 117 campuses.

The Outcome

Stamped! started in February 2026 as my Swift Student Challenge entry and became the first app I took from zero to the App Store on my own. In nine months it went from 1.0 to 2.8 on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision Pro, with 2.9 coming to all of them. The patterns I worked out here (one observable store, computed state instead of duplicates, a clean split between content, state, and UI) are the ones I've used in every app since, including Tally. See the app itself on the Stamped! website.
