The Fair City

Dublin architecture

Dublin is one of Europe's youngest-skewing capital cities, with a population where the average age is under 35. The Guinness brewery at St. James's Gate has been operating since 1759 — Arthur Guinness signed a remarkable 9,000-year lease on the site at £45 per year. Dublin is the birthplace of four Nobel Prize-winning writers: W.B. Yeats, George Bernard Shaw, Samuel Beckett, and Seamus Heaney — an extraordinary literary legacy for one small city.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoDublin Airport (DUB)

        Dublin Airport is the Republic of Ireland's primary international gateway and one of the busiest airports in Europe, handling over 30 million passengers annually. It is one of the few airports outside North America to offer US Customs and Border Protection preclearance — meaning travelers to the United States complete American immigration procedures before boarding in Dublin, arriving as domestic passengers.

      • CurrencyEuro (€)
      • LanguageIrish (Gaeilge) & English

        Ireland has two official languages: Irish (Gaeilge) and English. While Irish is taught in all schools and is spoken as a daily language in rural Gaeltacht regions along the west coast, English is the primary language of Dublin. Irish-language signage appears throughout the city, and you will hear Irish used in government, radio broadcasts, and cultural events. The Dublin accent is warm, musical, and distinctly its own.

      • Getting aroundDublin Bus & DART

        Dublin's DART (Dublin Area Rapid Transit) is a coastal commuter rail line offering stunning views of Dublin Bay as it runs between Malahide in the north and Greystones in the south. The Luas tram system's two cross-city lines connect the suburbs to the city center, intersecting at key hubs. The city's compact Georgian center is highly walkable, and a public bicycle scheme makes shorter journeys easy.
