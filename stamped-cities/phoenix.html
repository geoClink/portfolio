Valley of the Sun

Phoenix architecture

Phoenix is the only U.S. state capital with a population of over one million people, thriving in the heart of the Sonoran Desert. It is a city defined by year-round sunshine, stunning desert landscapes like Camelback Mountain, and a rapidly expanding tech industry.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoSky Harbor (PHX)

        Sky Harbor features the world's only 'Train over Taxiway' bridge, where the automated PHX Sky Train crosses over active runways while planes taxi underneath. It is consistently ranked as one of the most traveler-friendly airports in the U.S. due to its efficient layout and short security wait times.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        The city's speech is a typical Western American English, frequently interspersed with Spanish terms due to its proximity to the Mexican border. This linguistic blend is particularly evident in local place names and the city's diverse and celebrated culinary vocabulary.

      • Getting aroundValley Metro

        The light rail cars use heavy-duty air conditioning to maintain a comfortable 70°F during the desert heat. An extensive network of local and express buses covers the vast Phoenix metropolitan area. The PHX Sky Train is an automated people mover that connects the airport to the light rail system.

      13 landmarks in Phoenix

      1. 01

        Taliesin West

        12621 N Frank Lloyd Wright Blvd, Scottsdale, AZ 85259

        A UNESCO World Heritage site, this structure served as Frank Lloyd Wright’s beloved winter home and desert laboratory. The complex was built using 'desert masonry,' a technique where local rocks were set in concrete to harmonize with the McDowell Mountains. It features translucent canvas roofs designed to mimic the soft, filtered light of the vast Sonoran desert sky. The angled walls and low profiles were intended to echo the natural slopes of the surrounding arid landscape. It remains a masterclass in organic architecture, feeling as though it grew directly out of the desert floor.

      2. 02

        Arizona Biltmore

        2400 E Missouri Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85016

        Often called the 'Jewel of the Desert,' this historic resort is famous for its unique and geometric 'Biltmore Blocks.' These textile blocks were inspired by the trunk of a palm tree and were designed by Albert McArthur in collaboration with Frank Lloyd Wright. The building's striking copper roof has developed a signature green patina over many decades of desert sun exposure. Inside, the famous 'Gold Room' features a ceiling made entirely of gold leaf, the second largest in the world. This luxury destination has hosted every U.S. President since its opening, solidifying its place in American political history.

      3. 03

        Burton Barr Central Library

        1221 N Central Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        This library is a triumph of sustainable desert design, featuring a copper-clad exterior inspired by Arizona’s rich mining history. The Great Reading Room on the fifth floor boasts a 'floating' ceiling supported by an innovative system of stainless steel cables. On the summer solstice, the building’s skylights and columns align perfectly with the sun to create a natural light show. Its 'crystal' shape was specifically engineered to manage the intense heat while providing ample natural illumination for readers. It stands as a landmark of modern civic architecture that deeply respects the environmental constraints of the Southwest.

      4. 04

        Luhrs Tower

        45 W Jefferson St, Phoenix, AZ 85003

        This Art Deco skyscraper stands as a historic reminder of Phoenix’s early urban growth and architectural ambition. The tower is decorated with intricate terracotta ornamentation and features a grand marble lobby that exudes old-world elegance. It was the tallest building in the city for several years and gained pop culture fame in the background of Alfred Hitchcock’s 'Psycho.' Its zig-zag moderne details and symmetrical 'wedding cake' geometry make it a standout feature of the downtown skyline. Today, it remains a vibrant part of the city core, housing popular local bars and modern office spaces.

      5. 05

        Chase Tower

        201 N Central Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        As the tallest building in the state of Arizona, this skyscraper is a quintessential example of the International Style. It features a unique 'cloverleaf' footprint with three distinct wings that provide various panoramic views of the valley. The dark glass and anodized aluminum facade were specifically chosen to withstand the punishing rays of the Arizona sun. Its massive scale makes it the primary anchor of the Phoenix skyline, serving as a visual reference point for miles. Despite changes in ownership, it remains the most dominant corporate symbol in the Phoenix Central Business District.

      6. 06

        Heard Museum

        2301 N Central Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        This museum is a premier example of Spanish Colonial Revival architecture and is world-renowned for its Native American art. The design features traditional arched walkways, peaceful courtyards, and a red terracotta tile roof typical of the Southwest. Over the decades, modern expansions have been carefully integrated to maintain the aesthetic of the original 1929 structure. The lush landscaping and outdoor sculptures create a serene environment for visitors exploring the cultural history of Arizona. It remains a vital institution dedicated to the advancement and appreciation of American Indian art and life.

      7. 07

        Phoenix Financial Center

        3443 N Central Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85012

        Known locally as the 'Punchcard Building,' this mid-century landmark features narrow, vertical windows that resemble 1960s computer data cards. The curved design of the main tower was a Space Age tribute, though a planned second mirrored tower was never constructed. Its south-facing wall is mostly solid to act as a heat shield against the intense Arizona summer weather. This building is a favorite among fans of the Googie architectural style and retro-futuristic urban design. It continues to be a focal point of the Midtown district, representing the city’s post-war economic boom.

      8. 08

        Science Center (Dorrance Planetarium)

        600 E Washington St, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        This deconstructivist masterpiece was designed by Antoine Predock to evoke the dramatic canyons and mesas of the Arizona landscape. The building utilizes sharp angles and metallic surfaces to play with the intense light and shadow of the desert. Its most iconic feature is the blue-tiled Dorrance Planetarium dome, which sits nestled within the geometric concrete forms. A massive floating canopy provides a shaded outdoor space for visitors to gather away from the direct sun. The structure serves as an interactive 'learning laboratory' that brings scientific concepts to life through bold architecture.

      9. 09

        Orpheum Theatre

        203 W Adams St, Phoenix, AZ 85003

        The Orpheum is a stunning example of Spanish Baroque Revival and the last remaining 'movie palace' in the Phoenix area. Its interior is famous for an 'atmospheric' ceiling that features moving clouds and twinkling stars to mimic an outdoor garden. After a significant restoration in the 1990s, its original 1920s gold leaf and grand murals were returned to their former glory. The theater serves as a premier venue for Broadway shows, concerts, and professional dance performances. It remains a cornerstone of the city's cultural history, offering a opulent escape from the modern downtown surroundings.

      10. 10

        The Rosson House

        113 N 6th St, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        Located in the historic Heritage Square, this fully restored Queen Anne Victorian home is a rare survivor of Phoenix's early residential life. The house features a distinctive octagonal turret and intricate 'gingerbread' woodwork that was popular in the late 19th century. Built during a period of rapid growth, it stands in stark contrast to the modern glass and steel skyscrapers nearby. The interior has been meticulously preserved to serve as a museum detailing the Victorian era in the American Southwest. It remains a favorite site for history enthusiasts and school groups exploring the city's humble beginnings.

      11. 11

        Tovrea Castle

        5025 E Van Buren St, Phoenix, AZ 85008

        Commonly known as the 'Wedding Cake' of the desert, this three-tiered castle is one of Phoenix's most curious landmarks. It sits atop a prominent hill and is surrounded by a massive, carefully maintained cactus garden featuring over 5,000 plants. Originally intended to be a resort, the structure became a private residence after the Great Depression changed the owner's plans. The white stucco walls and tiered architecture make it a singular and highly recognizable sight along the Van Buren corridor. It serves as a lasting symbol of early 20th-century ambition and the whimsical 'desert castle' movement in Arizona.

      12. 12

        Westward Ho

        618 N Central Ave, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        This iconic Mediterranean Revival building was once the most luxurious hotel in the Southwest, hosting movie stars and U.S. Presidents. It is instantly recognizable by its massive broadcasting antenna, which rises significantly higher than the building's roofline. The hotel was famous for its hand-painted lobby ceilings and its status as the social heart of downtown Phoenix for decades. Following the decline of the grand hotel era, it was repurposed into a residential complex for seniors and people with disabilities. It continues to be a beloved fixture of the city skyline, representing the glamour of Phoenix's golden age.

      13. 13

        St. Mary's Basilica

        231 N 3rd St, Phoenix, AZ 85004

        As the oldest Roman Catholic parish in Phoenix, this mission-style basilica is a spiritual and architectural cornerstone of the downtown area. It is famous for its massive domes and for housing the largest collection of stained-glass windows in the state of Arizona. The architecture blends Romanesque and Spanish Colonial influences, with white-washed walls that reflect the desert sunlight. It received a special designation as a minor basilica from Pope John Paul II during his historic 1987 visit. The interior features stunning vaulted ceilings and intricate altar work that draws visitors from all over the world.
