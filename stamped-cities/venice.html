La Serenissima

Venice architecture

Venice is built on 118 small islands separated by 150 canals and linked by over 400 bridges — there are no roads in the historic center. The entire city rests on millions of wooden piles driven into the soft lagoon seabed centuries ago. Venice has been slowly sinking at a rate of 1–2mm per year while sea levels rise, and the MOSE flood barrier system was activated for the first time in 2020 to protect the city from acqua alta (high water).

Before you go

  • Fly intoVenice Marco Polo Airport (VCE)

    Venice Marco Polo Airport is located on the mainland, and the journey into the city itself is part of the experience. Visitors arrive by water taxi or the public Alilaguna boat service, gliding across the lagoon with the skyline of Venice rising from the water before them. The airport is named after Marco Polo, the Venetian merchant and explorer who traveled to China in the 13th century.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageItalian

    Venetian (Veneto) is a distinct Romance language spoken alongside Italian in the Veneto region, with roots that predate the influence of Tuscan Italian. Venice was the center of a Mediterranean trading empire for centuries, and its dialect contains traces of Greek, Arabic, and other languages absorbed through commerce. In the tourist-heavy center, English is widely spoken.

  • Getting aroundVaporetto Water Buses

    Venice has no roads — all public transport is water-based. The ACTV vaporetto (water bus) network operates routes along the Grand Canal and between the outer islands of Murano, Burano, and the Lido. The iconic private gondolas, steered by licensed gondoliers, are primarily a tourist experience today; residents use traghetti (gondola ferries) for quick canal crossings and private water taxis for speed.
