City of Counts

Barcelona architecture

The Sagrada Família, Antoni Gaudí's unfinished masterpiece, has been under construction for over 140 years, which is longer than it took to build the Great Pyramids. The city is also unique for its Eixample district, designed in a strict grid pattern with chamfered corners to allow more sunlight and better airflow.

Before you go

  • Fly intoEl Prat (BCN)

    The airport features the stunning Terminal 1, a bright, glass-walled structure designed by architect Ricardo Bofill to maximize natural Mediterranean light. It is consistently ranked as one of the most efficient airports in Europe, offering travelers sweeping views of the sea as they arrive or depart.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageSpanish & Catalan

    Barcelona is a bilingual city where Catalan is the primary language of identity and administration alongside Spanish. While Spanish is understood by everyone, you will see all public signage and announcements displayed in both languages.

  • Getting aroundBarcelona Metro

    The Barcelona Metro includes several 'ghost stations' that remain visible to passengers through the train windows. FGC trains complement the metro by reaching into the higher elevations of the city and the suburbs. The historic blue tram and Montjuïc cable cars provide specialized scenic transit for visitors.

  1. 01

    Sagrada Família

    Carrer de Mallorca 401, 08013 Barcelona, Spain

    The Sagrada Família stands as the most famous unfinished building in the world and a definitive symbol of Barcelona. Its interior is designed to resemble a spiritual forest, featuring branching columns that reach toward an intricate, light-filled ceiling. The exterior showcases hyper-detailed facades that combine traditional Gothic elements with organic Art Nouveau forms. Though construction began in 1882, the project continues today using advanced engineering to realize Gaudí’s complex geometric visions. Once completed, the central Jesus Tower will make it the tallest religious structure in all of Europe.

  2. 02

    Casa Batlló

    Passeig de Gràcia 43, 08007 Barcelona, Spain

    Locally known as the 'House of Bones,' this building is famous for its skeletal stone balconies and organic, mask-like window frames. The facade is decorated with a colorful mosaic of broken ceramic tiles that shimmer like the surface of a lake. Its roof is shaped like the arched back of a dragon, covered in iridescent scales that represent the legend of Saint George. Inside, the design avoids straight lines, opting instead for ergonomic wood carvings and light-dispersing blue tiles in the central well. It stands as a pinnacle of the Modernisme movement and a testament to Gaudí’s obsession with natural forms.

  3. 03

    Casa Milà (La Pedrera)

    Passeig de Gràcia 92, 08008 Barcelona, Spain

    Nicknamed 'The Quarry' due to its undulating limestone facade, Casa Milà is one of the most innovative residential buildings of the 20th century. The structure is famous for its self-supporting stone exterior, which allows for an interior floor plan completely free of load-bearing walls. On the roof, a fantastic landscape of chimneys resembles hooded warriors standing guard over the city. Its two large internal courtyards are painted with floral motifs and designed to flood every apartment with natural light and air. This building was the last private residence designed by Gaudí before he dedicated his life entirely to the Sagrada Família.

  4. 04

    Barcelona Pavilion

    Av. Francesc Ferrer i Guàrdia 7, 08038 Barcelona, Spain

    The Barcelona Pavilion is a world-renowned landmark of modern minimalism, originally built for the 1929 International Exposition. It uses a sophisticated palette of glass, steel, and four different types of marble to create a sense of fluid, continuous space. The design famously features the 'Barcelona Chair,' which was created specifically to provide a throne for the visiting Spanish royalty. Although the original structure was dismantled in 1930, a meticulous reconstruction was completed on the same site in the 1980s. Its clean lines and reflecting pools continue to serve as a primary inspiration for architects seeking to balance luxury with simplicity.

  5. 05

    Palau de la Música Catalana

    C/ Palau de la Música 4-6, 08003 Barcelona, Spain

    This concert hall is a masterpiece of Catalan Modernisme and is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site. It is the only auditorium in Europe that is illuminated entirely by natural light during the day, thanks to its expansive glass walls. The centerpiece of the theater is a stunning inverted dome of stained glass that depicts a golden sun surrounded by a choir of angels. Every surface is covered in intricate sculptures, colorful mosaics, and floral motifs that celebrate the rich heritage of Catalan music. Its innovative steel-frame construction allowed for a spacious interior that was highly advanced for the early 20th century.

  6. 06

    Torre Glòries (Agbar Tower)

    Avinguda Diagonal 211, 08018 Barcelona, Spain

    Torre Glòries is a bullet-shaped skyscraper designed to evoke the imagery of a water geyser erupting from the ground. Its shape was inspired by the rounded peaks of the nearby Montserrat mountain range and the architectural style of Antoni Gaudí. The building is wrapped in a double skin of aluminum and glass louvers that can be tilted to regulate temperature and light. At night, the facade comes alive with over 4,000 LED devices that create vibrant, shifting patterns of color. It has become a landmark of the Poblenou district and features an observation deck with panoramic views of the entire city.

  7. 07

    MACBA

    Plaça dels Àngels 1, 08001 Barcelona, Spain

    The Museum of Contemporary Art of Barcelona stands as a bright, luminous presence in the heart of the historic Raval neighborhood. Its white, geometric facade features a massive glass curtain wall that allows passersby to see the movement of visitors within the galleries. The interior is designed around a central ramp that provides a clear architectural flow through the varying exhibition spaces. Richard Meier’s use of natural light and minimalist forms creates a neutral backdrop that highlights the modern artworks on display. The large plaza in front of the building has also become a globally famous meeting point for the international skateboarding community.

  8. 08

    Mercat de Santa Caterina

    Av. de Francesc Cambó 16, 08003 Barcelona, Spain

    The Mercat de Santa Caterina is instantly recognizable by its spectacular undulating roof that seems to float over the market stalls. This roof is covered in 325,000 hexagonal ceramic tiles that represent the vibrant colors of the fruits and vegetables found inside. During its reconstruction, ancient archaeological remains of a former convent were discovered and integrated into the museum section of the market. The design successfully blends the original 19th-century Neo-Classical walls with a daring, contemporary wooden and steel superstructure. It serves as a bustling social and culinary hub for the residents of the historic Ribera district.

  9. 09

    Edifici Fòrum

    Plaça de Leonardo da Vinci 4, 08019 Barcelona, Spain

    The Fòrum Building is a massive blue triangular structure that appears to hover above a vast public plaza near the Mediterranean coast. Its rough-textured concrete facade is split by numerous internal courtyards and skylights that create a play of light and shadow. The building was designed as the center of the 2004 Universal Forum of Cultures and now houses the Museum of Natural Sciences. Beneath the main structure, a wide auditorium and exhibition halls take advantage of the site’s unique coastal topography. It stands as a bold example of deconstructivism, utilizing sharp angles and unexpected voids to challenge traditional museum design.

  10. 10

    Walden 7

    Ctra. Reial 106, 08960 Sant Just Desvern, Barcelona, Spain

    Walden 7 is a utopian residential complex designed as a vertical labyrinth of interconnected towers, bridges, and circular courtyards. The building’s exterior is painted in deep terracotta tones, while the internal walkways feature a vibrant, contrasting turquoise blue. It was built on the site of an old cement factory and was intended to create a new type of communal living space. The structure contains 446 apartments, many of which are split over multiple levels to provide residents with unique spatial experiences. It remains one of the most iconic examples of Ricardo Bofill’s experimental approach to high-density social housing.

  11. 11

    Apple Passeig de Gràcia

    Passeig de Gràcia 1, 08007 Barcelona, Spain

    Located in a grand Neo-Gothic building from 1890, this store represents a flawless merger of historic charm and modern retail. The restoration project meticulously preserved the original stone facade and intricate structural details while creating an open, light-filled interior. A central atrium allows natural light to reach the ground floor, highlighting the use of local Spanish limestone and minimalist glass. This store sits at the corner of Plaça de Catalunya, serving as a gateway to the city’s most prestigious shopping avenue. It stands as a prime example of how modern technology can be seamlessly integrated into the historical fabric of a European capital.

  12. 12

    Barcelona Cathedral

    Plaça de la Seu, 08002 Barcelona, Spain

    The Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulalia is a stunning example of Catalan Gothic architecture with a majestic Neo-Gothic facade. Its tranquil cloister is famously home to 13 white geese, a living tradition that honors the age of the city’s co-patron saint at her martyrdom. The interior is characterized by its high vaulted ceilings and a series of side chapels filled with gold-leafed altarpieces. Visitors can take an elevator to the roof to walk among the gargoyles and enjoy views of the winding streets of the Gothic Quarter. This religious landmark serves as the seat of the Archbishop of Barcelona and remains the spiritual heart of the old city.

  13. 13

    Hotel Arts Barcelona

    Carrer de la Marina 19, 08005 Barcelona, Spain

    Hotel Arts is a high-tech skyscraper that redefined Barcelona’s coastline as part of the massive 1992 Olympic urban renewal project. The building is distinct for its white steel exoskeleton, which provides structural support while giving the tower a transparent, airy appearance. At its base sits Frank Gehry’s massive golden 'Peix' sculpture, which uses stainless steel slats to shimmer in the Mediterranean sun. Inside, the hotel offers luxury living and dining spaces with floor-to-ceiling windows that provide endless views of the sea and city. It remains one of the tallest and most recognizable structures on the Barcelona skyline.

  14. 14

    Hospital de Sant Pau

    C. de Sant Antoni Maria Claret 167, 08025 Barcelona, Spain

    The Hospital de Sant Pau is the largest Art Nouveau complex in the world and a masterpiece of the architect Domènech i Montaner. It was designed as a 'city within a city' where patients could heal in a beautiful environment filled with gardens and sunlight. The pavilions are decorated with colorful ceramic mosaics, stained glass, and decorative carvings that represent hope and nature. Connected by a network of underground tunnels, the buildings ensured that the medical operations remained efficient and hygienic. Today, this UNESCO World Heritage site is a cultural center that showcases the peak of the Modernisme movement's social ambition.

  15. 15

    Montjuïc Communications Tower

    Plaça d'Europa, 08038 Barcelona, Spain

    This sleek white communications tower was built for the 1992 Olympics and is meant to evoke the shape of an athlete holding a torch. Its base is adorned with 'trencadís,' a mosaic of broken ceramic tiles that serves as a modern homage to the works of Antoni Gaudí. The tower’s unique orientation also allows it to function as a giant sundial, casting a shadow on the nearby Plaça d'Europa. It is made of stainless steel and concrete, featuring a tilted needle that reaches high above the Montjuïc hill. The structure has become an enduring icon of Barcelona’s successful transformation into a modern, Olympic-ready global city.

  16. 16

    Santa Maria del Mar

    Plaça de Santa Maria 1, 08003 Barcelona, Spain

    Santa Maria del Mar is often cited as the purest and most beautiful example of the Catalan Gothic architectural style. Unlike the heavy and ornate cathedrals of Northern Europe, this church is defined by its clean horizontal lines and extreme interior spaciousness. Its massive stone walls are supported by incredibly slender octagonal pillars that create three aisles of nearly equal height. The building was constructed by the local merchant and sailor community in the 14th century over a remarkably short period of 54 years. Its minimalist elegance and 'mystical' lighting from the high stained-glass windows make it a favorite for locals and visitors alike.
