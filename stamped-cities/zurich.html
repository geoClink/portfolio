Little Big City

Zurich architecture

Zurich is consistently ranked among the world's most livable cities and is Switzerland's largest city, yet it retains the feel of a compact, walkable European town. It sits at the northern tip of Lake Zurich, surrounded by hills with Alpine views. The city hosts over 50 museums within a relatively small area — including the Swiss National Museum and the Kunsthaus, one of Switzerland's most important art collections. Switzerland's tradition of direct democracy means residents vote on local, cantonal, and national issues multiple times a year.

Before you go

  • Fly intoZurich Airport (ZRH)

    Zurich Airport (Kloten) is consistently recognized as one of the best-run airports in Europe, celebrated for its punctuality, efficiency, and stress-free design. Its rail station, located directly beneath the terminal, provides a direct train connection to Zurich's main station (Hauptbahnhof) in just 10 minutes. The airport's ShopVille underground mall, connecting the terminals to the train station, is a bustling commercial hub used by daily commuters.

  • CurrencySwiss Franc (Fr.)
  • LanguageSwiss German, French & Italian

    Switzerland has four national languages: German, French, Italian, and Romansh. Zurich is in the German-speaking part of the country, but the everyday spoken language is Swiss German (Schweizerdeutsch) — a collection of regional dialects that differ significantly from standard High German. Standard German is used in writing and formal settings, while English is very widely spoken as a fourth language.

  • Getting aroundZVV Trams & S-Bahn

    Zurich operates one of the world's most punctual and comprehensive public transport networks. The ZVV (Zürcher Verkehrsverbund) integrates trams, buses, suburban S-Bahn trains, lake boats, and cable cars under a single fare system. Electric trams have run through the city center continuously since 1882. Trains run with legendary Swiss precision — a delay of two minutes is considered significant and reported publicly.
