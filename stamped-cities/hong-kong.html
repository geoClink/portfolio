Pearl of the Orient

Hong Kong architecture

Hong Kong is the world's most vertical city, boasting more skyscrapers than any other metropolis, including New York and Dubai. It is a place where dense urban jungles meet literal jungles, as over 40% of the territory is protected as lush country parks and nature reserves.

Before you go

  • Fly intoHKG (HKG)

    The airport features a massive 'Sky Bridge' that is high enough for an Airbus A380 to taxi underneath while passengers walk above it. Built on a massive reclaimed island, the airport is consistently ranked among the world's best for its efficiency, shopping, and high-speed rail connection to the city.

  • CurrencyHong Kong Dollar ($)
  • LanguageCantonese & English

    The city's vibrant energy is matched by its rapid 'code-switching' between Cantonese and English. English remains an official language and is used for all legal, business, and transit communications.

  • Getting aroundMTR & Star Ferry

    The MTR is world-renowned for its 99.9% on-time rate and its incredibly efficient Octopus card payment system. The historic Star Ferry has been carrying passengers between Kowloon and Hong Kong Island since 1888. Double-decker 'Ding Ding' trams have been operating on Hong Kong Island for over 120 years.

15 landmarks in Hong Kong

  1. 01

    Bank of China Tower

    1 Garden Rd, Central

    This prism-like skyscraper is one of the most recognizable structures in the world. Its unique design resembles growing bamboo shoots to symbolize livelihood and prosperity. The triangular glass facets are designed to reflect the sun and city lights beautifully. However, its sharp edges were famously controversial in local Feng Shui for 'cutting' the luck of neighbors. It remains a masterwork of structural expressionism that defines the Central skyline.

  2. 02

    HSBC Main Building

    1 Queen's Road Central

    This marvel of high-tech architecture was designed to be completely demountable. It features a massive internal atrium and innovative 'sun scoops' to reflect natural light inside. The ground floor is a public plaza that allows air and people to flow through freely. This open design strictly adheres to Feng Shui principles to ensure positive energy. It is widely considered one of the most expensive and advanced office buildings ever constructed.

  3. 03

    International Commerce Centre (ICC)

    1 Austin Rd West, West Kowloon

    Standing as the tallest building in Hong Kong, it acts as a gateway to Victoria Harbour. It sits directly opposite the IFC tower to create a stunning visual portal for the city. The top floors house the Ritz-Carlton, which is the highest hotel in the entire world. Visitors can enjoy 360-degree views of the territory from the Sky100 observation deck. Its sleek, modern glass exterior represents the cutting edge of Kowloon's urban development.

  4. 04

    Two International Finance Centre

    8 Finance St, Central

    This centerpiece of the island skyline features a distinctively tapered design and 'crown' top. Its appearance is often compared to a giant finger pointing directly toward the sky. Movie fans may recognize it from 'The Dark Knight' where Batman leaps from its summit. The building is technically advanced, featuring rare double-deck elevators to manage high foot traffic. It serves as a prestigious headquarters for many of the world's leading financial institutions.

  5. 05

    Jockey Club Innovation Tower

    PolyU, Hung Hom

    This fluid and seamless structure intentionally breaks the traditional podium-and-tower building typology. Its design utilizes interior and exterior courtyards to create informal spaces for student interaction. The building serves as a bold visual representation of the dynamic nature of the Polytechnic University. It provides a specialized environment for the next generation of designers in the region. The striking metallic facade makes it one of the most unique academic buildings in Asia.

  6. 06

    Lippo Centre

    89 Queensway, Admiralty

    These twin towers are locally nicknamed 'The Koala Tree' due to their extruded glass sections. These sections resemble koalas climbing a trunk, giving the building a playful yet complex silhouette. It is a landmark of postmodernism that uses a facade to create deep shadows and reflections. The structural design provides a unique visual rhythm that stands out in the Admiralty district. Its intricate glass geometry remains a favorite subject for architectural photographers today.

  7. 07

    Tai Kwun (Centre for Heritage and Arts)

    10 Hollywood Rd, Central

    This site is a massive revitalization of the former Central Police Station and Victoria Prison. It seamlessly blends historic colonial brick buildings with contemporary aluminum-clad structures. The new buildings feature a distinctive 'cast iron' texture inspired by old prison bars. It now serves as a vibrant hub for heritage, contemporary art, and leisure. This project is a world-class example of how to preserve history while modernizing urban space.

  8. 08

    HK Convention and Exhibition Centre

    1 Expo Dr, Wan Chai

    The building is famous for a sweeping roof that resembles a seabird taking flight. It was the historic site of the 1997 handover ceremony marking the end of British rule. The entire structure is built on land reclaimed from the waters of Victoria Harbour. Huge glass curtain walls offer stunning panoramic views of the sea and the city skyline. It remains one of the most important venues for international trade and diplomacy in Asia.

  9. 09

    Choi Hung Estate

    Choi Hung, Kowloon

    This is one of the oldest and most famous public housing estates in all of Hong Kong. Its name 'Choi Hung' translates to 'rainbow' in Cantonese, which inspired its colorful exterior. The buildings are painted in a vibrant gradient that has made it a global icon for photography. It represents the city's unique solutions for high-density living during the mid-20th century. The central basketball court serves as a popular communal gathering spot for residents and visitors.

  10. 10

    Chi Lin Nunnery

    5 Chi Lin Dr, Diamond Hill

    This large Buddhist temple complex was built entirely of cypress wood without using a single iron nail. It meticulously follows the traditional Tang Dynasty style of wooden architecture. The structure utilizes interlocking wooden brackets called 'dougong' to support the heavy tiled roofs. It offers a serene escape from the surrounding high-rise residential buildings of Diamond Hill. The complex is perfectly integrated with the nearby Nan Lian Garden to create a spiritual oasis.

  11. 11

    Apple IFC Mall

    8 Finance St, Central

    This flagship store is a triumph of glass engineering suspended 11 meters above the street. It bridges the gap between two wings of the mall to create a transparent shopping experience. The massive glass facade is specially reinforced to withstand the region's powerful typhoon-level winds. Inside, a signature glass spiral staircase connects the floors with minimalist elegance. It offers shoppers a unique 'window' to look out over the harbor and the traffic below.

  12. 12

    Jardine House

    1 Connaught Place, Central

    This tower was once the tallest building in Asia and is famous for its circular windows. These 1,748 porthole-style windows earned it the nickname 'The House of a Thousand Windows.' The circular design was a structural choice that allowed for a thinner and lighter facade. It stands on reclaimed land as an enduring icon of the city's 1970s economic boom. The building remains a key part of the Central financial district's architectural history.

It is cleverly anchored over the MTR Airport Express tunnels using complex engineering. The building provides vast 'found spaces' specifically designed for large-scale contemporary art installations. It has quickly become a global landmark for art lovers and architectural enthusiasts alike.

  14. 14

    The Peak Tower

    128 Peak Rd, The Peak

    Perched at Victoria Gap, this wok-shaped building is the famous terminal for the Peak Tram. The avant-garde design provides a dramatic viewing platform without blocking the mountain's natural ridgeline. It serves as a premier tourist destination for shopping and dining at the highest levels. The Sky Terrace 428 offers some of the most iconic panoramic views of Hong Kong. Its unique silhouette has made it a symbol of the city for travelers worldwide.

  15. 15

    Xiqu Centre

    88 Austin Rd W, Tsim Sha Tsui

    This center is dedicated to preserving the art of Cantonese Opera with a curtain-like facade. The exterior is wrapped in over 10,000 curved aluminum fins that create a shimmering effect. To avoid vibrations from the high-speed rail, the Grand Theatre is suspended 30 meters high. This engineering feat leaves a large, open public plaza for the community at ground level. It represents a beautiful blend of contemporary technology and traditional Chinese cultural heritage.
