City of Sails

Auckland architecture

Auckland is uniquely built upon a volcanic field containing over 50 dormant volcanoes, many of which serve as beautiful public parks and viewpoints today. It is known as the 'City of Sails' because it has more boats per capita than any other city in the world, reflecting the residents' deep connection to the sea.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoAuckland Airport (AKL)

        The airport serves as a cultural gateway, featuring intricate Māori carvings at the international arrivals gate that traditionally welcome travelers to the 'Land of the Long White Cloud.' It has recently expanded its international terminal to include duty-free areas that showcase the best of New Zealand's wines and artisan products.

      • CurrencyNew Zealand Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish & Māori

        English is the main language, but you will see a heavy and proud influence of Te Reo Māori in place names and daily greetings. Most official communications and signs are bilingual to honor the country's indigenous heritage.

      • Getting aroundBritomart & AT Metro

        Britomart is a major transit hub and serves as the world's only underground diesel railway station. AT Metro operates a fleet of electric trains that connect the southern, western, and eastern suburbs to the city center. Auckland's extensive ferry network is a vital link for residents living on the North Shore and Waiheke Island.

      5 landmarks in Auckland

      1. 01

        Sky Tower

        72 Victoria St W, Auckland CBD, Auckland 1010, New Zealand

        The Sky Tower is an iconic telecommunications and observation tower that defines the Auckland city skyline. It stands as the tallest man-made structure in New Zealand and offers breathtaking views stretching up to eighty kilometers in every direction. The structure is designed to withstand extreme winds and remains a marvel of modern engineering within the southern hemisphere. Thrill-seekers can experience the SkyJump platform or walk across the glass-bottom floors high above the street level. It serves as the centerpiece of the SkyCity entertainment complex, featuring multiple world-class restaurants and a popular casino.

      2. 02

        Auckland Art Gallery Toi o Tāmaki

        Wellesley St E, Auckland CBD, Auckland 1010, New Zealand

        This award-winning facility is a stunning fusion of the original 1887 heritage building and a sophisticated modern expansion. The new wing is famous for its soaring timber canopies crafted from sustainably harvested native Kauri wood. These elegant wooden structures are supported by tapered columns that mimic the surrounding trees of Albert Park. Large glass walls blur the boundary between the interior galleries and the lush outdoor landscape. The gallery remains the largest art institution in New Zealand, housing an extensive collection of both national and international masterpieces.

      3. 03

        Auckland War Memorial Museum

        The Domain, Parnell, Auckland 1010, New Zealand

        This Neoclassical masterpiece is one of New Zealand's most significant heritage buildings and cultural landmarks. It sits prominently on the rim of Pukekawa, a dormant volcano located within the expansive Auckland Domain parkland. The building features a massive Greek-style portico and a striking copper dome that was added during a major renovation. Inside, it houses the world's most extensive collection of Māori artifacts and Pacific Island treasures. It serves a dual purpose as both a leading research museum and a solemn memorial to the nation's fallen soldiers.

      4. 04

        PwC Tower at Commercial Bay

        15 Customs Street West, Auckland CBD, Auckland 1010, New Zealand

        The PwC Tower is a striking contemporary addition to the Auckland waterfront and the city's premier commercial address. Its distinctive curved glass facade was specifically engineered to withstand the high-velocity winds coming off the Waitematā Harbour. The tower forms the vertical heart of the Commercial Bay precinct, which has revitalized the downtown connection to the sea. Inside, the building features state-of-the-art office spaces and an innovative sky lobby for its corporate tenants. It stands as a symbol of Auckland's modern economic growth and its transformation into a globalized Pacific hub.

      5. 05

        Britomart Transport Centre

        12 Queen Street, Auckland CBD, Auckland 1010, New Zealand

        This facility is a world-class example of adaptive reuse, located within the former Chief Post Office building. The grand Edwardian Baroque exterior has been meticulously restored to its 1912 glory while hiding a high-tech transit hub beneath. A modern glass atrium connects the historic stone structure to the surrounding urban plazas and retail lanes. The interior design utilizes local volcanic basalt stone to honor the unique geological heritage of the Auckland region. It successfully merges early 20th-century civic pride with the functional demands of a 21st-century international city.
