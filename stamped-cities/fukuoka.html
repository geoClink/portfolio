Hakata

Fukuoka architecture

Fukuoka is Japan's fastest-growing major city and the gateway to Kyushu island. It is famous as the birthplace of tonkotsu ramen — the rich, creamy pork bone broth that spawned countless regional variations across Japan and the world. Fukuoka's yatai (open-air food stalls) culture sets it apart from all other Japanese cities; rows of intimate stalls line the Naka River each evening, offering ramen, yakitori, and oden under a neon glow. The city also has close historical ties to China and Korea, reflected in its architecture, cuisine, and culture.

Before you go

  • Fly intoFukuoka Airport (FUK)

    Fukuoka Airport is uniquely situated just two metro stops from Hakata Station in the city center — one of the closest major airport-to-center distances in Japan. This extraordinary convenience makes Fukuoka one of Asia's most accessible cities. The airport handles both domestic flights to all major Japanese cities and international routes to Korea, China, Southeast Asia, and beyond.

  • CurrencyJapanese Yen (¥)
  • LanguageJapanese

    Standard Japanese is spoken alongside a local Hakata dialect (Hakata-ben), known for its distinctive vocabulary and warm, direct tone. The Hakata dialect has spread nationally through comedy and TV, giving Fukuoka a strong cultural identity. The city's proximity to Korea means Korean is heard frequently in the city's many Korean restaurants and among the Korean tourist community.

  • Getting aroundFukuoka City Subway & Nishitetsu

    Fukuoka has three city subway lines that efficiently connect the airport, Hakata Station, and the main entertainment districts. The private Nishitetsu railway extends to southern Kyushu cities. Hakata Station is also a shinkansen hub, with bullet trains reaching Osaka in under 2.5 hours and Tokyo in 5 hours. The compact, walkable city center makes many of the best food and cultural experiences reachable on foot.

5 landmarks in Fukuoka

  1. 01

    Fukuoka Castle

    2-1-1 Jonai, Chuo Ward, Fukuoka

    Also known as Maizuru Castle, this large flatland castle was completed in 1607 by Lord Kuroda Nagamasa and served as the seat of the Fukuoka domain for 270 years. Though largely dismantled in the Meiji era, the remaining turrets and stone walls form the centerpiece of Maizuru Park.

  2. 02

    Kushida Shrine

    2-1 Kamikawabata-machi, Hakata Ward, Fukuoka

    The most beloved shrine in Fukuoka, founded in 757 AD and dedicated to two deities of longevity and commerce. It is the center of the Hakata Gion Yamakasa festival, one of Japan's most energetic festivals, in which massive 12-metre festival floats are displayed in the shrine forecourt throughout July.

  3. 03

    Canal City Hakata

    1-2 Sumiyoshi, Hakata Ward, Fukuoka

    A large entertainment complex built in 1996 with a canal running through its center, designed by American architect Jon Jerde as a city within a city. Its postmodern design with curved facades, bold colors, and a performance water fountain has made it Fukuoka's most distinctive piece of contemporary architecture.

  4. 04

    Dazaifu Tenman-gū

    4-7-1 Saifu, Dazaifu, Fukuoka Prefecture

    A revered Shinto shrine established in 919 AD over the grave of Sugawara no Michizane, the deified god of learning and scholarship. It attracts over two million visitors each year, particularly students seeking academic success before examinations.

  5. 05

    Ohori Park

    1-2 Ohorikoen, Chuo Ward, Fukuoka

    A large urban park centered on a lake that was once the outer moat of Fukuoka Castle, redesigned in 1929. The park's central island is connected by three distinctive bridges, and the restored ruins of the castle are visible from the park's northern edge.
