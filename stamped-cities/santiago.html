City of the Island

Santiago architecture

Santiago is nestled in a valley surrounded by the towering, snow-capped Andes Mountains, providing one of the most dramatic city backdrops on Earth. It is a modern metropolis that boasts the Gran Torre Santiago, which remains the tallest building in South America.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoArturo Merino Benítez (SCL)

        The airport offers one of the most breathtaking arrivals in aviation as planes descend alongside the massive peaks of the Andes. A major expansion completed in 2023 added a dedicated international terminal, featuring a unique roof design shaped like the rolling waves of the nearby Pacific Ocean.

      • CurrencyChilean Peso ($)
      • LanguageSpanish

        Chilean Spanish is famously fast and incorporates a massive amount of local slang known as 'Chilenismos.' It is a vibrant, distinct version of the language that may take a moment for traditional learners to adjust to.

      • Getting aroundSantiago Metro

        The Santiago Metro is widely considered the most modern and efficient subway system in Latin America. The network features seven lines and uses a mix of rubber-tired and steel-wheeled trains to navigate the city. Red (formerly Transantiago) operates a massive fleet of electric buses, making Santiago a leader in green urban transit.

      5 landmarks in Santiago

      1. 01

        Gran Torre Santiago

        Avenida Andrés Bello 2425, Providencia, 7510689 Santiago, Chile

        Standing as the tallest building in South America, this skyscraper is a definitive landmark of the Santiago skyline. It forms the centerpiece of the Costanera Center complex and was designed to withstand the region's frequent seismic activity. The glass facade reflects the changing colors of the sky and the surrounding Andean peaks throughout the day. Visitors can ascend to the 'Sky Costanera' observation deck for a breathtaking 360-degree view of the entire metropolitan area. It serves as a hub for international business, housing high-tech offices and a world-class shopping mall at its base.

      2. 02

        La Moneda Palace

        Moneda S/N, Santiago Centro, 8320000 Santiago, Chile

        Originally designed as a royal mint, this neoclassical palace is now the official seat of the President of Chile. The building is renowned for its massive stone walls and pure architectural lines which have survived centuries of history. Its courtyards, such as the Patio de los Naranjos, offer a tranquil escape featuring orange trees and historic fountains. The structure has been a silent witness to the country's most significant political transitions and historical events. Beneath the citizen square in front of the palace lies a modern cultural center that hosts major international art exhibitions.

      3. 03

        Bahá'í Temple of South America

        Diagonal Las Torres 2000, Peñalolén, 7910000 Santiago, Chile

        This architectural marvel is composed of nine translucent glass and marble wings that curve toward the sky like a blooming flower. Located in the Andean foothills, the temple was designed to capture and filter natural light during the daytime hours. At night, the interior lighting makes the entire structure glow softly against the backdrop of the dark mountain range. The surrounding gardens are meticulously landscaped with native flora to provide a space for quiet reflection and meditation. It is the final continental temple to be built for the Bahá'í faith, representing unity and spiritual openness for all people.

      4. 04

        National History Museum

        Plaza de Armas 951, Santiago Centro, 8320000 Santiago, Chile

        Situated on the northern side of the Plaza de Armas, this building originally housed the Royal Palace of the High Court. Its most iconic feature is the vibrant yellow clock tower which has looked over the city center for over two centuries. The interior galleries guide visitors through the complex timeline of Chile from the pre-Columbian era to modern times. Its neoclassical facade and arched walkways serve as a prime example of colonial-era civic architecture in the capital. The museum provides a critical cultural space for preserving the heritage and identity of the Chilean people.

      5. 05

        GAM (Gabriela Mistral Cultural Center)

        Av. Libertador Bernardo O'Higgins 227, Santiago Centro, 8320136 Santiago, Chile

        Named after Chile’s first Nobel laureate in Literature, this massive complex is a center for performing arts and community life. The building is instantly recognizable for its extensive use of weathered copper-toned steel and large, inviting glass panels. It was originally constructed as a government headquarters before being reclaimed and transformed into a public cultural hub. The open plazas within the structure are frequently used by local youth as a space for dance and urban art rehearsals. It represents a successful fusion of 1970s brutalist foundations with sleek, modern architectural interventions.
