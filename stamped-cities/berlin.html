Grey City

Berlin architecture

Despite its inland location, Berlin has more bridges than Venice, with over 1,700 spans crossing its various rivers and canals. It is a city that wears its history on its sleeve, where the remains of the Berlin Wall stand as a powerful monument to its mid-century division and eventual reunification.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoBerlin Brandenburg (BER)

        The long-awaited Brandenburg Airport features a minimalist 'Bauhaus-style' design that focuses on clean lines and functional efficiency. The main terminal hall is framed by a massive glass facade and walnut wood accents, creating a modern gateway that finally unified all of Berlin's air traffic under one roof.

      • CurrencyEuro (€)
      • LanguageGerman

        German is the official language, though Berlin’s international population makes it a very English-friendly city. Locals are known for 'Berliner Schnauze,' a direct and often blunt way of speaking that reflects the city's gritty, honest character.

      • Getting aroundU-Bahn & S-Bahn

        The U-Bahn is the underground subway, while the S-Bahn primarily operates on elevated tracks throughout the city. Berlin's transit operates on an 'honor system' with no physical turnstiles at the station entrances. Yellow BVG buses and a comprehensive tram network in the east complete the city's integrated transit.

      15 landmarks in Berlin

      1. 01

        Reichstag Building

        Platz der Republik 1, 11011 Berlin

        This historic structure serves as the seat of the German Parliament. It is most famous for its large transparent glass dome added during a major renovation. This dome was designed to symbolize the transparency and accessibility of the democratic process. Visitors can walk up a spiral ramp inside the dome to look down upon the debating chamber below. It remains one of the most visited landmarks in Berlin due to its architectural blend of old and new.

      2. 02

        Brandenburg Gate

        Pariser Platz, 10117 Berlin

        This neoclassical triumphal arch is perhaps the most famous landmark in all of Germany. It originally served as a city gate but spent the Cold War era stranded in the 'death strip' between East and West. Today, it stands as a powerful symbol of European unity and peace. The structure is topped by the Quadriga, a chariot pulled by four horses driven by the Goddess of Victory. It serves as the grand backdrop for many of the city's most significant public celebrations and protests.

      3. 03

        Berliner Fernsehturm

        Panoramastraße 1a, 10178 Berlin

        At 368 meters, this broadcast tower is the tallest structure in all of Germany. It was commissioned by the GDR government and inspired by the aesthetic of the Sputnik satellite. The tower was intended to demonstrate the technical prowess and superiority of the socialist state. It features a rotating observation deck and restaurant that provides a full 360-degree view of the city. Today, it is an inseparable part of the Berlin skyline and a major tourist attraction.

      4. 04

        Jewish Museum Berlin

        Lindenstraße 9-14, 10969 Berlin

        This museum is considered a zig-zagging deconstructivist masterpiece of modern architecture. The building's design features 'voids,' which are empty spaces representing the absence of Jewish life following the Holocaust. Its exterior is clad in zinc plates that will naturally weather and change color over time. Inside, the disorienting axes and slanted floors are meant to evoke feelings of exile and loss. It stands as a profound architectural memorial to the history of Jews in Germany.

      5. 05

        Berliner Philharmonie

        Herbert-von-Karajan-Straße 1, 10785 Berlin

        This concert hall is world-renowned for its organic, tent-like shape and bright golden exterior. It pioneered the innovative 'vineyard-style' seating arrangement that places the orchestra in the center. This design ensures that every member of the audience has an intimate and clear view of the performers. The acoustics are considered some of the finest in the world, specifically tailored for symphonic music. It serves as the home for the legendary Berlin Philharmonic Orchestra.

      6. 06

        Neue Nationalgalerie

        Potsdamer Straße 50, 10785 Berlin

        This 'temple of light and glass' is one of the most famous examples of the International Style. It features a massive steel roof supported by only eight columns to create a vast, open interior space. The building is considered a pure expression of its architect's minimalist philosophy and obsession with geometry. Most of the museum's permanent collection is actually housed in galleries located underground. It recently underwent a meticulous multi-year restoration to preserve its original 1960s aesthetic.

      7. 07

        Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church

        Breitscheidplatz, 10789 Berlin

        The damaged spire of the original church was left in its ruined state as a reminder of the war. It is paired with a striking modern octagonal chapel made of thousands of blue glass blocks. This juxtaposition creates a powerful dialogue between Berlin's violent past and its hopeful future. The interior of the modern chapel is bathed in a unique, serene blue light during the day. It remains a symbolic landmark of peace and reconciliation located in the heart of West Berlin.

      8. 08

        Federal Chancellery

        Willy-Brandt-Straße 1, 10557 Berlin

        This building serves as the executive office and headquarters for the Chancellor of Germany. It is officially one of the largest government headquarters in the world, dwarfing even the White House. The design features a massive central cube and two long office wings that span across the Spree river. Its architecture is characterized by large circular windows and vast amounts of exposed white concrete. It forms part of the 'Band des Bundes,' a ribbon of federal buildings that symbolically links the two halves of the city.

      9. 09

        Corbusierhaus

        Flatowallee 16, 14055 Berlin

        This massive housing block was built for the 1957 Interbau exhibition near the Olympic Stadium. It is a classic example of the 'vertical city' living concept developed by its famous architect. The building contains hundreds of apartments designed to provide light, space, and greenery to all residents. Its brutalist facade is characterized by the use of raw concrete and primary colors on the balconies. Today, the building is a protected monument and remains a popular residence for modernist architecture fans.

      10. 10

        Shell-Haus

        Reichpietschufer 60, 10785 Berlin

        This building was Berlin's first steel-frame high-rise and is an icon of early modernist design. It is famous for its 'stepped' facade that creates a wave-like appearance overlooking the Landwehr Canal. The unique geometry of the building allows for maximum natural light to enter the office spaces within. Despite the damage it sustained during World War II, it was carefully restored to its original glory. It now stands as one of the most elegant and important office buildings from the Weimar Republic era.

      11. 11

        Apple Rosenthaler Straße

        Rosenthaler Straße 44, 10119 Berlin

        This retail store is located in the historic Scheunenviertel district as part of a modern building ensemble. It features massive floor-to-ceiling glass panes that allow for a seamless view into the minimalist interior. The design incorporates local 'Berlin' elements such as custom terrazzo flooring and sustainably sourced oak. It sits directly opposite the Art Nouveau Hackesche Höfe, creating a unique architectural dialogue. This placement highlights the contrast between 19th-century craftsmanship and 21st-century technological minimalism.

      12. 12

        Humboldt Forum

        Schloßplatz, 10178 Berlin

        This project is a massive reconstruction of the former Berlin Palace, the winter residence of monarchs. It is a 'dual' building featuring three faithful Baroque facades and one strictly modern facade. The interior houses several major collections, including the Ethnological Museum of Berlin. It stands as the most significant and controversial cultural project completed in 21st-century Germany. The site serves as a forum for art, culture, and science in the very heart of the city.

      13. 13

        Sony Center

        Potsdamer Platz, 10785 Berlin

        This futuristic complex at Potsdamer Platz is known for its iconic tent-like roof structure. The roof was designed to resemble the silhouette of Mount Fuji as a nod to the company's heritage. It is a masterpiece of fabric and glass engineering that covers a large central public forum. The complex helped transform the former 'death strip' into a bustling high-tech urban hub after the Wall fell. It remains a popular destination for visitors seeking cinema, dining, and modern corporate architecture.

      14. 14

        GSW Headquarters (Rocket Tower)

        Charlottenstraße 4, 10969 Berlin

        This tower is a landmark of ecological architecture located in the historic center of the city. It is famous for its narrow 'pillbox' shape and a facade featuring thousands of motorized sun-shades. These shutters are painted in vibrant shades of pink and orange to regulate the building's temperature naturally. The design includes a 'flying roof' that helps facilitate a natural convection system for office ventilation. It served as an early pioneer for sustainable skyscraper design in the late 20th century.

      15. 15

        James-Simon-Galerie

        Bodestraße, 10178 Berlin

        This building serves as the modern gateway and visitor center for the historic Museum Island. Its slender, bone-white colonnades pay homage to the classical language of the surrounding 19th-century museums. The structure is built atop a massive forest of concrete piles to stabilize it on the swampy ground. It functions as the central point for ticket sales and provides a cafe overlooking the Spree. Underground, it links the various museum buildings through a continuous Archaeological Promenade.
