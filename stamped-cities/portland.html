City of Roses

Portland architecture

Portland is home to Mill Ends Park, which holds the Guinness World Record for the smallest park in the world at just two feet across. The city is a hub for eco-conscious living, famous for its massive number of independent breweries, food carts, and the expansive Forest Park.

Before you go

  • Fly intoPortland Int'l (PDX)

    The airport's 'Geometric Carpet' gained a massive cult following, inspiring its own merchandise and becoming a viral backdrop for travelers' photos. PDX is frequently voted the best airport in America for its ease of navigation, local retail shops, and its commitment to being a bike-friendly transit hub.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    Portland's language is characterized by a soft, eco-conscious tone typical of the Pacific Northwest. Conversations are often peppered with vocabulary related to sustainability, coffee culture, and the great outdoors, reflecting the city’s 'Keep Portland Weird' philosophy.

  • Getting aroundMAX Light Rail

    The MAX light rail system includes the deepest transit station in North America at 260 feet underground. Portland Streetcars provide a frequent loop around the central city and the innovative Pearl District. TriMet buses and the WES Commuter Rail complete the region's robust public transportation network.

10 landmarks in Portland

  1. 01

    The Portland Building

    1120 SW 5th Avenue, Portland, OR 97204

    A landmark of Postmodern architecture, this building was a deliberate rejection of the minimalist glass boxes of modernism. It features a colorful facade with oversized decorative elements like stylized ribbons and a massive statue of 'Portlandia' above the entrance. The design was originally controversial for its small windows and dark interior spaces. In 2020, it underwent a massive renovation that replaced the exterior with a high-performance rainscreen while carefully preserving its iconic aesthetic. Today, it continues to serve as a key administrative hub for the city's municipal offices.

  2. 02

    Pioneer Courthouse

    700 SW 6th Avenue, Portland, OR 97204

    This is the oldest federal building in the Pacific Northwest and a central anchor for the city's downtown district. Built in the Italianate style, the structure features a distinct cupola that offers a historic silhouette against the skyline. It famously survived multiple demolition threats in the 1960s thanks to dedicated local preservation efforts. The building acts as the focal point for Pioneer Courthouse Square, which is often called 'Portland's Living Room.' It remains a fully functioning courthouse, serving as a chambers for the U.S. Court of Appeals.

  3. 03

    US Bancorp Tower (Big Pink)

    111 SW 5th Avenue, Portland, OR 97204

    Known affectionately as 'Big Pink,' this skyscraper is famous for its rose-colored granite and pink-tinted glass windows. Its unique 'parallelogram' footprint was specifically designed to maximize natural light and views for the tenants inside. It stands as one of the most recognizable figures in the Portland skyline due to its height and color. The building features a specialized lighting system that allows the entire exterior to emit a soft, warm glow at night. It remains a premier office location that houses many of the city's top financial and legal firms.

  4. 04

    Wells Fargo Center

    1300 SW 5th Avenue, Portland, OR 97201

    This skyscraper currently holds the title of the tallest building in the state of Oregon. The tower is a masterpiece of New Formalism, featuring striking vertical fins made of white marble and clear glass. It was constructed during a period of rapid urban expansion when Portland was redefining its downtown core. The building's lobby contains a historic stagecoach that serves as a tribute to the bank's heritage in the American West. A large public plaza and intricate fountain system surround the base, providing a peaceful urban retreat.

  5. 05

    PAE Living Building

    151 SW 1st Avenue, Portland, OR 97204

    This structure is recognized as the first developer-led 'Living Building' to be completed in the world. It is a marvel of sustainable engineering, designed to produce more energy and water than it actually consumes. The building is constructed using Mass Timber, a renewable resource that helps to significantly reduce its total carbon footprint. Its architectural design intentionally echoes the historic timber warehouses found in the surrounding Old Town district. This project serves as a global model for how modern buildings can positively impact their environment.

  6. 06

    Union Station

    800 NW 6th Avenue, Portland, OR 97209

    This Romanesque Revival landmark is easily identified by its 150-foot clock tower featuring neon 'Go by Train' signs. The interior is celebrated for its grand marble floors, high vaulted ceilings, and original mission-style benches. It has served as the primary gateway for rail travel in the city for well over a century. Despite the decline of national rail travel in the mid-century, the station has been beautifully preserved for future generations. Today, it remains a bustling hub for Amtrak travelers arriving in and departing from the Pacific Northwest.

  7. 07

    The Commonwealth Building

    421 SW 6th Avenue, Portland, OR 97204

    Originally known as the Equitable Building, this was the first skyscraper in the world to be clad in aluminum. It also pioneered the use of a completely sealed 'curtain wall' system integrated with a modern air conditioning system. This structure is considered a major milestone in modern architecture for its shift away from heavy masonry. Its minimalist grid design and glass facade influenced decades of office building construction around the globe. It continues to be a highly regarded example of the International Style within the city's architectural history.

  8. 08

    Moda Center

    1 N Center Court Street, Portland, OR 97227

    The Moda Center is the official home of the NBA's Portland Trail Blazers and many major concerts. This arena is architecturally famous for its unique 'saddle-shaped' roof that stands out in the Rose Quarter. The interior was engineered with a specialized acoustic ceiling to provide superior sound quality for live events. It features clear sightlines from every seat to ensure an optimal viewing experience for sports fans. The exterior plaza, named after legendary coach Dr. Jack Ramsay, serves as a popular gathering spot before games.

  9. 09

    Custom House

    220 NW 8th Avenue, Portland, OR 97209

    This grand Italian Renaissance-style building was once the center of Portland’s busy maritime trading industry. It features a U-shaped floor plan that was designed to maximize natural light and air for the customs officers inside. The exterior is constructed from massive blocks of granite and sandstone to convey a sense of federal permanence. Inside, visitors can find ornate plasterwork and grand marble stairs that have been meticulously restored. The building has recently been converted into modern creative office space to serve the city's growing tech sector.

  10. 10

    Tilikum Crossing

    Tilikum Crossing, Portland, OR 97201

    This bridge is a stunning cable-stayed structure that has become a modern symbol of Portland's transit values. It is famously the only major bridge in the country that does not allow private cars on its deck. The bridge is dedicated entirely to light rail, buses, cyclists, and pedestrians to encourage sustainable commuting. Its sleek white cables are equipped with LED lights that change color based on the river's conditions. These lighting patterns are programmed to reflect the Willamette River's temperature, speed, and overall depth in real-time.
