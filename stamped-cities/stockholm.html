Beauty on Water

Stockholm architecture

Stockholm is built on 14 islands where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic Sea, connected by over 50 bridges. It is the birthplace of IKEA, Spotify, Minecraft, Skype, and Mojang — giving it one of the highest concentrations of tech unicorns per capita outside Silicon Valley. The city's Tunnelbana (metro) is often called the world's longest art gallery, with over 90 of its 100 stations decorated with sculptures, mosaics, paintings, and installations by Swedish artists.

Before you go

  • Fly intoStockholm Arlanda Airport (ARN)

    Stockholm Arlanda Airport, located 40km north of the city, is Sweden's busiest international airport and Scandinavia's third largest. The Arlanda Express high-speed train connects the terminal to Stockholm Central Station in just 18 minutes, making it one of Scandinavia's most efficient airport links. The terminal complex is well designed for long layovers, with a hotel and extensive shopping built directly into the departure zones.

  • CurrencySwedish Krona (kr)
  • LanguageSwedish

    Swedish is a North Germanic language closely related to Norwegian and Danish — speakers of these three languages can largely understand each other. Stockholm's residents have some of the highest English proficiency in the world outside native-speaking countries. Sweden's tradition of lagom — the concept of just the right amount, not too much and not too little — shapes the city's understated, egalitarian social culture.

  • Getting aroundTunnelbana & SL Trams

    Stockholm's metro (Tunnelbana, or T-bana) is famous worldwide for its station art — over 150 artists transformed the bare bedrock tunnels into vibrant, site-specific art installations. The SL transit authority operates a seamlessly integrated network of metro, trams, commuter rail, and ferries, all covered by a single SL Access card. The Djurgårdslinjen heritage tram still runs between the city center and the museum island of Djurgården.
