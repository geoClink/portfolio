The Bear and the Strawberry Tree

Madrid architecture

Madrid is the highest-altitude capital city in the European Union, sitting at 667 meters above sea level — high enough that the city experiences notably cold winters and hot summers. The Prado Museum houses one of the finest collections of European art in the world, with masterworks by Velázquez, Goya, and El Greco. Madrileños are famous for their late-night culture: dinner rarely starts before 9pm, and nightlife continues well past midnight on any given day of the week.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoAdolfo Suárez Madrid-Barajas (MAD)

        Madrid-Barajas Airport is one of Europe's largest by land area, featuring four terminals including the celebrated Terminal 4 — designed by Richard Rogers and Antonio Lamela — whose undulating bamboo-colored roof and wave-like ceilings have won international architecture awards. The metro line connects all terminals to the city center in around 25 minutes.

      • CurrencyEuro (€)
      • LanguageSpanish

        Castilian Spanish (Castellano) as spoken in Madrid is considered the prestige dialect of the Spanish-speaking world, and what most learners study as standard Spanish. The Madrid accent is known for its distinctive pronunciation of 'c' and 'z' sounds — a feature not found in Latin American Spanish. The city is a major global crossroads where you will hear dozens of languages.

      • Getting aroundMetro de Madrid

        Madrid's metro system is one of the largest in the world with 13 lines and over 300 stations, making it the most comprehensive way to navigate the city. A single ticket covers the entire system, and the metro runs until 1:30am on weekdays and 24 hours on weekends — reflecting the city's famously late-night culture. The city's EMT bus network complements the metro with over 200 bus routes running through the night.
