The White City

Tel Aviv architecture

Tel Aviv is nicknamed the 'White City' because it contains over 4,000 buildings designed in the Bauhaus style, the largest collection of its kind in the world. It is a Mediterranean hotspot famous for its 24-hour nightlife, vibrant tech scene, and miles of golden sandy beaches.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBen Gurion (TLV)

    Ben Gurion is frequently cited as one of the most secure airports in the world, utilizing multiple layers of invisible security and advanced screening technology. Despite the high security, the airport is known for its efficiency and the beautiful 'Rotunda' in Terminal 3, which features a central indoor fountain.

  • CurrencyIsraeli New Shekel (₪)
  • LanguageHebrew & English

    Hebrew is the primary language, but English is spoken fluently by nearly everyone in this tech-forward city. You will find that most signage and menus are available in both languages.

  • Getting aroundDan Bus & Light Rail

    The newly launched 'Red Line' is the first high-tech light rail in Tel Aviv and features several underground sections. Dan and Egged buses provide the primary transit network, with most routes operating until the start of Shabbat. Israel Railways connects Tel Aviv to Jerusalem in just 34 minutes via a high-speed electrified line.

16 landmarks in Tel Aviv

  1. 01

    Tel Aviv Museum of Art

    Sderot Sha'ul HaMelech 27, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6423902, Israel

    The Herta and Paul Amir Building is a stunning geometric masterpiece featuring a complex, 'folded' stone exterior. Its most famous feature is the 'Lightfall,' an 87-foot tall spiraling atrium that bridges several levels of galleries. This architectural void allows natural sunlight to penetrate deep into the subterranean exhibition spaces. The building’s parametric design creates a series of unique, non-linear paths for visitors to explore modern and contemporary art. It has become a global symbol of Tel Aviv's commitment to cutting-edge architecture and cultural growth.

    Azrieli Center

    Derech Menachem Begin 132, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6701101, Israel

    This iconic skyscraper complex consists of three distinct towers based on the primary geometric shapes: a circle, a triangle, and a square. The Circular Tower is the tallest of the three and features an observation deck with panoramic views of the entire Mediterranean coast. Located at a major transportation hub, the center serves as the pulsing heart of Israel's business and tech sectors. At the base of the towers sits one of the busiest high-end shopping malls in the country. The rhythmic glass facades have made this trio the most recognizable silhouette in the Tel Aviv skyline.

  3. 03

    Crazy House

    HaYarkon St 181, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6345314, Israel

    This surrealist residential building is one of the most polarizing and unique structures along the Tel Aviv coastline. The street-facing facade is inspired by the desert, featuring white sand-colored textures and wavy, biological balcony shapes. In contrast, the rear facade facing the sea is covered in a massive hand-painted mural of trees and vines. The design was heavily influenced by the organic architectural style of Antoni Gaudí and the work of Friedensreich Hundertwasser. It remains a rare example of whimsical expressionism in a city otherwise dominated by clean, straight lines.

  4. 04

    Shalom Meir Tower

    Herzl St 9, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6688210, Israel

    Built in 1965, the Shalom Meir Tower was Israel's first skyscraper and remained the tallest in the Middle East for several years. It was controversially constructed on the site of the historic Herzliya Hebrew Gymnasium, a move that sparked the modern preservation movement in Israel. The tower features a distinctive facade covered in thousands of small Italian glass tiles that reflect the Mediterranean sun. Today, the building houses a mix of commercial offices and a museum dedicated to the early history of Tel Aviv. Its base is famously decorated with mosaic murals depicting the development of the 'First Hebrew City.'

  5. 05

    Pagoda House

    Nahmani St 12, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6579412, Israel

    The Pagoda House is a premier example of the Eclectic style that defined Tel Aviv’s early luxury architecture in the 1920s. It famously blends various cultural motifs, including Japanese-style roofs, Islamic pointed arches, and classical Greek columns. This was the first private residence in the city to be equipped with a functional passenger elevator. After years of neglect, the building was meticulously restored to its former glory by a private owner in the late 1990s. It stands as a centerpiece of King Albert Square, representing the adventurous spirit of the city's founders.

  6. 06

    Rubinsky House

    Shenkin St 65, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6523303, Israel

    Located on the vibrant Shenkin Street, this building is a pristine representative of the UNESCO-recognized 'White City.' It features the classic Bauhaus elements of functionality and simplicity, with a focus on adapting to the local climate. The signature 'thermometer' windows provide natural ventilation and light for the central staircase while maintaining privacy. Long, curved balconies offer residents shaded outdoor space and create a rhythmic, aerodynamic aesthetic on the facade. It remains one of the most photographed and well-preserved examples of the International Style in the Middle East.

  7. 07

    Opera Tower

    Allenby St 1, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6330101, Israel

    The Opera Tower stands on the historic site that once housed Israel's first Parliament sessions and the national opera. Its postmodern design is characterized by pink granite cladding and a series of cascading, rounded balconies. The building's base features a luxury shopping mall, while the upper floors contain high-end residential apartments. Its location at the end of Allenby Street makes it a major landmark for those navigating the beachfront promenade. The tower serves as a visual bridge between the historic city core and the modern recreational coastline.

  8. 08

    Great Synagogue

    Allenby St 110, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6581702, Israel

    Originally built in the 1920s, the Great Synagogue underwent a massive architectural transformation in the late 1960s. Architect Aryeh Elhanani added a soaring Brutalist concrete colonnade that completely wrapped the original eclectic structure. This dramatic addition was designed to modernize the building and provide it with a more monumental presence on Allenby Street. The interior remains grand, featuring beautiful stained glass windows that are replicas of those found in famous European synagogues. It stands today as both a religious center and a witness to the shifting architectural tastes of the city.

    Habima National Theatre

    Tarsat Ave 2, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6428301, Israel

    The national theatre of Israel underwent a total renovation to create a transparent, light-filled connection with the city. Its most striking feature is the massive glass-walled foyer that glows like a beacon at the end of Rothschild Boulevard. The complex includes a beautifully designed sunken public garden that offers a quiet escape from the surrounding traffic. As the intersection point of several major streets, the theatre serves as the cultural anchor of the White City. The design emphasizes openness and accessibility, inviting pedestrians into the world of performing arts.

  10. 10

    Design Museum Holon

    Pinchas Eilon St 8, Holon, 5845407, Israel

    Located just south of Tel Aviv, this museum is a bold masterpiece of contemporary structural design. The building is famously wrapped in five massive bands of Corten steel that change color over time due to weathering. These ribbons of steel function as both primary structural supports and an aesthetic envelope for the internal galleries. The museum was the first in Israel dedicated entirely to design, hosting international exhibitions and local innovation. Its fluid, organic form provides a sharp and inspiring contrast to the surrounding residential neighborhood.

    Apple Azrieli Sarona

    Derech Menachem Begin 121, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6701203, Israel

    This flagship retail location is characterized by its massive, single-span curved glass walls that blur the line between indoor and outdoor space. The interior features a luminous ceiling that mimics natural daylight, creating a bright and airy atmosphere for customers. It is strategically placed at the base of the Sarona Tower, acting as a gateway to the modern business district. The design pays homage to the surrounding historic Templer colony by maintaining a low, non-obtrusive profile. It represents the pinnacle of contemporary glass architecture within one of Tel Aviv's most diverse urban environments.

    Azrieli Sarona Tower

    Derech Menachem Begin 121, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6701203, Israel

    The Azrieli Sarona Tower currently holds the title of the tallest building in Israel at 238 meters. Its rectangular form features a dramatic twist that makes the building appear to shift its orientation as it reaches for the clouds. The high-performance glass facade reflects the Mediterranean sky and the surrounding city with crystal clarity. At its base, the tower integrates with the historic Sarona park, a restored 19th-century German Templer colony. This juxtaposition of ultra-modern engineering and historic preservation makes it a unique centerpiece of the city's new financial district.

    Tel Aviv City Hall

    Ibn Gabirol St 69, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6416201, Israel

    This prominent Brutalist structure overlooks Rabin Square, the city's most important public gathering space. The building's rhythmic concrete grid and recessed windows are designed to provide natural shade in the intense Israeli sun. For decades, it has served as the administrative headquarters for the municipality and a backdrop for the nation's history. The large public plaza at its feet has hosted everything from political demonstrations to massive Independence Day celebrations. It stands as a powerful symbol of the city's civic life and its mid-century modern architectural heritage.

  14. 14

    The White City Center (Liebling Haus)

    Idelson St 29, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6332414, Israel

    The Liebling Haus serves as a key cultural hub dedicated to the research and preservation of Tel Aviv's White City heritage. It is a perfect example of the International Style, featuring a functional flat roof and a clean, unadorned white facade. The recessed balconies are a specific local adaptation designed to provide shade while allowing cool breezes to pass through. Inside, the building houses exhibitions that tell the story of the architects who fled 1930s Europe to build this modern oasis. It remains a vital educational resource for understanding the architectural revolution that shaped the modern city.

  15. 15

    Sourasky Central Library

    Tel Aviv University Campus, Ramat Aviv, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6997801, Israel

    Situated in the heart of Tel Aviv University, this library is a masterpiece of Israeli Brutalist architecture. The building’s design features a striking 'floating' upper floor supported by a series of heavy concrete pillars and voids. Raw concrete is used throughout to create a quiet, contemplative atmosphere that is ideal for academic research. Its innovative use of light and space won the prestigious Rokach Prize shortly after its completion. It continues to be an essential landmark on campus and a favorite study spot for generations of Israeli students.

  16. 16

    Independence Hall (Dizengoff House)

    Rothschild Blvd 16, Tel Aviv-Yafo, 6688119, Israel

    Originally built as the home for Tel Aviv’s first mayor, Meir Dizengoff, this building is the site of Israel's foundation. It was here in May 1948 that David Ben-Gurion read the Declaration of Independence to a small, crowded room. The building’s transition from a residence to an art museum and finally a national monument reflects the city's evolution. Its exterior was renovated in the 1930s to match the prevailing International Style with clean lines and functional windows. It remains one of the most historically significant locations in the country, attracting visitors from around the globe.
