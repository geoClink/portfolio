Big D

Dallas architecture

Dallas is a city of bold ambitions, featuring the largest contiguous urban arts district in the United States. It is also a sports mecca, home to the 'America's Team' Dallas Cowboys and one of the fastest-growing metropolitan populations in North America.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoDallas/Fort Worth (DFW)

        DFW is so large that it is physically bigger than the island of Manhattan and even operates its own ZIP code and police force. It functions as a massive inland port and serves as the primary global hub for American Airlines, featuring five semicircular terminals.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        The local accent is a crisp North Texas drawl where the word 'y'all' is considered an essential and polite grammatical tool. Communication is generally expansive and enthusiastic, matching the 'bigger is better' philosophy that Dallas is famous for.

      • Getting aroundDART

        DART operates the longest light rail network in the United States with four distinct color-coded lines. The Trinity Railway Express provides a heavy-rail link between downtown Dallas and Fort Worth. The historic McKinney Avenue Trolley offers free, vintage streetcar service through the Uptown neighborhood.

      14 landmarks in Dallas

      1. 01

        Reunion Tower

        300 Reunion Blvd E, Dallas, TX 75207

        Affectionately known as 'The Ball,' this 171-meter observation tower is Dallas's most recognizable landmark. Its geodesic dome is illuminated by 259 LED lights that display custom patterns at night. The structure is part of the Hyatt Regency complex and offers 360-degree views of the North Texas landscape. Visitors can experience interactive touchscreens that provide details about the city's history and landmarks. It remains a premier destination for gala events and romantic dinners high above the city streets.

      2. 02

        Bank of America Plaza

        901 Main St, Dallas, TX 75202

        The tallest building in Dallas is instantly recognizable for the bright green argon lighting that outlines its silhouette at night. It was designed to maximize window space, resulting in a unique stepped-back facade that creates various floor plate sizes. It remains a focal point of the Dallas Central Business District and a symbol of the 1980s building boom. The tower's lighting system was recently upgraded to LED to allow for more complex color displays and energy efficiency. Its 72 floors house premier office spaces and sophisticated amenities for the city's business elite.

      3. 03

        Perot Museum of Nature and Science

        2201 N Field St, Dallas, TX 75201

        A bold, cube-like structure that appears to float over a landscaped plinth of Texas stone. The most striking feature is a 46-meter glass-enclosed escalator that juts out from the side of the building, offering views of downtown as visitors ascend. The building's exterior features textured concrete panels that mimic the geological strata found in nature. It was designed with sustainability in mind, utilizing LED lighting and a rain-collection system for irrigation. This 'living lab' serves to inspire curiosity through its innovative architecture and world-class scientific exhibits.

      4. 04

        Fountain Place

        1445 Ross Ave, Dallas, TX 75202

        A rocket-shaped tower of emerald green glass designed by the legendary I.M. Pei. The building's geometry changes constantly as you walk around it, shifting from a thin blade to a wide prism. Its base is a water-filled plaza featuring over 170 fountains, designed to provide a cool, serene escape from the Texas heat. This tranquil environment is surrounded by cypress trees, creating a park-like atmosphere in the heart of the city. Right beside it, a newer residential tower brings a fresh take on the original crystalline design.

      5. 05

        Dallas City Hall

        1500 Marilla St, Dallas, TX 75201

        A world-famous example of Brutalism, the building is shaped like an inverted pyramid leaning at a 34-degree angle. Each floor is nearly 3 meters wider than the one below it, providing natural shade for the floors beneath. It famously served as the headquarters for 'OCP' in the 1987 film RoboCop, adding to its pop-culture legacy. The massive concrete structure fronts a seven-acre plaza that features a large reflecting pool and iconic Henry Moore sculptures. This design was intended to represent a bold, forward-looking government that stands firm and accessible to its citizens.

      6. 06

        Old Red Courthouse

        100 S Houston St, Dallas, TX 75202

        A Romanesque Revival masterpiece built from rusticated red sandstone sourced from the Pecos region. It features intricate carvings of wyverns and other mythical creatures along its roofline, giving it a gothic, storybook appearance. Today, it stands as a striking historic contrast to the glass skyscrapers of the West End district. The building has been meticulously restored to house a museum detailing the rich history of Dallas County. Its towering clock tower remains one of the most photographed historical points in North Texas.

      7. 07

        The Adolphus Hotel

        1321 Commerce St, Dallas, TX 75202

        Built by beer tycoon Adolphus Busch, this grand hotel was once the tallest building in the entire state of Texas. Its ornate Beaux-Arts exterior is topped with a distinctively detailed turret that resembles a Germanic castle. It has hosted royalty, presidents, and celebrities for over a century, maintaining its status as a peak of luxury. The hotel is rumored to be one of the most haunted spots in the city, with local legends focusing on a 'ghostly bride.' Its opulent lobby and historic tea room continue to draw visitors seeking classic European elegance.

      8. 08

        Winspear Opera House

        2403 Flora St, Dallas, TX 75201

        A 21st-century interpretation of the traditional opera house, featuring a transparent glass facade that reveals the vibrant red interior drum. The massive solar canopy extends out over the surrounding plaza, creating a comfortable microclimate for outdoor pre-show gatherings. Inside, the horseshoe-shaped auditorium provides world-class acoustics and an intimate setting for performances. The building is a centerpiece of the Dallas Arts District, connecting several major cultural venues via a shared pedestrian park. Its retractable stage equipment and adjustable acoustics make it one of the most technologically advanced performance spaces in the world.

      9. 09

        Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge

        Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge, Dallas, TX 75212

        Though an infrastructure project, its 122-meter central white arch is one of Dallas's most significant architectural icons. It was the first vehicular bridge in the United States designed by Santiago Calatrava, featuring his signature skeletal style. The bridge was intended to spark urban renewal in West Dallas and has successfully become a gathering point for the community. Its 58 white cables create a rhythmic visual pattern against the skyline when viewed from the Trinity River levee. At night, the arch is illuminated, serving as a shining beacon that connects the downtown core to the western neighborhoods.

      10. 10

        John F. Kennedy Memorial

        646 Main St, Dallas, TX 75202

        This 'cenotaph,' or empty tomb, was designed by Philip Johnson as a place of quiet reflection and isolation. It consists of 72 white concrete columns that appear to float, surrounding a low granite square engraved with the President's name. The memorial is a stark, minimalist structure located just blocks from the site of the assassination at Dealey Plaza. Its design intentionally lacks a roof to symbolize the freedom of the spirit. Visitors enter through narrow openings to find a serene, enclosed space that blocks out the noise of the surrounding city.

      11. 11

        Morton H. Meyerson Symphony Center

        2301 Flora St, Dallas, TX 75201

        The third of I.M. Pei’s major Dallas works, this concert hall is globally renowned for its superior acoustic engineering. The design features a massive, swirling glass lens that wraps around the limestone structure to create a dramatic, light-filled lobby. This space serves as a 'social room' where the Dallas Arts District community gathers before performances. Inside, the wood-lined Eugene McDermott Concert Hall is designed to mimic the great halls of Europe. Every detail, from the overhead acoustic canopy to the marble flooring, was chosen to enhance the symphonic experience.

      12. 12

        Chase Tower

        2200 Ross Ave, Dallas, TX 75201

        Famous for its unique 6-story 'keyhole' opening near the top, this postmodern skyscraper is a standout in the skyline. The opening was designed to allow a view through the building to the sky, symbolizing transparency and lightness. The curved glass top and the massive portal make it one of the most distinctive silhouettes in the city. The building features a large rotunda at its base and a public plaza with sculptures and greenery. It remains a prime example of the 1980s architectural trend of creating iconic, 'signature' corporate headquarters.

      13. 13

        Apple NorthPark Center

        8687 N Central Expy, Dallas, TX 75225

        Located inside NorthPark Center, this store reflects the minimalist white-brick and natural light aesthetic of the mall itself. The mall is considered a museum-quality space that features a world-class art collection for public viewing. This specific Apple location uses high-reaching glass doors and clean lines to blend into the sophisticated environment. NorthPark is consistently ranked as one of the premier shopping destinations in the United States. The interior of the store provides a spacious, open floor plan that encourages hands-on learning and technical support.

      14. 14

        Apple Knox Street

        3101 Knox St, Dallas, TX 75205

        This street-level store is situated in the trendy Knox-Henderson district, known for its high-end boutiques and dining. The location follows Apple's modern 'Town Square' design, featuring a large glass storefront that seamlessly integrates with the sidewalk. It provides easy access to the nearby Katy Trail, making it a popular stop for urban explorers. The architecture emphasizes transparency and community, with an interior layout designed for workshops and events. It stands as a prime example of modern retail architecture that prioritizes pedestrian engagement and neighborhood character.
