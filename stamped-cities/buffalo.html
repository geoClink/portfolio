The Queen City

Buffalo architecture

Buffalo was the first city in the world to have widespread electric streetlights thanks to the renewable energy harnessed from nearby Niagara Falls. It is also the birthplace of the Buffalo Wing, which was first served at the legendary Anchor Bar in 1964.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoBuffalo Niagara (BUF)

        The airport serves as the primary international gateway for millions of tourists visiting Niagara Falls, located just a short drive away. It is known for its award-winning design and its ability to remain operational during some of the most intense lake-effect snowstorms in the country.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        Residents typically speak with a distinctive 'Inland Northern' accent characterized by very crisp, bright vowels. The tone of the city is famously neighborly, often referred to as the 'City of Good Neighbors,' where small talk with strangers is standard practice.

      • Getting aroundNFTA-Metro

        The light rail system is completely free to ride within the downtown 'Free Fare Zone' above ground. An extensive bus network connects the city to the famous Niagara Falls border crossing. The historic DL&W Terminal is being revitalized to serve as a major multi-modal transit hub.

      15 landmarks in Buffalo

      1. 01

        Buffalo City Hall

        65 Niagara Square, Buffalo, NY 14202, United States

        This towering Art Deco masterpiece remains one of the largest and most flamboyant municipal buildings in the United States. Its intricate exterior friezes and interior murals celebrate Buffalo's history as a major gateway to the West and a hub of industry. The building features a unique sun-burst themed observation deck on the top floor that offers panoramic views across Lake Erie all the way to Canada. Inside, the Common Council Chamber is bathed in natural light through a massive stained-glass skylight that highlights the room's grand geometry. It stands as a testament to the city's wealth and architectural ambition during the early twentieth century.

      2. 02

        Darwin D. Martin House

        125 Jewett Pkwy, Buffalo, NY 14214, United States

        This estate is considered one of Frank Lloyd Wright's most important and highly developed Prairie School works from his early career. The complex features a series of interconnected buildings, including the main house, a conservatory, and a stunning 100-foot pergola. A replica of the 'Nike of Samothrace' statue stands at the end of the long vista, emphasizing Wright’s focus on integrated art. Its horizontal planes, deep eaves, and nearly 400 pieces of art glass windows exemplify the principles of organic architecture. Today, the site has been meticulously restored and serves as a premier museum for architecture enthusiasts worldwide.

      3. 03

        Guaranty Building

        28 Church St, Buffalo, NY 14202, United States

        Designed by Louis Sullivan and Dankmar Adler, this structure is a pioneering example of the modern high-rise skyscraper. The building is world-famous for its revolutionary steel-frame construction that allowed for large windows and unprecedented height. Its exterior is clad entirely in ornate, salmon-colored terra cotta tiles featuring intricate geometric and floral patterns. Sullivan's philosophy that 'form follows function' is visible in the distinct division of the building's base, shaft, and decorative cornice. It remains a global architectural landmark and continues to serve as an active office building and interpretive museum.

      4. 04

        Richardson Olmsted Campus

        444 Forest Ave, Buffalo, NY 14213, United States

        This massive Medina sandstone complex is instantly recognizable by its distinctive twin towers and Romanesque arches. Originally built as a state asylum, it was a historic collaboration between legendary architect H.H. Richardson and landscape designer Frederick Law Olmsted. The campus is considered the premier example of the Richardsonian Romanesque style, emphasizing heavy masonry and grand proportions. After decades of neglect, the central towers were beautifully repurposed into a luxury hotel and cultural center. The surrounding grounds continue to be restored to reflect Olmsted’s original vision of a therapeutic park-like setting.

      5. 05

        Buffalo AKG Art Museum

        1285 Elmwood Ave, Buffalo, NY 14222, United States

        The museum's original 1905 building is a striking Greek Revival gallery featuring an iconic caryatid porch overlooking Delaware Park. A recent expansion added the 'Gundlach Building,' a transparent and wrap-around glass structure designed by architect Shohei Shigematsu. This modern addition creates a stunning visual contrast between the heavy stone of Neoclassicism and the light transparency of 21st-century Modernism. The museum houses one of the world's most impressive collections of modern and contemporary art, including works by Picasso and Pollock. Visitors can now enjoy an enclosed town square that connects the various wings of the campus through a vibrant social space.

      6. 06

        Electric Tower

        535 Washington St, Buffalo, NY 14203, United States

        Modeled after the temporary centerpiece of the 1901 Pan-American Exposition, this octagonal tower is a beacon of Buffalo's historic power. It was built with brilliant white terra cotta to celebrate the successful arrival of hydroelectric power from nearby Niagara Falls. The tower's wedding-cake tiers are illuminated at night with vibrant colors, serving as a landmark for the downtown district. It has long been the site of the city's annual New Year's Eve ball drop and fireworks celebration. The lobby maintains its historic Beaux-Arts charm while the upper floors have been modernized for professional office use.

      7. 07

        Buffalo Savings Bank (Gold Dome)

        545 Main St, Buffalo, NY 14203, United States

        This monumental bank building is capped with a massive forty-eight-foot diameter dome covered entirely in real gold leaf. Located in the heart of the theater district, it serves as a literal and gleaming landmark for the downtown area. The interior is just as spectacular, featuring a stunning mural on the ceiling that depicts the signs of the zodiac. Its Beaux-Arts design utilizes heavy granite and marble to project a sense of permanence and financial security. Even as banking technology evolves, the 'Gold Dome' remains a functioning branch and a symbol of Buffalo's golden age.

      8. 08

        Concrete Central Grain Elevator

        Buffalo River near Fuhrmann Blvd, Buffalo, NY 14203, United States

        Buffalo is the city where the steam-powered grain elevator was invented, forever changing the global agriculture industry. These massive concrete silos once formed the largest grain port in the world, processing millions of bushels annually. Their stark, functionalist design famously inspired iconic European modernist architects like Le Corbusier during the early 20th century. Today, these 'cathedrals of industry' are protected as historic landmarks and used for heritage tours and artistic light shows. They stand as rugged monuments to Buffalo's industrial past along the banks of the Buffalo River.

      9. 09

        Kleinhans Music Hall

        3 Symphony Circle, Buffalo, NY 14201, United States

        Renowned by musicians for its near-perfect acoustics, this concert hall is ingeniously shaped like the hull of a ship. The building was a rare collaboration between the famous father-and-son architectural team of Eliel and Eero Saarinen. Its minimalist, fan-shaped design ensures that every seat in the house has an unobstructed view and impeccable sound quality. The hall is surrounded by a reflecting pool that enhances its serene, modernist aesthetic within the Symphony Circle neighborhood. It continues to serve as the permanent home of the Buffalo Philharmonic Orchestra and remains a masterpiece of the International Style.

      10. 10

        St. Louis Roman Catholic Church

        35 Edward St, Buffalo, NY 14202, United States

        This historic church features a 245-foot open-work stone spire, which is the tallest of its kind in the United States. As a premier Gothic Revival landmark, the building is constructed from solid stone and decorated with intricate carvings. It was originally built to serve the city's rapidly growing German immigrant population during the late nineteenth century. Inside, the sanctuary contains a rare 1893 Kimball pipe organ and brilliant stained-glass windows imported from Europe. The church remains an active parish and a towering spiritual presence in the downtown Buffalo skyline.

        Apple Walden Galleria

        1 Walden Galleria, Cheektowaga, NY 14225, United States

        Situated within Western New York's largest shopping destination, this store embodies Apple's signature clean and modern aesthetic. The design is characterized by a wide, all-glass storefront that seamlessly connects the retail space with the mall's corridors. Inside, the store features backlit walls, oak tables, and a dedicated forum for educational 'Today at Apple' sessions. It serves as the primary technology and service hub for the entire Buffalo-Niagara metropolitan region. The layout is designed to encourage exploration and hands-on interaction with the latest hardware and software products.

      12. 12

        Old Post Office (ECC City Campus)

        121 Ellicott St, Buffalo, NY 14203, United States

        This stunning Gothic Revival structure is famous for its towering 244-foot clock tower that marks the downtown skyline. The interior boasts a massive central skylit atrium surrounded by five stories of elegant arched balconies and stone carvings. Originally serving as the city's main post office, the building was saved from demolition and extensively restored. It was brilliantly repurposed to serve as the city campus for the local community college, blending historic charm with education. Its Flemish-inspired details make it one of the most unique and beloved federal buildings in the country.

      13. 13

        Seneca One Tower

        1 Seneca St, Buffalo, NY 14203, United States

        Standing at 161 meters, this is the tallest building in both Buffalo and the Western New York region. Its dark, monolithic facade defines the city's skyline and represents the Brutalist architectural movement of the early 1970s. Recently, the tower underwent a massive transformation to become a vibrant tech hub and residential complex. The building is uniquely positioned directly over the city's light rail line, making it a centerpiece for transit-oriented development. It now features modern plazas, collaborative workspaces, and some of the highest residential units in the state outside of Manhattan.

      14. 14

        Statler City

        107 Delaware Ave, Buffalo, NY 14202, United States

        Originally built as a premier luxury hotel, the Statler is a masterpiece of Renaissance Revival architecture on Niagara Square. It was designed to mirror the grand scale of City Hall and provided Buffalo with a world-class venue for high-society events. The building is famous for its grand ballrooms, particularly the Golden Ballroom, which features opulent gold-leaf detailing and crystal chandeliers. After its time as a hotel ended, it was saved by local developers to serve as a major event and convention space. It remains a vital landmark that preserves the elegance and hospitality history of Buffalo's commercial center.

      15. 15

        Gardner's Cottage (Martin House Complex)

        118 Summit Ave, Buffalo, NY 14214, United States

        This modest yet beautiful structure is an integral part of the larger Darwin Martin House estate. Frank Lloyd Wright designed the cottage specifically for the estate's caretaker, ensuring it complemented the main mansion's aesthetic. It reflects the same signature Prairie School principles, such as a strong horizontal emphasis and an open floor plan. While much smaller than the primary residence, the cottage shows Wright's ability to apply his architectural genius to a more intimate scale. Today, it has been fully restored and is used as part of the public museum's educational and administrative space.
