The Spice Island

Zanzibar architecture

Zanzibar is a semi-autonomous archipelago off the coast of Tanzania, known for its white-sand beaches, turquoise waters, and the spice trade that made it one of the most valuable places on Earth in the 19th century. Stone Town, Zanzibar's historic capital, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site where Arab, Persian, Indian, and African architectural traditions blend in a labyrinth of narrow streets, carved wooden doors, and mosques. Freddie Mercury, the legendary frontman of Queen, was born in Zanzibar in 1946.

Before you go

  • Fly intoAbeid Amani Karume International Airport (ZNZ)

    Abeid Amani Karume International Airport, named after Zanzibar's first post-revolution president, is located just 6km from Stone Town. It handles a mix of charter flights from Europe, domestic flights from Tanzania's mainland, and regional African connections. The airport has undergone significant expansion to handle growing tourist arrivals and is increasingly busy year-round. Ferries also connect Zanzibar to Dar es Salaam on the mainland.

  • CurrencyTanzanian Shilling (TSh)
  • LanguageSwahili & Arabic

    Swahili (Kiswahili) originated on the East African coast and the Zanzibar archipelago played a central role in its development as a trade language. The Zanzibari dialect of Swahili (Kiunguja) is considered the prestige standard — it is the dialect used in formal Swahili media and teaching worldwide. Arabic influence is strong from centuries of Omani rule, and Arabic phrases are woven into everyday Swahili speech. English is spoken in the tourism industry.

  • Getting aroundDala-dalas & Tuk-tuks

    Stone Town is best navigated on foot — its narrow alleyways are too tight for cars in many places, and getting lost in the winding streets is one of the great pleasures of a visit. Dala-dalas (shared minibuses) serve routes to the island's beaches and villages. Tuk-tuks are the main tourist transport within and around the old town. Bicycles and scooters can be rented for exploring the island at your own pace. Traditional dhow boats still sail between the islands.

    House of Wonders

    Forodhani, Stone Town, Zanzibar City, Tanzania

    Beit el-Ajaib, the Palace of Wonders, built in 1883 as the ceremonial palace of Sultan Barghash, was the first building in East Africa to have electricity and an elevator. The four-storey structure is the largest building in Stone Town.

    Old Fort, Zanzibar

    Old Fort, Forodhani, Stone Town, Zanzibar City, Tanzania

    The oldest building in Stone Town, an Arab fort built between 1698 and 1701 by the Omani Arabs who expelled the Portuguese from Zanzibar. The fort's courtyard is now an open-air amphitheatre hosting the Zanzibar International Film Festival and cultural events.
