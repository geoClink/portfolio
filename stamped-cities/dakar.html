Cap-Vert

Dakar architecture

Dakar is the westernmost city in continental Africa, perched on the Cap-Vert peninsula that juts into the Atlantic. Gorée Island, just 3km offshore, was one of the most significant slave trading centers in West Africa from the 15th to 19th centuries and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site visited by dignitaries and descendants of the diaspora from around the world. Dakar is also the traditional finish of the Dakar Rally, one of the world's most grueling motorsport events.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBlaise Diagne International Airport (DSS)

    Blaise Diagne International Airport, named after the first African elected to the French parliament, opened in 2017 replacing the old airport. Located 47km east of the city center, it is connected by the TER (Train Express Régional) rapid rail line that opened in 2021 — one of West Africa's most modern rail links. The airport serves as Senegal's primary hub for international connections across Africa, Europe, and the Americas.

  • CurrencyWest African CFA Franc (CFA)
  • LanguageFrench & Wolof

    French is Senegal's official language, used in government and education, while Wolof is the true lingua franca spoken by over 90% of the population regardless of ethnicity. Wolof's influence has produced a distinctive Senegalese French that adds warmth and rhythm to everyday speech. Senegal's culture is defined by the concept of teranga (hospitality), an obligation of generosity toward guests that runs through all aspects of social life.

  • Getting aroundTER Train & Car Rapide

    Dakar's transport has been transformed by the TER (Train Express Régional), which connects the airport to the city center in about 45 minutes and continues to the suburb of Diamniadio. The city's iconic car rapides — colorfully painted Renault minibuses covered in religious slogans and decorations — have served Dakar for decades and are a cultural symbol, though they are gradually being replaced by modern Dakar Dem Dikk buses. Traffic congestion in Dakar is extreme during peak hours.

    African Renaissance Monument

    Route des Almadies, Dakar, Senegal

    A 49-metre bronze statue completed in 2010 on one of the two volcanic hills (the Mamelles) above Dakar, making it the tallest statue in Africa. It depicts a man, woman, and child emerging from a volcanic eruption.

  2. 02

    House of Slaves

    Goree Island, Dakar, Senegal

    The Maison des Esclaves on Goree Island, a 1776 coral-stone building that served as a transit point for enslaved Africans awaiting transport to the Americas. Its Door of No Return — a small doorway opening directly to the Atlantic — has become one of the most powerful symbols of the transatlantic slave trade.

    Grande Mosquée de Dakar

    Allees Papa Gueye Fall, Dakar, Senegal

    The Grand Mosque of Dakar, completed in 1964 as a gift from Morocco to newly independent Senegal, featuring a 67-metre minaret of white marble that is the tallest in West Africa. It can accommodate 7,000 worshippers.

    IFAN Museum of African Arts

    Place Soweto, Dakar, Senegal

    The Institut Fondamental d'Afrique Noire museum, established in 1938, houses one of the finest collections of traditional African art and ethnographic objects from across sub-Saharan Africa, with over 10,000 objects in its collection.

  5. 05

    Lac Rose

    Lac Rose, Sangalkam, Dakar, Senegal

    Also known as Lac Retba, a shallow salt lake north of Dakar whose extraordinary pink color is caused by Dunaliella salina algae that produce red pigment when stressed by the lake's extreme salinity — ten times saltier than the ocean. Salt collectors wade into the lake with wooden boats to harvest crystals.
