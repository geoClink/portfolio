City of Seven Hills

Istanbul architecture

Istanbul is the only city in the world that spans two continents, physically connecting Europe and Asia across the Bosphorus Strait. Throughout history, it has served as the capital of four major empires, leaving behind a rich architectural legacy that ranges from Byzantine domes to Ottoman palaces.

Before you go

  • Fly intoIstanbul Airport (IST)

    The massive Istanbul Airport features a striking air traffic control tower designed in the shape of a tulip, a traditional symbol of the city and Ottoman culture. As one of the largest airports in the world, it serves as a global mega-hub with a terminal area that could fit several football stadiums under one roof.

  • CurrencyTurkish Lira (₺)
  • LanguageTurkish

    Turkish is the official language and is central to the city's social fabric and tea-sharing rituals. While English is common in tourist areas like Sultanahmet, a few words of Turkish will be warmly received by local shopkeepers.

  • Getting aroundTünel & Ferries

    The Tünel is the second oldest subterranean rail line in the world, opening originally in 1875. Massive public ferries are a vital part of daily life, moving thousands of commuters between Europe and Asia every hour. The Metrobus system runs in a dedicated lane across the Bosphorus Bridge to bypass heavy road traffic.

13 landmarks in Istanbul

  1. 01

    Topkapı Palace

    Cankurtaran, 34122 Fatih/İstanbul, Türkiye

    The primary residence of the Ottoman Sultans for nearly four hundred years remains a sprawling masterpiece of imperial architecture. Unlike many European single-block palaces, Topkapı consists of a series of beautiful pavilions and lush courtyards. The Harem section features some of the finest examples of handmade Iznik tilework found anywhere in the world. Its Imperial Council Chamber displays the classical Ottoman transition from Byzantine influences to a distinct local aesthetic. Today, the complex serves as a massive museum housing the sacred Islamic relics and the famous Spoonmaker's Diamond.

  2. 02

    Dolmabahçe Palace

    Vişnezade, Dolmabahçe Cd., 34357 Beşiktaş/İstanbul, Türkiye

    As the largest palace in Turkey, it was built to replace Topkapı as the Sultan's main residence in the mid-19th century. The building represents the Ottoman Empire's strategic architectural shift toward Western European styles and influence. It features an incredible fourteen tons of gold on its ceilings and houses the world's largest Bohemian crystal chandelier. The grand facade stretches over six hundred meters along the scenic waters of the Bosphorus strait. This site also holds historical significance as the place where Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, the founder of modern Turkey, passed away.

  3. 03

    Maiden's Tower (Kız Kulesi)

    Salacak, Üsküdar Harem Sahilyolu No:40, 34668 Üsküdar/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This legendary tower is situated on a small natural islet at the southern entrance of the Bosphorus. Throughout the centuries, it has served various roles including a customs station, a lighthouse, and a quarantine ward. The current masonry version features a distinctive Ottoman-style dome and a viewing balcony for visitors. It remains one of the most romantic and frequently photographed landmarks in the entire city. Local folklore often tells the story of a princess who was hidden here to protect her from a predicted snake bite.

  4. 04

    Chora Mosque (Kariye)

    Dervişali, Kariye Camii Sk. No:18, 34087 Fatih/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This building is world-famous for possessing the most well-preserved Byzantine mosaics and frescoes in existence. Originally built as a monastic church, the architecture serves as a prime example of the Palaiologan Renaissance. The intricate interior depictions of the lives of Christ and the Virgin Mary are considered masterpieces of medieval art history. While it has transitioned between being a church, a museum, and a mosque, its artistic integrity remains intact. The structure’s multiple domes and brickwork reflect the sophisticated engineering of the Late Byzantine era.

  5. 05

    Haydarpaşa Terminal

    Rasimpaşa, 34716 Kadıköy/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This monumental railway station was built as the western terminus for the ambitious Baghdad and Hedjaz Railways. It features a distinct European-style facade and stained-glass windows that were crafted by expert German artisans. The building was designed as a diplomatic gift from Kaiser Wilhelm II to Sultan Abdulhamid II. To support its massive weight, the structure sits on a foundation of over one thousand wooden piles driven into the seabed. It remains a beloved symbol of Istanbul's Anatolian connection and a majestic gate to the city from the sea.

  6. 06

    Apple Bağdat Caddesi

    Bağdat Cd. No:342, 34728 Kadıköy/İstanbul, Türkiye

    Designed as a green pocket in a dense urban environment, this building creates a beautiful tree-lined public plaza. Because of the natural slope of the site, the store appears as a minimalist single-story pavilion from the main street. The interior features a massive thirty-six-foot glass curtain wall that provides views into a private rear garden. Two large skylights are integrated into the roof to provide natural ventilation and light for the floors below. The use of warm travertine cladding was specifically chosen to echo the traditional Turkish architecture of the region.

  7. 07

    Hagia Sophia

    Sultan Ahmet, Ayasofya Meydanı No:1, 34122 Fatih/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This building is widely considered the pinnacle of Byzantine architecture and a turning point in engineering history. Its massive dome remained the largest in the world for nearly a thousand years after its completion. The interior contains a unique blend of Christian mosaics and monumental Islamic calligraphy medallions. These layers represent the city's complex evolution as the capital of both the Byzantine and Ottoman Empires. Today, it stands as a UNESCO World Heritage site and continues to function as an active mosque for the community.

  8. 08

    Galata Tower

    Bereketzade, Galata Kulesi, 34421 Beyoğlu/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This high, cone-capped cylindrical tower serves as the most dominant feature of the Galata district skyline. It was originally constructed by the Genoese colony as a defensive watchtower at the highest point of their city walls. Over the centuries, it has been repurposed as a fire detection tower and even a temporary prison. The observation deck at the top offers visitors a breathtaking 360-degree view of the Bosphorus and the Golden Horn. It remains a central cultural hub surrounded by trendy cafes and historic narrow cobblestone streets.

  9. 09

    Süleymaniye Mosque

    Süleymaniye Mah, Prof. Sıddık Sami Onar Cd. No:1, 34116 Fatih/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This structure is widely hailed as the greatest masterpiece of the legendary imperial architect Mimar Sinan. It was designed as a comprehensive complex that originally included a hospital, a library, and a primary school. The building’s design perfectly harmonizes Islamic artistic principles with sophisticated Byzantine structural elements. Its interior is famous for creating a sense of immense light and open space through its clever window placement. The mosque sits on the Third Hill of Istanbul, providing one of the most iconic silhouettes in the city's skyline.

  10. 10

    Istanbul Sapphire

    Eski Büyükdere Cd. No:1, 34415 Emniyet Evleri, Kağıthane/İstanbul, Türkiye

    This high-tech skyscraper was once the tallest building in Turkey and a symbol of the city's modern expansion. It features an innovative vertical garden system that is integrated between its unique double-glass facades. The tower includes luxury residences, a large shopping mall at its base, and a high-speed elevator system. Its observation deck remains one of the highest in Europe, providing views that stretch to the Black Sea on clear days. The building's eco-friendly design uses natural ventilation to reduce the energy required for climate control.

  11. 11

    Atatürk Cultural Center (AKM)

    Gümüşsuyu, Taksim Sk., 34437 Beyoğlu/İstanbul, Türkiye

    Located in the heart of Taksim Square, this building is the premier symbol of modern Turkish high culture. The 2021 reconstruction pays homage to the original 1969 design while incorporating cutting-edge architectural technology. It features a massive, bright red ceramic hemisphere that houses a world-class Opera House. The front facade is a giant transparent glass curtain that allows the public to see the artistic energy inside. This complex revitalized the surrounding district by blending the bustling square with elegant performance spaces.

  12. 12

    Basilica Cistern (Yerebatan Sarnıcı)

    Alemdar, Yerebatan Cd. 1/3, 34110 Fatih/İstanbul, Türkiye

    Often called the Sunken Palace, this is the largest of several hundred ancient cisterns located beneath the city. It features three hundred and thirty-six marble columns, many of which were salvaged from ancient ruined temples. Two of these columns are famous for having bases carved into the likeness of the Gorgon Medusa. The reflection of the columns in the shallow water creates a haunting and cathedral-like atmosphere for visitors. It stands as a brilliant example of Byzantine subterranean engineering that once provided water to the Great Palace.

  13. 13

    Apple Zorlu Center

    Zorlu Center, Levazım Mah. Koru Sok. No:2, 34340 Beşiktaş/İstanbul, Türkiye

    Nicknamed the Glass Lantern, this was the first Apple Store globally to be designed by Foster + Partners. The retail space is almost entirely subterranean and is topped by a minimalist glass cube floating in water. Four massive panes of structural glass are held together without any visible metal joins or fixings. This design allows natural daylight to flood down into the two levels below through a signature glass staircase. It is widely cited as a masterclass in modern structural glass engineering and minimalist architectural design.
