The Big Smoke

London architecture

London is a global cultural powerhouse that hosts over 170 museums, many of which offer free admission to the public year-round. Beneath its modern skyline lies a layer of Roman history, with parts of the original city wall still standing near the Tower of London.

Before you go

  • Fly intoHeathrow (LHR)

    Heathrow is a pioneer in aviation technology, featuring a fleet of autonomous, driverless 'PODS' that transport passengers between Terminal 5 and the business car parks. It remains one of the most connected airports on the planet, serving as a primary gateway for international flights from every corner of the globe.

  • CurrencyPound Sterling (£)
  • LanguageEnglish

    London is a linguistic hub where English is spoken in a vast array of accents, from traditional Cockney to the modern 'Multicultural London English.' It is a city where over 300 languages are spoken daily, making it one of the most diverse places on Earth.

  • Getting aroundThe Tube

    The London Underground, or 'The Tube,' was the first subterranean railway system ever built. The iconic red double-decker buses provide exhaustive coverage of the city's surface streets. The Elizabeth Line is the newest addition, offering high-speed cross-London travel in state-of-the-art trains.

  1. 01

    The Shard

    32 London Bridge St, London SE1 9SG, United Kingdom

    Designed as a 'vertical city,' The Shard stands as the tallest building in Western Europe. Its pyramid shape is composed of 11,000 glass panels that are specially angled to reflect the London sky. This means the building's color shifts dynamically with the changing weather and seasons. The glass 'shards' at the very top do not touch, allowing the structure to breathe naturally and ventilate heat. It sits directly atop London Bridge Station and houses premium offices, luxury residences, and a world-class hotel.

  2. 02

    St. Paul's Cathedral

    St. Paul's Churchyard, London EC4M 8AD, United Kingdom

    A masterpiece of the English Baroque style, St. Paul's dome has dominated the London skyline for over 300 years. The 'Whispering Gallery' inside the dome is world-famous for its unique acoustic properties. It remained the tallest building in London until 1963 and served as a beacon of hope during the Blitz. The cathedral has hosted many significant national events, including royal weddings and state funerals. Its intricate stonework and massive scale represent the architectural genius of Sir Christopher Wren.

  3. 03

    The Gherkin

    30 St Mary Axe, London EC3A 8BF, United Kingdom

    Formally known as 30 St Mary Axe, this was London's first truly environmentally progressive skyscraper. Its aerodynamic, cigar-like shape minimizes wind loads and allows for a highly efficient natural ventilation system. The gaps in each floor create a 'giant chimney' effect that pulls air through the building to reduce energy use. Remarkably, the only piece of curved glass on the entire building is the lens cap at the very top. This summit offers a stunning 360-degree view of the city's bustling financial district.

  4. 04

    Tower of London

    Tower Hill, London EC3N 4AB, United Kingdom

    This historic castle was founded by William the Conqueror and has served as a fortress, palace, and prison. The 'White Tower' at its center is the oldest part of the complex and was built to exert Norman authority. It is famously protected by the Yeoman Warders and currently houses the priceless Crown Jewels of the United Kingdom. Local legend states that if the resident ravens ever leave the Tower, the Kingdom of England will fall. Today, it stands as a UNESCO World Heritage site and a primary destination for history enthusiasts.

  5. 05

    Palace of Westminster

    Westminster, London SW1A 0AA, United Kingdom

    The seat of the British Parliament, this Gothic Revival masterpiece is most famous for its Elizabeth Tower. The tower houses the massive 'Big Ben' bell, which chimes on the hour across the capital. Its elaborate facade features an intricate display of golden stonework and statues representing English monarchs. While it looks like a relic of the Middle Ages, it was built with advanced Victorian engineering after a fire in 1834. It remains the most recognizable symbol of British political power and democratic history.

  6. 06

    Lloyd's Building

    1 Lime St, London EC3M 7HA, United Kingdom

    This structure is a leading example of 'Inside-Out' architecture, where functional services are located on the exterior. By placing stairs, elevators, and pipes on the outside, the architect maximized the available space for the interior markets. The High-Tech landmark is clad in gleaming stainless steel and glass to create a futuristic industrial aesthetic. At its heart lies the Underwriting Room, which houses the historic Lutine Bell used for shipping announcements. It remains one of the most daring and unique architectural statements in the City of London.

  7. 07

    The Walkie Talkie

    20 Fenchurch St, London EC3M 8AF, United Kingdom

    Officially located at 20 Fenchurch Street, this building is famous for its top-heavy design that expands as it rises. Its unique shape was specifically intended to maximize the high-value floor space on the upper levels. The top three floors house the 'Sky Garden,' which is London's highest public park and observation deck. The building's concave glass famously required sunshades after it concentrated solar rays onto the street below during construction. It offers a distinctive profile that has permanently altered the skyline of the financial district.

  8. 08

    Battersea Power Station

    Circus Rd W, Nine Elms, London SW11 8AL, United Kingdom

    Once a coal-fired plant providing 20% of London's electricity, this Art Deco icon is a global landmark. It is instantly recognizable by its four white chimneys and its iconic appearance on Pink Floyd's 'Animals' album cover. After decades of standing vacant, it has been transformed into a massive retail, dining, and residential hub. Visitors can now take an elevator called 'Lift 109' to the top of one chimney for panoramic views. The restoration has preserved the original industrial character while modernizing the space for 21st-century use.

  9. 09

    Tate Modern

    Bankside, London SE1 9TG, United Kingdom

    Housed in the former Bankside Power Station, the Tate Modern is a premier example of industrial adaptive reuse. Its massive Turbine Hall serves as a dramatic entrance and a space for large-scale contemporary art installations. In 2016, the 'Blavatnik Building' was added to the site as a twisting, perforated brick pyramid. The building’s somber brickwork and central vertical chimney stand as a stark monument on the South Bank. It is now one of the most visited modern art galleries in the entire world.

  10. 10

    Natural History Museum

    Cromwell Rd, South Kensington, London SW7 5BD, United Kingdom

    Often called a cathedral of nature, this building is famous for its ornate Romanesque architecture and terracotta facade. The design features intricate carvings of plants and animals, categorizing species as either living or extinct. The Hintze Hall features soaring arches and a massive blue whale skeleton suspended from the ceiling. It remains one of the most majestic indoor spaces in the United Kingdom and a center for scientific research. The museum attracts millions of visitors annually to explore its vast collection of biological and geological specimens.

  11. 11

    Apple London Headquarters

    Battersea Power Station, Kirtling St, London SW11 8AL, United Kingdom

    Occupying six floors within the historic Boiler House of the Battersea Power Station, this is Apple's primary UK hub. The office design is a feat of adaptive reuse, preserving the original 1930s industrial brickwork and steel. It features a futuristic, sun-drenched workspace organized around a massive central atrium. The entire facility is powered by 100% renewable energy to meet modern sustainability standards. It serves as the centerpiece for the massive South Bank regeneration project in the heart of London.

  12. 12

    The Barbican Centre

    Silk St, Barbican, London EC2Y 8DS, United Kingdom

    A landmark of Brutalist architecture, the Barbican was built on a site devastated by WWII bombings. It acts as a 'city within a city,' featuring towering concrete pillars and elevated pedestrian walkways. The complex includes a hidden tropical conservatory that is the second largest in London. Its rough, pick-hammered concrete finish is an iconic example of 20th-century utopian urban planning. Today, it is a premier arts center housing concert halls, theaters, and thousands of residents.

    Buckingham Palace

    Buckingham Palace, London SW1A 1AA, United Kingdom

    This building serves as the administrative headquarters and primary residence of the British Monarch. While it has roots in the 1700s, the iconic Neoclassical front facade was added in 1913. It features 775 grand rooms and the largest private garden in the city of London. The palace is a masterclass in the French Neoclassical style, designed to project national grandeur. It remains the site of the famous Changing of the Guard ceremony and numerous state receptions.

  14. 14

    Apple Regent Street

    235 Regent St, London W1B 2EL, United Kingdom

    This was Apple’s first store in Europe and was extensively reimagined in 2016 to modernize the space. It features a grand Grade II listed facade made of traditional Portland stone. Inside, the design removed the mezzanine to create a 'Town Square' feel with a luminous ceiling. The store includes indoor trees and a grand stone staircase that connects the two shopping floors. It represents a perfect bridge between London’s historic shopping heritage and Apple’s minimalist design language.
