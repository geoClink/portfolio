The Pearl of the Far East

Ho Chi Minh City architecture

Ho Chi Minh City — still widely known by its former name, Saigon — is Vietnam's largest and most energetic city. An estimated 8 million motorbikes share its streets, creating a constant fluid river of two-wheelers that visitors must learn to navigate on foot by walking slowly and steadily into traffic. The city's café culture is legendary — Vietnamese iced coffee (cà phê sữa đá), made with strong robusta beans and sweetened condensed milk, is one of the world's great coffee drinks.

Before you go

  • Fly intoTan Son Nhat International Airport (SGN)

    Tan Son Nhat Airport, still identified by the IATA code SGN from its days as Saigon, is Vietnam's busiest airport. It sits remarkably close to the city center, just a few kilometers from District 1, making the taxi ride in one of the shortest of any major Asian city. A long-planned second international airport at Long Thanh is under construction to handle the country's explosive growth in air travel.

  • CurrencyVietnamese Dong (₫)
  • LanguageVietnamese

    Vietnamese is a tonal language with six distinct tones, meaning the same syllable spoken at different pitches carries entirely different meanings. It uses a Latin-based alphabet (quốc ngữ) developed by 17th-century missionaries, making it more immediately readable to Western visitors than most Southeast Asian scripts. Southern Vietnamese as spoken in Ho Chi Minh City has a warmer, more melodic quality than the northern Hanoi dialect.

  • Getting aroundBuses & Grab Motorbikes

    Ho Chi Minh City's public bus network covers the entire city, but the most practical transport for visitors is Grab — Southeast Asia's dominant ride-hailing app — which offers both car rides and motorbike taxis. Metro Line 1 connecting Bến Thành Market to the eastern suburbs opened in late 2024, marking a landmark step in modernizing the city's transit infrastructure.
