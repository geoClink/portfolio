The District

Washington D.C. architecture

Washington D.C. has a strict height limit on buildings, ensuring that no structure is taller than the width of the adjacent street to keep the monuments as the focal point of the skyline. It is the only city in the world designed specifically to be a national capital, featuring grand avenues and massive green spaces like the National Mall.

Before you go

  • Fly intoDulles International (IAD)

    The Eero Saarinen-designed terminal at Dulles is considered a masterpiece of mid-century modernism, featuring a dramatic suspended roof that resembles a bird in flight. It serves as the primary international gateway for the U.S. capital and is famous for its 'Mobile Lounges' that transport passengers between terminals.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    Communication in the District is highly professional and often peppered with political, legal, and military acronyms. Conversations here are fast-paced and efficient, reflecting a culture deeply rooted in global governance, law, and international diplomacy.

  • Getting aroundThe Metro

    The Washington Metro features the Wheaton station escalator, which is the longest in the Western Hemisphere. Six color-coded rail lines connect the federal district to the Maryland and Virginia suburbs. The DC Circulator provides specialized bus service to the most popular tourist and business destinations.

11 landmarks in Washington D.C.

  1. 01

    The United States Capitol

    First St SE, Washington, DC 20004

    The seat of the U.S. Congress is a pinnacle of Neoclassical architecture. Its most iconic feature is the massive cast-iron dome, which was completed during the Civil War. Atop this dome stands the 'Statue of Freedom' looking out over the city. The building’s design was inspired by the Louvre and the Pantheon to symbolize a democratic republic. It remains a global icon of legislative power and architectural grandeur.

  2. 02

    Apple Carnegie Library

    801 K St NW, Washington, DC 20001

    This site is a profound revitalization of Washington D.C.’s first public library and first desegregated public building. The Beaux-Arts landmark was meticulously restored to its original grandeur using locally sourced Vermont marble. The heart of the building is a soaring double-height 'Forum' capped by a new glass skylight. Natural light floods the interior to create a modern retail experience within a historic shell. The site also houses the DC History Center to continue the building's legacy of learning.

  3. 03

    The White House

    1600 Pennsylvania Avenue NW, Washington, DC 20500

    Constructed of white-painted Aquia Creek sandstone, this Executive Mansion has housed every U.S. president since John Adams. The design is famously based on Leinster House in Dublin, Ireland. Despite its global fame, the building remains relatively low-profile compared to sprawling European palaces. This architectural choice was intended to reflect the modesty of the executive branch in a new democracy. The structure continues to serve as both a private residence and a global diplomatic hub.

  4. 04

    Library of Congress (Jefferson Building)

    101 Independence Ave SE, Washington, DC 20540

    This is a premier example of the Beaux-Arts style and is known for its incredible interior opulence. The Great Hall features marble floors, stained glass, and intricate murals throughout the space. It was designed to show that the United States could produce a building as grand as any European cathedral. The library houses over 170 million items, including a rare Gutenberg Bible. It serves as the research arm of Congress and the largest library in the world.

  5. 05

    National Museum of African American History and Culture

    1400 Constitution Ave NW, Washington, DC 20560

    This modern masterpiece on the National Mall features a distinctive 'Corona' exterior. The facade is made of 3,600 bronze-colored cast-aluminum panels inspired by traditional Yoruba ironwork. Its unique shape mimics a West African crown to celebrate heritage and history. The design is highly sustainable, using the intricate lattice to filter light and heat efficiently. This building provides a powerful visual narrative of the African American experience.

  6. 06

    The Pentagon

    1400 Defense Pentagon, Arlington, VA 22202

    This is the world's largest office building by floor area, featuring five concentric rings. Built during WWII in just 16 months, its shape was dictated by the existing road layout. Despite having 17.5 miles of corridors, the 'spoke' design allows for efficient movement. Any two points in the building can be reached on foot in under seven minutes. It stands as a massive concrete symbol of the United States military power.

  7. 07

    National Cathedral

    3101 Wisconsin Ave NW, Washington, DC 20016

    As the sixth-largest cathedral in the world, this structure is built entirely of solid Indiana limestone. It is a true Neo-Gothic structure utilizing traditional flying buttresses and ribbed vaults. The building is famous for its 'Space Window,' which contains an actual piece of moon rock. Visitors often search for the gargoyle of Darth Vader tucked high on the northwest tower. It remains a premier example of Gothic craftsmanship in the modern era.

  8. 08

    Hirshhorn Museum

    Independence Ave & 7th St SW, Washington, DC 20560

    This bold, circular Brutalist structure appears to hover above the ground on four massive concrete piers. Often described as a 'hollow drum,' the windowless exterior protects delicate art from direct sunlight. The interior ring features floor-to-ceiling glass looking into a quiet central courtyard. It provides a stark, modern contrast to the classical buildings found elsewhere on the Mall. The building itself is considered a primary work of art within the Smithsonian collection.

  9. 09

    Union Station DC

    50 Massachusetts Ave NE, Washington, DC 20002

    This monumental gateway was designed in the Beaux-Arts style to rival the ancient baths of Rome. The Main Hall features a 96-foot barrel-vaulted ceiling decorated with 70 pounds of gold leaf. It was a major project of the McMillan Plan to create a grand civic entrance. The station successfully cleared out smaller rail depots to centralize the city's transit. Today, it stands as a bustling hub for travelers, shoppers, and diners alike.

  10. 10

    The Watergate Complex

    2650 Virginia Ave NW, Washington, DC 20037

    This group of six interconnected buildings is known for curving, tooth-like balconies and avant-garde design. While most famous for the 1972 political scandal, it was the first 'integrated' development in the city. The project combined office, residential, and retail spaces into a single cohesive environment. Its fluid lines were specifically meant to mimic the movement of the nearby Potomac River. It remains a controversial yet iconic example of Italian modernist architecture in America.

  11. 11

    National Gallery of Art (East Building)

    4th St & Constitution Ave NW, Washington, DC 20565

    This building is widely considered a masterpiece of geometric modernism. It is composed of two triangular prisms connected by a vast, light-filled central atrium. Because the site was an irregular trapezoid, the architect used a 19-degree angle as a recurring motif. The exterior is clad in Tennessee pink marble to match the adjacent West Building. It serves as a sharp, angular home for the museum's modern art collection.
