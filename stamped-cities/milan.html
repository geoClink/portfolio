Fashion Capital

Milan architecture

The stunning Duomo di Milano, the city's centerpiece, took nearly 600 years to complete and is adorned with over 3,400 statues. While famous for high fashion, Milan is also Italy's financial heart and home to Leonardo da Vinci’s iconic mural, 'The Last Supper.'

Before you go

  • Fly intoMalpensa (MXP)

    Malpensa serves as a high-end gateway to Italy, featuring a 'Luxury Square' filled with flagship boutiques from famous Italian fashion houses. It is strategically located near the Alps, offering travelers a beautiful mountain view as they transit through the airport's spacious, modern terminals.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageItalian

    Italian is the main language, spoken here with a crisp Northern efficiency that reflects the city’s business-driven culture. English is very common in the fashion and financial districts, though Italian remains the language of the heart and the kitchen.

  • Getting aroundMilan Trams

    Milan still operates a fleet of 'Type 1928' trams that feature their original wooden bench seating. The Milan Metro consists of five lines, including the newest fully automated M4 line. Trenord commuter trains connect the city center to the beautiful Italian Lake District and suburban Lombardy.

16 landmarks in Milan

  1. 01

    Duomo di Milano

    Piazza del Duomo, 20122 Milano MI, Italy

    This iconic pink-marble Gothic cathedral took nearly six centuries to reach completion. It stands as the largest church in Italy and the third-largest in the world. The exterior is famously adorned with 135 slender spires and over 3,400 individual statues. Visitors can walk upon the rooftop terraces to see the golden Madonnina statue guarding the city. It remains the spiritual and geographic heart of Milan, drawing millions of pilgrims and tourists alike.

  2. 02

    Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II

    Piazza del Duomo, 20121 Milano MI, Italy

    Often called 'Milan's Drawing Room,' this is one of the world's oldest and most beautiful shopping malls. The structure is celebrated for its massive iron and glass vaulted ceilings and a central octagonal dome. Intricate mosaic floors decorate the walkways, including the famous tile bull that locals spin on for good luck. It connects the Piazza del Duomo to the Teatro alla Scala through a grand Neo-Renaissance arcade. Today, it houses high-end luxury retailers and some of the city's most historic cafes and restaurants.

  3. 03

    Bosco Verticale

    Via Gaetano de Castillia, 11, 20124 Milano MI, Italy

    The 'Vertical Forest' consists of two residential towers that have redefined sustainable urban living. The buildings are covered in over 800 trees and 15,000 perennial plants distributed across their staggered balconies. This living facade acts as a natural filter for sunlight and helps reduce urban carbon dioxide levels. The vegetation also serves as a microclimate that regulates humidity and protects residents from acoustic pollution. It has become a global symbol of how nature can be integrated into high-density modern architecture.

  4. 04

    Torre Velasca

    Piazza Velasca, 5, 20122 Milano MI, Italy

    This striking brutalist skyscraper is famous for its unique silhouette that resembles a medieval watchtower. The flared 'mushroom' top was a deliberate design choice intended to echo the historical fortifications of old Milan. It was built during the post-war reconstruction period as a modern interpretation of the traditional Lombardy architectural style. The structure houses a mix of commercial offices on the lower floors and private apartments in the expanded upper section. Despite initial controversy, it is now protected as a significant landmark of 20th-century Italian design.

  5. 05

    Fondazione Prada

    Largo Isarco, 2, 20139 Milano MI, Italy

    This art and culture center is located within a repurposed gin distillery dating back to the 1910s. It features the famous 'Haunted House,' a four-story building entirely clad in shimmering 24-karat gold leaf. The architectural design by Rem Koolhaas seamlessly blends industrial heritage with bold contemporary structures. Various exhibition spaces are connected by a series of courtyards that encourage public interaction and exploration. It stands as a prime example of how Milanese industrial sites can be transformed into world-class cultural hubs.

  6. 06

    Pirelli Tower

    Via Fabio Filzi, 22, 20124 Milano MI, Italy

    Commonly known as 'Il Pirellone,' this was the first skyscraper to be built in Italy. Its elegant hexagonal profile and slim glass-and-steel facade made it an instant icon of the Italian economic miracle. The building's innovative structural design heavily influenced the architecture of the Pan Am Building in New York City. For decades, it remained the tallest building in Italy and a symbol of Milan's status as a center of industry. It now serves as the administrative headquarters for the regional government of Lombardy.

  7. 07

    Castello Sforzesco

    Piazza Castello, 20121 Milano MI, Italy

    This massive brick fortress was once the primary seat of the powerful Dukes of Milan. The complex features impressive defensive walls, grand courtyards, and a distinctive central clock tower. Leonardo da Vinci himself contributed to the interior decorations of several rooms during the Renaissance. In the 20th century, the BBPR architectural group renovated the interiors to house several city museums. It remains one of the largest citadels in Europe and a vital center for Milan's historical and artistic heritage.

  8. 08

    Fondazione Feltrinelli

    Viale Pasubio, 5, 20154 Milano MI, Italy

    This incredibly long and thin glass structure was inspired by the traditional linear farmhouses found in the Lombardy region. The building's rhythmic concrete frame and sharp angles give it the appearance of a crystalline mountain ridge. It serves as a modern cultural center, housing a vast library, archives, and multi-functional office spaces. The design emphasizes transparency and openness, integrating seamlessly into the surrounding urban parkland. It represents the successful redevelopment of the Porta Volta area into a contemporary knowledge district.

  9. 09

    Milano Centrale

    Piazza Duca d'Aosta, 1, 20124 Milano MI, Italy

    Milan's primary railway station is a monumental blend of Art Nouveau, Art Deco, and Liberty architectural styles. It was specifically designed to look like a 'temple of transportation' to impress arriving visitors. The interior features massive steel arches, grand stone murals, and vast halls decorated with intricate sculptures. The sheer scale of the building reflects the ambitious civic pride of early 20th-century Italy. Today, it stands as one of the busiest and most architecturally significant transport hubs in all of Europe.

  10. 10

    Villa Necchi Campiglio

    Via Mozart, 14, 20122 Milano MI, Italy

    This elegant Rationalist mansion redefined the concept of Milanese luxury during the 1930s. It is hidden within a quiet garden in the city center and was designed for a family of wealthy industrialists. The villa famously featured the city's very first private heated swimming pool and an outdoor tennis court. Its interiors are a masterclass in high-quality materials, featuring rare marbles, fine woods, and custom-made furniture. It now operates as a museum, offering a rare glimpse into the private lives of the Milanese upper class.

    Apple Piazza Liberty

    Piazza del Liberty, 1, 20121 Milano MI, Italy

    This store is a masterclass in urban integration that revitalizes a previously overlooked city square. Instead of a traditional building, visitors enter through a 12-meter tall glass fountain that serves as a portal. The fountain creates a stunning visual effect and acts as a sound barrier against the bustling city streets. Below ground, the retail space is bright and expansive, featuring local Beola Grigia stone throughout. The exterior amphitheater doubles as a public plaza and an outdoor cinema for community events.

  12. 12

    UniCredit Tower

    Piazza Gae Aulenti, 3, 20154 Milano MI, Italy

    Standing as Italy's tallest building, this skyscraper is defined by its sleek LED-encrusted spire. The tower sits atop Piazza Gae Aulenti, a circular raised podium that serves as a modern civic center. Its sinuous glass facade is designed to reflect the changing light and colors of the Lombardy sky. The spire itself is 85 meters tall and is often lit up to mark significant city events or holidays. It has quickly become the most recognizable symbol of the 'New Milan' business district.

  13. 13

    Palazzo dell'Arte (Triennale)

    Viale Emilio Alemagna, 6, 20121 Milano MI, Italy

    This brick structure is widely considered the temple of Italian design and industrial arts. Built in the 1930s, it features a massive and rhythmic three-arched entrance that welcomes visitors. The building was designed to host the prestigious Milan Triennial international exhibition on a permanent basis. It currently houses the Museum of Italian Design, showcasing centuries of innovation and creativity. The clean lines and monumental scale are perfect examples of the architectural style favored during the interwar period.

    Generali Tower (Lo Storto)

    Piazza Tre Torri, 20145 Milano MI, Italy

    Affectionately known as 'The Twisted One,' this tower features a dramatic torsion effect as it rises. The floors rotate slightly at each level to respond to the surrounding urban pathways and forces. Located in the futuristic CityLife district, it is part of a trio of towers designed by world-renowned architects. The building's geometry creates a sense of dynamic movement that shifts depending on the viewer's angle. It serves as a major office hub while providing stunning panoramic views of the Alps.

  15. 15

    Ca' Brütta

    Via della Moscova, 2, 20121 Milano MI, Italy

    Literally translated as 'The Ugly House,' this building received its nickname from locals shocked by its radical design. It is now celebrated as a pioneering masterpiece of early 20th-century architecture and the Novecento style. The structure is famously divided into two blocks with a private street running through the center. It uses classical motifs like arches and columns in an unconventional and strictly modern arrangement. The use of colorful plaster and diverse materials created a facade that was unlike anything else in Milan at the time.

  16. 16

    San Siro Stadium

    Piazzale Angelo Moratti, 20151 Milano MI, Italy

    Known globally as the 'La Scala of Football,' this stadium is home to both AC Milan and Inter Milan. It is instantly recognizable for its eleven massive cylindrical concrete towers that support the upper tiers. The spiral ramps winding around the exterior create a powerful and raw industrial aesthetic. A giant red girder roof was added in 1990, making it a prominent landmark on the city's western outskirts. It remains one of the most atmospheric and largest sporting venues in European football history.
