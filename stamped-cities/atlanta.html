The Big Peach

Atlanta architecture

Atlanta is home to The Varsity, the world's largest drive-in restaurant, which can serve up to 30,000 people on a busy day. It is also the birthplace of Coca-Cola and played a pivotal role in the American Civil Rights Movement as the home of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.

Before you go

  • Fly intoHartsfield-Jackson (ATL)

    Hartsfield-Jackson has consistently held the title of the world's busiest passenger airport for decades due to its strategic location as a major global connecting hub. It features an underground 'Plane Train' that whisks passengers between its seven sprawling concourses every few minutes.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    The local speech is defined by 'Southern Hospitality' and a melodic, soft drawl that remains welcoming even in a fast-paced city. While it is a global business hub, you will still frequently hear polite honorifics like 'sir' and 'ma'am' used in everyday conversation.

  • Getting aroundMARTA

    The heavy rail system provides rapid cross-city transit between the airport and downtown. Complementing the trains, the regional Xpress bus service connects the broader metro area. The Atlanta Streetcar offers a loop through the historic Centennial Olympic Park district.

12 landmarks in Atlanta

  1. 01

    Bank of America Plaza

    600 Peachtree St NE, Atlanta, GA 30308

    This skyscraper stands as the tallest building in the Southern United States. Its most striking feature is the dark red granite facade and the open-lattice spire at the top. This spire is leafed in real 23-karat gold and glows with a warm orange light at night. It remains the most dominant landmark in the entire Atlanta skyline. The structure is a prime example of Postmodern architecture that blends height with classical shapes.

  2. 02

    Hyatt Regency Atlanta

    265 Peachtree St NE, Atlanta, GA 30303

    This building completely changed the world of hotel design upon its debut. It was the first atrium hotel, featuring a massive 22-story open-air lobby that was revolutionary in 1967. The blue-domed Polaris revolving restaurant on the roof quickly became a symbol of the city. Architect John Portman used the atrium design to create a vibrant indoor town square. He later replicated this successful concept in various cities across the globe.

  3. 03

    Ponce City Market

    675 Ponce De Leon Ave NE, Atlanta, GA 30308

    Originally built as a massive Sears, Roebuck & Co. retail hub, this is one of the largest brick buildings in the Southeast. It is a celebrated landmark of adaptive reuse that now houses a giant food hall and creative offices. The restoration carefully kept the original industrial windows and sturdy steel columns. This project successfully links the logistics history of Atlanta to its modern tech scene. Visitors can also enjoy various amusements and dining options on the expansive rooftop.

  4. 04

    High Museum of Art

    1280 Peachtree St NE, Atlanta, GA 30309

    This stunning white enamel-clad complex was designed by two different Pritzker Prize-winning architects. The original wing features a central four-story atrium inspired by the Guggenheim in New York. A 2005 expansion added several new buildings featuring a unique roof of light. This roof uses 1,000 light scoops to filter natural northern light into the galleries. This design protects the delicate artwork while highlighting its beauty for all visitors.

  5. 05

    Flatiron Building (English-American)

    84 Peachtree St NW, Atlanta, GA 30303

    This is the oldest skyscraper in Atlanta, completed five years before the famous Flatiron Building in New York. Its distinctive wedge shape was dictated by the intersection of Peachtree and Broad Streets. The exterior is clad in white terra cotta and limestone, showcasing the Chicago School style. It was saved from demolition and now serves as a modern hub for entrepreneurs. The building remains a vital piece of the city's architectural and economic history.

    Mercedes-Benz Stadium

    1 AMB Dr NW, Atlanta, GA 30313

    This stadium is a marvel of modern structural engineering with a unique pinwheel retractable roof. The roof consists of eight translucent petals that open and close like a camera aperture. Its exterior is made of an angular, metallic skin that reflects the changing Georgia sky. Inside, it features a 360-degree halo video board that is the largest of its kind. The stadium serves as a futuristic home for both professional football and soccer.

  7. 07

    The Fox Theatre

    660 Peachtree St NE, Atlanta, GA 30308

    Originally designed as a Shriner’s temple, this movie palace is a fantastical blend of Moorish and Egyptian architecture. The interior is famous for its sky ceiling featuring twinkling crystal stars and projected clouds. It showcases gold-leafed minarets, ornate onion domes, and exotic decorative motifs throughout the space. The venue is home to the Mighty Mo, one of the largest theater organs in the world. It remains one of the most beloved performing arts venues in the entire country.

  8. 08

    Swan House

    130 W Paces Ferry Rd NW, Atlanta, GA 30305

    Located at the Atlanta History Center, this mansion represents the pinnacle of Classical Revival architecture. It is named for the elegant swan motifs found frequently throughout the interior design. The grand stone facade and cascading water fountain evoke the feeling of an old Italian villa. This estate provides a fascinating look into the Gilded Age of the city's elite. It now serves as a museum where visitors can explore the storied past of Atlanta.

  9. 09

    1180 Peachtree (Symphony Tower)

    1180 Peachtree St NE, Atlanta, GA 30309

    This prominent Midtown skyscraper is best known for the two vertical glass fins extending from its roof. These fins are illuminated at night and strongly resemble the majestic sails of a ship. It was the first high-rise office building in the world to achieve LEED Gold certification. The design includes a sophisticated water reclamation system used for the tower's lush landscaping. It stands as a symbol of the city's commitment to sustainable modern architecture.

  10. 10

    The King Center

    449 Auburn Ave NE, Atlanta, GA 30312

    This is a sacred site located within the Martin Luther King Jr. National Historical Park. The architecture is humble and symbolic, featuring a vaulted crypt made of beautiful Georgia marble. This crypt sits peacefully in the center of a large, circular reflection pool. Surrounding the pool, Freedom Hall features brick arches and various African-influenced patterns. The entire complex serves as a powerful monument to the global legacy of Dr. King.

    Apple Lenox Square

    3393 Peachtree Rd NE, Atlanta, GA 30326

    This flagship retail space features a soaring 30-foot tall glass facade that opens to the plaza. The interior showcases a luminous ceiling made of custom fabric panels that mimic natural sunlight. It includes integrated indoor trees and a massive video wall for community learning sessions. The design reflects the status of the city as a major hub for technology and design. This modern space serves as both a store and a gathering place for the community.

  12. 12

    Westin Peachtree Plaza

    210 Peachtree St NW, Atlanta, GA 30303

    This 73-story cylindrical tower was the tallest hotel in the world when it was first completed. The circular design ensures that every guest room enjoys a panoramic view of the city. It features a signature revolving restaurant called The Sun Dial at the very top. External glass elevators offer a high-speed and scenic experience as they ascend the facade. The building remains one of the most recognizable icons of the downtown skyline.
