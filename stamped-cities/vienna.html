City of Music

Vienna architecture

Vienna has been home to more famous composers—including Mozart, Beethoven, and Strauss—than any other city in the world. Its legendary coffee house culture is recognized by UNESCO as an 'Intangible Cultural Heritage,' where locals go to read, write, and debate for hours.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoVienna International (VIE)

        The airport terminal features a massive 24/7 digital media wall that displays artistic visuals and real-time travel information to arriving guests. It is known for its extreme efficiency and its role as a key transfer point for travelers heading to Central and Eastern Europe.

      • CurrencyEuro (€)
      • LanguageGerman

        The city speaks Austrian German, which is often considered more melodic and softer than the German spoken in the north. English is very common, especially within the world-class museums and historic coffee houses.

      • Getting aroundWiener Linien

        Vienna is famous for its €365 annual pass, which allows residents to use all transit for exactly one Euro per day. The U-Bahn system is complemented by one of the largest and most efficient tram networks in the world. The City Airport Train (CAT) provides a premium 16-minute non-stop link to the city center.

      5 landmarks in Vienna

      1. 01

        St. Stephen's Cathedral

        Stephansplatz 3, 1010 Wien, Austria

        St. Stephen's Cathedral stands as the spiritual heart of Vienna and a masterpiece of Gothic architecture. Its most striking feature is the steeply pitched roof covered in over two hundred thousand multi-colored glazed tiles. These tiles form intricate geometric patterns along with the double-headed eagle, the historical symbol of the Habsburg dynasty. The south tower, affectionately called 'Steffl' by locals, dominates the city skyline and offers a panoramic view of the capital. Inside, the cathedral houses magnificent altarpieces and the stone-carved pulpit, which is considered a pinnacle of late Gothic art.

      2. 02

        Hundertwasserhaus

        Kegelgasse 36-38, 1030 Wien, Austria

        This unique apartment complex serves as a vibrant rejection of the traditional straight lines found in modern architecture. The facade is a kaleidoscope of bright colors and irregular textures, featuring uneven floors that mimic the natural forest ground. Trees grow directly from inside the rooms and onto the balconies, embodying the architect's vision of a 'peace treaty with nature.' It was built to provide social housing that prioritizes human creativity and environmental harmony over industrial efficiency. Today, it remains one of Vienna's most photographed landmarks and a testament to the city's artistic soul.

      3. 03

        Schönbrunn Palace

        Schönbrunner Schloßstraße 47, 1130 Wien, Austria

        Once the summer residence of the Habsburg monarchs, this Rococo palace is one of Europe's most impressive Baroque complexes. The building contains over fourteen hundred rooms, many of which are preserved with their original imperial opulence. Its exterior is painted in a distinctive shade of 'Imperial Yellow' that became a signature of the Austro-Hungarian Empire. The surrounding gardens feature the majestic Gloriette hill and one of the world's oldest zoos, Tiergarten Schönbrunn. It is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site, symbolizing the power and cultural influence of the long-reigning dynasty.

      4. 04

        DC Tower 1

        Donau-City-Straße 7, 1220 Wien, Austria

        DC Tower 1 is Austria's tallest skyscraper, serving as a landmark of modern vertical architecture in the Donau City district. Its most notable feature is the 'folded' glass facade, which gives the building the appearance of a massive dark crystal. The tower was designed with a strong focus on sustainability, incorporating high-efficiency energy systems throughout its structure. Visitors can ascend to the upper floors to enjoy a cocktail with a stunning 360-degree view of the Danube River. It houses a sophisticated mix of international offices, a luxury hotel, and high-end residential units.

      5. 05

        Secession Building

        Friedrichstraße 12, 1010 Wien, Austria

        The Secession Building was established as a protest against conservative art traditions and remains a symbol of Viennese Art Nouveau. It is topped by an iconic dome made of three thousand gilded laurel leaves, often referred to by locals as the 'golden cabbage.' The white, cubic walls serve as a stark backdrop for the intricate gold leaf ornamentation and mythological motifs. Inside, the basement houses Gustav Klimt’s world-famous Beethoven Frieze, a mural dedicated to the composer’s Ninth Symphony. The motto inscribed above the entrance famously declares the movement's commitment to artistic freedom for every generation.
