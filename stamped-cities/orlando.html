The City Beautiful

Orlando architecture

Orlando is the theme park capital of the world, hosting iconic destinations like Walt Disney World and Universal Studios within its sprawling metro area. Beyond the parks, the city is known for its beautiful lakes and its status as one of the fastest-growing tech and aerospace hubs in Florida.

Before you go

  • Fly intoOrlando International (MCO)

    The airport is famous for its massive indoor tropical atrium and its fleet of automated monorails that transport passengers from the main hub to the airside terminals. MCO stands for McCoy Air Force Base, a tribute to the site's former life as a military installation before it became a global tourist gateway.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    The language here is generally a very neutral 'Standard American,' largely to accommodate the millions of international tourists that visit annually. However, the presence of a large Puerto Rican community ensures that Spanish is also a major and influential part of the local linguistic landscape.

  • Getting aroundSunRail & Lynx

    SunRail is a double-decker commuter train system equipped with work tables and free Wi-Fi. The Lynx bus network provides the primary local transit, including the free LYMMO circulator in the downtown core. Brightline's high-speed rail now connects Orlando International Airport directly to South Florida.

11 landmarks in Orlando

  1. 01

    Cinderella Castle

    1180 Seven Seas Drive, Lake Buena Vista, FL 32830

    The flagship icon of Magic Kingdom, this structure was inspired by real European castles like Neuschwanstein. It uses 'forced perspective' architecture, where the bricks and windows get smaller near the top to make the building appear much taller. The exterior is actually made of fiberglass and steel rather than stone to withstand Florida hurricanes. Inside, guests can find a stunning mosaic gallery that tells the story of the titular princess. It remains one of the most photographed structures in the world and serves as the park's central compass.

  2. 02

    Spaceship Earth

    200 Epcot Center Dr, Lake Buena Vista, FL 32830

    This geodesic sphere serves as the centerpiece of EPCOT and is a marvel of modern engineering. It is classified as a 'class 2' geodesic sphere, comprised of 11,324 individual triangular panels. The structure is unique because it is raised 18 feet off the ground on six massive legs. A specialized drainage system captures rainwater through the panel gaps to replenish the park's lagoons. Guests traveling inside experience a slow-moving dark ride that explores the history of human communication.

  3. 03

    Dr. Phillips Center for the Performing Arts

    445 S Magnolia Ave, Orlando, FL 32801

    This world-class performing arts venue features a cantilevered roof that extends dramatically over the public plaza. The exterior is a striking combination of glass, stainless steel, and white precast concrete. It was designed to feel 'open' and accessible to the surrounding downtown community. The interior contains multiple theaters designed with world-class acoustics for Broadway shows and concerts. It has become a cornerstone of Orlando’s cultural revival since its opening in 2014.

  4. 04

    Chinese Pagoda at Lake Eola

    512 E Washington St, Orlando, FL 32801

    This bright red and gold Ting pavilion is situated on the scenic edge of Lake Eola Park. It was originally gifted to the city of Orlando by a sister city to promote cultural exchange. The structure serves as a prime example of traditional East Asian architecture within a modern urban setting. Visitors often use the pavilion as a shaded retreat for photography or bird watching. Its intricate roof details and vibrant colors make it a standout landmark against the downtown skyline.

  5. 05

    Orlando Public Library

    101 E Central Blvd, Orlando, FL 32801

    This massive structure is a premier example of Brutalist architecture in the Southeastern United States. The building's raw concrete exterior and geometric shapes make it a distinctive sight in the downtown core. It was intentionally designed by John M. Johansen to resemble a stylized stack of books. As the largest public library building in Florida, it houses extensive collections and a modern makerspace. The cedar-plank-textured concrete walls provide a unique tactile experience for those walking by.

    EA Orlando (Creative Village)

    501 W Amelia St, Orlando, FL 32801

    This modern glass-and-steel structure serves as a major anchor for the Creative Village district. The design features an industrial-chic interior that caters to a high-tech workforce. High transparency in the facade is intended to showcase the digital creation occurring within the walls. It creates a physical connection between the tech industry and the local urban environment. The building represents Orlando's growing reputation as a hub for game development and innovation.

  7. 07

    Truist Plaza (SunTrust Center)

    200 S Orange Ave, Orlando, FL 32801

    Standing as the tallest building in downtown Orlando, this tower is a landmark of the city skyline. It is easily recognized by the four green-lit pyramids that sit atop its roof. The post-modern design utilizes pink granite and reflective glass to create a commanding presence. It has served as the dominant commercial hub of the city since the late 1980s. The plaza at its base offers a sophisticated environment for business professionals and visitors alike.

  8. 08

    Camping World Stadium

    1 Citrus Bowl Pl, Orlando, FL 32805

    This historic stadium has undergone several massive renovations to maintain its world-class status. The open-air design features two large upper decks and a sprawling fan plaza. It has hosted prestigious events ranging from the FIFA World Cup to WrestleMania. The venue represents the long history of sports and entertainment in Orlando since the 1930s. Today, it remains a vital site for college football bowl games and major concerts.

  9. 09

    Orlando Science Center

    777 E Princeton St, Orlando, FL 32803

    This cylindrical building is a staple of Loch Haven Park and features a prominent white observatory dome. The architecture includes a multi-story glass atrium that floods the interior with natural light. Across four floors, the facility houses interactive exhibits designed to inspire curiosity in science. It serves as a major educational resource for families and schools across Central Florida. The rooftop terrace offers a unique vantage point for viewing the surrounding park and city.

  10. 10

    The Wheel at ICON Park

    8375 International Dr, Orlando, FL 32819

    This 400-foot tall observation wheel is a massive steel engineering feat on International Drive. Each of its stabilized capsules offers a panoramic view of the entire Central Florida region. It utilizes a sophisticated lighting system capable of displaying over 16 million different colors. While primarily an attraction, its structural presence has redefined the local skyline since 2015. It remains a popular destination for tourists looking to see the theme parks from above.

  11. 11

    Apple Millenia

    4200 Conroy Rd, Orlando, FL 32839

    Located within the luxury Mall at Millenia, this retail space features a soaring glass facade. The interior design emphasizes symmetry and light through a wide-open 'forum' layout. It utilizes premium materials like bead-blasted stainless steel and Italian light-gray stone. Following a major renovation in 2018, the store became a flagship example of modern minimalism. It serves as a sleek architectural anchor for Orlando's premier high-fashion shopping district.
