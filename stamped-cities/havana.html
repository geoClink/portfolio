Havana architecture

Havana is one of the most architecturally preserved cities in the Caribbean — its Old Havana (La Habana Vieja), a UNESCO World Heritage Site, contains one of the largest concentrations of Spanish colonial architecture in the Americas. The city is famous for its fleet of colorfully restored 1950s American cars, called 'almendrones,' which serve as shared taxis and have become an iconic symbol of Cuba. Havana was the birthplace of the son and mambo musical traditions that evolved into salsa, and live music spills from every doorway in the evenings.

Before you go

  • Fly intoJosé Martí International Airport (HAV)

    José Martí International Airport is named after Cuba's national hero and independence poet. It has four terminals serving different types of traffic, with Terminal 3 handling most international arrivals. The airport is located about 18km southwest of the city center. Given Cuba's unique relationship with the United States — and restricted American travel to the island — the airport sees a distinctive mix of passengers from Latin America, Canada, Europe, and increasingly from the United States.

  • CurrencyCuban Peso (₱)
  • LanguageSpanish

    Cuban Spanish has a distinctive and musical character — fast-paced, with a tendency to drop the final 's' from words and merge syllables. It is heavily influenced by African languages brought by enslaved people, which have shaped Cuban music, religion (Santería), and everyday vocabulary in ways unique in the Spanish-speaking world. Habaneros (Havana residents) are warm, expressive, and intensely proud of their city's culture, and conversation is an art form here.

  • Getting aroundAlmendrones & Coco Taxis

    Havana's most distinctive transport is the almendrón — vintage 1950s American cars in pastel colors that serve as shared taxis along fixed routes, carrying up to five passengers. Coco taxis, shaped like yellow coconuts on three wheels, are a popular tourist option for short trips. Bicitaxis (bicycle rickshaws) navigate the narrow streets of Old Havana. The Habana Bus Tour, a hop-on hop-off double-decker, connects the main tourist sites. Standard yellow metered taxis are available for point-to-point travel.
