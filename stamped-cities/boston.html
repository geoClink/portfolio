The Hub

Boston architecture

Boston is home to the first public park in the United States, the Boston Common, which was established in 1634. It is a city of 'firsts,' including the nation's first public school and its first subway system, blending deep colonial history with world-class academic institutions.

Before you go

  • Fly intoLogan International (BOS)

    Logan International is one of the few major U.S. airports located on a peninsula, making it directly accessible from downtown via a scenic water taxi or ferry. Its proximity to the harbor provides passengers with stunning views of the Boston skyline and the historic waterfront during takeoff.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    Boston is famous for its 'non-rhotic' accent, where the letter 'R' often disappears in the middle or end of words. The city’s communication style is notoriously direct and intellectually sharp, reflecting its deep-seated academic and historic roots.

  • Getting aroundThe 'T'

    The Green Line is the oldest subway line in the United States and operates unique streetcar-style vehicles. Commuter Rail lines extend from North and South Stations to reach the coastal and suburban communities. The MBTA ferry system offers a scenic alternative for commuters crossing the Boston Harbor.

15 landmarks in Boston

  1. 01

    200 Clarendon

    200 Clarendon St, Boston, MA 02116

    Known locally as The Hancock, this is the tallest building in New England. Its minimalist blue-glass facade is famous for a window controversy in the 1970s. Wind pressure originally caused massive panes to fall, requiring the structure to be stabilized with mass dampers. The building beautifully reflects the historic Trinity Church and Copley Square. It acts as a giant mirror that connects the city's architectural past with its modern present.

  2. 02

    Custom House Tower

    3 McKinley Square, Boston, MA 02109

    This was Boston's first skyscraper, built atop an existing 1849 Greek Revival customs house. Due to strict height restrictions in the early 1900s, it remained the city's only tall building for decades. Its four-faced clock is notable for being one of the largest in the world. The observation deck provides a stunning 360-degree view of the Harbor and the North End. Today, it has been repurposed to operate as a luxury Marriott vacation club.

  3. 03

    Prudential Tower

    800 Boylston St, Boston, MA 02199

    This tower was a cornerstone of the 1960s New Boston urban renewal project. It features the View Boston observation deck which offers panoramic vistas of the surrounding area. The building utilizes a highly sophisticated wind-mitigation system to ensure structural stability. It is directly connected to a massive shopping complex in the heart of the Back Bay. As the northern anchor of the local skyline, it remains a vital piece of the city's identity.

  4. 04

    Old State House

    206 Washington St, Boston, MA 02109

    This is the oldest surviving public building in Boston and the site of the Boston Massacre. The architecture is a classic example of British Colonial style featuring a distinct red brick facade. It includes a white balcony where the Declaration of Independence was first read to the public. The roof is adorned with statues of a lion and a unicorn representing the British monarchy. These symbols were once torn down during the Revolution but have since been carefully restored.

  5. 05

    Boston City Hall

    1 City Hall Square, Boston, MA 02201

    This building is one of the most famous and debated examples of Brutalist architecture in the world. Its massive concrete cantilevers were intended to express the transparency and accessibility of government. The rugged textures of the facade were heavily inspired by the work of architect Le Corbusier. Each section of the building was designed to visually reflect the specific function of the offices inside. Despite its polarizing nature, it is considered a structural marvel of the late 20th century.

  6. 06

    Trinity Church

    206 Clarendon St, Boston, MA 02116

    This church is recognized as the birthplace of the influential Richardsonian Romanesque architectural style. It is a powerful masterpiece constructed of rough-cut stone with massive arches and clay roofs. The building sits on 4,500 wooden piles driven into the marshy soil of the Back Bay. It remains a central anchor for Copley Square and the surrounding community. Its reflection in the glass of the nearby Hancock Tower is a legendary sight for photographers.

  7. 07

    Ray and Maria Stata Center (MIT)

    32 Vassar St, Cambridge, MA 02139

    This deconstructivist landmark on the MIT campus was designed by the world-renowned Frank Gehry. The building features tilting towers and colliding angles meant to look like a party of drunken skyscrapers. It was specifically built to foster collaboration between scientists and philosophers in an unconventional space. The interior houses experimental labs with no straight lines to encourage creative and innovative research. It remains one of the most whimsical and recognizable structures in the entire Cambridge area.

  8. 08

    The Rowes Wharf

    70 Rowes Wharf, Boston, MA 02110

    Rowes Wharf is widely considered the most recognizable gateway to the historic Boston Harbor. This grand Postmodern complex features a massive six-story coffered arch at its center. This arch allows pedestrians to walk directly from the city streets to the water's edge. The design blends traditional brick and copper detailing with the scale of modern development. It currently serves as a luxury hotel, residential space, and a busy local ferry terminal.

  9. 09

    Winthrop Center

    115 Winthrop Square, Boston, MA 02110

    This skyscraper is the new face of Boston's skyline and a global pioneer in sustainable design. It holds the title of the largest Passive House office building currently in existence. The structure is designed to use 65% less energy than standard modern office towers. Its slender glass profile features a public Great Hall at the base for social gatherings. This project has created a new social and environmental heart for the city's Financial District.

  10. 10

    Boston Public Library (McKim Building)

    700 Boylston St, Boston, MA 02116

    This was the first large-scale municipal library in the US and was dubbed a Palace for the People. The building features a beautiful Italian Renaissance courtyard and a grand marble staircase. On the facade, the names of history's great thinkers are carved into the stone blocks. This detail emphasizes Boston's historic identity as the Athens of America. It remains one of the most stunning and culturally significant interiors in the United States.

    Apple Boylston Street

    815 Boylston St, Boston, MA 02116

    This building is one of the largest Apple flagship retail stores found anywhere in the world. It features a stunning three-story glass spiral staircase as its primary central focal point. The transparent facade was designed to bridge the gap between historic masonry and modern retail. On the top floor, indoor trees and natural light create a unique garden atmosphere for visitors. The structural glass moment frame represents a significant feat of modern architectural engineering.

  12. 12

    Massachusetts State House

    24 Beacon St, Boston, MA 02133

    This historic building was famously called the hub of the solar system by Oliver Wendell Holmes. Its most recognizable feature is the massive dome covered in 23-karat gold leaf. The red brick Federal-style structure sits atop Beacon Hill overlooking the city of Boston. It serves as the primary seat of the Massachusetts state government and legislative bodies. The grand Hall of Flags and the legislative chambers are masterpieces of early American public design.

    Institute of Contemporary Art

    25 Harbor Shore Dr, Boston, MA 02210

    This building is a cantilevered marvel situated prominently on the Fan Pier in the Seaport. It was the first major museum built in Boston in over a century. The fourth-floor gallery extends 80 feet over the Harborwalk to create a gravity-defying effect. This design provides a dramatic lens through which visitors can view the water. Its blend of wood and metal shifted the city's architectural focus toward the modern waterfront.

  14. 14

    Faneuil Hall

    1 Faneuil Hall Square, Boston, MA 02109

    Known as the Cradle of Liberty, this building was a key meeting place during the American Revolution. It served as a marketplace where Samuel Adams and others gave famous speeches for independence. The roof features a unique grasshopper weathervane that has become a symbol of the city. This detail was famously used as a secret test to identify spies during the War of 1812. It continues to be a vibrant center for both historic tourism and modern retail.

  15. 15

    Federal Reserve Bank Building

    600 Atlantic Ave, Boston, MA 02210

    This skyscraper is often called the Washboard Building due to its unique terraced aluminum exterior. It features a massive breezeway at the base supported by a 600-ton steel truss system. The design includes specialized eyebrow sunshades to reduce heat gain from the summer sun. It stands as one of the most distinctive high-rises in the entire Financial District. The tower represents a bold era of engineering and corporate modernism from the late 1970s.
