City of Azaleas

Taipei architecture

Taipei 101, which held the title of the world's tallest building from 2004 to 2010, is designed to resemble a bamboo stalk and features a massive 660-tonne steel pendulum inside to dampen earthquake vibrations. The city's night markets — particularly Shilin and Raohe — are legendary among food travelers, offering hundreds of street foods in a single venue. Taiwan's bubble tea (boba milk tea) was invented in Taichung in the 1980s and became a global phenomenon largely exported from Taipei's café culture.

Before you go

  • Fly intoTaiwan Taoyuan International Airport (TPE)

    Taiwan Taoyuan International Airport, located southwest of the city, is consistently ranked among Asia's best airports for service and efficiency. It serves as a major transit hub between Northeast and Southeast Asia, and its Terminal 2 shopping and dining complex is a destination in itself. An airport MRT line, opened in 2017, provides a direct 35-minute connection to Taipei Main Station.

  • CurrencyNew Taiwan Dollar (NT$)
  • LanguageMandarin Chinese

    Mandarin Chinese is Taiwan's official language, though the local accent and vocabulary differ slightly from mainland Chinese. Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi) is widely spoken, especially among older residents, and you will hear it in markets and traditional neighborhoods. Japanese words absorbed during the 50-year colonial period are still part of everyday speech. English is commonly spoken in Taipei's tech and business communities.

  • Getting aroundMRT & YouBike

    Taipei's MRT is consistently ranked among the world's cleanest, most punctual, and most affordable metro systems. Its EasyCard contactless payment covers the MRT, city buses, YouBike rental stations, and even many convenience stores. The YouBike public bicycle system has thousands of docking stations throughout the city and is extremely popular for short trips. Eating and drinking on the MRT is strictly prohibited — a rule enforced with remarkable compliance.
