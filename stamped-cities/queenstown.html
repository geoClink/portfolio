Adventure Capital

Queenstown architecture

Queenstown is the undisputed adventure capital of the world and the birthplace of commercial bungy jumping at the historic Kawarau Bridge. Nestled on the shores of Lake Wakatipu and surrounded by the aptly named 'Remarkables' mountain range, it is a year-round destination for skiing, jet boating, and hiking.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoQueenstown Airport (ZQN)

        Queenstown Airport is famous among aviation enthusiasts for its stunning mountain approach, where planes must navigate between narrow alpine peaks to land. It is one of the most scenic airports in the Southern Hemisphere, offering travelers an immediate and breathtaking introduction to the rugged beauty of the New Zealand Alps.

      • CurrencyNew Zealand Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        The local English is exceptionally friendly and informal, centered around the city's adventurous outdoor lifestyle. Visitors will quickly pick up the relaxed Kiwi accent and the welcoming 'Kia Ora' used throughout the region.

      • Getting aroundOrbus

        Orbus is a simplified, flat-fare bus system designed to make transit accessible for both locals and visiting skiers. The system includes specialized racks on the front of buses for carrying mountain bikes and snow gear. Water taxis provide a scenic and fast alternative for crossing Lake Wakatipu between major resort hubs.

      4 landmarks in Queenstown

      1. 01

        Skyline Queenstown

        Brecon Street, Queenstown 9300, New Zealand

        Perched high atop Bob's Peak, this iconic facility serves as the terminus for the steepest cable car lift in the Southern Hemisphere. The complex recently underwent a massive architectural expansion to accommodate increasing visitor numbers while maintaining its dramatic mountain profile. Its floor-to-ceiling glass walls offer unparalleled 220-degree views of Lake Wakatipu, the Remarkables mountain range, and the bustling town below. Beyond the viewing decks, the structure anchors a multi-level adventure hub featuring a luge track, a world-class restaurant, and stargazing platforms. It stands as a premier example of high-altitude alpine architecture designed to withstand extreme weather while maximizing the natural beauty of the Otago landscape.

      2. 02

        TSS Earnslaw

        Steamer Wharf, Beach Street, Queenstown 9300, New Zealand

        Launched in 1912, this Edwardian twin-screw steamer is the only remaining coal-fired passenger steamship still in commercial service in the Southern Hemisphere. Designed by naval architect Hugh McRae, she was built by John McGregor and Company in Dunedin, then taken apart with every steel hull plate numbered, carried by train to Kingston at the foot of Lake Wakatipu, and reassembled on the shore. At 51.2 metres she was the largest steamship ever built in New Zealand. More than a century later she is still one of Central Otago's oldest tourist attractions, carrying passengers across the lake from Queenstown.

      3. 03

        Kawarau Gorge Suspension Bridge

        State Highway 6, Kawarau Gorge, Gibbston, New Zealand

        Completed in 1880 to carry traffic to the Central Otago goldfields, this suspension bridge designed by Harry Higginson hangs 43 metres above the turquoise Kawarau River. After road traffic moved to a new highway bridge on State Highway 6, the old crossing found a second life as the world's first commercial bungy jumping site. Today it also carries walkers, runners and cyclists on the Queenstown Trail across the rugged Kawarau Gorge.

      4. 04

        Queenstown Gardens

        Park Street, Queenstown 9300, New Zealand

        Beside Lake Wakatipu, these gardens began in 1866 when the town's first mayor planted two English oaks to mark the founding of the borough, and they officially opened a year later. Early residents planted exotic trees wherever they pleased, and the towering Douglas firs they left behind now shelter the whole headland. Paths loop past a rose garden, a pond and an 1891 band rotunda (rebuilt in 1999), with views of Lake Wakatipu, the Frankton Arm and the surrounding mountains. Every winter the gardens host the LUMA Southern Lights festival.
