Pearl of the West

Guadalajara architecture

Guadalajara is Mexico's second-largest city and the cultural capital of western Mexico. Mariachi music, tequila, and the charreada (Mexican rodeo) were all born in the state of Jalisco, of which Guadalajara is the capital. The city is home to the Hospicio Cabañas, a UNESCO World Heritage Site containing José Clemente Orozco's monumental murals — considered among the greatest works of Mexican art of the 20th century.

Before you go

  • Fly intoMiguel Hidalgo y Costilla Guadalajara International Airport (GDL)

    Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla International Airport, named after the priest who launched Mexico's independence movement, is Mexico's second busiest airport. Its location near the Tequila region means some international visitors arrive specifically for Jalisco's agave landscape, also a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The airport connects directly to North American cities and several European hubs.

  • CurrencyMexican Peso ($)
  • LanguageSpanish

    Mexican Spanish as spoken in Guadalajara has a warm, melodic quality and is considered one of the clearest Spanish accents in Mexico. Tapatíos (as Guadalajarans call themselves) are known for their strong regional pride and their passionate attachment to their city's traditions. The Nahuatl word 'guadalajara' itself is of debated origin, though the city was likely named after the Spanish city of the same name by colonizers.

  • Getting aroundTren Ligero & Macrobús

    Guadalajara's Tren Ligero (light rail) has two lines crossing the metropolitan area. The Macrobús — a Bus Rapid Transit system on dedicated lanes — handles the busiest corridors. A new Line 3 of the light rail opened in 2020, significantly expanding coverage. The city is investing heavily in cycling infrastructure, and a bike-sharing system (MiBici) has made cycling a realistic urban option. Uber operates widely in Guadalajara.

    Guadalajara Metropolitan Cathedral

    Av. 16 de Septiembre 16, Centro Historico, 44100 Guadalajara, Mexico

    The Cathedral Basilica of Our Lady of the Assumption, Guadalajara's central landmark built between 1541 and 1618, is recognizable by its distinctive neo-Gothic twin spires that replaced the original towers destroyed in an 1818 earthquake.

    Hospicio Cabañas

    Cabanas 8, El Santuario, 44360 Guadalajara, Mexico

    A neoclassical complex designed by Manuel Tolsa and completed in 1810, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Its chapel contains the monumental murals of Jose Clemente Orozco — particularly 'The Man of Fire' in the dome — considered masterpieces of 20th-century Mexican art.

  3. 03

    Teatro Degollado

    Degollado, 44100 Guadalajara, Mexico

    A magnificent 19th-century neoclassical opera house opened in 1866, considered one of Mexico's finest theatres. Its Corinthian colonnade facade frames a tympanum depicting Apollo and the Nine Muses. It remains the home of the Jalisco Philharmonic Orchestra.

    Tlaquepaque

    El Parian, Independencia 186, Tlaquepaque, Jalisco

    A historic artisan district incorporated into metropolitan Guadalajara, renowned for its colonial architecture, hand-painted talavera ceramics, blown glass workshops, and mariachi performances in the Jardin Hidalgo plaza.

  5. 05

    Barranca de Huentítán

    Barranca de Huentitan, Guadalajara, Jalisco

    A dramatic canyon carved by the Santiago River at the northern edge of Guadalajara, dropping over 600 metres from the city's plateau to the canyon floor. Its distinctive ecology supports hundreds of plant and bird species unique to the dry tropical canyon environment.
