Heart of Silicon Valley

Cupertino architecture

Cupertino is globally recognized as the headquarters of Apple Inc., centered around the massive, ring-shaped Apple Park campus. Before it was a tech hub, the area was known as the 'Valley of Heart's Delight' for its vast number of fruit orchards.

Before you go

  • Fly intoSan Jose Mineta (SJC)

    Mineta San Jose International is the primary airport for Silicon Valley, featuring high-tech amenities like robotic coffee baristas and interactive digital art installations. It is conveniently located just minutes away from the headquarters of the world's most influential technology companies.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    Conversation in Cupertino is heavily laden with tech industry jargon, startup terminology, and corporate acronyms. Because of its global workforce, the English spoken here is exceptionally precise and often serves as a common ground for residents from all over the world.

  • Getting aroundVTA & Caltrain

    VTA light rail and buses provide the primary local transit throughout the Santa Clara Valley. Caltrain offers a vital commuter link connecting Cupertino's tech workers to San Francisco and Palo Alto. Many major tech campuses also operate extensive private shuttle networks for their employees.

16 landmarks in Cupertino

  1. 01

    Apple Park

    One Apple Park Way, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Often referred to as the 'Spaceship,' Apple Park serves as a monumental testament to modern corporate architecture. The massive four-story ring-shaped structure is clad in the world's largest panels of curved glass to ensure a seamless view of the landscape. It is entirely powered by 100% renewable energy, largely sourced from its expansive rooftop solar installations. The interior features a lush 30-acre park filled with fruit trees and a large pond designed for employee recreation. This facility represents the final vision of Steve Jobs, who wanted the campus to feel more like a nature preserve than an office park.

  2. 02

    Steve Jobs Theater

    Apple Park Way & N Tantau Ave, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Sitting on a high point overlooking the main Apple Park ring, this theater is a feat of structural engineering. It features the largest carbon-fiber roof in the world, which is supported entirely by a 22-foot-high glass cylinder. Because there are no interior columns, visitors enjoy a completely unobstructed view of the surrounding greenery through the glass walls. To reach the 1,000-seat subterranean auditorium, guests descend a grand stone spiral staircase or use a custom circular glass elevator. This venue is exclusively used for major Apple product launches and high-profile company keynotes.

  3. 03

    Le Petit Trianon

    21250 Stevens Creek Blvd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    This National Historic Landmark is a stunning Neoclassical structure modeled after the Petit Trianon at the Palace of Versailles. Originally built as part of a grand winery estate, it reflects the architectural elegance of late 19th-century California. Today, it serves as the home for the California History Center on the De Anza College campus. The building features ornate stone detailing and classical columns that stand in stark contrast to the surrounding modern campus buildings. It remains a vital educational resource that preserves the rich history of the Santa Clara Valley.

  4. 04

    Apple Infinite Loop

    1 Infinite Loop, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Before the completion of Apple Park, Infinite Loop served as the primary face of Apple for over two decades. The campus consists of six distinct buildings arranged around a private U-shaped courtyard that encourages employee collaboration. It was within these walls that iconic products like the original iMac, iPod, and iPhone were developed and refined. The site features a centralized 'Town Hall' theater where Steve Jobs famously introduced several revolutionary technologies. Even today, it remains a bustling hub for Apple’s engineering and services teams.

  5. 05

    Fujitsu Planetarium

    21250 Stevens Creek Blvd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Located on the De Anza College campus, this planetarium is one of the most advanced astronomical education facilities in the world. Its distinctive dome structure houses a state-of-the-art digital projection system that brings the night sky to life. Following a major renovation in 2007, the theater now provides immersive 360-degree experiences for students and the public alike. It serves as a major community resource, hosting stargazing events and educational lectures for the entire Bay Area. The facility plays a crucial role in fostering interest in space science and technology among local youth.

  6. 06

    Cupertino City Hall

    10300 Torre Ave, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Cupertino City Hall stands as the administrative heart of the city, embodying a modest and functional Mid-Century Modern design. The building is part of a larger civic center complex that integrates government services with public gathering spaces. Its architecture reflects the era of rapid suburban growth when Cupertino officially incorporated in the 1950s and 60s. The site includes public gardens and memorials that commemorate the city's diverse cultural heritage. It serves as the primary venue for city council meetings where major decisions regarding local tech developments are made.

  7. 07

    Cupertino Library

    10800 Torre Ave, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The Cupertino Library is a state-of-the-art facility designed to serve one of the most tech-literate populations in the country. It is famous for its massive 16-foot aquarium, which serves as a central focal point for visitors on the first floor. A major expansion completed in 2019 added modern study rooms and expanded digital resources for local students. The building's contemporary design utilizes large windows to create a bright, inviting atmosphere for reading and community events. It consistently ranks as one of the busiest and most well-resourced libraries in the California state system.

    Quinlan Community Center

    10185 N Stelling Rd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The Quinlan Community Center serves as the primary social and cultural hub for the residents of Cupertino. Its architecture was specifically designed to harmonize with the natural beauty of the nearby Santa Cruz foothills. The facility features multiple classrooms, a large banquet hall, and spaces for local art exhibitions. It is located adjacent to Memorial Park, creating a seamless transition between indoor community services and outdoor recreation. Throughout the year, it hosts everything from senior programs to large-scale international cultural festivals.

  9. 09

    Main Street Parking Structure

    19429 Stevens Creek Blvd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    This parking structure is a rare example of urban infrastructure that prioritizes aesthetic design and Art Deco revivalism. It serves as a critical support for the walkable 'Main Street' district, which blends retail, office, and residential space. The exterior features decorative metalwork and lighting that transforms the garage into a landmark at night. Unlike traditional concrete structures, it includes integrated retail spaces at the ground level to maintain street-level energy. It stands as a symbol of Cupertino's modern effort to create dense, pedestrian-friendly urban environments.

  10. 10

    McClellan Ranch Milk Barn

    22221 McClellan Rd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The McClellan Ranch Milk Barn is a beautifully preserved remnant of Cupertino’s deep agricultural roots. Built in the 1930s, this vernacular structure stands as a reminder of the era when the valley was known as the 'Valley of Heart's Delight.' Before the technology boom, this region was one of the most productive fruit-growing and dairy areas in the country. Today, the barn is part of a 24-acre nature preserve that offers environmental education and birdwatching opportunities. It provides a stark and necessary contrast to the high-tech glass and steel campuses found elsewhere in the city.

  11. 11

    The Hills at Vallco (Phase 1)

    10123 N Wolfe Rd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The Hills at Vallco represents one of the most ambitious mixed-use redevelopment projects in the history of Silicon Valley. Designed by world-renowned architect Rafael Viñoly, the project replaces a dying shopping mall with a sustainable urban district. The design features a massive green roof that will eventually serve as a public park elevated above the city streets. Sculptural residential and office blocks are integrated into the landscape to promote a harmony between work and nature. This project reflects the city's future trajectory toward high-density, sustainable living for the tech workforce.

  12. 12

    Apple Park Visitor Center

    10600 N Tantau Ave, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The Apple Park Visitor Center serves as the only public gateway for tourists wishing to experience the new headquarters. Its cantilevered carbon-fiber roof appears to float effortlessly above the glass-walled interior without any visible support. Inside, visitors can explore a highly detailed augmented reality model of the entire campus using iPads. The building features an observation deck that provides a clear, elevated view of the main 'Spaceship' ring. Every material used, from the quartz stone staircases to the timber ceilings, perfectly mirrors the finishes found in the main office.

    Visual & Performing Arts Center (VPAC)

    21250 Stevens Creek Blvd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The VPAC is a striking contemporary performance venue that anchors the northern edge of the De Anza College campus. It is defined by its sweeping zinc-clad roofline and a grand glass lobby that glows brilliantly during evening performances. The auditorium is acoustically tuned to host everything from professional orchestral concerts to high-profile lecture series. Its high-tech design and prominent location make it a regional landmark for the performing arts in the South Bay. The facility also houses dedicated educational spaces for students to learn the intricacies of theater production and stagecraft.

  14. 14

    St. Jude the Apostle Church

    20880 Lazaneo Dr, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    This architectural landmark is notable for its dramatic, soaring roofline that mimics the shape of a large tent. The Mid-Century Modern design was intended to signify a place of refuge and community during the city's post-war expansion. Its interior features a spacious nave with warm wood finishes and mid-century artistic details. The church has stood for decades as a spiritual center amidst the rapidly changing landscape of Silicon Valley. It remains one of the most recognizable historic silhouettes along the busy Stevens Creek corridor.

  15. 15

    Apple Results Way

    Results Way, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    The Results Way campus is a major satellite facility that helped define Apple’s architectural transition in the early 2010s. It features a contemporary glass and concrete aesthetic that emphasizes transparency and clean lines. The campus was specifically designed to be pedestrian-focused, with most vehicle traffic routed into massive underground parking structures. Its central courtyards and cafe spaces provide employees with quiet areas for collaboration and relaxation. This campus remains a critical part of Apple's sprawling infrastructure, housing thousands of software and hardware engineers.

    Cupertino Memorial Park Amphitheater

    21121 Stevens Creek Blvd, Cupertino, CA 95014, USA

    Built into the natural contours of the land, this amphitheater is the premier outdoor performance venue in Cupertino. It serves as the primary stage for the city’s summer concert series and the popular 'Shakespeare in the Park' events. The organic design utilizes grass seating and stone tiers to provide an intimate connection between the audience and performers. Following a major renovation in 2015, the facility now features improved lighting and sound systems for large-scale cultural festivals. It stands as a vital community space where the city's tech-focused population can gather for traditional public arts.
