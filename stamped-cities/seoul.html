Soul of Asia

Seoul architecture

Seoul is one of the most technologically advanced cities on Earth, boasting the world's fastest average internet speeds and a 24-hour culture that never sleeps. It is a place where ancient Joseon Dynasty palaces are perfectly preserved in the shadows of gleaming high-tech headquarters.

Before you go

  • Fly intoIncheon Int'l (ICN)

    Incheon International is consistently rated as one of the best airports in the world, featuring unusual amenities like a full golf course, an indoor ice rink, and traditional Korean cultural performances. It even offers free shower facilities and high-end rest zones for transit passengers to recharge between long flights.

  • CurrencySouth Korean Won (₩)
  • LanguageKorean

    Korean is the official language, and the local culture places deep importance on honorifics and age-based respect in speech. English is taught in schools and is widely understood in shopping districts and by the younger tech-savvy population.

  • Getting aroundSeoul Subway

    The Seoul Subway system features electronically heated seats during the winter months for passenger comfort. A massive network of color-coded buses utilizes dedicated lanes to maintain high speeds during rush hour. The AREX airport express train offers a non-stop, high-speed connection between Incheon and Seoul Station.

13 landmarks in Seoul

  1. 01

    Lotte World Tower

    300 Olympic-ro, Songpa-gu, Seoul 05551

    This supertall skyscraper is the tallest building in South Korea and stands as a global engineering marvel. Its tapered design draws inspiration from traditional Korean ceramics and the elegant stroke of a calligraphy brush. The exterior features a sophisticated 'diagrid' lantern roof designed to withstand extreme winds and major earthquakes. Clad in glass and accented by vertical white metal fins, the facade mimics the shimmering texture of fine porcelain. It serves as a vertical city containing offices, luxury residences, a seven-star hotel, and a world-class observation deck.

  2. 02

    Apple Myeongdong

    2-gil, Myeong-dong, Jung-gu, Seoul 04534

    This retail flagship serves as a tranquil 'pocket park' within the densest commercial district of Seoul. The design features landscaped pocket spaces and a shaded grove of trees that transition seamlessly into a grand indoor atrium. A cantilevered 'Forum' gallery provides a unique vantage point for community gatherings and creative sessions. Traditional Korean influence is found in the Boardroom, where bespoke reflectors made of Hanji paper create a warm, textured glow. The store stands as a modern glass pavilion at the base of a 20-story tower, blending tech-centric design with organic elements.

  3. 03

    Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP)

    281 Eulji-ro, Jung-gu, Seoul 04537

    Designed by Zaha Hadid, this neo-futuristic landmark is famous for its fluid, curving forms that completely lack straight lines. The structure is clad in over 45,000 unique aluminum panels that create a pixelated glow when illuminated from within at night. It is considered the world's largest atypical architectural structure and functions more like a landscape than a traditional building. The roof is covered in greenery and slopes down to merge directly with the surrounding public park. As a hub for the creative industries, it remains the most iconic symbol of Seoul's status as a global design capital.

  4. 04

    Gyeongbokgung Palace (Gwanghwamun Gate)

    161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul 03045

    This site serves as the primary royal palace of the Joseon dynasty and a cornerstone of Korean history. The wooden structures are meticulously decorated with Dancheong, a traditional five-color system representing cosmic harmony and protecting the timber. The massive Gwanghwamun Gate acts as the grand southern entrance, featuring three arched passages and a heavy stone base. It creates a stark, beautiful contrast between the ancient royal grounds and the modern high-rises of downtown Seoul. The palace remains the most visited historical landmark in the country, showcasing the pinnacle of traditional Korean aesthetics.

  5. 05

    N Seoul Tower

    105 Namsangongwon-gil, Yongsan-gu, Seoul 04340

    Located at the summit of Namsan Mountain, this tower is Seoul’s most recognizable landmark for mid-century engineering. Originally designed for TV and radio broadcasting, it has evolved into a beloved cultural destination famous for its panoramic city views. The tower’s base is home to the N Plaza, a vibrant complex featuring observation decks, museums, and terraces. A unique environmental feature is its nightly LED display, which changes color to signal the current air quality in the city. It remains a legendary spot for visitors, famously adorned with thousands of 'Love Locks' left by couples.

  6. 06

    63 Building (63 City)

    50 63-ro, Yeongdeungpo-gu, Seoul 07345

    This iconic skyscraper on Yeouido Island is instantly recognizable by its brilliant golden glass facade. The windows are coated with a thin layer of 24-karat gold, causing the building to shift from yellow to deep orange as the sun sets. When it was completed in 1985, it held the title of the tallest building in Asia for nearly two decades. It stands as a powerful symbol of the 'Han River Miracle,' representing South Korea's rapid post-war economic growth. The building remains a multi-use hub, housing a prestigious observation deck, an aquarium, and various financial offices.

  7. 07

    Seoul City Hall

    110 Sejong-daero, Jung-gu, Seoul 04524

    The new Seoul City Hall is a bold piece of wave-shaped architecture that curves dramatically over its historic predecessor. This eco-friendly glass structure was designed to represent transparency and the fluid connection between the government and the people. Inside, the building boasts 'The Green Wall,' which is currently the world’s largest vertical garden spanning seven stories. The design acts as a bridge, preserving the colonial-era 1926 stone building while looking toward a sustainable future. It provides a massive public lounge and cultural space that is open to all citizens and visitors.

  8. 08

    Ewha Womans University Campus Center

    52 Ewhayeodae-gil, Seodaemun-gu, Seoul 03760

    This project redefines the traditional skyscraper by placing the majority of its massive volume beneath the earth. The architect created a 'campus valley' by slicing a deep rift into the ground to house classrooms, libraries, and cafes. Glass walls line the entire length of the rift, ensuring that natural sunlight penetrates deep into the subterranean levels. The roof of the structure is a lush, walkable garden that restores the greenery originally lost to construction. It is a masterclass in landform architecture, seamlessly blending a high-density institutional facility with the natural topography.

  9. 09

    GT Tower East

    411 Seocho-daero, Seocho-gu, Seoul 06611

    Often called the 'S-Curve' building, this tower features a mesmerizing glass facade that undulates in a wave-like pattern. Unlike buildings where curves are merely decorative, the entire structural frame here is shifted to create a rhythmic silhouette. The reflective blue glass makes the tower appear as a column of water standing in the rigid Gangnam grid. This expressionist design provides a striking visual break from the surrounding conventional rectangular skyscrapers. It serves primarily as a high-end office space, adding a sense of movement and grace to the Seoul skyline.

  10. 10

    Some Sevit (Floating Islands)

    2085-14 Olympic-ro, Seocho-gu, Seoul 06500

    These three interconnected man-made islands on the Han River are the world's first floating buildings with large-scale LED facades. The design of the islands follows a botanical theme, representing the three stages of a flower: seed, bud, and blossom. Each structure is anchored by heavy chains to the riverbed but floats on pontoons to rise and fall with the water level. At night, the islands transform into a vibrant light show, reflecting off the river and illuminating the waterfront. They serve as a unique cultural venue for exhibitions, performances, and high-profile international events.

  11. 11

    Leeum Museum of Art

    60-16 Itaewon-ro 55-gil, Yongsan-gu, Seoul 04348

    The Leeum is a unique cultural complex comprised of three distinct buildings designed by three different architectural giants. Museum 1 by Mario Botta uses terracotta to house traditional Korean art in a fortress-like structure. Museum 2 by Jean Nouvel is a sleek glass and steel box dedicated to contemporary international art. The Black Box by Rem Koolhaas utilizes black concrete to house the museum's educational and administrative facilities. Together, they create an architectural dialogue between history, modernity, and the materials used to define them.

  12. 12

    National Museum of Korea

    137 Seobinggo-ro, Yongsan-gu, Seoul 04383

    This monumental building is inspired by the strength and simplicity of a traditional Korean fortress wall. Its most striking feature is the Maru, an open-air central plaza that frames a perfect view of Namsan Mountain. The structure utilizes precast concrete and granite to convey a sense of timelessness and national permanence. It is designed to protect the country's most significant historical artifacts while remaining open and inviting to the public. The museum is surrounded by extensive gardens and ponds, integrating the massive structure into a peaceful park setting.

  13. 13

    Apple Gangnam

    448 Gangnam-daero, Gangnam-gu, Seoul 06123

    Located in the heart of Seoul’s busiest district, this store features a double-height facade with a specialized 'gradient frit' pattern. This glass treatment creates a blurring effect that mimics the constant motion of the Gangnam-daero traffic. The interior is designed as a calm sanctuary, featuring a grand timber ceiling and locally sourced materials. It emphasizes a sensory experience, using specific acoustic treatments to provide a quiet escape from the surrounding neon-lit streets. The store serves as a community hub, offering creative workshops and technical support in a high-tech, organic environment.
