The New Flower

Addis Ababa architecture

Addis Ababa, whose name means 'New Flower' in Amharic, is Africa's diplomatic capital — home to the African Union headquarters and the United Nations Economic Commission for Africa. At 2,355 meters above sea level, it is one of the world's highest capital cities. Ethiopia is one of the only African nations never colonized by a European power (with the exception of a brief Italian occupation from 1936–1941), a source of immense national pride. The city is also the gateway to one of the world's oldest Christian civilizations and the ancient rock-hewn churches of Lalibela.

Before you go

  • Fly intoAddis Ababa Bole International Airport (ADD)

    Addis Ababa Bole International Airport is Africa's second-busiest airport and the main hub for Ethiopian Airlines, consistently ranked as the continent's best airline. A major terminal expansion project completed in 2019 dramatically increased the airport's capacity and quality. Ethiopian Airlines uses Addis as a connecting hub between Europe, Asia, and the rest of Africa, making Bole one of the most important aviation crossroads in the world.

  • CurrencyEthiopian Birr (Br)
  • LanguageAmharic

    Amharic is Ethiopia's official working language and is written in the Ge'ez script (Ethiopic), one of the world's few original writing systems still in active use. Ethiopia is home to over 80 languages across its many ethnic groups, with Oromo, Tigrinya, and Somali also widely spoken. English is taught in schools and is used in higher education and government. Coffee originated in Ethiopia — the word 'coffee' itself traces back to the Ethiopian region of Kaffa — and the traditional coffee ceremony is a central part of social culture.

  • Getting aroundLRT & Minibuses

    Addis Ababa operates Africa's first modern Light Rail Transit (LRT) system, which opened in 2015 with Chinese engineering support. Two lines cross the city center in a north-south and east-west pattern. Blue and white minibuses (called 'blue donkeys' by locals) are the main public transport for most residents, supplemented by taxis and increasingly by ride-hailing services. The city is expanding rapidly and traffic congestion is a growing challenge.
