The Phoenix City

Warsaw architecture

Warsaw is known as the Phoenix City because it was almost completely destroyed during World War II — up to 85% of the city was razed, and its population fell from 1.3 million to under 1,000 by war's end. The city was painstakingly rebuilt from the ruins, and the Old Town reconstruction was so faithful to 18th-century paintings and architectural records that UNESCO added it to the World Heritage List in recognition of its extraordinary postwar reconstruction effort. Warsaw is also the birthplace of composer Frédéric Chopin.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoWarsaw Chopin Airport (WAW)

        Warsaw Chopin Airport, named after the city's most celebrated composer, is Poland's largest airport and one of Central Europe's key aviation hubs. LOT Polish Airlines operates its main hub here. The airport is conveniently located just 10km from the city center, reachable by direct train in about 25 minutes. A new Central Communication Port airport, planned as one of Europe's largest, is under development to eventually replace Chopin as Poland's primary hub.

      • CurrencyPolish Zloty (zł)
      • LanguagePolish

        Polish is a West Slavic language written in the Latin alphabet with additional diacritical marks. It is renowned for its complex grammar — with seven grammatical cases — and consonant clusters that challenge even seasoned language learners. Warsaw's younger population and business community speaks English well, and the city has absorbed significant international communities in recent years as Poland's economy has grown into one of Europe's largest. Polish hospitality is warm and direct.

      • Getting aroundMetro & Trams

        Warsaw's metro has two lines, with Line 2 cutting east-west through the city center and Line 1 running north-south. The ZTM transit authority integrates the metro, trams, and buses under a single fare system. Warsaw's tram network is one of the most extensive in Poland and continues to expand. The iconic red and yellow ZTM trams share space with modern low-floor vehicles. Uber, Bolt, and local app FreeNow are widely used. The city is also increasingly bike-friendly, with a popular Veturilo bike-sharing scheme.
