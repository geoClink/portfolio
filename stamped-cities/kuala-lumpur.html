KL

Kuala Lumpur architecture

The Petronas Twin Towers dominated the world's skyline as the tallest buildings from 1998 to 2004 and remain the world's tallest twin towers. They are connected by a sky bridge on the 41st and 42nd floors offering panoramic views over the city's forest-shrouded skyline. Kuala Lumpur is one of the world's most culturally diverse cities, where Malay, Chinese, Indian, and indigenous traditions coexist in its food, festivals, and architecture — and the food scene is legendary.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoKuala Lumpur International Airport (KUL)

        Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KLIA) is one of Southeast Asia's premier aviation hubs, featuring a striking steel and glass main terminal inspired by the surrounding rainforest canopy. KLIA2, the low-cost carrier terminal, is one of the largest dedicated budget airline terminals in the world. The KLIA Ekspres train connects the airport to the city center in just 28 minutes, making it one of Asia's fastest airport rail links.

      • CurrencyMalaysian Ringgit (RM)
      • LanguageMalay, English & Mandarin

        Malaysia is an extraordinarily multilingual country. Bahasa Malaysia (Malay) is the national language, while English is widely used in business, education, and tourism. Malaysian Chinese communities speak Cantonese, Mandarin, Hokkien, and other Chinese dialects. In KL, you will often hear Manglish — English mixed liberally with Malay, Cantonese, and Tamil words in a single sentence.

      • Getting aroundLRT, MRT & KL Monorail

        KL's public transport network includes multiple rail systems: the LRT with two urban lines, the MRT serving the greater Klang Valley, and the KL Monorail connecting the city center's hotel and shopping district. All are accessible with a single Touch 'n Go card. The city also runs a free GoKL City Bus service looping through central areas, making short trips between landmarks cost nothing.
