Brazil is a continental giant, occupying nearly half of South America and serving as the world's largest producer of coffee for over 150 years. It is a nation defined by its vibrant cultural melting pot, ranging from the Amazon rainforest to the world-famous Carnival celebrations in Rio.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoGuarulhos Int'l (GRU)

        As the primary international gateway to Brazil, Guarulhos is the busiest airport in South America by passenger volume. It is a massive complex that was significantly modernized for the World Cup and Olympics, featuring a dedicated terminal for long-haul international flights with extensive luxury amenities.

      • CurrencyBrazilian Real (R$)
      • LanguagePortuguese

        Portuguese is the sole official language and is spoken with a musical, open accent that differs significantly from the European version. Most locals appreciate any effort to speak 'Português' and are very patient with learners.

      • Getting aroundCuritiba BRT & Metrô

        Curitiba was the global pioneer of the 'Bus as a Train' concept, using iconic glass tube stations for level boarding. São Paulo's Metrô is one of the cleanest and most efficient in the world, serving millions of passengers across six lines. Rio de Janeiro utilizes the 'Bondinho' tram in Santa Teresa and a modern VLT light rail system in the revitalized port area.

      13 landmarks in Recife

      1. 01

        Kahal Zur Israel Synagogue

        Rua do Bom Jesus, 197, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-170, Brazil

        Kahal Zur Israel holds the immense historical distinction of being the first synagogue ever established in the Americas. It was founded in 1636 during the Dutch occupation of Brazil when religious tolerance briefly flourished in the region. Located on the iconic 'Street of the Jews,' the building stands as a testament to the Sephardic community that once thrived in Recife. The structure underwent an extensive archaeological excavation and restoration led by Lina Bo Bardi to reveal its original 17th-century foundations. Today, it operates as a Jewish museum and cultural center, preserving the memory of the first Jewish congregation in the New World.

      2. 02

        Ricardo Brennand Institute

        Alameda Antônio Brennand, s/n, Várzea, Recife, PE, 50741-904, Brazil

        The Ricardo Brennand Institute is a stunning cultural complex designed in the romantic style of a medieval Tudor castle. Set within a lush landscape of Atlantic Forest, the brick-and-stone architecture creates a surreal atmosphere of European antiquity in the heart of the tropics. It houses one of the world's most impressive private collections of armory, including hundreds of suits of armor and historical weapons. The museum also contains a vast collection of paintings and artifacts related to the Dutch presence in Brazil during the 17th century. It is consistently ranked as one of the best museums in South America due to its unique architecture and impeccably curated grounds.

      3. 03

        Santa Isabel Theater

        Praça da República, s/n, Santo Antônio, Recife, PE, 50010-040, Brazil

        Santa Isabel Theater is a magnificent Neoclassical monument that has served as the cultural heart of Recife since the mid-19th century. Its distinctive pink facade and grand Corinthian columns were designed by French engineer Louis Léger Vauthier. The theater has been a stage for major historical events, including abolitionist speeches and the highest social gatherings of the Pernambucan elite. Inside, the horseshoe-shaped auditorium is decorated with opulent gold leaf and rich red velvet, reflecting 19th-century luxury. As one of the few remaining examples of 'monument-theaters' in Brazil, it continues to host the city's most prestigious concerts and plays.

      4. 04

        Paço do Frevo

        Praça Tiradentes, s/n, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-230, Brazil

        Paço do Frevo is a vibrant cultural center dedicated to preserving the music and dance traditions of Frevo, a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. The museum is housed in the beautifully restored headquarters of the former Western Telegraph Company. While the exterior retains its classic eclectic character, the interior features a modern design with glass panels and bold typography. Visitors can participate in dance workshops, explore historical archives, and enjoy live musical performances throughout the year. The building serves as a permanent tribute to the energy and resilience of the Pernambucan carnival spirit.

      5. 05

        Malakoff Tower

        Praça do Arsenal da Marinha, s/n, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-360, Brazil

        The Malakoff Tower is a Moorish-inspired monument that once served as a grand gateway to the city's Naval Arsenal. Its architecture was heavily influenced by Tunisian styles, featuring symmetrical lines and a central clock tower. Throughout the 19th century, it functioned as both a naval signal tower and an important astronomical observatory. Today, it has been repurposed into a cultural center that regularly hosts contemporary photography and music exhibitions. The upper deck remains a popular spot for stargazing and offers a panoramic view of the Port of Recife and the surrounding historic district.

      6. 06

        Capibaribe Building (Edifício Capibaribe)

        Rua da Aurora, 137, Boa Vista, Recife, PE, 50050-000, Brazil

        The Capibaribe Building is a pioneering landmark of Brazilian Modernism in the Northeast region. Designed by Portuguese architect Delfim Amorim, it is famous for its extensive use of 'cobogós'—hollowed ceramic bricks that allow for natural ventilation. This innovative design was a direct response to the tropical climate of Recife, blending aesthetic beauty with functional cooling. The building’s slender profile and horizontal lines reflect the optimistic architectural spirit of the mid-20th century. It remains an iconic residential structure on the historic Rua da Aurora, overlooking the river that shares its name.

      7. 07

        Brennand Sculpture Park

        Molhe do Porto de Recife, s/n, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-000, Brazil

        The Brennand Sculpture Park is a breathtaking open-air museum situated on a reef breakwater directly in front of the historic city center. It was created to celebrate the 500th anniversary of Brazil's discovery and features over 90 works by the legendary Francisco Brennand. The focal point of the park is the 32-meter tall 'Crystal Tower,' a ceramic masterpiece that serves as a lighthouse-like symbol for the port. Access to the park is typically gained via a short boat ride from the Marco Zero plaza, offering unique views of the city skyline. This surreal ceramic garden has become the primary visual icon for Recife’s maritime identity.

      8. 08

        Mirante da Aurora

        Rua da Aurora, 1501, Santo Amaro, Recife, PE, 50040-000, Brazil

        Mirante da Aurora is a prominent example of Recife's modern skyward expansion and high-end residential engineering. This incredibly slender skyscraper dominates the northern riverfront with its contemporary glass and concrete facade. Its design maximizes the views of the Capibaribe River and the Atlantic Ocean for its residents. The building represents the new wave of verticality that is redefining the urban landscape of Pernambuco's capital. Standing at 148 meters, it is currently one of the tallest residential structures in the city, symbolizing luxury and contemporary urban life.

      9. 09

        Paço Alfândega

        Rua da Alfândega, 35, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-030, Brazil

        Paço Alfândega is an award-winning example of adaptive reuse, transforming an 18th-century customs house into a sophisticated commercial hub. The project carefully preserves the massive colonial stone arches and thick walls of the original structure while integrating modern steel and glass. In its previous lives, the building served as a port warehouse and a Franciscan convent during the colonial era. Today, it houses high-end shops, restaurants, and cultural spaces that cater to both locals and tourists. Its central atrium is frequently used for fashion shows and art installations, blending historic weight with modern elegance.

      10. 10

        Recife House of Culture

        Rua Floriano Peixoto, s/n, Santo Antônio, Recife, PE, 50020-060, Brazil

        The Recife House of Culture is a fascinating example of architectural transformation, housed in what was once the city's largest prison. Built in a panopticon-style cross shape, the design allowed guards to monitor all corridors from a single central point. Each of the former jail cells has been ingeniously converted into a boutique shop selling traditional Pernambucan crafts and art. The building’s heavy stone walls and iron bars remain as a stark reminder of its original punitive purpose. It now stands as one of the most significant artisanal markets in the Northeast, celebrating local creativity and heritage.

      11. 11

        Officina Francisco Brennand

        Propriedade Santos Cosme e Damião, s/n, Várzea, Recife, PE, 50741-904, Brazil

        Officina Francisco Brennand is a monumental studio-museum situated within the ruins of an early 20th-century brick factory. This sprawling 'cathedral' of ceramic art is home to thousands of sculptures, reliefs, and paintings by the late artist. The architecture is a gothic-surrealist blend that incorporates the original industrial kilns and chimneys into a mythical landscape. It is surrounded by lush gardens designed by the legendary landscape architect Roberto Burle Marx. The site serves as both a spiritual sanctuary for art and a profound exploration of human mythology through ceramic forms.

      12. 12

        Cais do Sertão Museum

        Avenida Alfredo Lisboa, s/n, Recife Antigo, Recife, PE, 50030-150, Brazil

        Cais do Sertão is an ultra-modern museum built within a revitalized port warehouse to honor the culture of the Brazilian hinterlands. Its most striking feature is a massive concrete facade perforated with 'cobogó' patterns that mimic the texture of dry branches in the Sertão. The museum utilizes cutting-edge interactive technology to tell the life story of the legendary musician Luiz Gonzaga. It creates a powerful architectural dialogue between the coastal sea of Recife and the arid traditions of the deep countryside. This award-winning building is considered a masterpiece of contemporary Brutalist-Regionalism in Brazil.

      13. 13

        iPlace Apple Premium Partner (Shopping Recife)

        Shopping Recife, Rua Padre Carapuceiro, 777, Boa Viagem, Recife, PE, 51020-900, Brazil

        The iPlace Apple Premium Partner store in Shopping Recife is the premier destination for Apple technology in Pernambuco. Located in the city's most traditional shopping mall, the store was upgraded to meet the highest global retail standards. It follows the minimalist 'Stage' concept, utilizing light wood textures, integrated interior greenery, and a spacious open floor plan. The store features a dedicated area for technical support and personalized training sessions led by Apple experts. Its expansive glass storefront reflects the modern and clean aesthetic that the brand is known for worldwide.
