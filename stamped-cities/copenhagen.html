City of Spires

Copenhagen architecture

Copenhagen is home to Bakken, the world's oldest operating amusement park, which opened its gates in 1583. It is also a global leader in urban sustainability, with an ambitious goal to become the world's first carbon-neutral capital city by the end of the decade.

Before you go

  • Fly intoKastrup (CPH)

    Kastrup is world-renowned for its Nordic design philosophy, featuring beautiful wooden floors and a cozy 'Hygge' atmosphere that feels more like a lounge than a transit hub. It is one of the oldest international airports in Europe and serves as a major gateway for the entire Scandinavian region.

  • CurrencyDanish Krone (kr)
  • LanguageDanish

    Danish is the native tongue, though the linguistic transition for travelers is seamless as nearly every resident is completely fluent in English. The local culture emphasizes 'Hygge,' which often translates to a warm and friendly style of social interaction.

  • Getting aroundThe Metro

    The Copenhagen Metro is a 24/7 fully driverless system that is renowned for its punctuality. S-trains provide the suburban rail backbone, connecting the city center to the surrounding Finger Plan districts. Movia 'Havnebusser' are bright yellow harbor buses that serve as water taxis across the city's canals.

16 landmarks in Copenhagen

  1. 01

    CopenHill

    Vindmøllevej 6, 2300 København S, Denmark

    This revolutionary waste-to-energy power plant doubles as an urban sports facility and a public park. The sloping roof features a 450-meter hiking trail and a year-round synthetic ski slope for residents. On its exterior, the building hosts the world's tallest artificial climbing wall at 85 meters high. The design aims to align with Copenhagen’s goal of becoming the world’s first carbon-neutral capital. It stands as a global example of 'hedonistic sustainability,' where a utility building provides genuine social value.

  2. 02

    The Black Diamond

    Søren Kierkegaards Plads 1, 1221 København K, Denmark

    This striking extension to the Royal Library is one of the most significant landmarks on the city's waterfront. Its sharp, tilted facade is composed of black granite from Zimbabwe that glimmers in the sunlight. A large glass longitudinal fissure cleaves the building in two, flooding the interior atrium with natural light. The structure houses not only books but also a concert hall, a café, and several exhibition spaces. It is connected to the original 1906 library by three overhead walkways that bridge the busy street below.

  3. 03

    Copenhagen Opera House

    Ekvipagemestervej 10, 1438 København K, Denmark

    Located on the island of Holmen, this opera house sits in a direct line with Amalienborg Palace and the Marble Church. The building is famous for its massive cantilevered roof which is one of the largest of its kind in the world. Its glass facade offers spectators a panoramic view across the harbor toward the historic city center. The main stage is finished with Jura Gelb limestone and the ceiling is adorned with 105,000 sheets of 24-carat gold leaf. It remains one of the most expensive and technologically advanced opera houses ever constructed.

  4. 04

    BLOX (Danish Architecture Center)

    Bryghuspladsen 10, 1473 København K, Denmark

    This complex structure is designed as a stack of glass boxes that straddles a busy ring road along the harbor. It serves as the headquarters for the Danish Architecture Center and includes apartments, offices, and a fitness center. The building was envisioned as an urban connector to link the quiet waterfront with the bustling city core. Its pixelated green glass exterior reflects the surrounding water and the historic buildings of the Bryghuspladsen area. Below ground level, the structure features a fully automated public car park that is a marvel of local engineering.

  5. 05

    Grundtvig's Church

    På Bjerget 14B, 2400 København NV, Denmark

    This unique place of worship is a rare masterpiece of Expressionist architecture located in the Bispebjerg district. Approximately six million yellow bricks were used in its construction to create its massive, soaring interior. The west facade resembles a giant church pipe organ, standing nearly 50 meters tall above the surrounding square. Inside, the design uses traditional Gothic proportions but strips away ornamentation to focus on the play of light on brickwork. It stands as a powerful tribute to the Danish philosopher N.F.S. Grundtvig and remains a site of quiet architectural wonder.

  6. 06

    Rundetårn

    Købmagergade 52A, 1150 København K, Denmark

    Built by King Christian IV, the Round Tower is the oldest functioning astronomical observatory in all of Europe. The most famous feature of the tower is its 209-meter long spiral ramp that winds seven and a half times around the core. This ramp was designed to allow a horse and carriage to transport heavy astronomical instruments to the top. The observatory at the summit is still used by amateur astronomers to view the night sky over Copenhagen. It also houses a beautiful library hall that now serves as a vibrant venue for art exhibitions and concerts.

  7. 07

    Axel Towers

    Axeltorv 2, 1609 København V, Denmark

    Axel Towers consists of five interconnected cylindrical buildings located directly across from the historic Tivoli Gardens. Each tower varies in height and is clad in a distinctive copper-colored zinc alloy that will weather beautifully over time. The circular layout of the buildings was designed to eliminate traditional 'back sides,' ensuring the towers look attractive from every angle. Public courtyards and elevated gardens at the base provide a fluid, open space for pedestrians in the city center. The towers house a mix of offices, law firms, and high-end dining establishments with panoramic city views.

  8. 08

    Amalienborg Palace

    Amalienborg Slotsplads, 1257 København K, Denmark

    Amalienborg is the winter residence of the Danish Royal Family and a masterpiece of Nordic Rococo architecture. The complex consists of four identical palace facades arranged around a grand octagonal courtyard. In the center of the square stands a monumental equestrian statue of King Frederick V, the palace's founder. Every day at noon, the Royal Guard performs a ceremonial changing of the guard ceremony in front of the palace. Visitors can explore the museum located in Christian VIII’s Palace to see private royal interiors spanning several generations.

  9. 09

    The Silo

    Fortkaj 30, 2150 København Nordhavn, Denmark

    This former industrial grain silo in the North Harbor district has been transformed into a distinctive high-rise residential building. Its raw concrete interior was preserved while the exterior was clad in a faceted, galvanized steel 'armor.' This metal skin protects the structure from the harsh salt air of the surrounding harbor environment. Large industrial-scale windows were cut into the concrete to provide residents with expansive views of the water. The top floor houses a public restaurant that offers a 360-degree view of Copenhagen’s rapidly developing skyline.

  10. 10

    Superkilen Park (Red Square)

    Nørrebrogade 210, 2200 København N, Denmark

    Superkilen is a vibrant urban park in the Nørrebro district designed to celebrate the cultural diversity of the local community. The 'Red Square' section features bright magenta and orange surfaces that serve as an extension of the local market life. It functions as an outdoor museum of global objects, featuring neon signs from Qatar and manhole covers from Zanzibar. The landscape design includes curved lines and bold colors that encourage play, skating, and social gathering. This project has won numerous awards for its innovative approach to social integration through architectural landscape design.

  11. 11

    Apple Kongens Nytorv

    Kongens Nytorv 26, 1050 København K, Denmark

    Housed in the historic Brønnums Hus, this flagship store is located on one of Copenhagen's most prestigious public squares. The renovation carefully preserved the 19th-century facade and its intricate stonework to respect the city's heritage. Inside, the design seamlessly integrates Apple's signature minimalism with restored period ceilings and grand architectural details. A central courtyard was opened to the public, creating a serene gathering space in the heart of the retail district. It serves as a prime example of modern retail taking residence within a high-value historic preservation project.

  12. 12

    8 House

    Richard Mortensens Vej 81, 2300 København S, Denmark

    The 8 House is a bow-shaped mixed-use development that allows residents to bike from the street level up to the 10th floor. A continuous public promenade stretches around the entire building, creating a unique sense of mountain-path community. The 'figure-eight' layout creates two distinct courtyards separated by a central knot that houses common facilities. By combining suburban life with urban density, it offers a new model for sustainable and social housing in the Ørestad district. It has been recognized globally for its innovative approach to integrating various types of residential and commercial spaces.

  13. 13

    Old Royal Library

    Søren Kierkegaards Plads 1, 1221 København K, Denmark

    This historic brick building serves as the traditional heart of the Danish Royal Library complex. It is famous for its peaceful hidden garden located in the central courtyard, providing a quiet escape from the city. The architecture is a prime example of Danish historicism, featuring red bricks and elaborate decorative masonry. Inside, the grand reading rooms have been preserved to offer a classic scholarly atmosphere for researchers and students. It is physically and conceptually linked to the modern Black Diamond, bridging the gap between old and new Copenhagen.

  14. 14

    Cykelslangen (The Bicycle Snake)

    Cykelslangen, 1561 København V, Denmark

    The Bicycle Snake is an elevated orange cycle bridge that provides a fast and scenic route across the harbor. Its slender steel structure allows cyclists to glide seven meters above the quay, separated from pedestrian traffic. The bridge's elegant, winding form has become a worldwide symbol of Copenhagen's commitment to bicycle infrastructure. By bypassing several sets of stairs and busy intersections, it has significantly reduced travel time for daily commuters. It offers a unique 'floating' sensation for riders as they move through the urban landscape of Fisketorvet.

  15. 15

    Nimb Hotel (Tivoli Gardens)

    Bernstorffsgade 5, 1577 København V, Denmark

    Located within the historic Tivoli Gardens, the Nimb Hotel features a striking Moorish-inspired facade. The building is decorated with white marble-stucco and topped with several ornate onion domes. At night, the entire silhouette is outlined by thousands of individual lightbulbs, creating a fairy-tale appearance. It originally served as a bazaar but was later converted into one of Scandinavia's most exclusive luxury hotels. Each room is uniquely decorated and offers residents a direct view of the magical park's central fountain and gardens.

  16. 16

    Royal Danish Playhouse

    Sankt Annæ Plads 36, 1250 København K, Denmark

    The Royal Danish Playhouse is situated at the end of the harbor, offering a dramatic view of the water. Its most striking feature is the long, glass-walled foyer that glows warmly like a lantern after sunset. Much of the theater's volume is actually submerged or extends over the harbor on slender concrete pillars. To reduce its environmental impact, the building utilizes a sustainable cooling system powered by the surrounding seawater. The wooden boardwalk outside serves as a popular public space for locals to enjoy the harbor atmosphere during the summer.
