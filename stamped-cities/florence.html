The Cradle of the Renaissance

Florence architecture

Florence is widely considered the birthplace of the Renaissance, the cultural and artistic movement that transformed Europe. It produced Michelangelo, Leonardo da Vinci, Galileo, Dante, and Machiavelli, among others. Brunelleschi's cathedral dome — the Duomo — was completed in 1436 and remains the largest brick dome ever constructed. The Uffizi Gallery houses one of the world's most important collections of Renaissance painting.

Before you go

  • Fly intoFlorence Peretola Airport (FLR)

    Florence Peretola Airport (Amerigo Vespucci Airport) is named after the Florentine explorer who gave America its name. The airport sits close to the city center, just 5km from the Duomo, making arrivals and departures unusually convenient for a major cultural destination. A new tramway extension directly linking the terminal to the city center opened in 2019.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageItalian

    Florence is historically significant in the story of the Italian language: the Tuscan dialect spoken here during the Renaissance became the foundation of modern standard Italian. Dante's Divine Comedy, written in Florentine vernacular, effectively created Italian literature. While locals speak standard Italian, a Florentine accent has its own distinctive character that Italians across the country recognize.

  • Getting aroundATAF Buses & Trams

    Florence's historic center is designated a Limited Traffic Zone (ZTL), restricting private cars from the medieval streets during much of the day. The city is best explored on foot or by bicycle — a popular bike-sharing network called Mobike operates throughout the center. The T1 and T2 tram lines connect the main train station to the outlying neighborhoods, while orange ATAF buses serve the wider metropolitan area.
