The City of Ships (or The Roman City)

Bath architecture

Bath is the only city in the UK designated as a UNESCO World Heritage site in its entirety. It is world-famous for its Roman-built baths, stunning honey-colored Georgian architecture like the Royal Crescent, and its deep association with author Jane Austen, who lived and wrote here.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBristol Airport (BRS)

    While Bath doesn't have its own airport, most visitors arrive via Bristol Airport or by a high-speed train from London Paddington, which arrives at the historic Bath Spa station—designed by the legendary engineer Isambard Kingdom Brunel.

  • CurrencyBritish Pound (£)
  • LanguageBritish English

    The local accent is a gentle 'West Country' lilt, characterized by soft 'r' sounds and a rhythmic pace. While modern and cosmopolitan, the city retains a polite, sophisticated tone reflective of its history as a 18th-century social resort.

  • Getting aroundFirst Bus / Bath Park & Ride

    The city center is highly walkable and mostly pedestrianized. For reaching the outskirts, the comprehensive bus network and the 'Park & Ride' system are the primary modes of transit, as driving in the narrow, historic streets can be a challenge.

15 landmarks in Bath

  1. 01

    The Roman Baths

    Abbey Churchyard, Bath BA1 1LZ

    Constructed around 70 AD as a grand bathing and socializing complex, this is one of the best-preserved Roman remains in the world. The site flows with natural thermal springs that still reach 46°C today. Visitors can walk on the original Roman pavements and see the Great Bath, which is lined with 45 sheets of lead. While you can no longer swim here, the site remains the spiritual and historical heart of the city, perfectly blending ancient engineering with a Victorian superstructure.

  2. 02

    The Royal Crescent

    1-30 Royal Crescent, Bath BA1 2LS

    Arguably the most famous example of Georgian architecture in the world, this crescent of 30 terraced houses is arranged in a sweeping semi-ellipse. Its facade features 114 Ionic columns and remains virtually unchanged since it was built in the 18th century. It represents the height of the 'Palladian' style that defines Bath’s golden-hued aesthetic. Today, it houses a luxury hotel, a museum at No. 1, and several private residences, overlooking the vast Royal Victoria Park.

  3. 03

    Bath Abbey

    Bath BA1 1LT

    A former Benedictine monastery, this Abbey is a premier example of Perpendicular Gothic architecture. It is famous for its 'Ladder of Angels' on the West Front and its magnificent fan-vaulted ceiling, which creates a forest of stone overhead. The Abbey is known as the 'Lantern of the West' due to the vast amount of stained glass that fills the interior with light. It stands on the site where the first King of all England, King Edgar, was crowned in 973 AD.

  4. 04

    Pulteney Bridge

    Bridge St, Bath BA2 4AT

    One of only four bridges in the world to have shops built across its full span on both sides, Pulteney Bridge is an architectural marvel crossing the River Avon. It was designed to connect the city of Bath to the newly developed land of Bathwick. Its design was inspired by the Ponte Vecchio in Florence. The view of the bridge alongside the horseshoe-shaped Pulteney Weir is the most photographed spot in the city, blending functional infrastructure with high-end retail elegance.

  5. 05

    Thermae Bath Spa

    The Hetling Pump Room, Bath BA1 1SJ

    This project is a striking example of modern architecture in a historic setting. It successfully integrated original Georgian buildings with a contemporary glass-and-stone structure. The highlight is the open-air rooftop pool, where visitors can bathe in the city’s naturally warm, mineral-rich waters while overlooking the Bath Abbey. It serves as the modern successor to the Roman Baths, proving that Bath's thermal water heritage is still a living, breathing part of the city.

    The Holburne Museum

    Great Pulteney St, Bath BA2 4DB

    Originally built as the Sydney Hotel, this building serves as the grand terminus to Great Pulteney Street. It is famous for its stunning facade and the controversial but award-winning modern glass extension at the rear. It was Bath’s first public art gallery and sits within the beautiful Sydney Gardens, a favorite walk of Jane Austen.

  7. 07

    The Circus

    The Circus, Bath BA1 2ET

    Consisting of three curved segments of Georgian townhouses, The Circus forms a perfect circle. John Wood the Elder designed it to be the same diameter as Stonehenge, symbolizing Bath's ancient roots. The facade features three different classical orders: Doric, Ionic, and Corinthian, stacked one above the other.

    Prior Park Mansion

    Ralph Allen Dr, Bath BA2 5AH

    A massive Palladian mansion built to demonstrate the beauty of Bath Stone. It sits at the head of a valley with sweeping views over the city. Its most famous feature is the Palladian Bridge, one of only four of its kind in the world, located in the sweeping landscaped gardens designed by Capability Brown.

    Bath Assembly Rooms

    Bennett St, Bath BA1 2QH

    In the 18th century, this was the heart of Bath's high society. It consists of a Ball Room, Tea Room, and Octagon Room. Jane Austen attended many balls here, and the building is famous for its original Whitefriars crystal chandeliers, which are among the finest in the world.

    Bath Guildhall

    High St, Bath BA1 5AW

    A grand administrative building featuring a Banqueting Room that is often cited as the most beautiful Georgian room in the UK. The building features ornate plasterwork, gold leaf, and massive portraits of British royalty. It still serves as the seat of local government and houses the city's historic markets.

    Beckford's Tower

    Lansdown Rd, Bath BA1 9BH

    A 120-foot neo-classical tower built for the eccentric wealthy novelist William Beckford. It was intended to house his library and art collection. The tower features a golden lantern at the top and offers the most panoramic views of the Somerset countryside. It stands as a symbol of Romantic-era architectural ambition.

    Theatre Royal Bath

    Saw Close, Bath BA1 1ET

    One of the oldest and most beautiful theaters in Britain. The interior is a masterpiece of Victorian design with red velvet and gold leaf. It has hosted the world’s most famous actors for over two centuries and remains a premier touring venue for West End productions.

    Milsom Place

    Milsom St, Bath BA1 1BZ

It manages to retain the original staircases and fireplaces of the 18th-century homes while providing a modern, open-air retail experience.

    Green Park Station

    Green Park Rd, Bath BA1 1JB

    A former railway station characterized by its massive vaulted glass roof. Instead of trains, it now hosts independent markets, local food stalls, and small businesses. It is a prime example of Bath’s ability to preserve industrial-era architecture for community-focused modern use.
