Emerald City

Seattle architecture

In the late 19th century, the entire downtown area of Seattle was raised by one story to avoid constant flooding, leaving a hidden network of underground passages that can still be toured today. It is the birthplace of Starbucks and Amazon, surrounded by the stunning natural beauty of Mount Rainier and Puget Sound.

Before you go

  • Fly intoSea-Tac (SEA)

    Sea-Tac Airport features a massive collection of Pacific Northwest public art, including glass sculptures and indigenous carvings that reflect the region's heritage. It is one of the fastest-growing airports in the country and serves as a major gateway for flights to Alaska and across the Pacific Ocean.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    English is the standard tongue, delivered with a polite but socially reserved tone often referred to as the 'Seattle Freeze.' While residents are helpful, visitors may notice a distinct preference for outdoor-focused conversation and a laid-back, tech-casual communication style.

  • Getting aroundLink Light Rail

    Link Light Rail is one of the fastest-growing rail systems in the country and connects the airport to the university. The Seattle Center Monorail was built for the 1962 World's Fair and still provides a vital downtown link. King County Metro operates an extensive bus fleet, including one of the largest trolleybus networks in the US.

11 landmarks in Seattle

  1. 01

    Space Needle

    400 Broad Street, Seattle, WA 98109

    Built for the 1962 World's Fair, this 'Googie' style landmark was designed to resemble a flying saucer on a tripod. It recently underwent a massive 'Century Project' renovation to modernize its structure and guest experience. The project replaced its cage-like wire floor with the world's first and only revolving glass floor, known as 'The Loupe.' This innovation offers visitors a direct and thrilling view 500 feet down to the Seattle Center below. The tower remains the most recognizable icon of the Pacific Northwest skyline.

  2. 02

    Seattle Central Library

    1000 4th Avenue, Seattle, WA 98104

    This building is a radical departure from traditional library design, consisting of five 'floating' platforms wrapped in a glass and steel skin. The exterior diamond-shaped grid provides structural support while allowing natural light to flood the interior spaces. A unique 'Books Spiral' features a continuous four-story ramp that allows patrons to browse the non-fiction collection without stairs. The interior also features bright chartreuse escalators that have become a popular icon for social media photography. It serves as the flagship of the Seattle Public Library system and a hub for community learning.

  3. 03

    The Spheres (Amazon HQ)

    2111 7th Avenue, Seattle, WA 98121

    These three interconnected glass domes serve as a unique workspace and conservatory for Amazon employees. They contain over 40,000 plants from 30 different countries, creating a vibrant tropical environment. The pentagonal hexecontahedron shape of the glass panels is a marvel of modern parametric design. This space was created to provide a 'biophilic' environment intended to spark creativity and reduce workplace stress. It stands as a centerpiece of Amazon's urban campus in the heart of downtown Seattle.

  4. 04

    Museum of Pop Culture (MoPOP)

    325 5th Avenue N, Seattle, WA 98109

    Inspired by the look of a smashed Fender Stratocaster guitar, the building’s exterior is composed of 21,000 metal shingles. The gold, silver, red, and blue colors change depending on the lighting to represent the fluidity of music. It features a massive central indoor space called 'Sky Church' with one of the world's largest LED screens. The design reflects the high-energy nature of the pop culture and science fiction exhibits housed within. This Frank Gehry masterpiece is located right at the base of the Space Needle.

  5. 05

    Columbia Center

    701 5th Avenue, Seattle, WA 98104

    The Columbia Center is the tallest building in Seattle and the entire state of Washington. Its design consists of three interlocking concave towers that look like a series of waves from above. Originally intended to be even taller, its final height was limited by FAA regulations due to airport proximity. It features a public Sky View Observatory on the 73rd floor offering 360-degree views of the region. The building's dark tinted glass and unique silhouette make it a dominant figure in the downtown skyline.

  6. 06

    Rainier Tower

    1301 5th Avenue, Seattle, WA 98101

    Known locally as the 'Beaver Building,' it sits atop a 121-foot curved concrete pedestal that tapers toward the ground. This unusual base was designed to create more open space at the street level for a large shopping plaza. Despite looking top-heavy, the pedestal is incredibly stable and engineered to withstand major seismic activity. It remains one of the most recognizable and gravity-defying silhouettes in the entire city of Seattle. The tower above the pedestal contains high-end office spaces and offers views of the surrounding skyscrapers.

  7. 07

    Pike Place Market

    85 Pike Street, Seattle, WA 98101

    This is one of the oldest continuously operated public farmers' markets in the United States. Built into a steep hill, the market features multiple lower levels that contain a labyrinth of unique shops. The architecture is primarily utilitarian and historic, preserving the gritty charm of Seattle's early commercial era. The 'Main Arcade' is world-famous for its neon signage and the historic 'Public Market Center' clock. It serves as 'the soul of Seattle' and is a vital hub for local farmers and craftspeople.

  8. 08

    Smith Tower

    506 2nd Avenue, Seattle, WA 98104

    Once the tallest building west of the Mississippi, this Neoclassical skyscraper is famous for its white terra-cotta facade. It still features manually operated elevators with brass gates that are maintained by dedicated operators. At the peak is the famous 'Wishing Chair' located inside the historic Chinese Room. A glass-floored 'Legends of Smith Tower' exhibit tells the story of the city's early 20th-century economic boom. The pyramid-shaped top remains a classic and elegant feature of the Pioneer Square neighborhood.

  9. 09

    Bullitt Center

    1501 E Madison Street, Seattle, WA 98122

    This building is often cited as the greenest commercial building in the entire world. It was designed to be completely self-sufficient with a massive solar 'halo' roof for power generation. The system harvests and treats rainwater for all purposes, including drinking water for its tenants. It features an 'irresistible stairway' designed to encourage people to walk rather than use energy-consuming elevators. Every material used in the construction was screened for toxicity to ensure a healthy indoor environment.

  10. 10

    Pacific Science Center (Arches)

    200 2nd Avenue N, Seattle, WA 98109

    Designed for the 1962 World's Fair, these soaring white Gothic-style arches sit elegantly over reflection pools. The architect intended for the design to make science feel both inviting and cathedral-like to the public. It is considered a premier example of 'New Formalism' architecture using modern pre-cast concrete. The campus originally served as the United States Science Pavilion before becoming a permanent museum. Today, it remains a beloved educational landmark for families and students in the Seattle Center.

  11. 11

    Apple University Village

    2650 NE 49th Street, Seattle, WA 98105

    This store is a flagship example of Apple's modern pavilion-style architecture in an outdoor mall setting. The building features a massive, flat timber ceiling that extends far beyond the glass walls for shade. The design focuses heavily on transparency and biophilia through the use of interior trees and glass. Large glass walls can disappear into the floor to merge the store with the surrounding plaza. It creates a seamless community space for customers to gather, learn, and explore new technology.
