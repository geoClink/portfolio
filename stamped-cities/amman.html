The White City

Amman architecture

Amman is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with settlements dating back over 8,500 years. The ancient Citadel (Jabal al-Qal'a) overlooks the city and contains ruins from the Bronze Age, Roman, Byzantine, and Umayyad periods. Jordan is home to Petra — the rose-red Nabataean city carved into rock cliffs, named one of the New Seven Wonders of the World — making Amman a natural gateway to one of the planet's most extraordinary archaeological sites.

Before you go

  • Fly intoQueen Alia International Airport (AMM)

    Queen Alia International Airport, named after the late Queen of Jordan who died in a helicopter crash in 1977, serves as the country's main gateway. A major expansion completed in 2012 — featuring a striking new terminal designed by Foster + Partners — tripled the airport's capacity and transformed it into one of the most architecturally impressive airports in the Middle East. The airport is located 35km south of Amman city center.

  • CurrencyJordanian Dinar (JD)
  • LanguageArabic

    Jordanian Arabic (Levantine dialect) is the everyday spoken language and is considered one of the most clearly pronounced and accessible Arabic dialects for learners. Modern Standard Arabic is used in formal writing and media. English is widely spoken in business, hotels, and by the young educated population. Jordan has a long tradition of welcoming refugees and hosting diaspora communities, making Amman a uniquely cosmopolitan city by regional standards.

  • Getting aroundJETT Buses & Taxis

    Amman does not have a metro or light rail system — the city is built across seven hills (originally, now more) and has historically resisted underground transit development. The Jordan Express Tourist Transport (JETT) runs intercity buses to major destinations including Petra and Aqaba. Within the city, yellow taxis are metered and widely available. A Bus Rapid Transit line was approved and is planned for the city's busiest corridor.
