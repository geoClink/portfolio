Space City

Houston architecture

Houston is home to the NASA Johnson Space Center, and the first word ever spoken from the surface of the moon was 'Houston.' It is the most diverse city in Texas, boasting a world-renowned Museum District and a culinary scene that represents over 70 different countries.

Before you go

  • Fly intoGeorge Bush (IAH)

    George Bush Intercontinental serves as a massive gateway hub for travel between the United States and Latin America. It features an underground people mover called the 'Subway'—which was originally built by Disney—connecting all terminals and the airport hotel.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish & Spanish

    The city's linguistic landscape is a vibrant 'code-switching' blend of English and Spanish, reflecting its deep multicultural roots. Visitors will often hear a mix of Southern hospitality and international dialects, making it one of the most linguistically diverse hubs in the American South.

  • Getting aroundMETRORail

    Main Street Square features a unique fountain that the light rail trains pass directly through. METRO operates an expansive bus network, including several high-occupancy vehicle lane express routes. The system is currently expanding to include more Bus Rapid Transit corridors to serve the growing population.

14 landmarks in Houston

  1. 01

    Apple Houston Galleria

    5085 Westheimer Rd, Houston, TX 77056, USA

    Located within the world-renowned Galleria mall, this store underwent a significant relocation to become a massive flagship. It features a sweeping floor-to-ceiling glass storefront that mirrors the mall's iconic barrel-vaulted glass roof. Inside, the space is defined by its wide-open floor plan, Sequoia wood tables, and a large Video Wall for interactive sessions. The design acts as a cooling, minimalist oasis within the bustling, high-energy environment of Texas’s largest shopping center. Its seamless integration into the mall's luxury wing reflects the brand's commitment to transparency and community engagement.

  2. 02

    JPMorgan Chase Tower

    600 Travis St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    As the tallest building in Texas, this five-sided tower remains a dominant fixture of the Houston skyline. The building's unique pentagonal shape was designed to provide a higher number of corner offices per floor. Its polished gray granite facade and floor-to-ceiling windows offer maximum views of the sprawling landscape and Buffalo Bayou. The plaza at the base features a massive 75-foot sculpture by Joan Miró entitled 'Personage with Birds.' Despite being over 40 years old, it continues to be a masterpiece of structural engineering and corporate modernism.

  3. 03

    Pennzoil Place

    711 Louisiana St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    A masterpiece of postmodernism, these twin trapezoidal towers are separated by only ten feet at their closest point. They are famously connected by a dramatic glass-pyramid atrium that serves as a public sunroom. The sharp-angled roofs were a radical departure from the flat-topped skyscrapers that defined the 1970s. Critics famously dubbed it the 'Building of the Decade' for its bold use of dark glass and geometric shadows. The design effectively challenged the International Style and paved the way for more expressive high-rise architecture.

  4. 04

    Williams Tower

    2800 Post Oak Blvd, Houston, TX 77056, USA

    Located in the Uptown district, this Art Deco-inspired skyscraper serves as a monumental beacon outside of the downtown core. It is famous for its rotating searchlight at the summit, which can be seen across the city at night. At its base sits the Gerald D. Hinnes Waterwall Park, which remains one of Houston's most photographed landmarks. The building's silver-gray glass and dramatic setbacks pay tribute to the classic New York skyscrapers of the 1920s. It stands as one of the tallest buildings in the world located outside a city's central business district.

  5. 05

    Houston City Hall

    901 Bagby St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    This building is a prime example of 'PWA Moderne' architecture, featuring a grand facade of Texas Cordoba limestone. The symmetrical design is intended to be monumental, reflecting the strength and stability of local government. Relief sculptures adorn the exterior, depicting various aspects of Texas history, industry, and agriculture. Inside, the lobby is lined with fine marble and features a stunning coffered ceiling with gold leaf detailing. The structure remains the operational heart of the city and a significant historical landmark in the Civic Center.

  6. 06

    The Astrodome

    8400 Kirby Dr, Houston, TX 77054, USA

    Once hailed as the 'Eighth Wonder of the World,' the Astrodome was the first multi-purpose domed stadium ever built. Its massive steel-framed dome and futuristic aesthetic defined Houston’s identity during the height of the Space Age. The building pioneered the use of 'Astroturf' after natural grass failed to grow under the semi-transparent ceiling panels. Though it is currently vacant, it remains a protected State Antiquities Landmark due to its global engineering significance. It stands as a nostalgic icon of mid-century optimism and the birth of the modern indoor sports experience.

  7. 07

    The Menil Collection

    1533 Sul Ross St, Houston, TX 77006, USA

    This museum is a landmark of modern design, featuring a sophisticated roof system of ferro-concrete 'leaves.' These elements filter natural light into the galleries to protect the art while creating a serene atmosphere. The use of gray cypress siding allows the structure to blend seamlessly into its quiet residential neighborhood. Renzo Piano’s design prioritizes a human scale, avoiding the monumentalism typical of many major art institutions. It is celebrated globally for its 'organic modernism' and its focus on the intimate relationship between art and the viewer.

  8. 08

    Bank of America Center

    700 Louisiana St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    This tower is a striking example of Dutch Gothic-inspired postmodernism, characterized by its three-step gabled roofline. The facade is clad in rough-textured Napoleon Red granite, giving the building a warm and imposing presence. Its interior lobby houses a smaller, historic-style banking hall located beneath a soaring 125-foot ceiling. The sharp peaks and intricate details make it one of the most recognizable silhouettes in the downtown skyline. It serves as a prime example of the 1980s architectural trend of blending historical motifs with modern office requirements.

  9. 09

    The Julia Ideson Building

    550 McKinney St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    Originally serving as the city's central library, this Spanish Revival masterpiece is a jewel of historic Houston. The exterior features intricate brickwork and terracotta tiles that stand in stark contrast to the nearby glass towers. Inside, the hand-painted beamed ceilings and grand marble staircases evoke a sense of early 20th-century elegance. It currently houses the city's extensive archives and special collections within its meticulously restored halls. The building remains one of the most romantic and culturally significant architectural spaces in the downtown area.

  10. 10

    One Shell Plaza

    910 Louisiana St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    At the time of its completion, this structure was the tallest reinforced concrete building in the world. It utilizes a highly efficient 'tube-in-tube' design to withstand the powerful winds associated with Gulf Coast hurricanes. The facade is elegantly clad in Italian travertine marble, featuring deeply recessed windows that provide natural solar shading. It remains a definitive example of the corporate International Style that transformed American cities in the 1970s. The building's understated but powerful presence continues to represent Houston's status as a global energy hub.

  11. 11

    Esperson Buildings

    808 Travis St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    This complex consists of two distinct structures: the Italian Renaissance Niels building and the Art Deco Mellie building. The Niels building is famously topped by a grand circular 'tempietto' that serves as a landmark on the skyline. Its younger companion, the Mellie, offers a sleeker and more streamlined aesthetic typical of the 1940s. Together, they represent the transition from ornate historical revivalism to the efficiency of modern design. These connected buildings remain some of the most prestigious and historically intact office spaces in the city.

  12. 12

    NASA Johnson Space Center (Building 30)

    2101 E NASA Pkwy, Houston, TX 77058, USA

    Building 30 houses the Mission Control Center, which has served as the nerve center for American human spaceflight for decades. The historic Apollo-era flight control room is preserved as a museum, looking exactly as it did during the moon landings. Its architecture is a functional example of Space Age Brutalism, emphasizing durability and technological integration. This site is where the iconic phrase 'Houston, the Eagle has landed' was first received from the lunar surface. It remains a working facility that continues to manage operations for the International Space Station.

  13. 13

    The Rothko Chapel

    3900 Yupon St, Houston, TX 77006, USA

    This non-denominational chapel is a unique fusion of modern architecture and contemplative fine art. The octagonal brick structure was designed specifically to showcase fourteen massive, dark-hued paintings by Mark Rothko. Its interior lighting is carefully controlled to direct the viewer’s focus entirely onto the meditative qualities of the work. Outside, the plaza features a reflecting pool and the 'Broken Obelisk' sculpture dedicated to Martin Luther King Jr. It serves as a global center for social justice advocacy and interfaith dialogue in a serene environment.

  14. 14

    POST Houston

    401 Franklin St, Houston, TX 77002, USA

    This project is a stunning adaptive reuse of the historic Barbara Jordan Post Office warehouse. Architects carved three massive light-wells through the original concrete to bring natural light into the deep floor plates. Sculptural 'Z-shaped' staircases connect the levels, creating a visually complex interior reminiscent of an Escher drawing. The roof features 'Skylawn,' a five-acre urban farm and park that offers incredible views of the downtown skyline. It has quickly become a premier cultural destination, housing a massive food hall, retail spaces, and a concert venue.
