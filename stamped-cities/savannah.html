The Hostess City of the South

Savannah architecture

Savannah was founded in 1733 by James Oglethorpe, who laid it out around a grid of leafy public squares; 22 of them still survive today. Its downtown is one of the largest National Historic Landmark Districts in the United States, designated in 1966.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoSavannah/Hilton Head (SAV)

        Savannah/Hilton Head International sits northwest of the city near Pooler, just off Interstate 95. As its name suggests, it serves travelers bound for both Savannah and Hilton Head, and it has been the site of Savannah's official weather records since 1948.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        Savannah speaks with an unhurried, gracious Southern lilt that matches its reputation for hospitality. Expect friendly greetings from strangers, plenty of 'y'all', and locals who are happy to share a story about the house you are standing in front of.

      • Getting aroundChatham Area Transit (CAT)

        Chatham Area Transit runs 17 fixed bus routes across the region. Its free 'dot' downtown service includes the Forsyth and Downtown loops. The Savannah Belles Ferry offers free crossings between River Street and Hutchinson Island.

      15 landmarks in Savannah

      1. 01

        Cathedral Basilica of St. John the Baptist

        222 E Harris St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Rising beside Lafayette Square, this Roman Catholic cathedral is the mother church of the Diocese of Savannah and also holds the rank of minor basilica. It was designed by architect Ephraim Francis Baldwin and dedicated in April 1876. Its Gothic Revival design makes it one of the grandest religious buildings in the historic district. It is a must for anyone exploring the squares of the old city.

      2. 02

        Owens–Thomas House

        124 Abercorn St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Facing Oglethorpe Square, this mansion is hailed as one of the finest examples of English Regency architecture in the United States. It was designed by the English architect William Jay and built in 1819, when it was known as the Richardson House. Telfair Museums now runs it as a house museum together with its slave quarters, which tell the story of the enslaved people who lived and worked here. It was named a National Historic Landmark in 1976.

      3. 03

        Mercer House

        429 Bull St, Savannah, GA 31401

        This Italianate mansion fills the southwestern block of Monterey Square. Designed by John S. Norris, it was begun in 1860 and completed in 1868. It became world famous through John Berendt's 1994 book 'Midnight in the Garden of Good and Evil', which retold the 1981 killing that took place inside. Today it is open to visitors as the Mercer Williams House Museum.

      4. 04

        Telfair Academy

        121 Barnard St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Built in 1818 to a design by William Jay, this Regency mansion began life as the Telfair family's townhouse. In 1886 it opened as a free art museum, making it the oldest public art museum in the American South and one of the first ten in the country. It is one of only a handful of Jay's buildings still standing and one of three sites run by Telfair Museums. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1976.

      5. 05

        Isaiah Davenport House

        324 E State St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Builder Isaiah Davenport put up this home in 1820 at the corner of Columbia Square. It is a classic example of the Federal style, with a balanced, symmetrical front. The Historic Savannah Foundation has run it as a house museum since 1963. It is a great first stop for understanding how Savannah's early 19th-century elite lived.

      6. 06

        Andrew Low House

        329 Abercorn St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Completed in 1849 by John S. Norris, this two-storey home is the oldest building on Lafayette Square. Andrew Low and his wife kept it as their American residence. Low was the father-in-law of Juliette Gordon Low, the founder of the Girl Scouts of the USA. The house lies within both the Savannah Historic District and the Juliette Gordon Low Historic District.

      7. 07

        Green–Meldrim House

        14 W Macon St, Savannah, GA 31401

        On the northwest corner of Madison Square stands one of the most lavish Gothic Revival houses in the American South. It was designed by John S. Norris and built in 1853. The house was declared a National Historic Landmark in 1976. It now belongs to the neighbouring St. John's Episcopal Church, which offers tours and uses it for meetings and receptions.

      8. 08

        Savannah City Hall

        2 E Bay St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Savannah's seat of government stands on Bay Street above the riverfront. Architect Hyman Witcover designed it in the Renaissance Revival style, and it was built between 1904 and 1905 before opening on January 2, 1906. The six-storey building rises about 43 meters and cost $250,000 to build. It is a contributing building in the Savannah Historic District.

      9. 09

        Congregation Mickve Israel

        20 E Gordon St, Savannah, GA 31401

        This Reform Jewish congregation dates back to 1733, the same year Savannah was founded. Its synagogue on Monterey Square, the congregation's third building, was completed in 1878 to designs by Henry G. Harrison. Unusually for a synagogue, it is built in the Gothic Revival style. The site also houses a museum of Jewish history.

      10. 10

        Independent Presbyterian Church

        Bull St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Founded in 1755, this is the first Presbyterian church in Georgia, built on land granted by King George II. Its building of 1800 was modelled on St Martin-in-the-Fields in London. After that church burned in 1889, a faithful reproduction was finished in 1891, this time with a steeple of iron instead of wood. Its spire remains a landmark along Bull Street.

      11. 11

        Savannah Cotton Exchange

        100 E Bay St, Savannah, GA 31401

        This was where cotton factors once gathered to set the price of the crop shipped from Savannah to markets such as New York and London. Architect William G. Preston designed it in the Queen Anne style. As planters began selling at inland markets, the trade declined and the exchange eventually closed. The three-storey building is now Freemasons' Hall, home of Solomon's Lodge No. 1.

      12. 12

        Talmadge Memorial Bridge

        US 17, Savannah, GA 31401

        This cable-stayed bridge carries US 17 over the Savannah River between downtown and Hutchinson Island. It replaced an earlier cantilever bridge from 1953 and opened in November 1991. Its main span stretches 1,100 feet (335 meters), with 185 feet of clearance below. The whole crossing runs for about 1.9 miles and frames the view from the riverfront.

      13. 13

        Juliette Gordon Low Birthplace

        10 E Oglethorpe Ave, Savannah, GA 31401

        Built in 1820, this three-storey home on Oglethorpe Avenue is where Juliette Gordon Low, founder of the Girl Scouts of the USA, was born in 1860. It is also known as the Wayne–Gordon House. The house was Savannah's first registered National Historic Landmark. Today it is a pilgrimage site for Girl Scouts visiting the place where their story began.

      14. 14

        William Scarbrough House

        41 Martin Luther King Jr Blvd, Savannah, GA 31401

        William Jay designed this house in 1819 for William Scarbrough, one of the principal owners of the steamship SS Savannah. It is nationally significant as an early example of Greek Revival architecture and one of Jay's few surviving American works. After many later alterations it has been largely restored to its early 19th-century appearance. Today it houses the Ships of the Sea Maritime Museum.

      15. 15

        Christ Church

        28 Bull St, Savannah, GA 31401

        Founded in 1733 along with the colony itself, Christ Church was the first church in the Province of Georgia, earning it the nickname 'the Mother Church of Georgia'. The present building on Johnson Square was designed by James Hamilton Couper and begun in 1838. It is the third church to stand here, after buildings of 1750 and 1806. The Episcopal parish is part of the Savannah Historic District.
