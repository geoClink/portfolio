The Windy City

Chicago architecture

The world's first skyscraper, the Home Insurance Building, was constructed here in 1884 using a revolutionary steel frame. Today, Chicago is a global center for architecture and jazz, famous for its deep-dish pizza and its stunning lakefront parks along Lake Michigan.

Before you go

  • Fly intoO'Hare Int'l (ORD)

    O'Hare is famous for its mesmerizing 744-foot neon light installation, titled 'Sky's the Limit,' located in the underground walkway between Concourses B and C. It serves as a massive United and American Airlines hub, connecting travelers to nearly every continent on the globe.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    The local dialect features a flat, broad vowel shift that is instantly recognizable across the Midwest. Phrases like 'Da Bears' have become iconic, and the city’s residents are known for a 'straight-talk' communication style that is both blunt and incredibly hearty.

  • Getting aroundThe 'L'

    The 'L' stands for elevated and is one of the few subway systems in the world operating 24/7. Metra commuter trains serve the wider suburban region from several historic downtown terminals. Pace buses complement the rail lines by providing essential transit throughout the surrounding counties.

  1. 01

    Willis Tower

    233 S Wacker Dr, Chicago, IL 60606

    Formerly the Sears Tower, this was the tallest building in the world for 25 years. It utilizes a revolutionary bundled tube structural system composed of nine framed tubes. This design provides immense strength against the powerful Windy City gusts. The 103rd-floor Skydeck features The Ledge, which consists of glass balconies extending four feet out. These transparent boxes offer visitors a thrilling straight-down view of the city streets below.

    Apple Michigan Avenue

    401 N Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60611

    This store is a masterclass in transparency located where the Chicago River meets North Michigan Avenue. The structure features 32-foot glass walls that blur the line between the interior and the riverwalk. Its ultra-thin carbon-fiber roof is famously shaped to resemble a MacBook laptop. The interior granite seating mimics the Spanish Steps and functions as a public town square. It serves as a modern community hub for both tech enthusiasts and local pedestrians.

  3. 03

    Tribune Tower

    435 N Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60611

    This neo-Gothic masterpiece was born from a famous 1922 international design competition. Its most unique feature is the fragment wall at the base of the building. This wall contains actual stones from 149 world landmarks including the Great Pyramid. The ornate crown is inspired by the Butter Tower of Rouen Cathedral in France. Flying buttresses at the top make it one of the most recognizable silhouettes in Chicago.

  4. 04

    875 North Michigan

    875 N Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60611

    Formerly the John Hancock Center, this building is a world icon of structural expressionism. Its massive X-bracing on the exterior is a functional skeleton that provides necessary stability. This tapered design allows the building to reach great heights while using less steel. It remains a permanent fixture of the skyline and houses the fastest elevators in North America. The tower offers a mix of office and residential spaces with spectacular lake views.

  5. 05

    Marina City

    300 N State St, Chicago, IL 60654

    These twin cylindrical towers are known locally as the Corn Cobs due to their unique shape. They were the first high-rise residential buildings in the United States to use tower cranes. The design features a continuous spiral parking ramp that occupies the first 19 floors. Semicircular balconies provide every residential unit with an expansive outdoor view of the river. The complex was originally designed as a city within a city to encourage urban living.

  6. 06

    St. Regis Chicago

    363 E Wacker Dr, Chicago, IL 60601

    This is currently the tallest building in the world designed by a female architect. The structure is composed of three interconnected towers with a unique undulating frustum shape. This rhythmic tapering is a functional choice that helps dissipate heavy wind pressure. A special blow-through floor on the 83rd story prevents the tower from swaying excessively. It stands as a contemporary marvel overlooking the intersection of the river and Lake Michigan.

  7. 07

    The Rookery

    209 S LaSalle St, Chicago, IL 60604

    The Rookery is one of Chicago's oldest high-rises, featuring a heavy Romanesque exterior. While the outside is made of red brick and granite, the inner court is a glass masterpiece. Frank Lloyd Wright was commissioned in 1905 to redesign the magnificent light-filled lobby. He added white marble and gold leaf accents to transform it into a temple of commerce. It remains a prime example of the transition from load-bearing walls to steel frames.

  8. 08

    Aqua Tower

    225 N Columbus Dr, Chicago, IL 60601

    This building is famous for undulating concrete balconies that resemble ripples on water. Each floor features a slightly different balcony shape to give residents unique city views. These varying extensions also serve to break up wind gusts to protect pedestrians below. It is a world-renowned landmark of topographic architecture and sustainable urban design. This was the first major skyscraper designed by the acclaimed architect Jeanne Gang.

  9. 09

    Carson Pirie Scott Building

    1 S State St, Chicago, IL 60603

    This structure is a Chicago School masterpiece designed by the legendary Louis Sullivan. It is celebrated for its revolutionary large Chicago windows that maximize natural light. The street-level entrance features incredibly intricate cast-iron ornamentation with organic motifs. Sullivan used these leafy designs to draw customers into the grand department store. It stands as a perfect realization of his famous philosophy that form follows function.

    The Merchandise Mart

    222 W Merchandise Mart Plaza, Chicago, IL 60654

    When this building first opened, it was the largest in the world by floor area. It was so massive that the government originally granted it its own zip code. The Art Deco styling features heavy stone towers and dramatic architectural setbacks. Every night, the river-facing facade becomes a 2.5-acre digital art canvas for public viewing. This projection system, known as Art on the MART, is the largest of its kind.

  11. 11

    Auditorium Building

    430 S Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60605

    This complex was a marvel of its era, combining a theater, hotel, and offices. The auditorium was the first of its kind to be fully electrified and climate-controlled. Louis Sullivan's golden ornamentation creates a breathtaking visual experience inside the theater space. Due to its massive weight, the building required a specialized floating foundation. Today, it serves as a central part of Roosevelt University while hosting world-class performances.

    Chicago Board of Trade Building

    141 W Jackson Blvd, Chicago, IL 60604

    This Art Deco sentinel stands prominently at the end of the LaSalle Street canyon. A three-story aluminum statue of Ceres, the goddess of agriculture, crowns the roof. Limestone carvings of bulls and wheat decorate the exterior to reflect its trading history. The grand three-story lobby is a masterpiece featuring marble floors and nickel-plated trim. It remains a global hub for commodity trading and a symbol of Chicago's financial power.

    The Wrigley Building

    400 N Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60611

    This gleaming white landmark serves as the gateway to the famous Magnificent Mile. Its design was inspired by the Giralda tower of the Seville Cathedral in Spain. The facade is clad in 250,000 pieces of glazed terra cotta in six shades of white. These tiles are still hand-washed today to maintain their brilliant, glowing appearance. A massive clock tower sits atop the building and is beautifully illuminated every single night.

  14. 14

    S.R. Crown Hall (IIT)

    3360 S State St, Chicago, IL 60616

    Widely considered a masterpiece of Mies van der Rohe, this building is a temple of Modernism. It features a revolutionary column-free space created by an exterior suspended roof. This glass-and-steel structure perfectly embodies the architect's famous less is more philosophy. The transparent environment provides a flexible workspace for the College of Architecture. It stands as a defining example of the International Style in the United States.

    Carbide & Carbon Building

    230 N Michigan Ave, Chicago, IL 60601

    This Art Deco skyscraper was reportedly designed to look like a giant green champagne bottle. The unique exterior is clad in dark green terra cotta and polished black granite. At the very top, the spire is leafed in real 24-karat gold for a luxurious finish. Originally an office for battery inventors, it now serves as a high-end luxury hotel. It remains one of the most colorful and stylish fixtures on Michigan Avenue.

  16. 16

    Frederick C. Robie House

    5757 S Woodlawn Ave, Chicago, IL 60637

    This house is the ultimate example of Frank Lloyd Wright's world-famous Prairie Style. The design mimics the flat and horizontal landscape of the American Midwest. It features long cantilevered roof eaves and continuous bands of intricate art glass windows. As a UNESCO World Heritage site, it is a landmark of organic architectural principles. The interior and exterior spaces flow together seamlessly to create a unified living environment.
