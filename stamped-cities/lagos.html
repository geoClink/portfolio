City guides / Africa / Nigeria

The Centre of Excellence

Lagos architecture

Lagos is Africa's largest city by population and one of the fastest-growing megacities on Earth, with an estimated 15-20 million people in the greater metropolitan area. It is the continent's largest economy by GDP and home to Nollywood — Nigeria's film industry, which produces more films annually than Hollywood and is second only to India's Bollywood in output. The city was built on a series of islands and lagoons and continues to expand both inland and through land reclamation into the Atlantic.

Before you go

  • Fly intoMurtala Muhammed International Airport (LOS)

    Murtala Muhammed International Airport is named after Nigeria's military head of state assassinated in 1976. It is West Africa's busiest airport and serves as the primary gateway to the continent's most populous nation. The airport has undergone multiple expansions to keep pace with Nigeria's rapid aviation growth, and a new terminal has added significant modern capacity in recent years.

  • CurrencyNigerian Naira (₦)
  • LanguageEnglish, Yoruba & Pidgin

    English is Nigeria's official language and is used in government, education, and media. In Lagos, Yoruba is the dominant indigenous language of the city's original inhabitants and surrounding region. Nigerian Pidgin English — a vibrant creole blending English with Yoruba, Igbo, and other languages — is the true lingua franca of the streets, used across all ethnic groups in casual conversation. The city's energy and wordplay in Pidgin are legendary.

  • Getting aroundBRT & Danfo Buses

    Lagos's primary formal transit system is the BRT (Bus Rapid Transit), which runs on dedicated lanes through the busiest corridors. The iconic yellow danfo minibuses, though informal and often overcrowded, remain the backbone of daily transit for millions. Water ferries cross Lagos Lagoon and offer a scenic alternative to road travel. The city is famously known for its traffic — locals call gridlock 'go-slow' — and many residents plan their days around it.
