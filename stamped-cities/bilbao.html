El Botxo

Bilbao architecture

Bilbao was founded by a municipal charter in 1300, granted by Diego López V de Haro, Lord of Biscay. The opening of the Guggenheim Museum in 1997 sparked such a dramatic urban revival that planners around the world now call it the 'Bilbao effect.' Locals affectionately call their city 'El Botxo' — 'the hole' — because it sits in a valley ringed by mountains.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoBilbao Airport (BIO)

        Bilbao Airport sits about 9 km north of the city in Loiu and is the busiest airport in the Basque Country and all of northern Spain. Its main terminal, opened in 2000, was designed by Santiago Calatrava and is an architectural landmark in its own right. The metro does not reach the airport yet, but an extension of Line 3 is planned.

      • CurrencyEuro (€)
      • LanguageSpanish & Basque

        Spanish is the most widely spoken language in Bilbao, followed by Basque (Euskara), which is co-official across the Basque Country. According to the city government, at least half of residents can speak some Basque, and schools offer bilingual education models. You'll spot Basque names everywhere, from street signs to landmarks like Azkuna Zentroa and Isozaki Atea.

      • Getting aroundMetro & Tram

        The Bilbao Metro opened in 1995 with stations designed by Norman Foster, and its curving glass entrance canopies are nicknamed 'fosteritos' after the architect. Three metro lines now meet at Zazpikaleak/Casco Viejo, and a modern tram line has run through the city since 2002. A funicular climbs from the centre up Mount Artxanda for sweeping views over the estuary.

      15 landmarks in Bilbao

      1. 01

        Guggenheim Museum Bilbao

        Abandoibarra Etorbidea 2, 48009 Bilbao, Spain

        Frank Gehry's titanium-clad museum is the building that reinvented Bilbao, rising from the old industrial riverfront of the Nervión in 1997. Its curving forms were designed to look random and catch the light, wrapped in some 33,000 paper-thin titanium plates that shimmer like fish scales. Gehry's team modelled the complex geometry with CATIA aerospace software, and the project was finished on time and on budget. In the 2010 World Architecture Survey, experts named it more often than any other building as one of the most important works completed since 1980.

      2. 02

        Zubizuri

        Campo Volantín – Uribitarte, Bilbao, Spain

        Zubizuri — Basque for 'white bridge' — is Santiago Calatrava's tied-arch footbridge across the Nervión, opened in 1997. Its curved walkway hangs from steel suspension cables strung from an overhead arch, linking the Campo Volantín bank with Uribitarte. The bridge became a symbol of the city's new optimism, though it also sparked a legal row when a walkway to the neighbouring Isozaki Atea towers was added against the architect's wishes.

        Built
        1997
        Architect
        Santiago Calatrava
        Today
        Footbridge
      3. 03

        Iberdrola Tower

        Euskadi Plaza, Bilbao, Spain

        Designed by César Pelli as the headquarters of the energy company Iberdrola, this 165-metre glass tower is the tallest building in Bilbao and the Basque Country. It stands in Abandoibarra, the former shipyard and dock district whose regeneration also produced the Guggenheim and the Euskalduna Palace. Construction finished in 2011, and King Juan Carlos I officially inaugurated the tower in February 2012. Two residential buildings by Carles Ferrater flank the skyscraper, and its entrance opens onto Euskadi Plaza.

      4. 04

        Azkuna Zentroa

        Arriquibar Plaza 4, Bilbao, Spain

        Ricardo Bastida's Alhóndiga opened in 1909 as the city's municipal wine warehouse, a sturdy Modernist block in the heart of the Ensanche. After decades of neglect, designer Philippe Starck reimagined the interior as a culture and leisure centre that opened in 2010. Inside, the brick shell shelters a cinema, library, gym and auditorium. It was renamed Azkuna Zentroa in 2015 in honour of the late mayor Iñaki Azkuna.

        1909

      5. 05

        Euskalduna Conference Centre and Concert Hall

        Abandoibarra Avenue 4, Bilbao, Spain

        The Euskalduna Palace rises beside the estuary on part of the land once occupied by the Euskalduna shipyards. Architects Federico Soriano and Dolores Palacios began work in 1994, and the building was inaugurated in February 1999. It houses a concert hall, an opera auditorium and conference facilities, and is home to the Bilbao Symphony Orchestra. With the Guggenheim just upstream, it anchors the riverside regeneration of Abandoibarra.

        Built
        1999
        Architect
        Federico Soriano & Dolores Palacios
        Today
        Concert Hall
      6. 06

        San Mamés Stadium

        Rafael Moreno Pitxitxi Kalea, Bilbao, Spain

        The new San Mamés replaced the historic 1913 ground of Athletic Bilbao and was inaugurated in September 2013. Designed by IDOM with architect César Azkarate, the all-seater stadium holds 53,331 spectators, making it the largest in the Basque Country. The stadium was completed in stages, with the full bowl finished in August 2014.

        Built
        2013
        Architect
        IDOM & César Azkarate
        Today

      7. 07

        Teatro Arriaga

        Arriaga Plaza, Bilbao, Spain

        Bilbao's opera house opened in 1890 on the bank of the Nervión, a grand Neo-Baroque work by Joaquín Rucoba, who also designed the City Hall. It is named after Juan Crisóstomo de Arriaga, the Bilbao-born composer once hailed as the 'Spanish Mozart.' The theatre has survived both a fire and a flood, each followed by a major reconstruction. Today it remains the main stage of the Old Town's riverfront.

      8. 08

        Bilbao Fine Arts Museum

        Doña Casilda Iturrizar Park, Bilbao, Spain

        Set entirely within Doña Casilda Iturrizar Park, the Fine Arts Museum occupies a neoclassical building of stone and red brick by Fernando Urrutia and Gonzalo Cárdenas, completed in 1945. A crisp modern wing of metal and glass followed in 1970, and a link between the two was finished in 2001. The collection spans from medieval art to El Greco, Goya, Gauguin and Francis Bacon. A new expansion by Foster + Partners opened in 2026.

        1945
        Architect
        Fernando Urrutia & Gonzalo Cárdenas

      9. 09

        Bilbao Cathedral

        Done Jakue Plazatxoa 1, Bilbao, Spain

        The Cathedral of Saint James began life between the 14th and 15th centuries as Bilbao's main parish church, making it one of the oldest buildings in the city. Its Gothic cloister and main vault contrast with the showy Gothic Revival west front and spire by Severino de Achúcarro, completed in 1887. Pilgrims on the Northern Way of the Camino de Santiago pass through, and in 2015 the church joined the route's UNESCO World Heritage listing. Look up at the buttresses to spot stone carvings of local merchants.

        1887

      10. 10

        Basilica of Begoña

        Begoña, Bilbao, Spain

        The Basilica of Begoña is dedicated to Our Lady of Begoña, patron saint of Biscay. Work on the three-nave church began in 1511 to a design by Sancho Martínez de Arego, and vaulting it took the better part of a century. Its arched main entrance shows the shift from Gothic to Renaissance, while the current tower was added between 1902 and 1907. The bell tower holds 24 bells cast in Switzerland.

        1511
        Architect
        Sancho Martínez de Arego
        Style

      11. 11

        Vizcaya Bridge

        Portugalete – Las Arenas (Getxo), Biscay, Spain

        Built in 1893 at the mouth of the Nervión, the Vizcaya Bridge is the world's oldest transporter bridge, carrying a suspended gondola across the water between Portugalete and Las Arenas in Getxo. It was designed by Alberto Palacio, a disciple of Gustave Eiffel,. The 160-metre span hangs 45 metres above the river, and lifts inside the towers let visitors walk along the top. Locals still call it the 'Puente Colgante,' the hanging bridge.

        Built
        1893
        Architect
        Alberto Palacio
        Height
        45 m (148 ft)

      12. 12

        Bilbao-Abando railway station

        Gran Vía 1, 48001 Bilbao, Spain

        Bilbao-Abando, officially Abando Indalecio Prieto, is the city's main railway terminus, with the first station on the site opening in 1863. The present building was commissioned by Renfe and opened in 1948. Its showpiece is a vast stained-glass window above the platforms — 15 metres wide and 10 metres tall, made of 301 pieces — crafted by Jesús Arrechubieta from a sketch by Miguel Pastor Veiga. It is the first thing many travellers see when they arrive in Bilbao by train.

        Built
        1948
        Today
        Railway Station
      13. 13

        Bilbao City Hall

        Ernesto Erkoreka Plaza 1, 48007 Bilbao, Spain

        Bilbao's City Hall was completed in 1892 by Joaquín Rucoba, the same architect behind the Teatro Arriaga, on the former site of a convent on the right bank of the estuary. Its eclectic, Baroque-inspired façade faces the river across a bascule bridge. Inside, the highlight is the Arab Hall, a lavish Neo-Mudéjar room modelled on the decoration of Granada's Alhambra, used for official receptions and weddings.

        1892
        Architect
        Joaquín Rucoba
        Style
        Eclectic

      14. 14

        Isozaki Atea

        Uribitarte, Bilbao, Spain

        Isozaki Atea — 'Isozaki Gate' in Basque — is a pair of 83-metre residential towers designed by Japanese architect Arata Isozaki and completed in 2008. They are the tallest residential buildings in Bilbao and the Basque Country, framing a staircase that serves as a gateway from the Zubizuri footbridge into the city's Ensanche. The twin towers anchor a five-building complex whose lower blocks rise six to eight floors. Their lower levels house shops, with apartments above.

        2008

      15. 15

        Mercado de la Ribera

        Casco Viejo, Bilbao, Spain

        Facing the Nervión on the edge of the Old Town, the Ribera Market was designed by architect Pedro Ispizua and dates from 1929. With about 10,000 square metres of floor space, it is often described as the biggest covered market in Europe. Fishmongers, butchers and greengrocers fill its stalls, along with a section reserved for local farmers' produce. A refurbishment begun in 2009 renewed its structure and stalls for a new century of shoppers.

        Built
        1929
        Architect
        Pedro Ispizua
        Today
        Market
