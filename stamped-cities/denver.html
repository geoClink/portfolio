The Mile High City

Denver architecture

The 13th step of the Colorado State Capitol is exactly 5,280 feet high, giving the city its 'Mile High' nickname. Situated at the base of the Rocky Mountains, it serves as the ultimate base camp for outdoor enthusiasts and professional athletes training at high altitudes.

Before you go

  • Fly intoDenver International (DEN)

    The airport's iconic white peaked roof is made of Teflon-coated fiberglass and was designed to mimic the snow-capped peaks of the nearby Rockies. It is the largest airport in North America by land area and is well-known for its extensive collection of public art and persistent urban legends.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    Denver is known for its clear and largely unaccented 'Mountain West' English, which is often cited as one of the most neutral dialects in the country. The tone is casual and outdoorsy, with a high degree of social emphasis on environmental awareness and wellness.

  • Getting aroundRTD

    The 'A Line' airport train uses heavy commuter rail cars to reach Union Station in 37 minutes. RTD light rail lines spread across the southern and western suburbs to ease highway congestion. The Free MallRide shuttle provides high-frequency electric bus service along the 16th Street pedestrian mall.

  1. 01

    Wells Fargo Center

    1700 Lincoln St, Denver, CO 80203

    Known affectionately as the 'Cash Register Building' due to its unique curved roofline, this skyscraper is a fixture of the skyline. This iconic shape was a strategic response to Denver's 'mountain view' ordinances to avoid blocking views of the Rockies. The building features a massive three-story lobby made of elegant rose-colored granite. A sophisticated heating system is integrated into the architecture to prevent snow buildup on its unique glass curves. It remains one of the most recognizable examples of late-century corporate architecture in the American West.

  2. 02

    Denver Art Museum

    100 W 14th Ave Pkwy, Denver, CO 80204

    The Frederic C. Hamilton Building is a jagged, titanium-clad structure designed to mimic the peaks of the Rocky Mountains. Its walls lean at sharp angles, ensuring there is not a single vertical wall in the entire structure. This deconstructivist masterpiece is considered one of the most challenging engineering feats in the city's history. It utilizes a complex internal steel skeleton to support its highly irregular and cantilevered shape. The design successfully reflects the crystalline formations of local minerals found throughout the Colorado landscape.

  3. 03

    Denver Union Station

    1701 Wynkoop St, Denver, CO 80202

    Often called the 'living room' of Denver, this historic Beaux-Arts station serves as a vibrant transit hub and luxury hotel. Its Great Hall features soaring 65-foot ceilings, arched windows, and incredibly intricate plasterwork throughout. The exterior is famous for its massive orange 'Union Station' neon sign that welcomes travelers to the city center. Following a massive renovation, it now acts as the primary connection point between the airport and various mountain rail lines. The space successfully blends historic preservation with modern retail and high-end dining experiences.

  4. 04

    Colorado State Capitol

    200 E Colfax Ave, Denver, CO 80203

    Modeled after the US Capitol in Washington D.C., this building is world-famous for its brilliant 24-karat gold-leaf dome. The gold was originally donated to commemorate the pioneers of the Colorado Gold Rush. Inside, the building features rare Colorado Rose Onyx, a stone found in only one specific quarry in the state. Visitors frequently visit the 13th step of the western entrance, which is famously marked as exactly one mile above sea level. It remains the functional seat of the Colorado General Assembly and the office of the Governor.

  5. 05

    Denver Public Library (Central)

    10 W 14th Ave Pkwy, Denver, CO 80204

    This building is a whimsical and controversial Postmodern masterpiece designed to spark public conversation. The structure is a composition of various geometric shapes, including a giant drum and a red abstract 'mountain' form. Each section is clad in different materials like limestone and copper to differentiate the various functional zones. The 7th-floor reading room features a massive wooden structure that creates the atmosphere of a modern cathedral. From this elevated vantage point, visitors can enjoy some of the best unobstructed views of the city's skyline.

  6. 06

    United States Air Force Academy Cadet Chapel

    2306 Academy Dr, USAF Academy, CO 80840

    Located in Colorado Springs, this is one of the most famous examples of Modernist architecture in the United States. It features 17 massive aluminum-clad spires that strikingly resemble fighter jets pointing toward the heavens. The gaps between these spires are filled with colorful stained glass to create a vivid interior atmosphere. As visitors move toward the altar, the spectrum of light transitions beautifully from dark to light. The structure serves as a multi-faith place of worship for the cadets of the Air Force Academy.

  7. 07

    Clyfford Still Museum

    1250 Bannock St, Denver, CO 80204

    This museum is considered a masterclass in the architectural use of highly textured, poured-in-place concrete. The building’s exterior consists of vertically fluted concrete that looks like it was meticulously combed with a giant tool. This creates a constant play of light and shadow that shifts beautifully throughout the day. Inside, a perforated concrete ceiling allows soft, diffused natural light to fill the quiet gallery spaces. These light conditions were specifically designed to protect and highlight the massive abstract expressionist paintings found within.

  8. 08

    One River North

    3930 Blake St, Denver, CO 80205

    One River North is one of Denver's newest and most radical residential designs located in the RiNo district. The building features a 'canyon' carved directly into its glass facade to create a striking visual gap. This multi-story open-air space includes a hiking trail with water features and lush local greenery. It represents a new era of 'biophilic' design by bringing the experience of the Rockies into the urban core. The structure challenges traditional residential architecture by blurring the lines between the built environment and nature.

  9. 09

    The Brown Palace Hotel

    321 17th St, Denver, CO 80202

    As Denver's oldest hotel, this building is a masterpiece of the Italian Renaissance Revival style. It was constructed using red Colorado granite and Arizona sandstone for a distinctive and regal appearance. The interior features a stunning eight-story atrium topped with a massive, intricate stained-glass skylight. It holds the distinction of being the first hotel in America to feature an atrium of this grand scale. Remarkably, the hotel utilizes its own 720-foot deep artesian well to provide water to every guest room.

    1144 Fifteenth

    1144 15th St, Denver, CO 80202

    This sleek, crystalline skyscraper was designed to mimic the jagged edges and verticality of a mountain peak. As one of the tallest additions to Denver’s skyline in decades, it serves as a modern landmark. Its glass curtain wall reflects the ever-changing Colorado sky and the surrounding city buildings. The lobby is designed with a massive 'living wall' to incorporate nature into the corporate environment. Built to the highest environmental standards, it represents a shift from heavy masonry toward light, sustainable glass structures.

    Apple Cherry Creek

    3000 E 1st Ave, Denver, CO 80206

    This sleek, minimalist pavilion is located in the heart of Denver's premier upscale shopping district. The store features massive floor-to-ceiling glass panels that dissolve the boundary between the store and the street. A flat, thin roof creates a seamless transition between the indoor 'Genius Grove' and the outdoor plaza. The interior is finished with light-colored oak and honed granite for a clean, modern aesthetic. It serves as a high-tech anchor for the Cherry Creek area while maintaining a sophisticated, low-profile presence.

  12. 12

    Denver International Airport (Jeppesen Terminal)

    8500 Peña Blvd, Denver, CO 80249

    The Jeppesen Terminal is world-famous for its iconic white peaked roof made of Teflon-coated fiberglass. This design is intended to resemble both the snow-capped Rocky Mountains and the tepees of Native Americans. Because the roof material is translucent, the terminal is flooded with soft natural light during the day. It is an architectural landmark recognized globally for its innovative and massive 'tensile' structural system. The terminal serves as a grand symbolic gateway for millions of travelers entering the Mountain West.

  13. 13

    Daniels & Fisher Tower

    1601 Arapahoe St, Denver, CO 80202

    When completed, this clock tower was the tallest building west of the Mississippi River. It was modeled after the famous St. Mark's Bell Tower in Venice, Italy, to bring European style to Denver. The structure features a massive four-faced clock and a popular 20th-floor observation deck. While the attached department store was demolished in the 1970s, preservationists successfully fought to save the tower. It remains the most recognizable historic silhouette located on the bustling 16th Street Mall today.

    Ellie Caulkins Opera House

    1385 Curtis St, Denver, CO 80204

    Nicknamed 'The Ellie,' this building is famously described as a sophisticated 'building within a building.' An acoustically perfect lyric opera house was constructed inside the historic shell of the 1908 Municipal Auditorium. The result is a stunning blend of neo-classical exterior masonry and a contemporary, curved interior. Warm woods and gold leaf finishes create a luxurious atmosphere for world-class performances. It serves as the elegant centerpiece of the expansive Denver Performing Arts Complex.

  15. 15

    The Crawford Hill Mansion

    150 E 10th Ave, Denver, CO 80203

    This French Renaissance-style mansion was the social center for Denver's elite during the late 19th century. Built for a prominent newspaper publisher, it features exquisite marble fireplaces and rich mahogany woodwork throughout. The grand ballroom once hosted the city's most exclusive social circle known as the 'Sacred Thirty-Six.' It stands as a reminder of the massive wealth generated during the Colorado mining boom. Today, it remains one of the finest surviving examples of Gilded Age architecture in the American West.
