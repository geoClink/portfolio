      Before you go

      • Fly intoGuarulhos Int'l (GRU)

        As the primary international gateway to Brazil, Guarulhos is the busiest airport in South America by passenger volume. It is a massive complex that was significantly modernized for the World Cup and Olympics, featuring a dedicated terminal for long-haul international flights with extensive luxury amenities.

      • CurrencyBrazilian Real (R$)
      • LanguagePortuguese

        Portuguese is the sole official language and is spoken with a musical, open accent that differs significantly from the European version. Most locals appreciate any effort to speak 'Português' and are very patient with learners.

      • Getting aroundCuritiba BRT & Metrô

        Curitiba was the global pioneer of the 'Bus as a Train' concept, using iconic glass tube stations for level boarding. São Paulo's Metrô is one of the cleanest and most efficient in the world, serving millions of passengers across six lines. Rio de Janeiro utilizes the 'Bondinho' tram in Santa Teresa and a modern VLT light rail system in the revitalized port area.

      13 landmarks in Campinas

      1. 01

        Sirius (LNLS)

        Rua Giuseppe Máximo Scolfaro, 10000, Polo II de Alta Tecnologia, Campinas, SP, 13083-100, Brazil

        Sirius is one of the world's most advanced fourth-generation synchrotron light sources and the largest scientific infrastructure in Brazil. This massive ring-shaped facility uses extreme precision engineering to produce high-brightness light for analyzing the atomic structure of materials. A unique feature of its construction is a massive jointless concrete slab designed to prevent even the slightest vibration from interfering with the particle beams. The exterior shell is a blend of industrial efficiency and high-tech aesthetics, reflecting the cutting-edge research happening within its walls. It serves as a global hub for scientists working in fields ranging from drug development to nanotechnology and renewable energy.

      2. 02

        Mercado Municipal

        Praça Carlos Gomes, s/n, Centro, Campinas, SP, 13013-140, Brazil

        Commonly known as the 'Mercadão,' this Neo-Moorish brick building is an architectural landmark in the heart of downtown Campinas. It was originally designed by the renowned architect Ramos de Azevedo and once served as a railway warehouse for the Mogiana company. The structure is characterized by its decorative arched windows, intricate brickwork, and a distinctive roofline that stands out in the urban landscape. Inside, the market is a vibrant tapestry of colors and smells, housing dozens of stalls selling fresh produce, spices, and traditional snacks. It remains the most important center for local commerce and a vital cultural meeting point for the city’s residents.

      3. 03

        Torre do Castelo

        Praça 23 de Outubro, s/n, Jardim Chapadão, Campinas, SP, 13070-071, Brazil

        This Art Deco water tower is one of the most beloved icons of Campinas and was originally built to balance the city's water distribution. The tower features six vertical ribs and a distinctive circular balcony that offers a 360-degree panorama of the surrounding coffee-growing regions. At the top, visitors can access an observation deck that provides one of the best sunset views in the state of São Paulo. The base of the tower serves as a small museum, detailing the history of the city's urban development and water works. Its location in a central roundabout makes it a constant visual reference for everyone traveling through the Castelo district.

      4. 04

        Catedral Metropolitana

        Praça José Bonifácio, s/n, Centro, Campinas, SP, 13013-050, Brazil

        The Metropolitan Cathedral of Campinas is a massive Neoclassical structure that took over 70 years to complete using traditional puddled clay. Its interior is famous for its dark, solemn atmosphere and incredibly detailed wood carvings executed by Victoriano dos Anjos. The facade is inspired by Italian Renaissance architecture and features imposing towers that dominate the central Praça José Bonifácio. As the largest building in the world made of puddled clay, it represents a unique feat of historical engineering in the region. It serves not only as a religious center but also as a primary heritage site that tells the story of the city's coffee-wealth era.

      5. 05

        Estação Cultura

        Praça Marechal Floriano Peixoto, s/n, Centro, Campinas, SP, 13010-061, Brazil

        Formerly the Campinas railway station, this Victorian-inspired building was the gateway to the city during the golden age of coffee production. The structure features extensive use of imported English ironwork and traditional brick masonry, following the aesthetic of British colonial stations. After the decline of passenger rail, the station was carefully restored and converted into a major cultural hub for concerts and art exhibitions. Its vast platforms and original tracks now host festivals that celebrate the city's diverse cultural and ethnic history. It stands as a powerful reminder of the industrial revolution’s impact on the interior of São Paulo state.

      6. 06

        Biblioteca Central Unicamp

        Rua Sérgio Buarque de Holanda, 421, Cidade Universitária Zeferino Vaz, Campinas, SP, 13083-859, Brazil

        The Central Library of Unicamp is a landmark of Brazilian Brutalist architecture, designed by the celebrated architect João Filgueiras Lima, known as Lelé. The building utilizes heavy, exposed concrete forms and large overhangs to provide shade in the intense tropical sun. Internal courtyards and skylights are strategically placed to bring natural light and fresh air into the reading rooms without the need for excessive air conditioning. As the intellectual heart of the university, it houses one of the most significant academic collections in Latin America. Its rugged, functionalist aesthetic represents the era of rapid university expansion and institutional modernization in Brazil.

      7. 07

        Jockey Club Campineiro

        Rua Dr. Thomaz Alves, 39, Centro, Campinas, SP, 13010-160, Brazil

        The Jockey Club is an eclectic architectural masterpiece that was intended to reflect the extreme wealth of the Campinas coffee barons in the early 20th century. Its facade and interior were inspired by the Paris Opera, featuring expensive materials like Carrara marble, bronze, and Bohemian crystal. The building served as the premier social venue for the city's elite for decades, hosting grand balls and high-society meetings. After falling into disrepair, it has been restored to host corporate events and cultural exhibitions while maintaining its historic grandeur. It remains one of the finest examples of the Neo-Renaissance style in the state's interior.

      8. 08

        Estádio Brinco de Ouro

        Av. Imperatriz Dona Tereza Cristina, 11, Jardim Guarani, Campinas, SP, 13100-200, Brazil

        The 'Golden Earring' stadium is a modernist sports landmark and the home of the Guarani Futebol Clube. Its unique circular design and cantilevered concrete roof structures were quite revolutionary for Brazilian stadium design in the 1950s. The stadium sits in a natural depression, which helped create its nickname as it appears to nestle into the hillside like an earring. It has hosted several major international matches and remains a place of intense pilgrimage for local football fans. The architecture emphasizes clear sightlines and a sense of intimacy between the spectators and the pitch.

      9. 09

        Palácio dos Jequitibás

        Av. Anchieta, 200, Centro, Campinas, SP, 13015-903, Brazil

        Palácio dos Jequitibás is the administrative headquarters of the city government and a textbook example of high-density administrative Modernism. The building is elevated on massive concrete pilotis, creating a shaded public plaza at the ground level for civic gatherings. Its facade is a rhythmic grid of concrete and glass that allows for functional office space while providing views of the downtown skyline. The name honors the Jequitibá trees that are native to the region and symbolic of the city's natural heritage. At 20 stories tall, it remains one of the tallest and most recognizable government buildings in the interior of Brazil.

      10. 10

        Teatro Castro Mendes

        Praça Correa de Lemos, s/n, Vila Industrial, Campinas, SP, 13035-050, Brazil

        Originally constructed as a large movie palace, the Castro Mendes was eventually transformed into the city’s primary municipal theater. It is situated in the historic Vila Industrial district, a neighborhood that was once the heart of the city's manufacturing sector. The theater underwent a major restoration recently to improve its technical capabilities, including modern stage machinery and acoustic enhancements. It now hosts the City Symphony Orchestra and a wide variety of national touring theater productions. The building serves as a vital anchor for the cultural revitalization of the western side of the downtown area.

      11. 11

        Mata de Santa Genebra Gatehouse

        Rua Pedro de Magalhães Porto, 440, Bosque de Barão, Campinas, SP, 13084-510, Brazil

        The gatehouse of Santa Genebra is a rare surviving fragment of the rural architecture that characterized the region's colonial coffee plantations. It follows the traditional 'Bandeirista' style, with thick walls and a simple, functional layout adapted for the Brazilian interior. Today, it serves as the entrance to an ecological sanctuary that preserves a significant portion of the original Atlantic Forest. The building is used as a research center where students and scientists study the local biodiversity and reforestation techniques. It represents the historical transition of Campinas from a rural agricultural outpost to a modern technological center.

      12. 12

        Galleria Shopping Campinas

        Rod. D. Pedro I, km 131, Jardim Nilópolis, Campinas, SP, 13091-901, Brazil

        Galleria Shopping revolutionized the local retail experience by introducing an open-air mall concept that prioritizes natural light and vegetation. The building features massive glass skylights and internal gardens with cascading waterfalls that create a tropical, park-like atmosphere. Its design was a direct response to the 'closed box' malls of the era, aiming to integrate the shopping experience with the outdoor environment. The mall is built on multiple levels that follow the natural slope of the terrain, using stone and wood to enhance its organic feel. It remains a premier destination for those seeking an upscale lifestyle experience along the Dom Pedro highway corridor.

      13. 13

        iPlace Apple Premium Partner (Iguatemi)

        Iguatemi Campinas, Av. Iguatemi, 777, Vila Brandina, Campinas, SP, 13092-902, Brazil

        This store is the definitive Apple destination for the interior of São Paulo and is located in the region’s oldest shopping mall. It was recently upgraded to the Premium Partner status, which involves a complete redesign of the interior to meet Apple’s global standards. The aesthetic is strictly minimalist, featuring stone flooring, cantilevered light oak tables, and a completely transparent glass facade. It provides a dedicated space for tech support and training, known as the 'Apple 2.0' retail experience. This location serves a vast region of the state, bridging the gap between the capital’s flagship stores and the growing tech hub of Campinas.
