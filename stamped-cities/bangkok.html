City of Angels

Bangkok architecture

Bangkok's ceremonial name is the longest place name in the world, consisting of 168 letters that describe it as the 'Great City of Angels.' It is a vibrant sensory explosion where ornate gold-leaf temples sit alongside world-class street food stalls and bustling modern shopping districts.

Before you go

  • Fly intoSuvarnabhumi (BKK)

    Suvarnabhumi Airport features stunning gold-roofed architecture and is decorated with massive statues depicting traditional Thai myths and Buddhist art. The airport is one of the most photographed locations in the world on Instagram, known for its sprawling glass terminals and its role as a key gateway to Southeast Asia.

  • CurrencyThai Baht (฿)
  • LanguageThai

    Thai is a tonal language where the use of polite ending particles like 'Khrap' or 'Ka' is essential for showing respect. While English is widespread in tourism and commerce, basic Thai greetings are always met with a warm smile.

  • Getting aroundBTS & MRT

    The BTS Skytrain is an elevated railway that allows passengers to bypass Bangkok's legendary street-level traffic jams. The MRT Subway serves as the underground counterpart, connecting the business districts to the historic areas. Colorful Chao Phraya Express boats and long-tail canal taxis provide a unique water-based transit experience.

15 landmarks in Bangkok

  1. 01

    Grand Palace (Chakri Maha Prasat)

    Na Phra Lan Rd, Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang, Phra Nakhon, Bangkok 10200, Thailand

    This historic complex served as the official residence of the Kings of Siam for over one hundred and fifty years. The main hall is architecturaly unique for blending an Italian Renaissance lower half with a traditional tiered Thai roof. Its construction marked the founding of Bangkok as the capital and remains the spiritual heart of the nation. While the King no longer resides here, it is still used for grand royal ceremonies and important state functions. Visitors must adhere to a strict dress code to respect the sacred nature of the emerald green grounds.

  2. 02

    King Power MahaNakhon

    114 Narathiwas Rd, Silom, Bang Rak, Bangkok 10500, Thailand

    Widely known as the 'Pixel Tower,' this skyscraper is a masterpiece of deconstructivist architecture in the heart of the business district. Its facade features a spiraling ribbon of architectural cut-out cubes that give the impression the building is dissolving into the sky. This design was engineered to provide shaded terraces and unique views for the luxury residences located within the pixels. The rooftop is home to a famous glass skywalk that offers a terrifyingly beautiful 360-degree view of the city. It represents Bangkok's evolution into a global hub for modern design and high-end urban living.

  3. 03

    Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)

    158 Thanon Wang Doem, Wat Arun, Bangkok Yai, Bangkok 10600, Thailand

    This iconic riverside temple is famous for its towering Khmer-style spire that dominates the Chao Phraya River. The spire is meticulously decorated with millions of pieces of broken Chinese porcelain that were once used as ballast on trading ships. It represents Mount Meru, the center of the universe in Buddhist cosmology, and glows beautifully during sunrise and sunset. Steep stairs allow visitors to climb partway up for a panoramic view of the river and the historic Thonburi district. As one of Thailand's most photographed sites, it serves as a timeless symbol of Thai resilience and artistry.

  4. 04

    Wat Phra Kaew

    Na Phra Lan Rd, Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang, Phra Nakhon, Bangkok 10200, Thailand

    The Temple of the Emerald Buddha is considered the most sacred Buddhist site in all of Thailand. It features breathtaking gold-leaf-covered stupas and vibrant mosaics made of colorful glass and ceramic. The architecture is a pure and exquisite expression of the Rattanakosin style that defines the city's early history. Surrounding the main chapel are galleries featuring murals that depict the entire Ramakien epic in vivid detail. Every inch of the structure is covered in intricate ornamentation, reflecting the deep religious devotion of the Thai people.

  5. 05

    MOCA Bangkok

    499 Kamphaeng Phet 6 Rd, Lat Yao, Chatuchak, Bangkok 10900, Thailand

    The Museum of Contemporary Art is a purpose-built space designed to showcase an extensive collection of modern Thai masterpieces. The building is carved from a single block of granite, giving it a monolithic and powerful presence. Intricate floral patterns are cut into the stone to allow dappled light to enter the galleries, mimicking sunlight through trees. Inside, the museum spans five floors and features a world-renowned section dedicated to surrealist and religious art. It stands as a quiet, contemplative sanctuary for art lovers away from the city's main tourist corridors.

  6. 06

    G Tower

    9 Rama IX Rd, Huai Khwang, Bangkok 10310, Thailand

    This commercial skyscraper stands out at the Rama IX intersection due to its unique 'G' shaped leaning structure. The diagonal design was a major engineering feat, symbolizing the 'G' in the Grand Canal Land development project. It features a sleek, futuristic glass facade that reflects the rapidly developing business district surrounding it. Inside, the tower provides column-free office spaces and modern retail outlets at its base for commuters. It has quickly become a landmark symbol of Bangkok's modern commercial ambition and architectural experimentation.

  7. 07

    ICONSIAM

    299 Charoen Nakhon Rd, Khlong Ton Sai, Khlong San, Bangkok 10600, Thailand

    This massive luxury riverside complex is inspired by the 'Krathong' folded leaf float and the traditional canals of Bangkok. Its glass facade is uniquely pleated like traditional Thai silk, creating a shimmering effect along the Chao Phraya River. The building is designed to be a 'city of the future,' blending luxury retail with a deep respect for Thai culture. Inside, the 'SookSiam' area brings together food and crafts from all seventy-seven provinces in a market-style setting. It also features a world-class fountain show that uses light and water to tell stories of the nation's history.

  8. 08

    Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC)

    939 Rama I Rd, Wang Mai, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

    The Bangkok Art and Culture Centre is a circular building that serves as the heart of the city's independent art scene. It features a signature massive spiral ramp that allows visitors to experience art in a continuous, flowing motion. The high ceilings and natural skylights create a bright, airy atmosphere perfect for viewing large-scale contemporary installations. In addition to main galleries, the center houses small libraries, craft shops, and independent theaters for local artists. It stands as a vital public space for creative exchange in the middle of a bustling commercial shopping district.

  9. 09

    Elephant Tower (Chang Building)

    3300 Phahonyothin Rd, Chom Phon, Chatuchak, Bangkok 10900, Thailand

    The Chang Building is a postmodern landmark that literally takes the shape of an elephant, Thailand's national animal. It consists of three vertical towers connected by a horizontal block at the top to form the body and legs. Architectural details like circular windows for eyes and balconies for tusks complete the literalist and playful design. While some critics find it kitschy, it remains one of the most beloved and recognizable structures in the city. It serves as a reminder of the experimental architecture that emerged during Bangkok's economic boom in the late 1990s.

  10. 10

    State Tower (Lebua)

    1055 Si Lom, Silom, Bang Rak, Bangkok 10500, Thailand

    This skyscraper is easily identified by its massive golden rooftop dome and elegant neoclassical balconies. It gained global pop-culture fame after being featured as a major filming location for the movie 'The Hangover Part II.' The building is one of the tallest mixed-use structures in Southeast Asia, housing luxury suites and award-winning restaurants. Its design evokes a sense of old-world grandeur mixed with modern height and engineering. The views from its upper floors are widely considered the best in the city for watching the river wind through Bangkok.

  11. 11

    Apple Central World

    4, 4/1-4/2 Rama I Rd, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

    This retail structure features a breathtaking 'Tree Canopy' design centered around a spiraling timber core. The entire building is encased in a glass cylinder, providing a 360-degree view of the busy Ratchaprasong intersection. It is the first of its kind to feature a wrap-around outdoor plaza that encourages public interaction on multiple levels. The cantilevered roof provides shade while maintaining a minimalist and sustainable aesthetic for visitors. It has successfully transformed a busy transit hub into a serene architectural sanctuary for tech enthusiasts and locals alike.

    Apple Iconsiam

    299 Charoen Nakhon Rd, Khlong Ton Sai, Khlong San, Bangkok 10600, Thailand

    Apple’s first official store in Thailand features two massive glass facades that overlook the historic Chao Phraya River. The design creates a seamless indoor-outdoor transition, allowing visitors to walk from the mall straight to a lush rooftop terrace. Inside, the space is decorated with local stone and indoor trees to mirror the 'Garden of the River' concept. The clean, modernist lines provide a sharp contrast to the intricate pleats of the surrounding ICONSIAM mall. It serves as both a high-tech retail space and a premium observation deck for the riverfront.

  13. 13

    Robot Building

    191 South Sathon Rd, Yan Nawa, Sathon, Bangkok 10120, Thailand

    Originally designed for the Bank of Asia, this building uses literalist architecture to resemble a giant computer-controlled robot. Functional elements such as antennas and louvered windows are integrated into the 'head' and 'eyes' of the robot form. The design was intended to celebrate the arrival of the computer age in the Thai banking sector during the 1980s. It has long been a favorite of architecture enthusiasts for its playful departure from standard skyscraper designs. Even after decades, it remains a standout piece of postmodernism in the heart of the Sathorn business district.

  14. 14

    Jim Thompson House

    6 Soi Kasemsan 2, Rama I Rd, Wang Mai, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

    This museum is a cluster of six traditional teak houses that were transported from central Thailand and reassembled in the 1950s. It was the residence of an American silk merchant who mysteriously disappeared while on vacation in Malaysia. The houses are raised on stilts and feature beautiful carvings that showcase the peak of traditional Thai woodworking craftsmanship. The surrounding garden is so lush that it feels like a jungle hidden within the heart of the concrete city. Today, it stands as a preserved tribute to Thai culture and one of the world's most enduring mysteries.

  15. 15

    Nai Lert Park Heritage Home

    4 Soi Somkid, Ploenchit Rd, Lumphini, Pathum Wan, Bangkok 10330, Thailand

    This heritage home is a rare surviving example of a sprawling Thai colonial-style mansion from the early twentieth century. The structure is built entirely of teak and features a wide, open-air floor plan designed for natural ventilation. It was the private estate of a prominent Thai businessman and has been preserved as a time capsule of high-society life. The grounds include a historic pier and a vast green garden that provides a quiet escape from the city's noise. It now functions as a museum and exclusive event space, keeping the elegance of old Bangkok alive for new generations.
