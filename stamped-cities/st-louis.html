Gateway to the West

St. Louis architecture

The ice cream cone was popularized at the 1904 World's Fair in St. Louis when a waffle vendor helped a neighboring ice cream stall that had run out of dishes. The city is anchored by the Gateway Arch, the tallest man-made monument in the Western Hemisphere, symbolizing the expansion into the American West.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoSt. Louis Lambert (STL)

        The terminal's iconic white domes, designed by architect Minoru Yamasaki, were so revolutionary that they directly inspired the later designs of JFK in New York and CDG in Paris. Lambert was also the site where Charles Lindbergh's 'Spirit of St. Louis' was funded and planned before his historic solo flight.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        The local English is a unique linguistic intersection, blending Midwestern vowels with a hint of Southern vocabulary. You will frequently hear the hospitable 'Midwestern nice' cadence, and local terms like 'the Lou' are commonly used to describe the city with pride.

      • Getting aroundMetroLink

        MetroLink is consistently ranked as one of the most efficient light rail systems in the United States. The system utilizes former freight rail corridors to provide rapid transit between the airport and the Illinois suburbs. MetroBus provides local connectivity, with several routes focusing on the city's historic Forest Park.

      14 landmarks in St. Louis

      1. 01

        Gateway Arch

        11 N 4th St, St. Louis, MO 63102

        The Gateway Arch is the world's tallest arch and the tallest man-made monument in the Western Hemisphere. Designed as a weighted catenary curve made of stainless steel, it serves as a symbol of the westward expansion of the United States. Its unique tram system takes visitors to the top for panoramic views across the Mississippi River and downtown St. Louis. The monument is the centerpiece of the Gateway Arch National Park, which was recently renovated to improve pedestrian access from the city. It remains an engineering marvel that defines the city's skyline and attracts millions of global visitors annually.

      2. 02

        Wainwright Building

        705 Chestnut St, St. Louis, MO 63101

        The Wainwright Building is widely considered the first true skyscraper in architectural history. It pioneered modern tall building aesthetics by utilizing a steel frame to emphasize its soaring verticality. The facade is world-famous for its intricate red terracotta friezes and a distinct three-part composition inspired by classical columns. Louis Sullivan’s design established the 'form follows function' mantra that would dominate 20th-century architecture. Today, it remains a landmark example of the Chicago School and serves as an important office space for the state.

      3. 03

        St. Louis Union Station

        1820 Market St, St. Louis, MO 63103

        This historic structure once served as the largest and busiest passenger rail terminal in the world. Its Richardsonian Romanesque 'Headhouse' features a breathtaking 65-foot barrel-vaulted ceiling decorated with gold leaf and Tiffany glass. The Grand Hall now hosts a nightly light show that transforms the historic lobby into a vibrant visual experience. After the decline of rail travel, the station was brilliantly repurposed into a massive hotel and entertainment complex. It now houses a 200-foot Ferris wheel and an aquarium, breathing new life into a St. Louis icon.

      4. 04

        Cathedral Basilica of Saint Louis

        4431 Lindell Blvd, St. Louis, MO 63108

        Known for its massive green-tiled dome, this cathedral contains one of the largest mosaic collections in the Western world. Over 41 million glass tesserae in 7,000 different colors cover the interior walls and ceilings. The mosaic project was so massive that it took 20 different artists nearly 80 years to complete the entire design. Architecturally, the building is a stunning hybrid of Neo-Byzantine and Romanesque Revival styles that creates a sense of immense scale. It remains the spiritual heart of the city and serves as a major destination for both pilgrims and art historians.

      5. 05

        City Museum

        750 N 16th St, St. Louis, MO 63103

        Housed in a former shoe warehouse, the City Museum is an architectural playground built from repurposed industrial objects. The building serves as an 'assemblage' of found materials, including two real airplanes perched precariously on the roof. Inside, visitors can descend a 10-story spiral slide that was originally used to move shoe crates between floors. It was created by artist Bob Cassilly, who viewed the city's urban decay as a source of creative potential. This surrealist masterpiece challenges traditional museum concepts by encouraging guests to climb and explore the structure itself.

      6. 06

        Anheuser-Busch Brew House

        1200 Lynch St, St. Louis, MO 63118

        The Brew House is the historic heart of one of the world's most famous brewery complexes. This red-brick Romanesque masterpiece is adorned with ornate gargoyles, clock towers, and intricate hop-leaf motifs. As a National Historic Landmark, it stands as a monument to St. Louis's deep German heritage and industrial history. The interior features massive copper kettles and a multi-story atrium that allows for the traditional brewing process. It remains an active production site and a symbol of the city’s identity as a global leader in the brewing industry.

      7. 07

        The Fox Theatre

        527 N Grand Blvd, St. Louis, MO 63103

        The Fabulous Fox is one of the grandest 'movie palaces' ever constructed in the United States. Its interior features a wild 'Siamese Byzantine' style that blends Moorish, Far Eastern, and Indian motifs into a gilded fantasy. After falling into disrepair, it was saved from demolition and meticulously restored to its 1929 opulence. Today, it hosts major Broadway touring productions and serves as a primary anchor for the Grand Center Arts District. The theater’s massive chandelier and ornate Wurlitzer organ remain as testaments to the golden age of cinema.

      8. 08

        One Hundred (100 Above Park)

        100 N Kingshighway Blvd, St. Louis, MO 63108

        One Hundred is a striking residential tower that redefined the skyline of the Central West End. Designed by Jeanne Gang, its jagged glass facade is specifically engineered to maximize natural light and views of Forest Park. The tiered, angled design allows for the unique inclusion of outdoor terraces on every residential floor. Its sustainable features and contemporary aesthetic make it a standout of 21st-century architecture in the city. The building serves as a modern counterpoint to the historic masonry structures that surround the park.

      9. 09

        Soldiers Memorial Military Museum

        1315 Chestnut St, St. Louis, MO 63103

        This sleek Art Deco monument is dedicated to honoring the service and sacrifice of local veterans. The building is known for its clean limestone lines and four massive 'Centurions' statues that guard the entrance. Its symmetrical design features an open-air loggia that serves as a quiet space for reflection in the downtown core. Following a massive renovation in 2018, the museum now features state-of-the-art galleries and expanded community spaces. It stands as a centerpiece of the city's civic mall, bridging the gap between historical remembrance and modern museum standards.

      10. 10

        Pulitzer Arts Foundation

        3716 Washington Blvd, St. Louis, MO 63108

        The Pulitzer Arts Foundation is a minimalist sanctuary designed to create an intimate experience with contemporary art. The structure is defined by two long parallel wings separated by a central reflecting water court. Its cast-in-place concrete walls are famous for their incredibly smooth finish, which feels almost like silk to the touch. Architect Tadao Ando designed the space so that natural light would change the appearance of the art throughout the day. It is widely considered one of the most serene and architecturally significant museum buildings in the Midwest.

      11. 11

        The Old Courthouse

        11 N 4th St, St. Louis, MO 63102

        The Old Courthouse is a premier example of Greek Revival architecture and a site of immense historical significance. It served as the location for the pivotal Dred Scott trials, which eventually led to the American Civil War. The building’s impressive cast-iron dome was modeled after St. Peter's Basilica in Rome and completed during the 1860s. Today, it stands as the historic counterpoint to the Gateway Arch within the same National Park. It houses museum exhibits that detail the legal history of westward expansion and the struggle for civil rights.

      12. 12

        Saint Louis Art Museum

        1 Fine Arts Dr, St. Louis, MO 63110

        The Saint Louis Art Museum is the only permanent structure remaining from the legendary 1904 World's Fair. Built from durable Bedford limestone, it sits atop Art Hill and overlooks the picturesque Grand Basin of Forest Park. In 2013, a hyper-modern East Wing expansion by David Chipperfield added a floor-to-ceiling glass contrast to the original design. This combination of Beaux-Arts and contemporary architecture allows the museum to house a vast collection spanning 5,000 years. It remains one of the top comprehensive art museums in the country and is free to the public.

      13. 13

        The Jewel Box

        1 Wells Dr, St. Louis, MO 63110

        The Jewel Box is an Art Deco greenhouse that functions as a 'glass cathedral' within Forest Park. Its unconventional design features cantilevered glass walls that step up to a total height of 50 feet. Unlike traditional greenhouses, it utilizes a strong steel frame that supports massive vertical panes of glass. This engineering allows the interior to be flooded with natural light, perfect for exotic floral displays and events. It was recently restored to its original splendor and remains a popular site for weddings and seasonal horticultural shows.

      14. 14

        Apple Saint Louis Galleria

        1155 Saint Louis Galleria, St. Louis, MO 63117

        The Apple Saint Louis Galleria serves as the flagship retail and technology hub for the St. Louis metropolitan area. Located within the prominent Galleria mall, the store features a signature massive backlit glass entrance. The interior design follows Apple's modern minimalist aesthetic with an open-concept 'Genius Grove' for customer support. It underwent a major redesign to incorporate the latest retail features focused on community learning and creative workshops. The store's clean lines and high-tech atmosphere reflect the company’s global brand identity in a classic shopping destination.
