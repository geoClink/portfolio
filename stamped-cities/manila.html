City guides / Asia / Philippines

The Pearl of the Orient

Manila architecture

Manila is one of the world's most densely populated cities, with over 70,000 people per square kilometer in some districts. The Philippines is the only predominantly Christian nation in Asia, a legacy of over 300 years of Spanish colonial rule from 1565 to 1898. The walled city of Intramuros, built by the Spanish in 1571, still stands at the heart of Manila and remains one of the most intact examples of Spanish colonial urban planning in the world. The Philippines is also one of the world's leading producers of English-language call center services.

Before you go

  • Fly intoNinoy Aquino International Airport (MNL)

    Ninoy Aquino International Airport is named after Benigno Aquino Jr., the opposition senator assassinated on the airport tarmac in 1983 — an event that triggered the People Power Revolution and the end of Marcos's dictatorship. The airport serves one of the world's busiest aviation markets and has four terminals. A new Manila International Airport (Bulacan) is under construction to relieve congestion at the existing facility.

  • CurrencyPhilippine Peso (₱)
  • LanguageFilipino & English

    The Philippines has two official languages: Filipino (based on Tagalog) and English. English is used in government, education, and business, giving the Philippines one of the highest English proficiency rates in Asia. Filipino is a blended language that incorporates Spanish loanwords extensively — numbers, days of the week, and many household items are Spanish-derived. The country has over 175 distinct languages spoken across its 7,000+ islands.

  • Getting aroundJeepneys & MRT

    Manila's iconic jeepneys — originally converted U.S. military jeeps left after World War II — are one of the Philippines' most recognizable cultural symbols. These colorfully decorated vehicles serve fixed routes and remain a primary form of public transport. The MRT-3 and LRT lines cross key urban corridors but struggle with capacity during peak hours. Grab (ride-hailing) is widely used by locals and visitors for more reliable point-to-point travel. Manila's notorious traffic is one of the most challenging in the world.
