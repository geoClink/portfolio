The Commercial Capital

Colombo architecture

Colombo is Sri Lanka's commercial capital and largest city, though Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte serves as the legislative capital. The city is a dynamic blend of colonial-era architecture — Dutch, Portuguese, and British — with modern towers and ancient Buddhist temples. Sri Lanka is home to some of the world's finest Ceylon tea, and Colombo's Pettah market district is one of South Asia's most vibrant and concentrated wholesale bazaars. The island's position on ancient Indian Ocean trade routes made it a crossroads of cultures for over 2,000 years.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBandaranaike International Airport (CMB)

    Bandaranaike International Airport, named after Solomon Bandaranaike (assassinated prime minister), is located about 30km north of Colombo in Katunayake. A new expressway has reduced the journey time to the city significantly. The airport serves as the hub for SriLankan Airlines and handles a mix of regional and long-haul international routes. A new airport terminal expansion has modernized the facility significantly.

  • CurrencySri Lankan Rupee (Rs)
  • LanguageSinhala, Tamil & English

    Sri Lanka has three official languages: Sinhala (spoken by the Sinhalese majority), Tamil (spoken by Tamil communities in the north and east and by many estate workers), and English (used in government, law, and business). Colombo is notably multilingual — in a single conversation you may hear Sinhala, Tamil, and English seamlessly mixed. Sri Lankan English has its own distinctive accent and vocabulary, shaped by the country's unique colonial history.

  • Getting aroundCTB Buses & Tuk-tuks

    Colombo's primary public transport is the CTB (Ceylon Transport Board) bus network, supplemented by private buses on the same routes. Three-wheelers (tuk-tuks) are ubiquitous and the most practical way for visitors to navigate the city. Colombo's commuter rail connects the city center to suburbs along the coast. A light rail transit project (LRT) is under construction to create a modern rapid transit option for the city's growing population.

5 landmarks in Colombo

  1. 01

    Gangaramaya Temple

    61 Sri Jinarathana Rd, Colombo 00200, Sri Lanka

    A complex Buddhist temple founded in the late 19th century that has grown into one of Colombo's most eclectic and important religious sites, blending Sri Lankan, Thai, Indian, and Chinese architectural elements. The annual Navam Perahera procession in February is one of Sri Lanka's most spectacular festivals.

  2. 02

    National Museum of Colombo

    Sir Marcus Fernando Mawatha, Colombo 00700, Sri Lanka

    Sri Lanka's largest museum, founded in 1877 in a colonial Italianate building, houses the largest collection of art and artifacts documenting Sri Lankan civilizations from prehistoric times to the colonial era. Its most treasured exhibits include the royal regalia of the last King of Kandy.

  3. 03

    Independence Memorial Hall, Colombo

    Independence Avenue, Colombo 00700, Sri Lanka

    A monument and hall built in 1948 to commemorate Sri Lanka's independence from Britain, modeled on the Audience Hall of the ancient kingdom of Kandy. The open-air hall is the venue for the annual Independence Day ceremony on February 4.

  4. 04

    Pettah Market

    Main Street, Pettah, Colombo 00011, Sri Lanka

    Colombo's oldest and most chaotic wholesale market district, whose streets have been trading ground since the Dutch colonial era. Each street specializes in a different commodity — electronics, textiles, spices, jewelry — and the entire district buzzes with a sensory intensity.

  5. 05

    Galle Face Green

    Galle Face Green, Colombo 00300, Sri Lanka

    A 500-metre long urban promenade on the Indian Ocean seafront, originally created by the Dutch in 1859. Today it is Colombo's most popular public gathering space, particularly at sunset when families fly kites and vendors sell isso vadai (prawn fritters) and young coconuts.
