Emerald City

Sydney architecture

The Sydney Opera House is an engineering marvel with a roof that weighs over 15 tons and is covered in more than a million Swedish-made ceramic tiles. The city is built around the world's largest natural harbor, which features over 240 kilometers of shoreline and dozens of secluded harbor beaches.

Before you go

  • Fly intoKingsford Smith (SYD)

    Kingsford Smith is one of the oldest continually operating airports in the world, located just 8 kilometers from the city center on the edge of Botany Bay. It offers passengers a spectacular approach over the Pacific Ocean, providing a bird's-eye view of the iconic Sydney Harbour Bridge and the Opera House upon arrival.

  • CurrencyAustralian Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    The city speaks a laid-back version of English where 'No worries' serves as both a greeting and a national philosophy. You will find that communication is generally informal, friendly, and peppered with unique Australian idioms.

  • Getting aroundSydney Ferries

    Sydney Ferries are the city's most iconic form of transit, offering incredible views of the Opera House and Harbor Bridge. The Sydney Trains network provides a massive heavy-rail service that connects the CBD to the sprawling western suburbs. A new driverless Metro line has recently opened, featuring high-frequency trains and platform screen doors.

17 landmarks in Sydney

  1. 01

    Sydney Opera House

    Bennelong Point, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    The Sydney Opera House is a masterpiece of 20th-century architecture and an iconic symbol of Australia's cultural identity. It is world-famous for its gleaming white shell-shaped roof, which was designed to echo the sails of boats in the harbor. These 'shells' are constructed from precast concrete panels covered in over a million custom-made Swedish ceramic tiles. Situated on Bennelong Point, the structure is surrounded by the waters of Sydney Harbour on three sides, providing a dramatic backdrop for performances. Since its completion in 1973, it has become one of the busiest performing arts centers in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage site.

  2. 02

    Sydney Harbour Bridge

    Sydney Harbour Bridge, Sydney NSW, Australia

    Affectionately nicknamed 'The Coathanger' by locals, this is the world's largest steel arch bridge and a marvel of engineering. It serves as the primary transportation link between the Sydney central business district and the North Shore. The bridge's massive structure features four decorative pylons faced with granite, which were quarried locally in Moruya. While primarily a piece of infrastructure, its scale and the presence of six million hand-driven rivets make it a dominant architectural landmark. It offers an adventurous 'BridgeClimb' experience that allows visitors to reach the very summit of the arch for unparalleled city views.

  3. 03

    Sydney Tower Eye

    100 Market St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    As the tallest structure in Sydney, the Sydney Tower Eye provides a literal high point for any visit to the city center. The slender structure is stabilized by 56 high-strength steel cables that anchor it firmly against the forces of nature. Its golden-hued turret contains multiple levels, including a revolving restaurant and a 360-degree observation deck. The engineering of the tower is designed to withstand extreme wind conditions and seismic activity while maintaining a minimal footprint at street level. It remains a fixture of the skyline, easily identifiable by its unique 'spindle' design and prominent position atop the Westfield shopping center.

  4. 04

    Queen Victoria Building (QVB)

    455 George St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    The Queen Victoria Building is a Romanesque Revival masterpiece that occupies an entire city block on George Street. It is famous for its massive central dome, which features an intricate glass inner skin and elaborate external copper sheathing. Originally built as a marketplace, the building's interior is a treasure trove of mosaic tilework, stained glass, and grand staircases. Two iconic mechanical clocks hang within the voids, each telling a different story of Australian and British royal history. After a major restoration in the 1980s, it transitioned into a luxury shopping destination while preserving its 19th-century elegance.

  5. 05

    One Central Park

    28 Broadway, Chippendale NSW 2008, Australia

    One Central Park is a global landmark for sustainable urban design, recognizable by its lush, living exterior. The building is covered in vertical hanging gardens that use a sophisticated hydroponic system to flourish without soil. A massive cantilevered heliostat mirrors sunlight into the park below, ensuring the green space remains vibrant throughout the day. It represents a bold experiment in 'living' architecture, aiming to bridge the gap between high-density living and nature. The project has received numerous awards for its innovative approach to environmental sustainability and public art integration.

  6. 06

    Crown Sydney

    1 Barangaroo Ave, Barangaroo NSW 2000, Australia

    Crown Sydney is the city's tallest habitable building, soaring above the revamped Barangaroo waterfront precinct. Its striking, twisting form was inspired by three petals rising toward the sky, creating a sculptural addition to the harbor. The glass facade is composed of thousands of unique panels that shimmer and change color with the movement of the sun and water. Inside, the building houses ultra-luxury hotel rooms, high-end residences, and a world-class casino. It has quickly become a new destination for fine dining, offering some of the most exclusive culinary experiences in the country.

  7. 07

    Art Gallery of New South Wales

    Art Gallery Rd, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    The Art Gallery of New South Wales is a fascinating blend of 19th-century Neoclassicism and 21st-century modernism. The original sandstone building greets visitors with a grand temple-front facade overlooking The Domain. In 2022, the 'Sydney Modern' extension opened, featuring light-filled pavilions that descend toward the harbor. A standout feature of the new wing is a decommissioned WWII oil tank that has been brilliantly repurposed as a subterranean art space. This expansion has doubled the gallery’s capacity to showcase its extensive collection of Australian, Indigenous, and international art.

  8. 08

    St Mary's Cathedral

    St Marys Rd, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    St Mary's Cathedral is the spiritual heart of Sydney and the longest church in all of Australia. Constructed from local yellow-block sandstone, it glows with a warm, golden hue in the afternoon sunlight. It is a premier example of English Gothic Revival architecture, characterized by its pointed arches and soaring spires. The interior is adorned with intricate stained glass windows imported from Birmingham, which cast colorful light across the nave. Situated next to Hyde Park, its two 74-meter spires have become an indispensable part of the city's traditional skyline.

    Customs House

    31 Alfred St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    Customs House is a beautifully preserved heritage-listed building located in the heart of Circular Quay. One of its most famous internal features is a 1:500 scale model of the Sydney city center situated beneath a walkable glass floor. The exterior of the building elegantly displays the transition from Georgian to Victorian architectural styles through its various wings. Originally the site of maritime administration, it has been transformed into a vibrant public library and exhibition space. Its rooftop offers a highly regarded restaurant that provides diners with spectacular views of the ferries and the harbor.

  10. 10

    Dr Chau Chak Wing Building

    14 Ultimo Rd, Ultimo NSW 2007, Australia

    Commonly referred to as the 'crumpled paper bag,' this building is a striking example of deconstructivist architecture. It is notably the first and only building in Australia to be designed by the world-renowned architect Frank Gehry. The unique facade was achieved using approximately 320,000 custom-designed bricks, all laid by hand to create the undulating curves. Inside, the structure houses the UTS Business School, featuring oval classrooms and a polished stainless steel staircase. Its radical design serves as a bold statement of creativity and innovation within the Ultimo education precinct.

    International Towers Sydney

    300 Barangaroo Ave, Barangaroo NSW 2000, Australia

    This trio of sustainable skyscrapers forms the commercial cornerstone of the ambitious Barangaroo urban renewal project. The towers utilize a radial geometry designed to maximize sunlight penetration and provide expansive harbor views for workers. Each building is distinguishable by its unique solar shading fins, which are finished in vibrant shades of red, yellow, and blue. As a carbon-neutral complex, it represents Sydney's commitment to world-class environmental standards in office design. The precinct around the towers has transformed a former industrial terminal into a thriving hub for finance and technology.

    Museum of Contemporary Art (MCA)

    140 George St, The Rocks NSW 2000, Australia

    The Museum of Contemporary Art is a bold architectural statement located at the historic edge of The Rocks. It is housed in the former Maritime Services Board building, an Art Deco sandstone structure that exudes mid-century authority. In 2012, the museum was modernized with the addition of the Mordant Wing, featuring a series of interlocking black and white volumes. This extension created a striking visual contrast between the heritage sandstone and the stark contemporary geometry. The rooftop cafe is widely considered to have one of the best vantage points for viewing the Sydney Opera House.

    Apple Tower House

    197 Pitt St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    Apple Tower House is a stunning flagship store that masterfully merges historical preservation with futuristic retail design. It is located within the 1930s City Tattersalls Club building, a site with deep social roots in Sydney’s history. The restoration project involved meticulously cleaning the original Art Deco sandstone and timber detailing to its former glory. A massive glass entry allows natural light to flood the tech-filled interior, which features Apple's signature minimalist tables. This location serves as both a high-tech retail hub and a celebration of Sydney's rich architectural heritage.

  14. 14

    Sydney Town Hall

    483 George St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    Sydney Town Hall is the grandest example of Victorian Second Empire architecture found anywhere in Australia. Constructed from local Sydney sandstone, it features an ornate clock tower that has watched over the city for over a century. The building is home to the 'Grand Old Lady,' a massive pipe organ that remains one of the largest in the world. Interestingly, the building sits on the site of the city's first official cemetery, a fact noted in its historical archives. Today, it continues to serve as the city's primary civic meeting place and a prestigious venue for concerts and gala events.

  15. 15

    Quay Quarter Tower

    50 Bridge St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    Quay Quarter Tower is recognized globally as the world’s first successful 'upcycled' skyscraper project. By retaining 65% of the original 1970s AMP Centre structure, the architects significantly reduced the building's carbon footprint. The new design features five shifting glass volumes that rotate toward the harbor to maximize the iconic views. These volumes create spacious rooftop terraces that provide green outdoor space for the office workers high above the city. This innovative approach to urban renewal has won numerous international awards for its sustainability and architectural ingenuity.

    Rose Seidler House

    71 Clissold Rd, Wahroonga NSW 2076, Australia

    The Rose Seidler House is a definitive landmark of Mid-Century Modernism, located in the leafy northern suburbs of Sydney. Designed by the young Harry Seidler for his parents, the house was a radical departure from the suburban norms of the 1950s. It features a bold 'floating' design with floor-to-ceiling glass walls that invite the surrounding bushland inside. The open-plan interior was filled with contemporary European furniture and the latest household technology of the era. Now a museum, it remains perfectly preserved as a testament to the introduction of Bauhaus principles to the Australian landscape.

  17. 17

    Salesforce Tower (Sydney Place)

    180 George St, Sydney NSW 2000, Australia

    Salesforce Tower currently stands as the tallest office building in Sydney, anchoring the new Sydney Place precinct. The building is a masterclass in structural expressionism, with its external bracing system proudly displayed through the glass skin. It was designed to prioritize the pedestrian experience, creating new lanes and public plazas that link Circular Quay to the CBD. The tower features world-leading sustainability credentials and highly flexible workspaces designed for the post-pandemic era. It serves as a vertical community, housing thousands of workers while contributing to the revitalization of the northern city skyline.
