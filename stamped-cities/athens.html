The Cradle of Civilization

Athens architecture

Athens is one of the world's oldest cities, with a recorded history spanning over 3,400 years. It is the birthplace of democracy, Western philosophy, and the Olympic Games. The Acropolis, crowned by the Parthenon, has stood overlooking the city for nearly 2,500 years and remains one of the most recognizable monuments on Earth.

Before you go

  • Fly intoAthens International Airport Eleftherios Venizelos (ATH)

    Athens International Airport, known as Eleftherios Venizelos, opened in 2001 and is one of the most efficient airports in southern Europe. It features a striking design inspired by ancient Greek architecture and houses a small archaeological museum within the terminal, where artifacts unearthed during its construction are displayed for travelers to enjoy between flights.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageGreek

    Greek is one of the world's oldest documented languages, with a written history stretching back over 3,000 years. While modern Greek differs from its ancient form, many everyday words trace directly to classical roots. Most people in central Athens and tourism-facing industries speak English.

  • Getting aroundMetro & Bus

    Athens has a modern three-line metro system that connects the airport directly to the city center and the port of Piraeus. Several stations serve as underground museums, with ancient artifacts discovered during construction displayed behind glass on the platforms. The historic yellow trolleybuses have served the city center since the 1940s.
