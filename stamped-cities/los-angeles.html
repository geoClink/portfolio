City of Angels

Los Angeles architecture

The original Spanish name for Los Angeles was 63 characters long: 'El Pueblo de Nuestra Señora la Reina de los Ángeles de Porciúncula.' Today, it is a global entertainment capital where the historic Hollywood sign overlooks a sprawling metropolis of 88 distinct incorporated cities.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoLos Angeles Int'l (LAX)

        The airport is anchored by the iconic 'Theme Building,' a Space-Age mid-century landmark that looks like a landed UFO on four concrete legs. LAX is currently undergoing a massive multi-billion dollar modernization project that includes a new automated people mover to connect travelers to the metro rail.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish & Spanish

        Los Angeles is a massive multicultural melting pot where 'Valley' slang, entertainment industry shorthand, and Spanish loanwords frequently intermingle. It is a city where conversations are as likely to revolve around film production as they are to be held in a mix of English and various Asian or Latin American languages.

      • Getting aroundLA Metro

        The A Line is now the longest light rail line in the world, stretching from Long Beach to Azusa. Metrolink provides regional heavy rail service that reaches out to six different Southern California counties. The iconic Angels Flight funicular is the world's shortest railway and serves the steep Bunker Hill district.

      15 landmarks in Los Angeles

      1. 01

        Walt Disney Concert Hall

        111 South Grand Avenue, Los Angeles, CA 90012

        A landmark of contemporary architecture, this building is clad in matte-finish stainless steel panels that curve and billow like sails. The auditorium interior is designed to feel like a wooden ship with a massive pipe organ resembling a bundle of french fries. It is globally recognized for its acoustic perfection and its role in revitalizing Downtown Los Angeles. Visitors can explore the hidden rooftop garden which offers a quiet escape from the urban bustle. The structure remains one of the most photographed examples of deconstructivism in the world.

      2. 02

        Apple Tower Theatre

        802 South Broadway, Los Angeles, CA 90014

        This project is a meticulous restoration of Los Angeles' first cinema designed specifically for talkie movies. The renovation included seismic strengthening and the full reconstruction of a long-lost exterior clock tower. Original terra-cotta, marble, and decorative plaster were carefully cataloged and restored to their 1927 glory. The central dome features a stunning new fresco of the California sky that illuminates the interior. Today, the theater’s balcony serves as the Genius Level, overlooking a modern retail forum located under the restored proscenium arch.

      3. 03

        Griffith Observatory

        2800 East Observatory Road, Los Angeles, CA 90027

        Perched on the slope of Mount Hollywood, this building is a blend of Greek and Beaux-Arts styles with Art Deco touches. It features three distinctive copper-clad domes and offers panoramic views of the Hollywood Sign and the Pacific Ocean. The interior contains the famous Foucault pendulum which visually demonstrates the Earth's constant rotation. It has served as a key filming location for Hollywood classics like Rebel Without a Cause. Since its opening in 1935, it has remained one of the most popular public observatories in the United States.

      4. 04

        The Bradbury Building

        304 South Broadway, Los Angeles, CA 90013

        As the oldest commercial building in the central city, its unassuming brick exterior hides one of the most beautiful interiors in California. It features a stunning five-story central court illuminated by a massive overhead skylight. The space is famous for its open-cage elevators, marble stairs, and intricate wrought-iron railings. It is considered a masterpiece of Victorian design and was famously used as a futuristic set in the film Blade Runner. The building continues to house active offices while remaining a mandatory stop for architectural tourists.

      5. 05

        Theme Building (LAX)

        201 World Way, Los Angeles, CA 90045

        An icon of Mid-Century Modern Googie architecture, this structure resembles a four-legged UFO that has just landed. It was built as the ambitious centerpiece of the jet-age expansion for Los Angeles International Airport. The dramatic arches are constructed of steel-reinforced concrete that support a suspended observation level. The building originally housed a revolving restaurant that offered guests a 360-degree view of the busy runways. It was designated as a Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument in 1993 to preserve its unique space-age silhouette.

      6. 06

        Hollyhock House

        4800 Hollywood Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90027

        This UNESCO World Heritage site represents Frank Lloyd Wright’s first significant commission in the city of Los Angeles. The design utilizes a transitional style called California Romanza which features heavy, sloped walls and cast-concrete floral motifs. The house is centered around a courtyard to blur the lines between indoor and outdoor living spaces. This layout acted as an important precursor to the modern California lifestyle and open-plan architecture. Today it serves as a public museum and a centerpiece for the Barnsdall Art Park.

      7. 07

        The Getty Center

        1200 Getty Center Drive, Los Angeles, CA 90049

        This massive campus of travertine-clad buildings overlooks the Santa Monica Mountains from a prominent hilltop. The architecture is a study in light and grid-based design, utilizing over 1.2 million square feet of Italian stone. The complex is designed to feel like an Italian hill town, featuring circular galleries and peaceful gardens. A central stream acts as a natural cooling system for the outdoor plazas and walkways. It serves as one of the world's most influential centers for art research and public exhibition.

      8. 08

        Wilshire Grand Center

        900 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90017

        This skyscraper stands as the tallest building in the United States west of the Mississippi River. It is the first major Los Angeles tower to feature a curved, sloping roof instead of a flat helipad design. Its glass 'sail' shape is topped with a massive LED spine that displays digital art across the city. The building serves as a luxury hotel and premium office space in the heart of the Financial District. Its innovative structural design was engineered to withstand significant seismic activity while maintaining a slender profile.

      9. 09

        Eastern Columbia Building

        849 South Broadway, Los Angeles, CA 90014

        Often called the 'Jewel of Downtown,' this Art Deco skyscraper is clad in brilliant turquoise terra cotta. The exterior is trimmed with gold leaf and deep blue accents to create a striking visual presence. It features a prominent four-sided clock tower that has become a landmark of the Broadway corridor. Originally a department store, it is now one of the city's most prestigious residential loft addresses. The structure remains a prime example of the Zigzag Moderne style that defined 1930s Los Angeles.

      10. 10

        Stahl House (Case Study House #22)

        1635 Woods Drive, Los Angeles, CA 90069

        This minimalist glass-and-steel box appears to float over the glowing lights of the Los Angeles basin. Built as part of the Case Study House program, it demonstrated modern materials for post-war living. It is one of the most famous houses in history, representing the ultimate dream of Hollywood Hills luxury. The L-shaped plan wraps around a swimming pool that cantilevers over a steep hillside precipice. It continues to inspire architects globally as a masterwork of Mid-Century Modern residential design.

      11. 11

        Academy Museum of Motion Pictures

        6067 Wilshire Boulevard, Los Angeles, CA 90036

        This complex blends the historic 1939 May Company department store with a massive new glass-and-concrete sphere. The 'Sphere Building' houses a 1,000-seat theater and is topped by a terrace with Hollywood Hill views. Advanced engineering was utilized to 'float' the sphere above the ground on massive seismic base isolators. The interior galleries showcase the history, technology, and social impact of global cinema. It serves as a major cultural anchor on the Miracle Mile, celebrating the art of filmmaking.

      12. 12

        Union Station

        800 North Alameda Street, Los Angeles, CA 90012

        Known as the 'Last of the Great Railway Stations,' this terminal is a gateway to the American West. Its architecture is a stunning blend of Spanish Colonial Revival and Art Deco motifs. The interior features massive leather club chairs, terracotta tiled floors, and enclosed garden courtyards. The soaring ceilings and hand-painted wood beams have made it a favorite backdrop for Hollywood noir films. It continues to serve as the main transit hub connecting the entire Southern California region.

      13. 13

        Capitol Records Building

        1750 Vine Street, Los Angeles, CA 90028

        This is the world's first circular office building, designed to resemble a stack of vinyl records. A red light at the peak blinks the word 'Hollywood' in Morse code for the city. Below ground, it houses legendary echo chambers used by artists from Frank Sinatra to The Beatles. The spire on top completes the silhouette, resembling the needle of a classic record player. It remains one of the most recognizable icons of the Hollywood skyline and the music industry.

      14. 14

        Chiat/Day 'Binoculars' Building

        340 Main Street, Venice, CA 90291

        This whimsical masterpiece of Postmodernism is located in the coastal neighborhood of Venice. The building is split into three distinct styles, including a forest of copper-clad columns. Its most famous feature is the massive black binoculars that serve as the entrance to the garage. It represents the quirky, artistic spirit of the California tech and advertising boom of the 1990s. The structure now serves as an office for Google, maintaining its legacy of creative innovation.

      15. 15

        Apple The Grove

        189 The Grove Drive, Los Angeles, CA 90036

        This project is a stunning example of transparent retail architecture in a popular outdoor mall. The store features a massive 60-foot glass facade with two giant doors that slide open completely. Inside, a mirrored ceiling creates a sense of infinite height and reflects the activity below. A lush indoor forest of ficus trees reflects the surrounding outdoor California landscape. It is considered a masterpiece of light, air, and minimalist engineering within a commercial setting.
