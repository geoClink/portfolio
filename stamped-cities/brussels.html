Capital of Europe

Brussels architecture

Brussels is the de facto capital of the European Union and home to NATO headquarters — making it one of the most diplomatically significant cities in the world. The city is the global capital of Art Nouveau architecture, with more buildings in this style per square kilometer than anywhere else on Earth, thanks to architect Victor Horta's prolific 19th-century output. Belgium has more Michelin-starred restaurants per capita than France, and Brussels is the undisputed world capital of chocolate, waffles, and Belgian beer.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBrussels Airport (BRU)

    Brussels Airport (Zaventem) is Belgium's main international hub and one of the busiest cargo airports in Europe. A direct rail link from the airport to Brussels-Central, Brussels-Midi, and Brussels-North stations makes getting into the city straightforward. The airport is close to EU institutions, making it one of Europe's most frequently used airports by diplomats and government officials.

  • CurrencyEuro (€)
  • LanguageFrench, Dutch & German

    Belgium is a linguistically complex federal state with three official communities: French-speaking Wallonia, Dutch-speaking Flanders, and a small German-speaking community. Brussels itself is officially bilingual (French and Dutch), with French dominant in the city. In practice, many Brusselois speak a unique blend called 'Bruxellois' — a historical local dialect mixing French, Flemish, and Spanish words that is still heard among older residents. English is widely spoken due to the EU and NATO presence.

  • Getting aroundSTIB/MIVB Metro & Trams

    Brussels has a well-integrated public transport network operated by STIB/MIVB with a metro, pre-metro (underground trams), surface trams, and buses. The metro has four lines connecting the city center to surrounding communes. Trams are a distinctive feature of the city and some routes date back over 130 years. The Eurostar train links Brussels-Midi to London St. Pancras in under 2 hours, and Thalys connects it to Amsterdam and Paris in similar time, making Brussels a true crossroads of European rail travel.
