America's Finest City

San Diego architecture

San Diego calls itself the 'Birthplace of California': in 1769 the friar Junípero Serra founded Mission San Diego de Alcalá here, the first of the Alta California missions. Balboa Park is also home to the Spreckels Organ, the world's largest pipe organ in a fully outdoor venue.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoSan Diego Int'l (SAN)

        San Diego International sits just three miles northwest of downtown and is the busiest single-runway airport in the United States. It opened in 1928 as Lindbergh Field, honoring aviator Charles Lindbergh. Planes approach low over the city, and its closeness to downtown is why the FAA caps the height of San Diego's skyscrapers.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        English is the main language, but the Spanish heritage of this border city shows in its place names. San Diego itself is named for the Spanish saint Didacus of Alcalá, and locals proudly call their home 'America's Finest City.'

      • Getting aroundSan Diego Trolley

        The San Diego Trolley began service in 1981 and is the oldest of America's second-generation light rail systems. Its Blue, Orange, Green and Copper lines run daily, and the Blue Line ends at San Ysidro, right next to the border crossing into Mexico. It is part of the San Diego Metropolitan Transit System, which also runs the city's buses.

      15 landmarks in San Diego

      1. 01

        Salk Institute for Biological Studies

        10010 N Torrey Pines Rd, La Jolla, CA 92037

        Founded by polio-vaccine pioneer Jonas Salk, this research campus on the La Jolla bluffs was completed in 1965 and is widely seen as the defining work of Louis Kahn. Two symmetric wings of warm, pinkish poured concrete face each other across a travertine plaza. On the advice of architect Luis Barragán, Kahn left the plaza bare except for a single thin stream of water running toward the Pacific. Teak-framed study windows soften the concrete, and the building won the AIA Twenty-five Year Award in 1992.

      2. 02

        Geisel Library

        UC San Diego, La Jolla, CA

        The main library of UC San Diego is a reinforced-concrete icon whose design has been described as a fascinating nexus between Brutalism and Futurism. William Pereira's firm designed it, and it opened in 1970 as the Central Library. It was renamed in 1995 for Audrey and Theodor Seuss Geisel, better known as Dr. Seuss. The building stands about 33 meters tall over eight floors and houses more than seven million volumes, including the Dr. Seuss Collection.

      3. 03

        Museum of Us

        1350 El Prado, San Diego, CA

        The Museum of Us occupies the California Building and its ornate California Tower, the grand entry to the 1915–16 Panama–California Exposition. Architect Bertram Goodhue designed the buildings in the Spanish Colonial Revival style, crowning the California Building with a dome and a soaring tower. Unlike most exposition buildings, the California Quadrangle was meant to be permanent. Visitors arrive over the Cabrillo Bridge and pass through the Plaza de California into Balboa Park.

      4. 04

        Cabrillo Bridge

        El Prado, San Diego, CA

        Completed in 1914 for the Panama–California Exposition, this bridge carries El Prado across Cabrillo Canyon into Balboa Park. Designed by Frank P. Allen Jr. and Thomas B. Hunter, it stretches about 450 feet and stands roughly 120 feet high. It still provides the park's grand western approach, leading straight to the California Tower. The American Society of Civil Engineers named it a Local Historic Civil Engineering Landmark in 1986.

      5. 05

        Spreckels Organ Pavilion

        1549 El Prado, San Diego, CA 92101

        Built for the 1915 Panama–California Exposition and opened on December 31, 1914, this pavilion shelters the open-air Spreckels Organ. With more than 5,000 pipes, it is the world's largest pipe organ in a fully outdoor venue. Architect Harrison Albright designed the pavilion, whose metal benches seat about 2,500 listeners. When new, the organ cost $33,500 and the pavilion around it $66,500.

      6. 06

        Casa de Balboa

        Balboa Park, San Diego, CA

        Opened in 1915 as the Commerce and Industries Building, this Spanish Colonial Revival hall has had many names, including the Canadian Building and the Electric Building. It lines El Prado, Balboa Park's main promenade, with an ornamented exposition-era facade. After a fire, it was reconstructed in the 1970s. Today it houses the Museum of Photographic Arts, the San Diego History Center and the San Diego Model Railroad Museum.

        1915
        Style
        Spanish Colonial Revival

        Museums
      7. 07

        Santa Fe Depot

        1050 Kettner Blvd, San Diego, CA

        The Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway opened this union station on March 8, 1915, just in time for visitors to the Panama–California Exposition. Designed by Bakewell and Brown, it is a landmark of the Spanish Colonial Revival style, replacing a small Victorian depot of 1887. It was renamed San Diego Union Station in 1919 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Amtrak trains still use it today.

      8. 08

        San Diego County Administration Center

        1600 Pacific Hwy, San Diego, CA

        Nicknamed the 'Jewel on the Bay,' this civic palace fronting San Diego Bay was completed in 1938, largely with funding from the Works Progress Administration. Architects Samuel Wood Hamill, William Templeton Johnson, Richard Requa and Louis John Gill blended Beaux-Arts and Spanish Revival styles. The building rises about 150 feet above the waterfront. The building used innovative construction techniques against earthquakes and was hailed as a prototype of American civic center architecture.

      9. 09

        U.S. Grant Hotel

        326 Broadway, San Diego, CA

        One of San Diego's oldest hotels, the U.S. Grant opened downtown in 1910 to designs by Harrison Albright. Its Classical Revival and Beaux-Arts facade rises 11 stories above Broadway. Inside are 270 guest rooms along with ballrooms and meeting rooms. Listed on the National Register of Historic Places, it is now part of Marriott's Luxury Collection.

      10. 10

        Spreckels Theatre

        Broadway, San Diego, CA

        Built in 1912 for philanthropist John D. Spreckels to commemorate the opening of the Panama Canal, this theater was touted as the first modern commercial playhouse west of the Mississippi. Harrison Albright designed the building, which the National Register describes as Chicago-style. It was created for live theater and converted to show motion pictures in 1931. The theater has been closed since a 2021 sale, with plans to renovate it.

      11. 11

        One America Plaza

        600 W Broadway, San Diego, CA

        San Diego's tallest building is a 34-story, 500-foot obelisk designed by Helmut Jahn of Murphy/Jahn with KMA Architecture and completed in 1991. Its pointed crown is often compared to the tip of a Phillips-head screwdriver. The tower reaches the maximum height the FAA allows downtown because of nearby San Diego International Airport. The America Plaza trolley station sits right at its base.

      12. 12

        Petco Park

        100 Park Blvd, San Diego, CA

        Home of the San Diego Padres since 2004, this ballpark anchors the East Village beside the Gaslamp Quarter. Populous (then HOK Sport) designed it with architect Antoine Predock, while Spurlock Poirier shaped the landscape. A historic preservation firm was also part of the design team. It replaced San Diego Stadium as the Padres' home, cost about 450 million dollars and is owned mostly by the city.

      13. 13

        San Diego–Coronado Bridge

        State Route 75, San Diego, CA

        Opened on August 3, 1969, this prestressed concrete and steel girder bridge sweeps 2.1 miles across San Diego Bay to Coronado. The Navy required 200 feet of clearance for its ships, so engineers stretched the approach into a dramatic 80-degree curve to keep the grade gentle. Robert Mosher was the principal architect, aiming for a bridge that would also be a landmark for the city. Tolls were charged until 2002.

      14. 14

        Mission San Diego de Alcalá

        10818 San Diego Mission Rd, San Diego, CA 92108

        Founded on July 16, 1769, by the Spanish friar Junípero Serra, this was the first of the Alta California missions and gave the city its name. The original mission burned in 1775 during an uprising by local Kumeyaay people. The present church, built in the early 19th century, is the fifth to stand on the site. It is a National Historic Landmark and still serves as a parish church.

        1769
        Architect

        Style

      15. 15

        Old Point Loma Lighthouse

        Cabrillo National Monument, San Diego, CA

        Built by the U.S. government in 1855, soon after California became a state, this lighthouse stands high on the Point Loma peninsula at the mouth of San Diego Bay. The modest Cape Cod–style building is only about 14 meters tall, but its light shone from some 460 feet above the sea. It is sometimes wrongly called the 'Old Spanish Lighthouse' even though it dates from the American era. No longer in service, it is now a museum within Cabrillo National Monument.
