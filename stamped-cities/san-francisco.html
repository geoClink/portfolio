The Golden Gate City

San Francisco architecture

San Francisco is famous for the Golden Gate Bridge, its iconic cable cars, and steep streets like Lombard, the 'crookedest street in the world.' It is a cultural and tech powerhouse where the historic Haight-Ashbury district's legacy of the 1960s meets the futuristic high-rises of Salesforce and Uber.

Before you go

  • Fly intoSFO

    SFO is home to the 'Wag Brigade,' a team of therapy animals including a famous pig named LiLou that helps calm stressed travelers. The airport is a global leader in sustainability, featuring a terminal that was the first in the world to be LEED Gold certified and a strict ban on plastic water bottles.

  • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    English is the primary language, though the city's tech-heavy influence has introduced a specialized vocabulary of 'disruption' and 'innovation.' The culture is highly progressive and diverse, with sustainability and social mindfulness often driving public discourse.

  • Getting aroundBART & Cable Cars

    The Cable Cars are the only moving National Historic Landmark in the United States and are powered by underground cables. BART is the high-speed regional backbone, famously crossing under the Bay via the Transbay Tube. Muni operates a diverse fleet of light rail, trolleybuses, and historic F-Line streetcars.

  1. 01

    Transamerica Pyramid

    600 Montgomery St, San Francisco, CA 94111

    Once controversial for its futuristic shape, the pyramid design was actually chosen to allow light and air to reach the streets below. Its tapered silhouette reduces the shadow cast on the surrounding Financial District, a key concern during its initial planning. The four-story base is reinforced with a 52-mile network of steel and 16,000 yards of concrete to withstand major earthquakes. Two distinct 'wings' house elevators and stairs, allowing the floor plates to remain open and functional. The structure is capped by a 212-foot spire clad in aluminum panels that glows brightly during holiday celebrations.

  2. 02

    Salesforce Tower

    415 Mission St, San Francisco, CA 94105

    Standing as the tallest building in San Francisco, its curved, tapering silhouette symbolizes the city's tech-driven evolution. The building is anchored into bedrock 310 feet deep, making it one of the safest structures in the world against seismic activity. Its crown features an 11-story kinetic art installation called 'Day for Night' that projects low-resolution video of the city. High-tech floor-to-ceiling windows provide 360-degree views while utilizing advanced air filtration systems for occupant health. The tower is directly integrated into the Salesforce Transit Center, creating a seamless link between work and regional transportation.

  3. 03

    Ferry Building

    1 Ferry Building, San Francisco, CA 94105

    This historic terminal served as the primary gateway to the city before the completion of the Golden Gate and Bay Bridges. Its iconic 245-foot clock tower was modeled after the 12th-century Giralda bell tower located in Seville, Spain. After surviving both the 1906 and 1989 earthquakes, the building underwent a massive restoration to preserve its nave. Today, it functions as a world-class food hall that celebrates the diverse culinary heritage of Northern California. The building continues to serve its original purpose as a ferry terminal, connecting San Francisco to various points across the Bay.

  4. 04

    Palace of Fine Arts

    3601 Lyon St, San Francisco, CA 94123

    Originally constructed for the 1915 Panama-Pacific International Exposition, this structure was intended to resemble a fictional Roman ruin. The massive rotunda and sweeping colonnades are decorated with statues of weeping women representing the melancholy of life without art. It is the only structure from the world's fair that remains in its original location today. The surrounding lagoon serves as a peaceful sanctuary for local wildlife and offers a popular backdrop for photography. Though it was originally built with temporary materials, it was completely reconstructed in the 1960s using permanent poured concrete.

  5. 05

    The Painted Ladies

    710-720 Steiner St, San Francisco, CA 94117

    This world-famous row of Victorian houses is affectionately known as 'Postcard Row' due to its frequent appearance in media. These Queen Anne-style homes are celebrated for their intricate three-color paint schemes that highlight their decorative millwork. They represent the resilient survivors of the thousands of Victorians that once populated the city's residential districts. The backdrop of the modern downtown skyline creates a striking visual contrast between the old and new San Francisco. Despite their status as a global tourist attraction, these historic structures remain private residences and part of a vibrant neighborhood.

  6. 06

    Coit Tower

    1 Telegraph Hill Blvd, San Francisco, CA 94133

    Perched atop Telegraph Hill, this slender fluted column was built using a bequest from the eccentric socialite Lillie Hitchcock Coit. The tower is globally recognized for its interior murals, which were painted by 25 different artists during the Great Depression. These murals depict various scenes of California industry and agriculture, reflecting the social realism of the 1930s. The tower’s simple Art Deco design has made it a permanent and beloved fixture of the San Francisco skyline. Visitors can take an elevator to the observation deck for panoramic views of the Golden Gate Bridge and Alcatraz.

  7. 07

    Saint Mary's Cathedral

    1111 Gough St, San Francisco, CA 94109

    This bold landmark of modern religious architecture features a roof composed of four striking hyperbolic paraboloids. The white travertine exterior and soaring 190-foot ceiling are designed to draw the eyes of visitors upward toward the heavens. Massive concrete pylons support the immense weight of the roof, allowing for a vast, column-free interior space. While locals occasionally compare its unique shape to a washing machine agitator, it is internationally hailed as a masterpiece of engineering. The interior is bathed in colorful light from a narrow cross-shaped window that runs along the seams of the roof.

  8. 08

    City Hall

    1 Dr. Carlton B. Goodlett Pl, San Francisco, CA 94102

    As the crown jewel of the Civic Center, this building features a gold-leafed dome that is taller than the United States Capitol's dome. Rebuilt after the original was destroyed in 1906, it serves as a grand example of Beaux-Arts architecture and civic pride. The interior is famous for its grand rotunda and a ceremonial staircase that is a popular location for weddings. To protect its historical integrity, the entire structure sits on hundreds of rubber and steel shock absorbers for seismic safety. It occupies two full city blocks and remains the primary seat of government for the City and County of San Francisco.

  9. 09

    Ghirardelli Square

    900 North Point St, San Francisco, CA 94109

    This historic complex was once a major chocolate factory before becoming the first successful adaptive reuse project in the United States. The brick clock tower and iconic light-up sign were meticulously preserved to maintain the site's industrial character. It successfully kept the city’s maritime history alive while transforming a private factory into a vibrant public plaza. Today, the square is a hub for retail, dining, and artisanal chocolate production on the city's northern waterfront. It serves as a model for how cities can repurpose industrial infrastructure for the benefit of modern tourism and commerce.

  10. 10

    de Young Museum

    50 Hagiwara Tea Garden Dr, San Francisco, CA 94118

    Located within the heart of Golden Gate Park, this museum is clad in over 160,000 square feet of perforated copper panels. The copper facade is designed to oxidize over time, eventually turning green to blend in with the surrounding park foliage. Its dramatic Hamon Observation Tower twists as it rises to offer 360-degree views of the Pacific Ocean and the city. Perforations in the exterior walls allow light to filter into the galleries, mimicking the effect of sunlight through a forest canopy. The building’s low-slung, organic profile ensures that it remains a complementary addition to the natural landscape of the park.

    Apple Union Square

    300 Post St, San Francisco, CA 94108

    This landmark of kinetic architecture features two massive 42-foot-tall sliding glass doors that open the store to the street. It introduced the 'Genius Grove' concept, which places actual Ficus Alii trees within the retail space for a natural feel. A public plaza located at the rear of the building features a restored fountain by artist Ruth Asawa. The entire structure is powered by 100% renewable energy, including solar panels integrated into the roof. Its minimalist glass and steel design serves as a modern anchor for the historic retail district of Union Square.

    Mira Tower

    280 Spear St, San Francisco, CA 94105

    This 400-foot residential tower is easily identified by its twisting, geometric facade that spirals toward the sky. The architect utilized a sophisticated curtain wall system to create a modern interpretation of the classic San Francisco bay window. This unique design maximizes natural light and provides every resident with expansive views of the Bay Bridge. The building's high-density footprint serves as a sustainable solution for the growing needs of the Transbay district. It has quickly become a standout example of parametric design in the city's evolving skyline.

    Hyatt Regency San Francisco

    5 Embarcadero Center, San Francisco, CA 94111

    The building is famous for housing the world's largest lobby atrium, as recognized by the Guinness World Records. Its exterior is defined by a dramatic 45-degree sloping facade that creates a unique silhouette on the Embarcadero. Inside, the 17-story atrium acts as a 'concrete canyon' featuring futuristic glass elevators and monumental sculptures. It remains a premier example of the 1970s architectural trend toward grand, indoor urban spaces. The hotel's design allows for tiered balconies that offer guests a front-row view of the bustling lobby below.

    Grace Cathedral

    1100 California St, San Francisco, CA 94108

    This soaring French Gothic landmark on Nob Hill is actually constructed of reinforced concrete designed to resist seismic activity. It is famous for its 'Ghiberti doors,' which are exact replicas of the Gates of Paradise found in Florence. The cathedral houses two intricate labyrinths that visitors use for walking meditation and spiritual reflection. Stunning stained glass windows throughout the building depict both religious scenes and modern scientific achievements. As the seat of the Episcopal Bishop of California, it remains a central hub for community gatherings and musical performances.

  15. 15

    Golden Gate Bridge

    Golden Gate Bridge, San Francisco, CA 94129

    The most photographed bridge in the world is famous for its 'International Orange' color, which was originally intended as a primer. Its massive suspension cables contain enough individual wires to encircle the entire Earth three times over. Built during the Great Depression, the bridge was engineered to sway significantly to withstand high winds and tidal currents. The Art Deco styling of the towers and streetlamps reflects the aesthetic preferences of the 1930s era. It remains a vital transit link and a global symbol of American engineering and artistic vision.

  16. 16

    Lombard Street

    1000 Lombard St, San Francisco, CA 94133

    Known as the 'crookedest street in the world,' this landmark was redesigned in 1922 to solve a 27% grade issue. The eight sharp hairpin turns allow vehicles to safely navigate a hill that was previously too steep for cars. Beautifully maintained hydrangea gardens surround the red brick roadway, creating a lush and colorful environment. This project is a masterpiece of urban landscape architecture that transformed a functional necessity into a world-famous attraction. It continues to be one of the most visited sites in the city, offering views of Coit Tower and the Bay.

  17. 17

    Alcatraz Cellhouse

    Alcatraz Island, San Francisco, CA 94133

    Once the ultimate high-security federal prison, 'The Rock' was designed to be escape-proof due to the Bay's freezing currents. The main cellhouse was the largest reinforced concrete building in the world at the time of its completion. It famously housed notorious criminals like Al Capone and Robert 'The Birdman' Stroud during its operation. Today, the island serves as a haunting monument to the American justice system and a National Park. It is also a vital sanctuary for several species of nesting seabirds that inhabit the island’s rocky cliffs.

    The Castro Theatre

    429 Castro St, San Francisco, CA 94114

    This Spanish Colonial Baroque masterpiece is recognized by its massive neon sign that serves as the heart of the Castro District. It is famous for its ornate, 'tent-like' ceiling and grand Wurlitzer pipe organ that is played before screenings. The theatre has long served as a sanctuary for LGBTQ+ history and a hub for independent cinema culture. Its elaborate facade and interior murals reflect the theatrical opulence of the early 20th-century movie palaces. Today, it remains one of the few remaining large-scale single-screen cinemas in the United States.

    Oracle Park

    24 Willie Mays Plaza, San Francisco, CA 94107

    Widely considered the most beautiful ballpark in America, it is situated directly on the shores of the San Francisco Bay. Fans in kayaks frequently wait in 'McCovey Cove' to catch home run balls that splash into the water. The stadium’s architecture features classic brick facades that pay homage to the city's industrial and maritime history. Inside, the park offers a modern experience with a giant glove and Coca-Cola bottle playground in the outfield. It remains the home of the San Francisco Giants and a premier venue for major outdoor concerts.

    Japanese Tea Garden

    75 Hagiwara Tea Garden Dr, San Francisco, CA 94118

    Originally built for the 1894 World's Fair, this is the oldest public Japanese garden in the United States. It features a high-arched drum bridge, several pagodas, and a serene Zen rock garden for contemplation. The landscape was meticulously designed to provide a meditative escape within the larger Golden Gate Park. Native Japanese plants and carefully pruned trees create an authentic atmosphere that changes with the seasons. It stands as a masterpiece of traditional landscape architecture and a symbol of cultural exchange between Japan and America.

    City Lights Bookstore

    261 Columbus Ave, San Francisco, CA 94133

    Founded by poet Lawrence Ferlinghetti, this bookstore became the epicenter of the counter-cultural Beat Generation. It gained international fame during the Howl obscenity trial, which defended the right to publish controversial literature. The historic Italianate building features creaky wooden floors and hand-painted signs that preserve its bohemian spirit. It functions as both a retail shop and an independent publisher, continuing to champion progressive voices. Today, it is officially designated as a landmark and remains a site of pilgrimage for writers from around the world.

    The Exploratorium (Pier 15)

    Pier 15, The Embarcadero, San Francisco, CA 94111

    This massive former shipping pier was transformed into a world-renowned museum focused on science, art, and human perception. The renovation is a brilliant example of industrial reuse that maintains the original integrity of the waterfront structure. A highlight of the building is the glass-walled 'Observatory' located at the very end of the pier. This space offers visitors 360-degree views of the San Francisco Bay and the eastern span of the Bay Bridge. The facility itself acts as a living laboratory, utilizing the surrounding environment for various scientific exhibits.

    Sentinel Building (Columbus Tower)

    916 Kearny St, San Francisco, CA 94133

    This iconic flatiron building is easily recognized by its distinctive copper-green facade and rounded corner windows. It sits at a prominent intersection that marks the boundary between North Beach and the Financial District. The building is famously owned by filmmaker Francis Ford Coppola and serves as the home for his American Zoetrope studio. Its unique shape was designed to maximize the footprint of a narrow, triangular lot in the early 20th century. The ground floor houses a cafe that has become a popular gathering spot for locals and film enthusiasts alike.

    Levi’s Plaza

    1155 Battery St, San Francisco, CA 94111

    This complex serves as the global headquarters for Levi Strauss & Co. and is a model for corporate urban campus design. The red-brick structures pay homage to the city's industrial past while being integrated into a lush, park-like setting. A man-made stream runs through the plaza, providing a peaceful retreat for both employees and the general public. The architecture emphasizes human-scale design, offering a contrast to the dense glass towers of the nearby Financial District. It successfully blends office functionality with open green space on the city's scenic northern waterfront.
