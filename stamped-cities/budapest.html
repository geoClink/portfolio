The Pearl of the Danube

Budapest architecture

Budapest was formed in 1873 by the unification of three cities: Buda, Óbuda, and Pest, separated by the Danube River. The city is renowned for its thermal bath culture, with over 80 geothermal springs beneath the city feeding a network of historic bathhouses. The Hungarian Parliament Building, completed in 1904, is the third largest parliament building in the world.

Before you go

  • Fly intoBudapest Ferenc Liszt International (BUD)

    Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport is named after Hungary's most celebrated composer. Its Skycourt terminal, opened in 2011, features a distinctive wave-shaped roof and serves as one of Central Europe's busiest aviation hubs. The airport offers direct connections to over 100 destinations across Europe and beyond.

  • CurrencyHungarian Forint (Ft)
  • LanguageHungarian

    Hungarian (Magyar) is a Finno-Ugric language unrelated to any of its European neighbors, making it famously difficult for outsiders to learn. Despite this, English is widely spoken by younger Budapestians and throughout the tourism industry. Locals appreciate any attempt to say a few words in Magyar.

  • Getting aroundBKK Metro & Trams

    Budapest's metro Line 1 (the Millennium Underground) is the oldest electric underground railway in continental Europe, dating to 1896 and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The city's extensive tram network includes Line 4-6, one of the busiest tram lines in the world. A single BKK transit card covers all buses, trams, metro lines, and suburban HÉV trains.
