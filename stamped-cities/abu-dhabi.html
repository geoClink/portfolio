Land of the Gazelle

Abu Dhabi architecture

Legend says the city was founded after a gazelle led a wandering tribe to fresh water on an island. Today, it has transformed into a cultural powerhouse that balances its Bedouin heritage with futuristic landmarks like the Louvre Abu Dhabi and the Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque.

Before you go

  • Fly intoZayed International (AUH)

    The massive Terminal A features a distinctive X-shaped design that significantly reduces walking times for transit passengers. Its undulating roof is inspired by the rolling sand dunes of the desert, and the interior can accommodate up to 45 million travelers annually with ultra-modern biometric technology.

  • CurrencyUAE Dirham (د.إ)
  • LanguageArabic & English

    While Arabic is the official language, English is the primary bridge for the city's vast expat population. You will find that business and street signs are almost universally bilingual.

  • Getting aroundART & Buses

    Abu Dhabi utilizes high-tech, trackless 'Automated Rapid Transit' (ART) vehicles that look like a blend of a bus and a train. The city's bus network is entirely air-conditioned and features dedicated waiting shelters to protect riders from the heat. Regional buses also provide a high-frequency link to the neighboring city of Dubai.

  1. 01

    Apple Al Maryah Island

    The Galleria, Al Maryah Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This unique retail flagship sits atop a dramatic black granite pyramid water feature in the heart of Abu Dhabi. Water cascades down the sides of the base to provide a natural cooling effect for the surrounding promenade. Visitors access the store through a mirrored stainless steel portal and a stone-lined transitional hallway. The main floor features soaring glass walls that offer 360-degree views of the Al Maryah Island waterfront. It serves as a striking example of how modern retail design can integrate seamlessly with maritime surroundings.

  2. 02

    Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

    Sheikh Rashid Bin Saeed Street, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    As one of the world's largest mosques, this landmark features 82 domes and more than 1,000 marble columns. The exterior is clad in pure white Macedonian marble to symbolize peace and purity. Inside the main prayer hall, visitors can find the world's largest hand-knotted carpet and massive Swarovski chandeliers. The architecture masterfully blends Mamluk, Ottoman, and Fatimid styles into a single cohesive vision. Reflective pools surround the structure to amplify its grand silhouette during the evening hours.

  3. 03

    Louvre Abu Dhabi

    Saadiyat Cultural District, Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This 'museum city' in the sea is famous for its vast silver dome that spans 180 meters in diameter. The dome is composed of 7,850 unique metal stars layered in a complex geometric pattern. When sunlight passes through these layers, it creates a magical 'rain of light' effect on the galleries below. This design was specifically inspired by the overlapping palm fronds found in traditional desert oases. The museum hosts a massive collection of global art that bridges the gap between Eastern and Western cultures.

  4. 04

    Capital Gate

    Al Khaleej Al Arabi Street, Al Rawdah, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    Known as the Leaning Tower of Abu Dhabi, this skyscraper holds the record for the world's furthest leaning man-made tower. The structure intentionally inclines 18 degrees westward, which is four times the lean of the Tower of Pisa. It utilizes a pre-cambered core and a complex diagrid structure to manage the extreme gravitational pressures. The facade is made of more than 12,000 individual glass panes to maintain its curved organic shape. It stands as a pinnacle of modern engineering and a focal point of the city's exhibition district.

  5. 05

    Aldar Headquarters

    Al Raha, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This building is celebrated as the first circular skyscraper in the Middle East region. It is designed as a giant disc-shaped volume standing vertically on its edge. The striking shape was achieved through the use of a steel diagrid that eliminates the need for any internal columns. Architects intended the circular form to symbolize unity, intellectual stability, and eternity. It serves as a prominent corporate landmark located along the Al Raha coastal development.

  6. 06

    Emirates Palace

    West Corniche Road, Al Ras Al Akhdar, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    The Emirates Palace is a world-renowned luxury hotel that serves as a marvel of traditional Arabian architecture. The structure features 114 individual domes, with the central dome rising significantly above the main entrance. Its interior is lavishly decorated with gold leaf, mother-of-pearl, and expansive marble surfaces. The building is surrounded by over 1,000 hectares of meticulously landscaped gardens and fountains. It remains one of the most expensive and prestigious hotels ever constructed in the world.

  7. 07

    Etihad Towers (Tower 2)

    West Corniche, Al Bateen, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This complex consists of five sleek towers that define the modern skyline of the Abu Dhabi Corniche. Tower 2 is the tallest in the group and houses a famous observation deck on the 74th floor. The curved glass facades are engineered to reflect the changing colors of the Arabian Gulf throughout the day. Residents and visitors can enjoy panoramic views of the Presidential Palace and the surrounding islands. The towers serve as a mixed-use hub for luxury residential living, high-end office space, and retail.

  8. 08

    W Abu Dhabi - Yas Island

    Yas Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This hotel is globally famous for being the only hospitality structure built directly over a Formula 1 race track. Its most defining feature is the massive Grid Shell that arches over the race circuit. This shell consists of over 5,000 pivoting glass panels equipped with a custom LED lighting system. At night, the entire building can change colors to create a spectacular visual show during racing events. The design successfully integrates futuristic aesthetics with the high-energy environment of international motorsport.

  9. 09

    Al Bahr Towers

    Al Saada Street, Al Dhafrah, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    The Al Bahr Towers are famous for their dynamic, computer-controlled 'intelligent' facade system. Each tower is covered in 2,000 umbrella-like elements that open and close based on the sun's position. This modern interpretation of the traditional 'Mashrabiya' lattice reduces interior solar heat gain by 50 percent. By automating the shading, the building significantly minimizes the energy required for air conditioning. It stands as a global benchmark for sustainable skyscraper design in extreme desert climates.

  10. 10

    Qasr Al Hosn

    Rashid Bin Saeed Al Maktoum Street, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    Qasr Al Hosn is the oldest stone building in Abu Dhabi, dating back to a watchtower built in 1761. The structure was originally built from coral and sea stones to protect the city's only freshwater well. It eventually evolved into a fortified palace that served as the home for the ruling Al Nahyan family. Following a meticulous restoration, the fort now serves as a museum and cultural heart of the nation. It provides a striking historical contrast to the glass skyscrapers that now surround the site.

    Abrahamic Family House

    Saadiyat Cultural District, Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This multifaith complex houses a mosque, a church, and a synagogue within a single shared site. Each of the three identical cubic volumes is distinguished by a unique architectural skin. The mosque uses delicate lattice, the church features vertical pillars, and the synagogue uses crisscrossing V-shapes. This design creates a powerful visual statement regarding peaceful coexistence and shared human values. The central courtyard serves as a common ground where visitors of all faiths can meet and interact.

    Siemens Middle East HQ (Masdar City)

    Masdar City, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This building serves as a global benchmark for sustainable and energy-efficient office design in the desert. The structure is raised off the ground to create a shaded public plaza for employees and visitors. It utilizes a highly efficient box-within-a-box facade system to drastically reduce thermal heat gain. The architecture specifically captures cooling breezes to naturally ventilate the open communal spaces. It is the architectural centerpiece of Masdar City's mission to create a zero-carbon urban environment.

  13. 13

    Qasr Al Watan

    Al Ras Al Akhdar, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    Qasr Al Watan is the official Presidential Palace and a major cultural landmark for the United Arab Emirates. The palace's architecture is a grand tribute to Arabian artistry, featuring white granite and limestone facades. Its central dome is one of the largest in the world and serves as a focal point for the Great Hall. While it houses the working offices of the President, large portions are open for public exploration. The interior is filled with intricate geometric patterns and a massive library dedicated to Arab history.

    Guggenheim Abu Dhabi (Upcoming)

    Saadiyat Cultural District, Saadiyat Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This colossal museum ensemble features a collection of cones and cubes that redefine the coastal landscape. The distinctive cones are inspired by traditional Barjeel wind towers once used to cool desert homes. Upon its expected completion in 2025, it will become the largest Guggenheim museum in the world. The structure is surrounded by water on three sides to create a dramatic peninsula on Saadiyat Island. It is designed to be a premier global destination for modern and contemporary art.

    Warner Bros. World Abu Dhabi

    Yas Island, Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates

    This structure is recognized as the world's largest indoor theme park, covering a massive climate-controlled footprint. The interior features a permanent twilight sky that creates a consistent atmosphere for the themed lands. Its exterior is a clean and golden-hued rectangular volume that acts as a canvas for the iconic shield logo. This development represents a major investment in large-scale indoor entertainment to counter the extreme summer heat. It houses dozens of rides and attractions themed after famous cinematic and animated characters.
