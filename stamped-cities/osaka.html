The Nation's Kitchen

Osaka architecture

Osaka is Japan's undisputed culinary capital, famous for a local culture of kuidaore — eating until you drop. Its signature street foods, takoyaki (octopus balls) and okonomiyaki (savory pancakes), are eaten at standing stalls throughout the city. Osaka Castle, originally built by warlord Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1583, played a central role in Japan's unification and remains one of the country's most visited historical landmarks.

Before you go

  • Fly intoKansai International Airport (KIX)

    Kansai International Airport is a feat of engineering built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay. When the island began sinking faster than expected after construction, engineers had to jack up the terminal to compensate — it has been raised multiple times since opening in 1994. A second runway was added on a second artificial island, and the airport now handles tens of millions of passengers annually.

  • CurrencyJapanese Yen (¥)
  • LanguageJapanese

    Osaka's local dialect — Kansai-ben (or Osaka-ben) — is arguably the most recognized regional dialect in Japan, known for its distinctive rhythm and comedic energy. While standard Japanese is understood everywhere, Osakans are proud of their dialect and use it in daily life. The city is the birthplace of much of Japan's popular stand-up comedy (manzai), and you will often notice the wit and warmth in everyday conversations.

  • Getting aroundOsaka Metro & JR Loop

    Osaka has one of the world's most efficient subway systems, with 9 color-coded Osaka Metro lines forming a dense grid across the city. The JR Osaka Loop Line circles the city center much like Tokyo's Yamanote Line. A single ICOCA card covers Osaka Metro, JR lines, and Hankyu and Hanshin private railways, making navigation remarkably seamless.
