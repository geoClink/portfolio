City of Gardens

Victoria architecture

Victoria is home to Fan Tan Alley, the narrowest street in Canada, located in the heart of the oldest Chinatown in the country. It is famous for its mild climate and stunning floral displays, earned through its 'City of Gardens' nickname and the world-famous Butchart Gardens.

Before you go

  • Fly intoVictoria International (YYJ)

    While Victoria International handles most regional flights, the city is also a world hub for seaplanes that land directly in the Inner Harbour. This unique 'water airport' allows travelers to step off a plane and immediately be in the center of the city's historic parliament and museum district.

  • CurrencyCanadian Dollar ($)
  • LanguageEnglish

    English is the main language, often described as having a more traditional or 'British' influence compared to the rest of Canada. This is reflected in the city's formal etiquette and the long-standing local tradition of afternoon tea.

  • Getting aroundBC Transit

    Victoria was the first city in North America to implement a fleet of double-decker buses for regular transit. The BC Ferry system is a vital link, connecting the island city to mainland Vancouver. Harbor Ferries offer a 'water taxi' style of transit for short trips across the Victoria Inner Harbour.

  1. 01

    British Columbia Parliament Buildings

    501 Belleville St, Victoria, BC V8V 1X4

    This building serves as the seat of the Legislative Assembly of British Columbia. This Neo-baroque masterpiece features a massive central dome topped with a gold-leaf statue of Captain Vancouver. At night, the building is famously outlined by over 3,000 light bulbs to create a glowing silhouette. This lighting tradition began in 1897 to celebrate Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee and continues today. The expansive front lawns host major civic celebrations and protests, acting as the province's primary public square.

  2. 02

    The Fairmont Empress

    721 Government St, Victoria, BC V8W 1W5

    This is one of Canada's oldest and most iconic grand railway hotels. Its ivy-covered stone walls and distinctive chateau-style rooflines define the aesthetic of Victoria’s Inner Harbour. The building was designed to resemble a French chateau to attract wealthy travelers of the Edwardian era. It has hosted royalty, world leaders, and celebrities for well over a century. The hotel remains globally famous for its traditional afternoon tea service held in the elegant lobby lounge.

  3. 03

    Craigdarroch Castle

    1050 Joan Crescent, Victoria, BC V8S 3L5

    This 'bonanza castle' was originally built for the Scottish coal baron Robert Dunsmuir. This grand Victorian mansion features thirty-nine rooms and spans over twenty thousand square feet. It is famous for its intricate woodwork and one of North America's finest collections of Victorian-era stained glass. The castle sits on a prominent hill overlooking the city as a symbol of late-19th-century industrial wealth. Visitors can climb the eighty-seven steps of the grand oak staircase to reach the tower for panoramic views.

  4. 04

    Royal BC Museum

    675 Belleville St, Victoria, BC V8W 9W2

    This modernist cultural hub houses the vast natural and human history of the province. Its brutalist-inspired concrete exterior protects immersive galleries that transport visitors through time. A highlight of the museum is the meticulously recreated 19th-century 'Old Town' with its cobblestone streets. The site also includes the sleek and modern IMAX Victoria theatre wing for cinematic educational experiences. It stands as a primary research institution and the most visited museum in British Columbia.

  5. 05

    Fan Tan Alley (Building Complex)

    Fan Tan Alley, Victoria, BC V8W 1W4

    This historic brick complex is located at the heart of Canada's oldest Chinatown. It is world-famous for being the narrowest commercial street in all of North America. Originally, this district was a hidden hub for gambling houses and opium dens in the late 1800s. The red-brick architecture and traditional 'moon gates' have been preserved to maintain their historic character. Today, the alley houses local boutiques, record shops, and art galleries that cater to tourists and locals alike.

    Victoria Public Library (Central)

    735 Broughton St, Victoria, BC V8W 3H2

    The Central Branch features a striking glass-walled atrium that brings natural light deep into the building. This design focuses on transparency and accessibility to create a welcoming civic atmosphere. It acts as a modern anchor for the downtown core, serving thousands of patrons daily. The quiet reading rooms provide a stark contrast to the busy streetscape visible through the windows. It remains a vital community resource for both digital access and traditional literature collection.

  7. 07

    St. Andrew’s Cathedral

    740 View St, Victoria, BC V8W 1J8

    This High Victorian Gothic structure was heavily inspired by medieval European cathedrals. It is particularly famous for its asymmetrical design and two towers of different heights. The interior features vibrant stained glass windows and a massive, powerful pipe organ. This building reflects the religious architecture of 19th-century Quebec brought to the Pacific coast. It serves as the cathedral for the Roman Catholic Diocese of Victoria and remains an active place of worship.

  8. 08

    Hatley Castle

    2005 Sooke Rd, Victoria, BC V9B 5Y2

    This massive manor is located on the picturesque Royal Roads University campus in Colwood. It is a stunning example of Edwardian architecture and is surrounded by meticulously kept formal gardens. The castle was originally built for James Dunsmuir, who served as the Lieutenant Governor of British Columbia. Pop culture fans may recognize the exterior as 'Xavier’s School for Gifted Youngsters' from the X-Men films. It currently serves as an administrative center while offering heritage tours to the general public.

  9. 09

    The Customs House

    816 Government St, Victoria, BC V8W 1W9

    This site is a major heritage rehabilitation project located directly on the Victoria Inner Harbour. The original French Renaissance-style facade was meticulously preserved during the building's recent transformation. This historic shell was integrated into new ultra-luxury residences that offer modern waterfront living. It stands as a prime example of Victoria's 'facadism' approach to urban development and historic preservation. The building continues to bridge the gap between the city's maritime past and its affluent future.

    Mayfair Shopping Centre

    3147 Douglas St, Victoria, BC V8Z 6E3

    This retail hub represents Victoria's modern shift toward minimalist glass and steel architecture. While the city lacks a standalone flagship 'Glass Cube,' this center features many stores with that aesthetic. The storefronts utilize floor-to-ceiling glass and open-concept interiors to create a contemporary shopping experience. It serves as the primary fashion and technology destination for the Greater Victoria region. Recent expansions have emphasized natural light and clean lines to reflect modern retail design trends.

  11. 11

    Christ Church Cathedral

    930 Burdett Ave, Victoria, BC V8V 3G8

    This is one of Canada's largest churches, designed to mirror 13th-century French Gothic cathedrals. The building is constructed from massive stone blocks and features a stunningly detailed rose window. It is renowned for its exceptional acoustics, making it a favorite for choral performances. The cathedral's imposing presence on a hill makes it a landmark in the city's skyline. It serves as the mother church of the Anglican Diocese of British Columbia and the Islands.

  12. 12

    Victoria City Hall

    1 Centennial Square, Victoria, BC V8W 1P6

    This building is a charming example of Second Empire architecture in Western Canada. It is easily recognizable by its ornate mansard roof and prominent central clock tower. As one of the few remaining 19th-century civic buildings in the region, it holds significant historical value. The structure has undergone several expansions while maintaining its original Victorian architectural integrity. It remains the functional heart of the city's municipal government and Centennial Square's primary focus.

  13. 13

    The Janion Building

    1612 Store St, Victoria, BC V8W 1V3

    This building was originally constructed as a luxury hotel for steamship travelers arriving in the city. After sitting derelict for many decades, it was recently converted into innovative 'micro-lofts.' The renovation successfully preserved the historic red-brick facade on the street-facing side of the block. A modern glass-and-steel addition was added to the waterfront side to increase the residential footprint. This project has won several awards for its successful approach to heritage conservation and urban density.

    The Bay Centre

    1150 Douglas St, Victoria, BC V8W 3M9

It features a massive glass-roofed atrium that creates a light-filled environment for shoppers. The center acts as a vital retail link between the Inner Harbour and the Douglas Street corridor. It remains a central meeting point for locals and a major destination for international tourists.

  15. 15

    Market Square

    560 Johnson St, Victoria, BC V8W 3C6

    This is a collection of late-19th-century brick warehouses surrounding a central open-air courtyard. Once a bustling center for the city's merchants, it was rehabilitated in the 1970s for public use. The renovation preserved the original timber beams and stone foundations to keep its industrial character. Today, it serves as an independent shopping and dining hub featuring local breweries and boutiques. The courtyard is a popular spot for live music and community festivals throughout the summer months.
