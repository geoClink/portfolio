City of 1000 Minarets

Cairo architecture

Cairo is the largest city in the Arab world and the only one that still hosts an original 'Wonder of the Ancient World'—the Great Pyramid of Giza. Its skyline is a dense forest of historic minarets, earning it a nickname that reflects its status as a historic center of Islamic architecture.

Before you go

  • Fly intoCairo International (CAI)

    The airport's Terminal 3 is a modern glass facility designed to reflect Egypt’s transition into a modern era while incorporating subtle Pharaonic motifs. It serves as the primary gateway for the millions of tourists who flock to see the treasures of the Nile and the new Grand Egyptian Museum.

  • CurrencyEgyptian Pound (E£)
  • LanguageArabic

    Egyptian Arabic is the local dialect and is uniquely understood across the entire Arab world due to Egypt's influential film industry. English is common in tourist areas and among professional circles.

  • Getting aroundCairo Metro

    The Cairo Metro was the first full metro system built on the African continent and is a vital lifeline for the city. The system includes dedicated cars specifically for women to ensure comfort during crowded rush hours. A new monorail system is currently under construction to connect the city center to the New Administrative Capital.

15 landmarks in Cairo

  1. 01

    Great Pyramid of Giza

    Al Haram, Giza Governorate 3512201, Egypt

    The Great Pyramid of Giza is the oldest and largest of the three pyramids in the Giza complex. Built as a monumental tomb for Pharaoh Khufu, it stood as the tallest man-made structure in the world for over 3,800 years. This engineering marvel is comprised of roughly 2.3 million stone blocks, each weighing several tons on average. Its precise alignment to true north demonstrates the advanced astronomical and mathematical knowledge of the Ancient Egyptians. Today, it remains the only survivor of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World and draws millions of visitors annually.

  2. 02

    Cairo Tower

    Gezira Island, Zamalek, Cairo Governorate 4272002, Egypt

    This free-standing concrete tower features a distinctive lattice-work design intended to resemble a blooming lotus plant. For half a century, it held the record as the tallest structure in North Africa and remains an icon of Egyptian modernism. The tower's exterior is covered in a delicate mesh of granite and concrete that provides both structural support and aesthetic beauty. It features a revolving restaurant and an observation deck at the top that offers 360-degree views of the Nile and the Pyramids. Strategically located on Gezira Island, it serves as a central orientation point for the entire city of Cairo.

  3. 03

    Mosque of Muhammad Ali

    Al Abageyah, El Khalifa, Cairo Governorate 4252360, Egypt

    Situated majestically atop the Citadel of Cairo, this Ottoman mosque is one of the most visible landmarks in the city. It is frequently referred to as the Alabaster Mosque due to the extensive use of that stone on its interior and exterior walls. The mosque's twin needle-like minarets are the highest in Egypt, reaching deep into the skyline of Old Cairo. Its grand central dome is surrounded by four semi-domes, creating a vast and light-filled interior prayer hall. From its terrace, visitors can enjoy one of the most expansive views of the city, often stretching all the way to Giza.

  4. 04

    Baron Empain Palace

    El-Orouba St, Heliopolis, Cairo Governorate 4460303, Egypt

    Inspired by the Hindu temple of Angkor Wat, this unique palace in Heliopolis is a standout example of eclectic architecture. The structure was built using reinforced concrete and features elaborate exterior carvings of mythical creatures, Shiva, and Vishnu. It was commissioned by a Belgian industrialist who helped develop the surrounding neighborhood into a modern suburb. Local legends of hauntings and secret underground tunnels have surrounded the building for decades, adding to its mysterious allure. Following a major restoration, the palace now serves as a historical museum detailing the development of Heliopolis.

  5. 05

    Grand Egyptian Museum

    Cairo-Alexandria Desert Rd, Kafr Nassar, Al Haram, Giza Governorate 3513204, Egypt

    The Grand Egyptian Museum is currently the largest archaeological museum in the world dedicated to a single civilization. Its chamfered triangle design is a modern architectural echo of the ancient pyramids that stand just kilometers away. The massive glass facade provides a transparent link between the treasures inside and the historical landscape of the Giza plateau. One of its primary highlights is the first-ever complete display of the Tutankhamun collection in its entirety. The museum functions as a massive cultural hub, featuring research centers, conservation labs, and expansive public gardens.

  6. 06

    Al-Azhar Mosque

    El-Darb El-Ahmar, Cairo Governorate 4252030, Egypt

    Established in 972, Al-Azhar was the first mosque founded in the city and eventually became one of the world's oldest universities. Its architecture is a beautiful palimpsest of Fatimid, Mamluk, and Ottoman styles added over a millennium. The mosque features a vast marble-paved courtyard that serves as a serene transition space from the busy streets of Islamic Cairo. Five distinct minarets rise above the complex, each representing a different era of Egyptian Islamic history. It remains a global center for Islamic learning and a cornerstone of the city's spiritual and educational identity.

  7. 07

    The Mogamma

    Tahrir Square, Downtown Cairo, Cairo Governorate 4251001, Egypt

    The Mogamma is a massive administrative building that has long served as the heart of Egyptian bureaucracy in Tahrir Square. Its curved facade and repetitive window patterns are characteristic of mid-century Modernist and Brutalist government architecture. For decades, it was the place where every Egyptian citizen had to go to process official government paperwork. The building is currently undergoing a significant transformation to be repurposed into a high-end luxury hotel. This change represents a major shift in the urban planning of Downtown Cairo as government functions move to the new capital.

  8. 08

    Bab Zuwayla

    Al-Muizz li-Din Allah St, El-Darb El-Ahmar, Cairo Governorate 4252010, Egypt

    Bab Zuwayla is one of the three remaining gates in the fortified walls of the Old City of Cairo. Its two massive semi-circular towers were once used as watchpoints and can still be climbed for stunning views over Al-Muizz Street. Historically, the gate was a site for royal ceremonies and, more notoriously, the place where public executions were carried out. The architecture is a prime example of Fatimid military engineering, featuring heavy stone construction and arched entryways. Today, it serves as a symbolic entrance to the historic district and a popular landmark for those exploring Islamic Cairo.

  9. 09

    The Hanging Church

    Kom Ghorab, Old Cairo, Cairo Governorate 4243101, Egypt

    Formally known as Saint Virgin Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church, this site is built atop the gatehouse of the ancient Babylon Fortress. The church is called 'Hanging' because its nave is suspended over a passage, supported by the fortress's bastions. Inside, the unique wooden roof is shaped like Noah’s Ark, a significant symbolic element in Coptic architectural tradition. It houses a 13th-century marble pulpit featuring 13 pillars that represent Jesus Christ and his twelve Apostles. As one of the oldest churches in Egypt, it remains a vital center for the Coptic community and a key stop in Coptic Cairo.

  10. 10

    Cairo Opera House

    Opera Square, Gezira Island, Zamalek, Cairo Governorate 4272010, Egypt

    The Cairo Opera House was a gift from Japan to Egypt, replacing the historic Khedivial Opera House that burned down in the 1970s. This postmodern complex blends traditional Islamic architectural motifs with the latest in modern theatrical technology. It serves as the primary venue for performing arts in Egypt, hosting world-class ballet, opera, and orchestral performances. The building's white facade and geometric shapes create a serene cultural oasis on the southern tip of Gezira Island. Its multi-theater design allows for various cultural events to happen simultaneously, fostering a vibrant local arts scene.

  11. 11

    Iconic Tower

    Central Business District, New Administrative Capital, Cairo 11865, Egypt

    The Iconic Tower stands as the architectural centerpiece of Egypt's New Administrative Capital. It currently holds the title of the tallest building in Africa, surpassing all other skyscrapers on the continent. The design is inspired by the form of an ancient Pharaonic obelisk, featuring a sleek glass exterior that tapers as it rises. It serves a mixed-use purpose, housing luxury offices, a high-end hotel, and an observation deck with views of the expanding desert city. The tower represents Egypt's modern urban vision and the relocation of the country's central government functions.

  13. 13

    Abdeen Palace

    Moustafa Kamel Square, Abdeen, Cairo Governorate 4251201, Egypt

    Abdeen Palace is widely considered one of the most sumptuous and historically significant palaces in the world. Originally commissioned by Khedive Ismail, it was built to replace the Citadel as the official seat of the Egyptian government. The palace features over 500 suites decorated with exquisite Italian, French, and Turkish artwork and furnishings. It houses several specialized museums, including a massive collection of royal weapons and historical clocks. Today, while it serves as a primary workplace for the President of Egypt, large portions of it remain open as a national museum.

  14. 14

    Mosque-Madrasa of Sultan Hassan

    Al-Qala'a St, El-Khalifa, Cairo Governorate 4252301, Egypt

    The Mosque-Madrasa of Sultan Hassan is regarded by many as the finest example of Mamluk architecture in existence. Its massive scale was unprecedented for the 14th century, featuring a soaring entrance portal that remains one of the tallest in the city. The interior courtyard is surrounded by four grand vaulted halls, known as iwans, each dedicated to one of the four Sunni schools of law. Its intricate stone carvings and marble mosaics demonstrate the peak of Islamic craftsmanship during the Mamluk period. It stands directly opposite the newer Al-Rifa'i Mosque, creating a dramatic architectural dialogue in the heart of Old Cairo.

  15. 15

    Museum of Egyptian Antiquities (The Red Museum)

    Tahrir Square, Ismailia, Qasr el-Nil, Cairo Governorate 4251002, Egypt

    This iconic red Neoclassical building has been the face of Egyptology since it first opened its doors at the turn of the 20th century. Designed by French architect Marcel Dourgnon, it was the first purpose-built museum for ancient artifacts in the Middle East. Even as newer museums open, this building remains a masterpiece of European architecture in the center of Cairo. The museum's vast galleries once housed over 120,000 items, ranging from tiny amulets to massive royal statues. It remains a critical cultural landmark in Tahrir Square and continues to host significant portions of Egypt's historical legacy.
