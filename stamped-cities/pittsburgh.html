The Steel City

Pittsburgh architecture

Pittsburgh is known as the 'City of Bridges' because it has 446 of them—more than Venice, Italy. Once the center of the global steel industry, it has reinvented itself as a leader in healthcare, education, and robotics, all while maintaining its rugged, hilly charm.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoPittsburgh Int'l (PIT)

        The airport was the first in the world designed as an 'Airside Mall,' a concept that revolutionized the way travelers shop and dine while waiting for flights. It is currently undergoing a 'Terminal Modernization Program' to create a new, sustainable landside facility that reflects the region's natural beauty.

      • CurrencyUS Dollar ($)
      • LanguageEnglish

        The city is home to one of the most unique dialects in America, known as 'Pittsburghese,' famously featuring the second-person plural pronoun 'Yinz.' Visitors will find the local talk to be friendly and unpretentious, reflecting the blue-collar pride of its industrial roots.

      • Getting aroundThe T / Inclines

        The light rail system, known as 'The T,' includes an underground portion in the downtown district. Historic funiculars like the Duquesne Incline still scale the city's steep hills as part of the daily transit. A network of dedicated busways allows express buses to bypass traffic during peak commute hours.

      1. 01

        PPG Place

        1 PPG Pl, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

        A complex of six glass buildings designed to resemble a 'crystal cathedral.' The main tower features 231 glass spires and is clad in over one million square feet of silver-reflective glass. Its design is a postmodern tribute to the Gothic Victoria Tower in London. The central plaza features a popular ice rink in the winter and a choreographed fountain in the summer. This iconic silhouette remains one of the most recognizable features of the Pittsburgh skyline.

      2. 02

        Cathedral of Learning

        4200 Fifth Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15260

        The centerpiece of the University of Pittsburgh and the tallest educational building in the Western Hemisphere. This Late Gothic Revival skyscraper is famous for its 'Nationality Rooms' representing various global cultures. The three-story high Commons Room at the base features soaring stone arches built without any steel support. It serves as a functional academic hub and a monument to the city's immigrant heritage. Students and visitors alike gather here to study in an atmosphere resembling a medieval castle.

      3. 03

        The Gulf Tower

        707 Grant St, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        Modeled after the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, this Art Deco masterpiece was once the headquarters of Gulf Oil. It is most famous for its stepped pyramid roof, which serves as a weather beacon. The lights at the top change color to indicate temperature, precipitation, humidity, and wind speed. This system acts as a giant barometer for the downtown area and the surrounding neighborhoods. The building's limestone facade and elegant proportions make it a standout example of early 20th-century skyscraper design.

      4. 04

        Fallingwater

        1491 Mill Run Rd, Mill Run, PA 15464

        Located just south of the city, this is arguably the most famous private residence in the world. It is built directly over a waterfall on Bear Run, utilizing cantilevered concrete trays that echo natural rock. It is a defining example of 'Organic Architecture,' where the building is designed to be an extension of nature. The interior spaces blur the lines between indoor living and the lush surrounding forest. Today it is preserved as a museum and holds a prestigious spot on the UNESCO World Heritage list.

      5. 05

        Smithfield United Church

        623 Smithfield St, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

        One of the most unique structural designs in the city, this church features a soaring aluminum spire. It was one of the first buildings in the world to use aluminum as a primary structural material. This design choice highlighted Pittsburgh’s status as the global home of the Aluminum Company of America. The interior features beautiful stained glass and intricate woodwork that creates a serene atmosphere. The structure stands as a testament to the intersection of industrial innovation and religious architecture.

      6. 06

        Union Trust Building

        501 Grant St, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        Originally built by industrialist Henry Clay Frick, this Flemish Gothic structure is famous for its two tiered towers. The building’s roof is covered in intricate terra cotta dormers that catch the light at different angles. After a massive restoration, the interior rotunda was returned to its original glory with a stained-glass dome. It features a grand circular balcony system that provides a dramatic view of the central atrium. Today, it serves as a premier mixed-use space combining high-end offices with retail and dining.

      7. 07

        Alcoa Building

        611 William Penn Pl, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        A pioneer of the 'curtain wall' skyscraper, this building was the first in the world clad entirely in aluminum. The windows are uniquely rounded to look like those on an airplane for a futuristic aesthetic. These windows rotate 360 degrees so they can be cleaned safely from the interior of the building. It was designed to showcase aluminum as a viable, lightweight alternative to steel for urban construction projects. The structure has since been converted to residential use, proving its adaptability for modern living.

      8. 08

        The Tower at PNC Plaza

        300 Fifth Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

        Designed to be the 'greenest office tower in the world,' this building features a double-skin facade for ventilation. In 'breathable' mode, the windows open to allow fresh air to circulate throughout the workspace. This innovative design significantly reduces the building's overall dependency on traditional air conditioning systems. It also features an 800-square-foot vertical 'living wall' near the ground level for biophilic benefits. The tower represents a major step forward in sustainable high-rise architecture for the 21st century.

      9. 09

        Allegheny County Courthouse

        436 Grant St, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        This structure is considered by many architects to be the finest example of Romanesque Revival in the country. The massive granite building features a heavy, fortress-like exterior and a famous 'Bridge of Sighs.' This bridge historically connected the courthouse to the former jail located across the street. Its rhythmic arches and massive clock tower influenced a generation of American civic architecture following its completion. The central courtyard offers a quiet, stony sanctuary in the heart of the busy downtown district.

      10. 10

        Heinz Hall

        600 Penn Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

        Originally the Lowe’s Penn Theatre, this lavish movie palace was saved from demolition and beautifully converted. The interior is a riot of gold leaf, silk tapestries, and crystal chandeliers that evoke old-world elegance. It features a stunning three-story grand lobby that welcomes guests to world-class musical performances. A lush garden plaza was added later to serve as the cultural heart of the downtown district. Today it serves as the home of the Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra and hosts various touring Broadway shows.

        Apple Shadyside

        5436 Walnut St, Pittsburgh, PA 15232

        Located in the historic Shadyside neighborhood, this store occupies a two-story space that previously housed a Gap. Unlike modern glass cubes, this location features an adaptive reuse of a traditional brick storefront. The interior follows a modern 'town square' design utilizing warm oak wood and indoor trees. A sophisticated stone facade ensures the building blends seamlessly into the upscale Walnut Street shopping district. It represents Apple's effort to integrate high-tech retail into established, pedestrian-friendly urban environments.

      12. 12

        U.S. Steel Tower

        600 Grant St, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        The tallest building in Pittsburgh stands as a monument to the city's vast industrial heritage. It is famous for its unique triangular shape and the use of 'Cor-Ten' weathering steel. This specialized steel developed a protective layer of dark brown rust to prevent further corrosion over time. Its 18 massive exterior columns are hollow and filled with a water-antifreeze mixture for fire protection. This engineering feat eliminated the need for traditional concrete casing on the exterior supports.

      13. 13

        Frick Building

        437 Grant St, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        Built by industrialist Henry Clay Frick, this tower was designed to overshadow the neighboring building of a rival. The lobby is exceptionally opulent, featuring white Italian marble and bronze lions guarding the entrance. A stunning stained-glass window by John LaFarge provides a colorful focal point in the grand interior space. Interestingly, the original basement became the main entrance after the city lowered the surrounding streets in 1912. It remains a prime example of Neo-Classical style and a reminder of Gilded Age competition.

      14. 14

        Benedum Center for the Performing Arts

        237 7th St, Pittsburgh, PA 15222

        Originally opened as the Stanley Theatre, it was once one of the largest movie houses in the nation. The careful restoration preserved the lavish interior, including a signature 4,700-pound crystal chandelier. Extensive use of aluminum leafing throughout the theater serves as a nod to Pittsburgh's industrial history. It now stands as the premiere venue for the city's prestigious opera and ballet companies. This building is a vital anchor for the Cultural District and hosts numerous high-profile entertainment events.

      15. 15

        Koppers Building

        436 Seventh Ave, Pittsburgh, PA 15219

        A crowning jewel of Pittsburgh’s skyline, this building is most recognizable for its chateau-style copper roof. The roof has oxidized to a distinctive green and is beautifully illuminated at night to highlight its shape. The interior lobby features three different types of marble and intricate geometric metalwork from the era. It remains one of the best-preserved examples of 1920s commercial Art Deco architecture in the country. This structure stands as a lasting symbol of the city's economic strength during the early 20th century.
