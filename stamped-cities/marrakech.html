The Red City

Marrakech architecture

By law, every building in Marrakech must be painted in shades of salmon-pink or ochre to match the historic red sandstone of its 12th-century walls. This creates a stunning, uniform glow at sunset, particularly in the bustling Jemaa el-Fnaa square, where storytellers and snake charmers gather.

      Before you go

      • Fly intoMenara Airport (RAK)

        Menara Airport is often cited as one of the most beautiful in the world, featuring a terminal that blends modern glass with intricate Arabesque geometric patterns. The design allows natural light to filter through the facade, casting beautiful shadows that reflect the traditional 'mashrabiya' woodwork of Morocco.

      • CurrencyMoroccan Dirham (د.م.)
      • LanguageArabic & French

        The linguistic landscape is a rich mix of Moroccan Arabic (Darija), Berber, and French. While Arabic is official, French is widely used in hospitality and by the city's many international residents.

      • Getting aroundALSA Electric

        Marrakech has introduced high-tech electric buses for its main routes to promote environmental sustainability. Local 'Petit Taxis' are a popular way to move within the city, with each car required to be the signature ochre color. Horse-drawn 'Caleches' remain a traditional and scenic way to tour the historic city walls and the Medina.

      8 landmarks in Marrakech

      1. 01

        Koutoubia Mosque

        Avenue Mohammed V, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        The spiritual heart of Marrakech is dominated by its iconic 12th-century minaret that rises high above the Medina. This sandstone masterpiece famously served as the architectural blueprint for the Giralda in Seville and the Hassan Tower in Rabat. The exterior is crafted from distinct pink sandstone and features four decorative copper globes atop its pointed spire. Local law strictly dictates that no other building in the city may surpass the height of this sacred and historic minaret. It remains the most important guiding landmark for locals and travelers navigating the winding, labyrinthine streets of the old city.

      2. 02

        Musée Yves Saint Laurent

        Rue Yves Saint Laurent, Guéliz, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        This contemporary museum stands as a stunning tribute to the legendary French fashion designer's creative legacy and love for Morocco. The exterior facade features intricate, lace-like brickwork designed to mimic the delicate weave and texture of high-fashion fabric. Inside, the modern design utilizes Moroccan terrazzo and local marble to create a sophisticated and minimalist atmosphere for visitors. The building successfully blends French haute couture aesthetics with a distinct North African soul and architectural language. It has quickly become a premier global destination for both modern architecture enthusiasts and international fashion history lovers.

      3. 03

        Bahia Palace

        Avenue Imam El Ghazali, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        Constructed in the late 19th century, this palace was intended to be the most brilliant and massive architectural feat of its time. The sprawling layout includes 150 different rooms connected by a series of grand, sun-drenched courtyards and marble-lined plazas. Every surface is adorned with intricate Zellige tilework and hand-painted cedar wood ceilings that define the pinnacle of Islamic art. It serves as a masterpiece of the Moroccan style, showcasing the immense wealth and power of the Grand Vizier. Visitors can wander through the lush harem gardens and admire the mathematical precision of the deeply carved stucco walls.

      4. 04

        Ben Youssef Madrasa

        Place Ben Youssef, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        Once the largest Islamic college in North Africa, this 16th-century site represents the absolute pinnacle of Moroccan craftsmanship. The central courtyard is anchored by a stunning reflective pool that perfectly mirrors the surrounding intricate wood and stone carvings. Every wall is a dense canvas of Zellige tilework, carved cedar wood, and premium imported Italian Carrara marble. This madrasa hosted over 800 students in its small dormitory cells for several centuries during the height of its influence. It remains one of the most significant and well-preserved examples of Saadian-era architecture found in the country today.

      5. 05

        El Badi Palace

        Ksibat Nhass, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        Known historically as 'The Incomparable,' this ruined palace was once funded by the immense wealth of the trans-Saharan gold trade. Its massive orange sandstone walls now provide a unique nesting ground for the city's famous and protected white storks. While much of its original splendor was stripped away by later dynasties, the sheer scale of the ruins remains hauntingly beautiful. The vast sunken gardens and empty reflection pools offer a glimpse into the grand royal life of the Saadian dynasty. It serves as a powerful reminder of both the heights of Moroccan imperial power and the inevitable passage of time.

      6. 06

        Royal Mansour Marrakech

        Rue Abou Abbas El Sebti, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        Commissioned by the King of Morocco, this property is frequently cited as one of the most beautiful and luxurious hotels in the world. Over 1,200 master craftsmen were employed to ensure every inch of the structure reflects traditional Moroccan artisanal excellence. The design features a hidden underground tunnel system to ensure staff can provide seamless service to the private three-story riads. From silver-inlaid furniture to hand-carved plaster, the building represents the absolute peak of 21st-century palatial luxury and design. It acts as a living showcase of the best Moroccan architectural techniques preserved and perfected for future generations.

      7. 07

        Saadian Tombs

        400 Rue de La Kasbah, Medina, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        This royal necropolis dates back to the late 16th century and serves as the final resting place for the Saadian dynasty. The complex is world-renowned for its Chamber of Twelve Columns, which features majestic Carrara marble and an exquisite cedarwood dome. For centuries, the tombs were hidden behind high walls and remained forgotten until their rediscovery via aerial photography in 1917. The site is divided into two main mausoleums set within a tranquil garden filled with over one hundred mosaic-covered graves. It stands today as a breathtaking testament to the golden age of Islamic art and Moroccan craftsmanship during the reign of Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur.

      8. 08

        Dar Si Said Museum

        8 Rue de la Bahia, Riad Zitoun Jdid, Marrakech 40000, Morocco

        Dar Si Said is a magnificent 19th-century palatial residence that now serves as the National Museum of Weaving and Carpets. Built by the brother of the Grand Vizier who commissioned Bahia Palace, it showcases a similarly opulent display of Moroccan domestic architecture. The building is unique in the Medina for being constructed over multiple levels and featuring a spectacular upper-floor reception hall. Its central riad garden contains a beautifully painted wooden pavilion that is considered a masterpiece of the era's decorative arts. Today, it preserves an extensive collection of tribal jewelry, traditional weapons, and world-class textiles from across the Moroccan kingdom.
