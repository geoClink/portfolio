Brasília architecture

Brazil is a continental giant, occupying nearly half of South America and serving as the world's largest producer of coffee for over 150 years. It is a nation defined by its vibrant cultural melting pot, ranging from the Amazon rainforest to the world-famous Carnival celebrations in Rio.

Before you go

  • Fly intoGuarulhos Int'l (GRU)

    As the primary international gateway to Brazil, Guarulhos is the busiest airport in South America by passenger volume. It is a massive complex that was significantly modernized for the World Cup and Olympics, featuring a dedicated terminal for long-haul international flights with extensive luxury amenities.

  • CurrencyBrazilian Real (R$)
  • LanguagePortuguese

    Portuguese is the sole official language and is spoken with a musical, open accent that differs significantly from the European version. Most locals appreciate any effort to speak 'Português' and are very patient with learners.

  • Getting aroundCuritiba BRT & Metrô

    Curitiba was the global pioneer of the 'Bus as a Train' concept, using iconic glass tube stations for level boarding. São Paulo's Metrô is one of the cleanest and most efficient in the world, serving millions of passengers across six lines. Rio de Janeiro utilizes the 'Bondinho' tram in Santa Teresa and a modern VLT light rail system in the revitalized port area.

  1. 01

    Cathedral of Brasília

    Esplanada dos Ministérios, Brasília - DF, 70050-000

    A modernist masterpiece featuring 16 curved concrete columns weighing 90 tons each. The design represents hands reaching toward heaven, with a nave bathed in light from vast stained-glass panels. Visitors enter through a dark underground tunnel to experience a dramatic transition into the bright, airy interior.

  2. 02

    National Congress of Brazil

    Praça dos Três Poderes, Brasília - DF, 70160-900

    The center of Brazilian legislative power, consisting of twin 28-story towers flanked by a convex dome (Chamber of Deputies) and a concave dome (Federal Senate). It is arguably the most famous silhouette in the city, symbolizing the balance of the nation's political structure.

    Itamaraty Palace

    Esplanada dos Ministérios, Bloco H, Brasília - DF, 70170-900

    Also known as the 'Palace of the Arches,' this is the headquarters of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. It features a facade of concrete arches over a reflecting pool. The interior is a marvel of engineering, boasting a grand spiral staircase and large open halls without any supporting columns.

    Juscelino Kubitschek Bridge

    Setor de Clubes Esportivos Sul, Brasília - DF

    A striking steel bridge with three asymmetrical arches that 'hop' across Lake Paranoá. The design mimics the movement of a stone skipping across water. It has become a symbol of the city’s modernity and is a popular spot for viewing the sunset over the lake.

    Via Presidencial, Brasília - DF, 70150-903

    The official residence of the President of Brazil. Its name translates to 'Palace of the Dawn.' The building is famous for its iconic white marble columns, which have become a logo for the city of Brasília itself. It sits on a peninsula jutting into Lake Paranoá.
