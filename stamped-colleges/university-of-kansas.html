University of Kansas

Many of KU's early buildings on Mount Oread, such as Spooner Hall, are built of rough gray Oread limestone quarried close to the hill itself.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoKansas City International Airport (MCI)

    Kansas City International, east of Lawrence, is the main airport for the region.

  • Getting aroundWalking / KU on Wheels

    The hilltop campus is walkable. KU on Wheels and Lawrence Transit run as one coordinated system, and KU students, faculty and staff ride with their KU Card.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are crimson and blue, and the teams are the Jayhawks.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Spooner Hall

    14th St and Oread Ave, Lawrence, KS

    Henry Van Brunt designed KU's first library, built in 1893 and 1894 of Oread limestone with red sandstone trim. A sculpted owl sits on the peak of its west gable.

    Built
    1894
    Architect
    Henry Van Brunt
    Style
    Richardsonian Romanesque
    Today
    Academic Building
  2. 02

    Dyche Hall

    14th St and Oread Ave, Lawrence, KS

    Dyche Hall, home of the Natural History Museum, is built of Oread limestone with carved arches and grotesques of Cottonwood limestone. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1974 for its Romanesque style.

    Built
    1901
    Architect
    Root & Siemens; Henry Bennett
    Style
    Romanesque
    Today
    Natural History Museum
  3. 03

    Strong Hall

    Jayhawk Dr and Poplar Ln, Lawrence, KS

    Montrose Pallen McArdle first planned a grand Renaissance building with a rotunda, but the legislature cut the plans. The administration building was finished in 1911 in the Classical Revival style.

    Built
    1911
    Architect
    Montrose Pallen McArdle
    Style
    Classical Revival
    Today
    Administration Building
  4. 04

    Bailey Hall

    Jayhawk Dr and Sunflower Rd, Lawrence, KS

    Built in 1905 as the Chemistry Building, this Romanesque hall was designed by John G. Haskell, one of the architects of the Kansas State Capitol.

    Built
    1905
    Architect
    John G. Haskell
    Style
    Romanesque
    Today
    Academic Building

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Memorial Campanile

    Graduates walk through the World War II Memorial Campanile on their way down the Hill at commencement. Legend says walking through it before then means you won't graduate, so admire it from outside.

  • Strong Hall Jayhawk

    The bronze Jayhawk outside Strong Hall was a gift from the class of 1956. Stop for a photo with it.

History

Founded 1864

From Wikipedia

Two branch campuses are in the Kansas City metropolitan area on the Kansas side: the university's medical school and hospital in Kansas City, Kansas; and the Edwards Campus in Overland Park. There are also educational and research sites in Garden City, Hays, Leavenworth, Parsons, and Topeka, an agricultural education center in rural north Douglas County, and branches of the medical school in Salina and Wichita. The university is a member of the Association of American Universities and classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity".
