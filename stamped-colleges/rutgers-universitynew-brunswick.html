Planning the visit

  • Fly intoNewark Liberty International Airport (EWR)

    Newark Liberty, northeast of New Brunswick, is reached by NJ Transit trains on the Northeast Corridor Line.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Rutgers buses / NJ Transit

    Free Rutgers buses link the College Avenue, Busch, Livingston and Cook/Douglass campuses, and NJ Transit trains stop at New Brunswick station.

  • Colors and teams

    The school color is scarlet, and the teams are the Scarlet Knights.

    Geology Hall

    Queens Campus, Rutgers University, New Brunswick, NJ 08901

    Henry Janeway Hardenbergh designed this stone hall, built in 1871–72 to house science classes and the Rutgers Geology Museum. The museum now fills the whole building.

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Willie the Silent

    The 1928 bronze of William the Silent, Prince of Orange, known to students as Willie the Silent, is one of the best-loved statues on campus. Find him on Voorhees Mall.

  • Old Queens Bell

    The bell in the cupola of Old Queens rings for convocation, commencement and other great occasions, and inspired the song "The Bells Must Ring." Look up at the cupola from the Queens Campus.

History

Founded 1766

From Wikipedia

It is the oldest and flagship campus of the university, the other satellite campuses being in Camden and Newark. The New Brunswick campus is composed of several smaller campuses that are some distances away from each other: College Avenue, Busch, Livingston, Cook, and Douglass, the latter two sometimes referred to as "Cook/Douglass", as they are adjacent and intertwined. All of these campuses lie along State Route 18. Rutgers–New Brunswick also includes several buildings in downtown New Brunswick.
