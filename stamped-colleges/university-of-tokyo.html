Tōdai

University of Tokyo

The Hongō campus stands on the former Edo residence of the Maeda lords of Kaga. Its famous Red Gate, the Akamon, was built in 1827 to welcome a shōgun's daughter who married into the family.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoTokyo Haneda Airport (HND)

    Haneda and Narita both serve Tokyo; Haneda is the closer of the two to the city centre. Trains and the subway connect both airports with stations near the Hongō campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Tokyo Metro

    Tōdai-mae station on the Namboku Line is about three minutes' walk from campus. Hongō-sanchōme, Nezu and Yushima stations are each about eight minutes away.

  • Colors and teams

    Japanese is the language of daily life. Students call the university Tōdai, short for Tōkyō Daigaku, and the Akamon has long been a nickname for the university itself.

6 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Yasuda Auditorium

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    Businessman Yasuda Zenjirō paid for this red-brick clock tower, designed by Yoshikazu Uchida and Hideto Kishida and completed in 1925. It is the symbol of the campus and was the center of the 1968–69 student protests.

    Built
    1925
    Architect
    Yoshikazu Uchida; Hideto Kishida
    Style
    Gothic Revival
    Today
    Auditorium and university offices
  2. 02

    Akamon

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    Lord Maeda Nariyasu of the Kaga Domain built this vermilion gate in 1827 to welcome Yōhime, daughter of shōgun Tokugawa Ienari, as his bride. It is a gabled yakuimon gate with a guardhouse on each side and a national Important Cultural Property.

    Built
    1827
    Style
    Yakuimon (Edo-period gate)
    Today
    Campus gate
  3. 03

    Main Gate (University of Tokyo)

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    Itō Chūta designed the campus's official main gate on Hongō-dōri, completed in 1912. From here the ginkgo avenue leads straight to Yasuda Auditorium.

    Built
    1912
    Architect
    Itō Chūta
    Today
    Campus gate
  4. 04

    General Library (University of Tokyo)

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    The library's earlier building burned in the 1923 Great Kantō Earthquake along with 750,000 books. John D. Rockefeller paid for this replacement by Yoshikazu Uchida, completed in 1928, whose front looks like a row of books.

    Built
    1928
    Architect
    Yoshikazu Uchida
    Style
    Gothic Revival
    Today
    General Library
  5. 05

    Fukutake Hall

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    Tadao Ando's first building at the university was finished in 2008 near the Akamon, funded by Benesse chairman Sōichirō Fukutake. Almost half of the building lies underground so it sits lightly among its older neighbours.

    Built

  6. 06

    Daiwa Ubiquitous Computing Research Building

    7-3-1 Hongō, Bunkyō, Tokyo 113-8654

    Kengo Kuma designed this research building, funded by Daiwa House and finished in 2014. Staggered strips of cedar wrap its façade, and hundreds of sensors manage the temperature, humidity and air inside.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Sanshirō Pond

    This pond and garden were laid out in 1638 for the Maeda lords of the Kaga Domain. It took its nickname from Natsume Sōseki's novel Sanshirō, whose hero first meets the woman he admires here, and it makes a quiet stop on a walk around campus.

  • Golden Ginkgo Avenue

    In late autumn the ginkgo trees lining the avenue from the Main Gate to Yasuda Auditorium turn bright yellow. It is one of the most loved autumn views in Tokyo, and the ginkgo leaf is the university's emblem.

  • Hachikō Reunited

    On the Faculty of Agriculture grounds, a bronze statue unveiled in 2015 shows the loyal dog Hachikō leaping up to greet his owner, Professor Hidesaburō Ueno. It stands just inside the faculty's main gate.

History

Founded 1877

From Wikipedia

The University of Tokyo is a public research university in Bunkyō, Tokyo, Japan. Founded in 1877 as the nation's first modern university by the merger of several pre-westernisation era institutions, its direct precursors include the Tenmongata, founded in 1684, and the Shōheizaka Institute.
