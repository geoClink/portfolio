La Sorbonne

University of Paris (Sorbonne)

The Sorbonne takes its name from the College of Sorbonne, founded around 1253 by Robert de Sorbon. Today's building is a late-19th-century reconstruction, but Cardinal Richelieu's domed chapel from the 1600s still stands in its courtyard.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoParis Charles de Gaulle Airport (CDG)

    Charles de Gaulle is the main international gateway to Paris. RER line B runs from the airport into the city and stops at Luxembourg, a short walk from the Sorbonne.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Metro / RER

    The Latin Quarter is compact and easy to walk. Cluny–La Sorbonne metro station and Luxembourg RER station are both a few minutes from the Sorbonne.

  • Colors and teams

    French is the language of lectures and daily life. The Latin Quarter got its name from the Latin once spoken by the university's medieval scholars.

5 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    The Sorbonne

    47 Rue des Écoles, 75005 Paris

    The building that gave the University of Paris its nickname has housed scholars since the College of Sorbonne opened here in 1253. Henri-Paul Nénot's grand reconstruction wraps a ceremonial courtyard, a peristyle and the Grand Amphitheatre, all still shared by the university's successor institutions.

    Built
    1901
    Architect
    Henri-Paul Nénot
    Style
    Beaux-Arts
    Today
    Shared seat of the University of Paris's successor universities
  2. 02

    Sorbonne Chapel

    17 Rue de la Sorbonne, 75005 Paris

    Jacques Lemercier designed this domed chapel for Cardinal Richelieu's rebuilding of the College of Sorbonne, and Richelieu's tomb lies beneath the dome. It is the only building left from that 17th-century campus; the rest was replaced in the 1800s.

    Built
    1648
    Architect
    Jacques Lemercier
    Style
    French Baroque
    Today
    Historic chapel (mostly closed since a 1999 storm)
  3. 03

    Sainte-Geneviève Library

    10 Place du Panthéon, 75005 Paris

    Henri Labrouste's reading room is a landmark of iron architecture: sixteen slender cast-iron Ionic columns carry openwork iron arches beneath twin barrel vaults. Facing the Panthéon, it is the main inter-university library of the universities of Paris.

    Built
    1851
    Architect
    Henri Labrouste
    Style
    Beaux-Arts with exposed ironwork
    Today
    Inter-university library
  4. 04

    École de Médecine

    12 Rue de l'École-de-Médecine, 75006 Paris

    Built as the Academy of Surgery, Jacques Gondouin's building was the home of the Faculty of Medicine of Paris from 1795 to 1971. It even gave the street its name: Rue des Cordeliers became Rue de l'École-de-Médecine.

    Built
    1774
    Architect
    Jacques Gondouin
    Style
    English Palladian
    Today
    Head office of Paris Cité University
  5. 05

    Faculty of Law of Paris

    Place du Panthéon, 75005 Paris

    Louis XV ordered a new home for the law faculty on the Place du Panthéon, and Jacques-Germain Soufflot, the Panthéon's own architect and a former law student here, designed it. It opened in 1774 and still serves law students today.

    Built
    1774
    Architect
    Jacques-Germain Soufflot
    Style
    Neoclassical
    Today
    Panthéon-Assas University

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Touch Montaigne's Shoe

    Across the rue des Écoles from the Sorbonne, students touch the foot of Paul Landowski's seated Montaigne statue for luck before exams. Visitors can stop by the statue and see the shoe polished bright by generations of hands.

  • Richelieu's Cardinal Hat

    In the Sorbonne Chapel, a cardinal's hat hangs above Cardinal Richelieu's marble tomb, as tradition dictates. Visitors can see it when the chapel opens for guided tours and Heritage Days.

  • Heritage Days at the Sorbonne

    Each September during the European Heritage Days, the Sorbonne opens its reception halls and Grand Amphitheatre to the public for free. Visitors can walk up onto the amphitheatre's stage and tour rooms that are normally closed.

History

From Wikipedia

The University of Paris was the leading university in Paris, France, from 1150 to 1970, except for 1793–1806 during the French Revolution. Emerging around 1150 as a corporation associated with the cathedral school of Paris, it was considered the second-oldest university in Europe. Officially chartered in 1200 by King Philip II and recognised in 1215 by Pope Innocent III, it was nicknamed after its theological College of Sorbonne, founded by Robert de Sorbon and chartered by King Louis IX around 1257.
