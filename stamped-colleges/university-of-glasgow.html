Gilmorehill

University of Glasgow

In 1870 the university left its old High Street home for a new Gothic Revival campus on Gilmorehill designed by Sir George Gilbert Scott, and it brought its 17th-century Lion and Unicorn Staircase along with it.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoGlasgow Airport (GLA)

    Glasgow Airport lies just west of the city, about a 20-minute drive from the West End campus. Express buses connect it with the city centre.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Subway

    The campus sits in the West End above Kelvingrove Park. Hillhead station on the Glasgow Subway is a short walk away.

  • Colors and teams

    English is spoken everywhere, often with a strong Glaswegian accent and Scots words; the main hill-top campus is known simply as Gilmorehill.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    University of Glasgow Memorial Chapel

    University Avenue, Glasgow G12 8QQ

    John James Burnet's Scots Gothic chapel closes the west side of the Main Building's West Quadrangle and honours the university's dead of the First World War. Inside are carvings by Archibald Dawson and ten stained-glass windows by Douglas Strachan.

  2. 02

    Lion and Unicorn Staircase

    University Avenue, Glasgow G12 8QQ

    Carved in 1690 for the university's Old College on the High Street, this stone staircase with its lion and unicorn was moved to Gilmorehill in 1870. It now leads up beside the Memorial Chapel on the west side of the Main Building.

    Hillhead Street, Glasgow G12 8QE

    William Whitfield's 12-storey Brutalist tower opened in 1968 as the centrepiece of the university's 1960s expansion across Hillhead. It is a landmark of Glasgow's West End.

  4. 04

    Glasgow University Union

    32 University Avenue, Glasgow G12 8LX

    At the foot of University Avenue, Alan McNaughton's Scots Baronial building has been home to one of the oldest students' unions in the UK since 1931. Its debating tradition is famous: the union founded the World Universities Debating Championships.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Rub the Unicorn's Horn

    The Lion and Unicorn Staircase, carved in 1690, was moved stone by stone from the Old College on the High Street. Patting the unicorn or rubbing its horn is said to bring good luck, especially before exams.

  • Keep Off the Quad Grass

    It is thought to be bad luck for students to walk on the grass of the quadrangles before they graduate. Visitors can admire the East and West Quads from the paths, where graduation processions led by a piper take place.

  • The Cloisters

    The vaulted Cloisters (the Undercroft) between the East and West Quadrangles have appeared in films and TV series including Outlander and Cloud Atlas. Walk through and take a photo under the fluted columns.
