Child of the Sun

Florida Southern College

College president Ludd Spivey hired Frank Lloyd Wright in 1938, and over two decades Wright drew 18 designs for the campus. Money was always short, so many of the buildings went up with student labor, and the result is the largest collection of Wright buildings at a single site.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoTampa International Airport (TPA)

    Lakeland sits on Interstate 4 between Tampa and Orlando, and Tampa International to the west is the usual way in. Lakeland Linder International Airport is in the city itself.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Esplanades

    Wright's covered esplanades, built between 1940 and 1958, link every one of his campus buildings except Danforth Chapel, which sits right beside Pfeiffer Chapel. Their low canopies are spaced like the orange trees that once grew here.

  • Colors and teams

    The campus nickname comes from Wright himself, who said his buildings would rise 'out of the ground and into the light, a child of the sun'.

    Annie Pfeiffer Chapel

    111 Lake Hollingsworth Drive, Lakeland, FL 33801

    Wright's first religious building since Unity Temple, a hexagonal chapel clad in about 6,000 textile blocks set with some 50,000 glass cubes. Its steel-braced tower, nicknamed "God's bicycle rack" by students, was rebuilt after a 1944 hurricane.

    William H. Danforth Chapel

    Johnson Avenue, Florida Southern College, Lakeland, FL 33801

    A small chapel beside Pfeiffer Chapel with a pointed roof that juts out like a ship's prow over a wall of leaded stained glass, the only leaded glass on campus. Its original cypress doors, Wright-designed benches and fireplace are still in place.

    Thad Buckner Building

    Johnson Avenue, Florida Southern College, Lakeland, FL 33801

    Built as the first Roux Library, it pairs a circular reading room with a hexagonal stack wing, Wright's first circular floor plan. Clerestory windows light the reading room's stepped terraces, which now hold exhibits and a visitor center.

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Annie Pfeiffer Chapel

    The college calls Frank Lloyd Wright's chapel the soaring heart of the campus; it is crowned by a bell tower of concrete and colored glass. Visitors can see it on a guided Wright tour that starts at the Sharp Family Tourism and Education Center.

  • Water Dome Fountains

    Wright's largest water feature was finally built to his full design in 2007, and its fountains run at set times during the day. Visitors can view it at any time from the surrounding esplanades.
