Postera Crescam Laude

University of Melbourne

One campus walk covers a century of Australian architecture: Victorian Gothic labs from the 1880s, Walter Burley Griffin's Newman College, Australia's first purpose-built university library and Roy Grounds' Brutalist John Medley Building.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoMelbourne Airport (MEL)

    Melbourne Airport at Tullamarine is about 20 km north-west of the Parkville campus. The SkyBus runs to Southern Cross Station in the city, a short tram ride from the university.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Swanston Street trams

    Trams run up Swanston Street from the city centre to the university's front door. The Parkville campus itself is compact and easy to explore on foot.

  • Colors and teams

    The university's Latin motto, Postera crescam laude, means 'I shall grow in the esteem of future generations'.

    Old Pathology Building

    156-292 Grattan Street, Parkville VIC

    Built in 1885 by Reed, Henderson & Smart, this early English Gothic building has been in continuous university use for well over a century. Now named the Elisabeth Murdoch Building, it is on the Victorian Heritage Register.

  2. 02

    Newman College

    887 Swanston St, Parkville VIC

    American architects Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin built this college between 1916 and 1918, with a domed dining room between two stone residential wings. They even designed its chairs and tables, and it is on the Australian National Heritage List as one of the best examples of Griffin's work.

  4. 04

    Baillieu Library

    Corner of Grattan Street and Royal Parade, Parkville VIC

    Australia's first purpose-built university library, designed by architecture lecturer John Scarborough and opened in 1959. Its foundations were laid strong enough for a tower of up to 19 storeys that was never built.

    John Medley Building

    Kernot Road, Parkville VIC

    Sir Roy Grounds designed this Brutalist block, built from 1969 to 1971 to relieve the crowded Old Arts Building. Its two six-storey towers rise above a five-storey base.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Old Quad and the Eight-Hour Day

    The Old Quad was the university's first building, and in 1856 the stonemasons building it downed tools and joined the marches that won the eight-hour working day. Visitors can walk through the Old Quad, the campus's historic heart.

  • Spot the Old Arts clock oddity

    Students and visitors are invited to study the Roman numerals on the Old Arts Building clock tower and spot what is odd about them.

  • The last piece of the original fence

    A single segment of the wrought-iron fence that once encircled the whole campus survives outside the Medical Building. The grounds were opened to the public in exchange for free gas lighting from the City of Melbourne; visitors can find the remaining fence section.
