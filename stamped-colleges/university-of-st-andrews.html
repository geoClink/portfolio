Scotland's First University

University of St Andrews

Founded in 1413, St Andrews spans six centuries of architecture in one small seaside town, from the Late Gothic St Salvator's Chapel to James Stirling's Brutalist Andrew Melville Hall, designed to resemble passing ships.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoEdinburgh Airport (EDI)

    Edinburgh Airport is the main gateway for St Andrews, about an hour and a half away by road. By rail, the nearest station is Leuchars, a short bus or taxi ride from town.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Bus

    St Andrews is a small town and the university buildings are spread along its old streets, so walking is the easiest way around. Local buses link the town with Leuchars station.

  • Colors and teams

    English is spoken throughout; the university's Scots name is University o St Andras and its Gaelic name Oilthigh Chill Rìmhinn.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    St Salvator's Chapel

    North Street, St Andrews KY16 9AL

    Founded in 1450 by Bishop James Kennedy, who is buried inside, this Late Gothic chapel is the university's main chapel. It has been refurbished in the 1680s, the 1860s and throughout the 20th century.

  2. 02

    St Mary's College

    South Street, St Andrews KY16 9JU

    Founded in 1538, the college keeps much of its original 16th-century north and west ranges around a quiet quad. In the quad grow a thorn tree said to have been planted by Mary, Queen of Scots, and the King James Library founded in 1612.

    Built
    1538
    Style
    16th-century collegiate
    Today
    School of Divinity
  3. 03

    Younger Hall

    North Street, St Andrews KY16 9AJ

    Paul Waterhouse's graduation hall on North Street mixes Neo-Classical and Art Deco ideas. Its foyer is lined in grey and green marble, and the main hall seats almost a thousand people.

    1929

  4. 04

    Andrew Melville Hall

    North Haugh, St Andrews KY16 9SU

    James Stirling built this hall of residence from prefabricated concrete modules on a ridge above the St Andrews Links, and it looks like a fleet of passing ships. It is now Category A listed and recognised as one of Scotland's key modern buildings.

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Patrick Hamilton's PH

    The letters PH set in the cobbles outside St Salvator's Quad mark where Patrick Hamilton was burned at the stake in 1528. Tradition says any student who steps on them will fail their degree, so visitors step around them too.

  • Pier Walk

    After chapel each week, students in red gowns walk the length of the historic harbour pier, with the busiest walk at the end of Freshers' Week. Visitors can watch the procession or walk the pier themselves.
