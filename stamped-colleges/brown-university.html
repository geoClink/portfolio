Planning the visit

  • Fly intoRhode Island T. F. Green International Airport (PVD)

    T. F. Green is in Warwick, about 20 minutes south of College Hill. Boston Logan is roughly an hour and a half away by car or train.

  • Getting aroundWalking / RIPTA buses

    Brown sits on steep College Hill above downtown Providence and is easiest to explore on foot. RIPTA buses connect the hill with downtown and the train station.

  • Colors and teams

    Students call the John Hay Library simply 'the Hay'.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    University Hall

    The College Green, Providence, RI 02912

    Brown's first building, raised in 1770 and once the largest building in Rhode Island. Framed by the Van Wickle Gates, this Georgian hall is called one of the icons of early American college architecture.

    Built
    1770
    Architect
    Built by Nicholas Brown and Company
    Style
    Georgian
    Today

  2. 02

    Sayles Hall

    The College Green, Providence, RI 02912

    A rock-faced granite hall with brownstone trim, built in 1879–1881 in memory of a student who died young. Under its pine roof trusses sits the largest surviving Hutchings-Votey organ of its type, played at a midnight Halloween concert each year.

    Built
    1881
    Architect
    Alpheus C. Morse
    Style
    Richardsonian Romanesque
    Today
    Lecture and assembly hall
  3. 03

    John Hay Library

    20 Prospect St, Providence, RI 02912

    Funded by Andrew Carnegie and named for his friend, Secretary of State John Hay, this white Vermont marble library opened in 1910 across from the Van Wickle Gates. It now keeps Brown's rare books, manuscripts and archives.

    Built
    1910
    Architect
    Shepley, Rutan and Coolidge
    Style
    Beaux-Arts
    Today
    Rare books and special collections
  4. 04

    Rockefeller Library

    10 Prospect St, Providence, RI 02912

    Brown's main library, completed in 1964, is a bold Brutalist counterpoint to the marble John Hay Library across Prospect Street.

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Van Wickle Gates

    The centre Van Wickle Gates open only for Convocation and Commencement, and superstition says a student who passes through them more than twice will not graduate. Visitors can see the 1901 wrought-iron gates on Prospect Street and walk through the side gates instead.

  • Bronze Bruno

    A seven-foot bronze of Bruno, Brown's bear mascot, stands on the College Green on a pedestal holding a slate rock said to be one Roger Williams stepped on in 1636. Stop by for a photo with the mascot.

History

Founded 1765

From Wikipedia

Brown University is a private Ivy League research university in the College Hill neighborhood of Providence, Rhode Island, United States. The university is the seventh-oldest institution of higher education in the United States, founded in 1764 as the College in the English Colony of Rhode Island and Providence Plantations. One of nine colonial colleges chartered before the American Revolution, it was the first American college to codify that admission and instruction of students was to be equal regardless of the religious affiliation of students.
