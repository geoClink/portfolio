University of Pennsylvania

Penn's campus spans eras: Frank Furness's fiery red Victorian library, the first student union on any American campus, and Louis Kahn's breakthrough Richards Laboratories all sit within a short walk.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoPhiladelphia International Airport (PHL)

    PHL is about 15 minutes by car from campus. SEPTA's Airport Line train runs to University City station, next to the campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / SEPTA

    University City is flat and easy to walk. SEPTA trolleys, buses and the Market–Frankford Line connect campus with Center City Philadelphia.

  • Colors and teams

    The university goes by 'Penn' or 'UPenn' for short.

    Penn Museum

    3260 South St, Philadelphia, PA 19104

    A campus landmark of courtyards, gardens, a reflecting pool and a dramatic rotunda, designed by a team of Penn's own architecture faculty. Only a third of the grand original plan was built, and it holds a 13-ton granite sphinx of Ramesses II.

    1899

  5. 05

    Richards Medical Research Laboratories

    3700 Hamilton Walk, Philadelphia, PA 19104

    The building that made Louis Kahn's career: clusters of laboratory towers flanked by brick shafts for stairs and air ducts, inspired by the medieval towers of Italy. Its clear split between 'served' and 'servant' spaces set new directions for modern architecture.

    Built
    1960
    Architect

    Style

    Today

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • The Button

    Claes Oldenburg's Split Button sits on College Green, and student lore says it is the button that popped off Benjamin Franklin's vest, rolled down Locust Walk and split in two. It is a central campus landmark and a popular photo stop.

  • Ivy Day stones on College Hall

    Since 1873 each graduating class has set an engraved Ivy Stone into a campus building, and there are now nearly 200 around campus. Start at College Hall and look for the class-year stones in the walls.

  • Hey Day on Locust Walk

    Each spring, juniors in straw-style hats march down Locust Walk to College Hall to be declared seniors, a tradition dating back to Class Day in 1865. Visitors can watch the procession pass along the walk.
