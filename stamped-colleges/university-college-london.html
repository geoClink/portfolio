London's first university

University College London

UCL's main building took 158 years to finish: William Wilkins started it in 1827, the money ran out in 1829 with only the portico and dome complete, and the full U-shaped plan was not finished until 1985.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoHeathrow Airport (LHR)

    Heathrow lies west of central London. The Piccadilly line runs straight from the airport to Russell Square, a short walk from UCL's Bloomsbury campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / London Underground

    The Bloomsbury campus is compact and easy to explore on foot. Euston Square, Warren Street and Goodge Street Underground stations are all on its doorstep.

  • Colors and teams

    Founded in 1826 as the first university in England open to students of any religion, UCL still gets teased by King's students as the 'Godless' college of Gower Street.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    UCL Main Building

    Gower Street, London WC1E 6BT

    William Wilkins, architect of the National Gallery, gave UCL its grand classical portico and ornate dome facing Gower Street. Beneath the dome, the Octagon and Flaxman Gallery hold John Flaxman's sculpture casts, and the oldest parts of the building are Grade I listed.

    Built
    1829
    Architect
    William Wilkins
    Style
    Neoclassical
    Today
    Main library, Octagon and university offices
  2. 02

    Cruciform Building

    Gower Street, London WC1E 6BT

    Alfred Waterhouse's last commission, a red-brick Gothic hospital laid out as a cross, with operating theatres at the centre and airy wards in the wings to keep patients apart. Built for University College Hospital, it now houses UCL's Wolfson Institute and parts of the Medical School.

    Built
    1906
    Architect
    Alfred Waterhouse (completed by Paul Waterhouse)
    Style
    Gothic Revival
    Today
    Biomedical research and medical school
  3. 03

    Institute of Education building

    20 Bedford Way, London WC1H 0AL

    Denys Lasdun, architect of the National Theatre, built this long Grade II* listed concrete block along Bedford Way between 1970 and 1976. Its Brutalist bulk cutting through Bloomsbury's Georgian squares made it one of London's most debated buildings.

    Built
    1976
    Architect
    Denys Lasdun & Partners
    Style
    Brutalist
    Today
    UCL Institute of Education
  4. 04

    Senate House

    Malet Street, London WC1E 7HU

    Next door to UCL, Charles Holden's Portland stone Art Deco tower is the headquarters of the University of London, which UCL belongs to. Its 19 floors rise 64 metres, and George Orwell's Ministry of Truth in Nineteen Eighty-Four is often read as a portrait of it.

    Built
    1937
    Architect
    Charles Holden
    Style
    Art Deco
    Height
    64 m (210 ft)
    Stories
    19
    Today
    University of London headquarters and Senate House Library

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Jeremy Bentham's auto-icon

    The preserved skeleton of philosopher Jeremy Bentham, UCL's 'spiritual founder', sits dressed in his own clothes (with a wax head) in a glass case at the entrance of the Student Centre; in 2013 it was even brought to a UCL council meeting. Visitors can see it on public display.

  • Photo at the Portico

    The Portico of the Wilkins Building in the Main Quad is UCL's most photographed and most iconic spot. Visitors can take the classic photo of its columns and dome from the Quad.

  • Flaxman Gallery under the dome

    Beneath the Octagon dome, the Flaxman Gallery holds the largest single collection of works by sculptor John Flaxman, including the full-scale St Michael Overcoming Satan, and appeared in Christopher Nolan's film Inception. Visitors can view the sculptures.
