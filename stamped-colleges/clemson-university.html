Planning the visit

  • Fly intoGreenville-Spartanburg International Airport (GSP)

    Greenville-Spartanburg International, east of Clemson along Interstate 85, is the nearest commercial airport.

  • Getting aroundWalking / CATbus

    The central campus is walkable. Clemson Area Transit's CATbus is fare-free and runs routes across campus and into the city of Clemson.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are orange and regalia (purple), and the teams are the Tigers.

    Hanover House

    Cherry Road, Clemson, SC 29634

    A French Huguenot family built this cypress house with a gambrel roof in 1714–1716 in the South Carolina Lowcountry. It was moved to the campus in the 1940s to save it from the flooding of Lake Moultrie.

    Lee and Lowry Hall

    Clemson, SC 29634

    Harlan McClure, dean of architecture, designed this International Style complex, completed in 1958 as the Structural Science Building. Three wings enclose two courtyards, and it is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

    Memorial Stadium

    Avenue of Champions, Clemson, SC 29634

    Known as Death Valley, the stadium opened in 1942 with 20,500 seats built for $125,000. It has grown into one of the largest stadiums in college football.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Howard's Rock

    This piece of quartzite sits on a pedestal atop The Hill in Memorial Stadium, and players rub it before running down onto the field at home games. Visitors can see it at the east end of the stadium.

  • Lover's Lane in President's Park

    Campus legend says couples who walk hand in hand through President's Park will marry. Visitors stroll the park at the edge of campus.

  • Bowman Field

    Bowman Field, the lawn in front of Tillman Hall, was the parade ground for Clemson's cadets. Visitors take in the view of the Tillman Hall clock tower from the field.
