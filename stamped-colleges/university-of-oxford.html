City of Dreaming Spires

University of Oxford

Oxford has no single campus: its colleges and university buildings are woven through the medieval city centre, so the Radcliffe Camera, the Sheldonian and the Divinity School all sit within a few minutes' walk of each other.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoLondon Heathrow Airport (LHR)

    Heathrow is the usual gateway for international visitors to Oxford. Direct coaches run from the airport to Oxford's city centre in roughly an hour and a half.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Bus / Bike

    The historic centre is compact and best explored on foot. Bicycles are everywhere, and city buses connect the centre with the railway station and outlying colleges.

  • Colors and teams

    Oxford has its own vocabulary: terms are called Michaelmas, Hilary and Trinity, and the Radcliffe Camera is simply the 'Rad Cam'.

8 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Radcliffe Camera

    Radcliffe Square, Oxford OX1 3BG

    James Gibbs's great domed rotunda stands alone in the middle of Radcliffe Square, which is why it has become the symbol of Oxford itself. Built to house a library funded by the physician John Radcliffe, it is now a reading room of the Bodleian.

    Built
    1748
    Architect
    James Gibbs
    Style
    Baroque
    Today
    Bodleian Library reading room
  2. 02

    Sheldonian Theatre

    Broad Street, Oxford OX1 3AZ

    Christopher Wren based his design on the open-air Theatre of Marcellus in Rome, then solved the problem of a 70-foot roof span with an ingenious timber truss. Inside, a painted ceiling shows Truth descending on the Arts and Sciences.

    Built
    1669
    Architect
    Christopher Wren
    Style
    English Baroque
    Today
    Ceremonies, concerts and lectures
  3. 03

    Divinity School

    Broad Street, Oxford OX1 3BG

    Begun in 1427, this is the oldest surviving building made for university use. Its stone ceiling is a showpiece of Perpendicular Gothic, an intricate lierne vault studded with 455 carved bosses.

    Built
    1483
    Architect
    William Orchard (vault)
    Style
    Perpendicular Gothic
    Today
    Part of the Bodleian Library
  4. 04

    Tom Tower

    St Aldate's, Oxford OX1 1DP

    Wren completed Cardinal Wolsey's unfinished gatehouse at Christ Church in 1681–82, choosing a Gothic style to match the founder's work at a time when nobody was building Gothic. Its ogee dome houses Great Tom, the six-ton bell that gives the tower its name.

    Built
    1682
    Architect
    Christopher Wren
    Style
    Gothic
    Today
    Christ Church gate tower and belfry
  5. 05

    Clarendon Building

    Broad Street, Oxford OX1 3BG

    Nicholas Hawksmoor's temple-fronted building on Broad Street was finished in 1715 next to the Sheldonian and the Bodleian. Its grand portico and rooftop statues make it one of the most monumental pieces of the university's ceremonial heart.

    Built
    1715
    Architect
    Nicholas Hawksmoor
    Style
    Neoclassical
    Today
    Bodleian Libraries offices
  6. 06

    Bridge of Sighs

    New College Lane, Oxford OX1 3BW

    Officially Hertford Bridge, this enclosed skyway links the Old and New Quadrangles of Hertford College over New College Lane. Designed by Sir Thomas Jackson and finished in 1914, it is nicknamed for its supposed likeness to the bridge in Venice.

    Built
    1914
    Architect
    Thomas Graham Jackson
    Style
    Covered skyway
    Today
    College skyway
  7. 07

    Oxford University Museum of Natural History

    Parks Road, Oxford OX1 3PW

    A neo-Gothic cathedral of science, shaped by the ideas of John Ruskin. A glass roof on cast-iron columns covers the central court, and each stone column in the arcades is cut from a different British rock.

    Built
    1860
    Architect
    Thomas Newenham Deane and Benjamin Woodward
    Style
    Neo-Gothic
    Today
    Natural history museum
  8. 08

    Magdalen Tower

    High Street, Oxford OX1 4AU

    The bell tower of Magdalen College rises 44 metres over the High Street and the River Cherwell. It is the central focus of Oxford's May Morning celebrations each spring.

    1509
    Style
    Gothic
    Height

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Great Tom's 101 Strokes

    Every night at 9:05pm (9pm old 'Oxford time') the Great Tom bell in Christ Church's Tom Tower rings 101 times, once for each of the college's original 100 scholars plus one added in 1663. Stand on St Aldate's to hear it.

  • May Morning at Magdalen Tower

    At 6am on 1 May the Magdalen College Choir sings the Hymnus Eucharisticus from the top of the Great Tower, a ceremony going back about 500 years. Crowds gather on the High Street and Magdalen Bridge below to listen.

  • Bridge of Sighs

    Hertford Bridge, nicknamed the Bridge of Sighs after the Venice bridge it is said to resemble, links two parts of Hertford College over New College Lane. Stop on the lane to see and photograph this city landmark.

History

Founded 1096

From Wikipedia

It grew rapidly from 1167, when Henry II prohibited English students from attending the University of Paris. After escalating conflict between students and the Oxford townspeople, some Oxford academics fled northeast to Cambridge, where they established the University of Cambridge in 1209. The two universities share many common features and are jointly referred to as Oxbridge.
