Washington University in St. Louis

Brookings Hall was the administration building of the 1904 World's Fair, and Francis Field on campus was the main stadium of the 1904 Summer Olympics, the first held in the Western Hemisphere.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoSt. Louis Lambert International Airport (STL)

    Lambert is about 23 km northwest of downtown St. Louis. MetroLink's Red Line stops at both airport terminals.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Campus Circulator / MetroLink

    A free Campus Circulator shuttle loops the Danforth Campus, and MetroLink Blue Line trains stop at Skinker station beside it.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are red and green, and the teams are the Bears, with a mascot named Forest the bear.

  1. 01

    Brookings Hall

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    Cope and Stewardson designed this Collegiate Gothic gateway, built from 1900 to 1902 as the centerpiece of the new campus. It served as the administration building of the 1904 World's Fair and is still the university's administrative center.

    1902
    Architect

    Graham Chapel

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    Dedicated in 1909, this Collegiate Gothic chapel was modeled after King's College Chapel at Cambridge, though on a far smaller scale. It hosts concerts, plays and the university's Assembly Series lectures.

    Busch Hall

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    Named for Anheuser-Busch co-founder Adolphus Busch, this was the first building begun on the campus, with its cornerstone laid in 1900. It was the chemistry building until 1950 and now houses humanities departments.

  4. 04

    Ridgley Hall

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    The university's first library until the early 1960s, Ridgley displayed Queen Victoria's Diamond Jubilee gifts during the 1904 World's Fair. Its old reading room is now the ornate Holmes Lounge.

    Built
    1901
    Architect
    Cope and Stewardson
    Style

  5. 05

    Eads Hall

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    In 1922, physics professor Arthur Holly Compton ran experiments in this hall's basement showing that light behaves as particles, work that won him the 1927 Nobel Prize. During the 1904 World's Fair it was used by the Board of Lady Managers.

    Built
    1902
    Architect
    Cope and Stewardson
    Style

    Olin Library

    Danforth Campus, Washington University, St. Louis, MO 63130

    Dedicated in 1962 and renovated in 2003, Olin is the main library of the university and the largest library in Missouri. It holds a broadside of the Declaration of Independence printed before the signing.

    Francis Olympic Field

    Forsyth Blvd, St. Louis County, MO 63105

    Built for the 1904 World's Fair, this stadium was the main venue of the 1904 Summer Olympics, and its permanent stands were an early use of reinforced concrete. It is still home to the Bears' football, soccer and track teams.

5 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Don't Step on the Seal

    Campus legend says that stepping on the university seal under Brookings Hall will keep you from graduating, though touching the Chancellor's Medallion breaks the curse. Find the seal and walk around it.

  • Declaration of Independence in Olin

    Olin Library displays a broadside copy of the Declaration of Independence, printed before the document was signed to spread the news across the colonies. Stop by the exhibit to see it in person.

  • Lunch at Holmes Lounge

    The old library reading room in Ridgley Hall is now Holmes Lounge, one of the most beautiful rooms on campus. Grab lunch there and take in the surroundings.

  • The Painted Underpass

    The underpass linking the South 40 to the main campus is covered in painted announcements for campus events. Walk through to see the latest designs.

  • Assembly Series in Graham Chapel

    Graham Chapel hosts the Assembly Series, the university's long-running lecture program, which has brought speakers such as Eleanor Roosevelt and Gloria Steinem to campus. Check the schedule and catch a talk.
