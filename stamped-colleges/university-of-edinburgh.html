Athens of the North

University of Edinburgh

The university's buildings trace Edinburgh's architectural story: Robert Adam and William Playfair's Neoclassical Old College, Playfair's neo-Gothic New College on the Mound, Scotland's first purpose-built concert hall, and a Brutalist library by Basil Spence's firm.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoEdinburgh Airport (EDI)

    Edinburgh Airport is west of the city, and the Edinburgh Trams line runs from the terminal straight into the city centre.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Bus / Tram

    Most university buildings are within walking distance of the Old Town. Lothian Buses and the tram connect the city, though the hills make for good exercise.

  • Colors and teams

    Edinburgh students call Teviot Row House simply 'Teviot'. The university's Gaelic name is Oilthigh Dhùn Èideann.

6 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Old College

    South Bridge, Edinburgh EH8 9YL

    Robert Adam designed this grand Neoclassical quadrangle on South Bridge, and William Henry Playfair completed it to a modified design in 1827; the dome came later. It now houses the School of Law and the Talbot Rice Gallery.

    McEwan Hall

    Bristo Square, Edinburgh EH8 9AG

    Sir Robert Rowand Anderson's domed graduation hall, in Italian Renaissance style, was paid for by the brewer William McEwan and finished in 1897. It is Category A listed.

    New College

    Mound Place, Edinburgh EH1 2LX

    William Henry Playfair's neo-Gothic towers on the Mound look down on Princes Street Gardens. Opened in 1846 for the Free Church of Scotland, it has been the university's School of Divinity since 1935.

  4. 04

    St Cecilia's Hall

    50 Niddry Street, Edinburgh EH1 1LG

    Completed in 1763, this was Scotland's first purpose-built concert hall. Robert Mylne gave it an oval concert room under a domed ceiling behind a plain Neoclassical front, and today it holds the university's musical instrument collection.

  5. 05

    Teviot Row House

    13 Bristo Square, Edinburgh EH8 9AJ

    Opened in 1889, Teviot is the oldest purpose-built student union building in the world. Sydney Mitchell designed it in a turreted Scottish Baronial style on Bristo Square.

    Edinburgh University Library

    The eight-storey Main Library on George Square was designed by J. M. Marshall and Andrew Merrylees of Spence, Glover and Ferguson and opened in 1967. Its long horizontal bands are a fine example of Brutalist library design.

2 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Rub David Hume's Toe

    Former Edinburgh student David Hume's statue on the Royal Mile has a toe polished bright by students, tourists and passers-by rubbing it for luck. Visitors can give it a rub, though Hume himself was a famous critic of superstition.

  • Spot the Gilded Youth on Old College

    The dome of Old College, added in 1887, is crowned by John Hutchison's gilded statue of Youth. Visitors can look up at it from South Bridge or walk into the quadrangle, which also hosts graduation ceremonies and festival events.

History

Founded 1583

From Wikipedia

The University of Edinburgh is a public research university based in Edinburgh, Scotland. Founded by the town council under the authority of a royal charter from King James VI in 1582 and officially opened in 1583, it is one of Scotland's four ancient universities and the sixth-oldest university in continuous operation in the English-speaking world. The university played a crucial role in Edinburgh becoming a leading intellectual centre during the Scottish Enlightenment and contributed to the city being nicknamed the "Athens of the North".
