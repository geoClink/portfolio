Home of the Eagles

Eastern Michigan University

Founded in 1849 as the Michigan State Normal School, Eastern was the fourth teachers' college in the United States and the first outside New England.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoDetroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW)

    Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport in Romulus, east of Ypsilanti, is the nearest major airport.

  • Getting aroundWalking / TheRide buses

    The main campus is compact and easy to walk. TheRide buses link Ypsilanti and the campus with Ann Arbor.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are green and white, and the teams are the Eagles.

    Starkweather Hall

    901 W Forest Ave, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

    Built in 1896 with a gift from Mary Ann Starkweather for the Students' Christian Association, this fieldstone building is the oldest on campus. When it opened, it was the only religious center at any teachers' college in the country.

    Pease Auditorium

    494 College Pl, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

    Smith, Hinchman & Grylls designed this concert hall, dedicated in 1915 in memory of music professor Frederic H. Pease. Its 81-rank Aeolian-Skinner organ was installed in the early 1960s and restored in the late 1990s.

    Eastern Michigan University, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

  4. 04

    McKenny Hall

    Eastern Michigan University, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

    Opened in 1931 as Charles McKenny Union, this was the campus's first student union. Over the years it held a bowling alley, a ballroom and bookstores; today it houses offices and a Chick-fil-A.

    Eastern Michigan University, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

    Opened in 1998, the four-story library is named for EMU graduate Bruce T. Halle, founder of Discount Tire. Most of its books are kept in an automated retrieval system several stories below the building.

    Bowen Field House

    Eastern Michigan University, Ypsilanti, MI 48197

    Opened in 1955, this 6,000-seat field house was home to Eagles basketball until the Convocation Center opened in the late 1990s. It still hosts track and field, wrestling and gymnastics.

4 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Ypsilanti Water Tower

    Built in 1890 on the highest point in Ypsilanti, the stone water tower borders the campus and is the city's best-known landmark. Locals give directions by it, so take a photo with it.

  • The Pray-Harrold Kiosk

    Covering the kiosk outside Pray-Harrold with flyers for campus events is a longstanding Eastern tradition. Stop by and read what's happening this week.

  • Concert at Pease Auditorium

    Pease Auditorium has been the campus concert hall since 1915. Catch a performance and listen for its 81-rank Aeolian-Skinner pipe organ.

  • The Ghost of Starkweather Hall

    Campus legend says Mary Ann Starkweather, who paid for the hall, haunts it because it no longer serves the religious purpose she intended. Walk past the oldest building on campus and look up at its tower.
