Planning the visit

  • Fly intoBoston Logan International Airport (BOS)

    Logan is about 8 km from Harvard Square across the harbour. The MBTA Silver Line and Red Line connect the airport to the Harvard stop.

  • Getting aroundWalking / MBTA Red Line

    Harvard Yard and its neighbours are best explored on foot. The Red Line's Harvard station opens right onto Harvard Square, a short ride from downtown Boston.

  • Colors and teams

    Students simply say 'the Yard' for Harvard Yard, the leafy heart of the campus where the oldest halls stand.

6 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Massachusetts Hall

    Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138

    Harvard's oldest surviving building, a plain brick Georgian hall raised between 1718 and 1720. Built as a dormitory for 64 students, it quartered 640 American soldiers during the Siege of Boston and now holds the president's office.

    Built
    1720
    Architect
    John Leverett and Benjamin Wadsworth
    Style
    Georgian
    Today
    Offices and student rooms
  2. 02

    University Hall

    Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138

    Charles Bulfinch, one of America's first great architects, designed this crisp white granite hall, built in 1813–1815 from Chelmsford stone. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1970.

    1815
    Architect

    Today

  3. 03

    Memorial Hall

    North of Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138

    A towering High Victorian Gothic memorial to Harvard men who fought for the Union in the Civil War. Under one roof it holds Sanders Theatre, the great timbered dining hall now called Annenberg Hall, and a hushed memorial transept.

    Built
    1877
    Architect
    William Robert Ware and Henry Van Brunt
    Style
    High Victorian Gothic
    Today
    Theatre, dining hall and memorial
  4. 04

    Sever Hall

    Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138

    H. H. Richardson's mature masterpiece in Harvard Yard: a warm red-brick block of rounded arches and carved brickwork from the late 1870s. It became a National Historic Landmark in 1970.

    Architect

  5. 05

    Widener Library

    Harvard Yard, Cambridge, MA 02138

    Built by Eleanor Elkins Widener in memory of her son, lost on the Titanic, this colonnaded giant opened in 1915. A grand flight of steps leads to Corinthian columns, and behind them 57 miles of shelves wind through ten levels of stacks.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Touch John Harvard's Left Foot

    Touching the left foot of the John Harvard statue in Harvard Yard is said to bring good luck, and it has been polished to a golden shine. Guides call it the 'statue of three lies', and visitors often pose for a photo with it.

  • Read Both Sides of Dexter Gate

    Dexter Gate on Massachusetts Avenue reads 'Enter to grow in wisdom' on the outside and 'Depart to serve better thy country and thy kind' on the inside. Visitors can walk through and read both inscriptions.

  • Sheriffs at Johnston Gate

    For centuries, the sheriffs of Middlesex and Suffolk Counties have ridden into Harvard Yard on horseback on Commencement Day, traditionally through Johnston Gate. Visitors can see the 1889 gate, the Yard's main entrance, at any time.
