Home of the first permanent anatomical theatre

University of Padua

The University of Padua has had its seat in the Palazzo del Bo since 1493, and inside it is the world's first permanent anatomical theatre, a model copied by universities across Europe.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoVenice Marco Polo Airport (VCE)

    Venice Marco Polo is the nearest major airport. From there, buses and trains via Venice Mestre reach Padua.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Tram / Bus

    The university's historic buildings are spread through Padua's old centre, so walking is the easiest way to see them. Trams and buses cover the longer hops.

  • Colors and teams

    Italian is the language of the city and of most teaching at the university.

4 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Anatomical Theatre of Padua

    Via VIII Febbraio 2, 35122 Padua

    Inaugurated in 1595 by Girolamo Fabrici of Acquapendente, this is the first permanent anatomical theatre in the world. It became the model for the anatomical theatres built at Europe's main universities in the 17th century.

    Built
    1595
    Architect
    Paolo Sarpi and Dario Varotari
    Style
    Renaissance
    Today
    Historic theatre in the Palazzo del Bo
  2. 02

    Orto botanico di Padova

    Orto Botanico, Padua

    Founded in 1545, this is the world's oldest academic botanical garden still in its original place. Its plan is a perfect square inside an 84-metre circle, split into quarters by paths that point to the four compass directions. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

    Built
    1545
    Architect
    Daniele Barbaro
    Style
    Renaissance garden
    Today
    University botanical garden
  3. 03

    La Specola

    La Specola, Padua

    A 14th-century castle tower that was turned into an astronomical observatory in 1767. Its lower observatory sits 16 metres up and its upper observatory 35 metres up, and the university used it until the 1930s.

    Built
    1767
    Architect
    Unknown (medieval castle tower)
    Style
    Medieval tower
    Today
    Historic observatory
  4. 04

    Palazzo Maldura

    Palazzo Maldura, Padua

    This 16th-century palazzo was remodelled in 1769 by Giovan Battista Novello for the lawyer Andrea Maldura. Its rooms keep many frescoes, and today it houses the university's Department of Language Studies and Literature.

    Built
    1769
    Architect
    Giovan Battista Novello
    Style
    Venetian palazzo
    Today
    University department

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Galileo's Lectern

    The wooden lectern Galileo is said to have lectured from, built for him by his own students according to tradition, stands at the entrance of the Sala dei Quaranta in Palazzo Bo. Visitors can see it on a guided tour of the palace.

  • Don't Jump the Chain

    Local superstition holds that students should never jump over the chain in front of Palazzo Bo, or their studies will suffer. Visitors can see the chain and step around it.

  • Caffè Pedrocchi

    The historic café beside Palazzo Bo has been a meeting place for students and professors since 1831, and students may sit in its green room without buying anything. Visitors can stop for its mint coffee and look for the bullet hole left from the 1848 student uprising in the white room.
