Emory University

Emory moved to Atlanta from Oxford, Georgia, in 1915 after Coca-Cola founder Asa Griggs Candler gave land and a million dollars. Henry Hornbostel then set the campus look of pink Georgia marble and red tile roofs.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoHartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL)

    Hartsfield–Jackson, south of downtown Atlanta, is the nearest airport. Emory's Druid Hills campus is northeast of downtown.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Emory Cliff shuttles

    The Cliff, Emory's shuttle system, runs routes around campus and to nearby neighborhoods. Its buses run on biodiesel made from recycled cooking oil.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are blue and gold, and the teams are the Eagles. Emory has two mascots: Swoop the Eagle and Dooley the skeleton.

5 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • The Gravity Monument

    Hidden in the trees off Dowman Drive is a pink marble monument that Roger Babson gave Emory in 1963 to honor the sciences. Many say it looks more like a tombstone in the woods. See if you can find it.

  • Lullwater Preserve and Tower

    Lullwater Preserve is a 154-acre stretch of woods and water that walkers and joggers enjoy. Follow the trail to the old granite tower Walter Candler built beside his dam to power his house.

  • Words Under the Eaves

    Henry Hornbostel set the look of the Quad with pink Georgia marble and red tile roofs. Look up under the eaves of the older buildings to find carved words naming what was once taught inside.

  • Source Route

    Behind the Carlos Museum, a set of stairs leads down into Baker Woodland. It is part of an environmental sculpture installed in the 1970s. Take the steps into the woods.

  • Robinson Clock Tower Bells

    The clock tower on Cox Hall carries carillon bells dedicated in 2001 to honor trustee J. William Robinson and his wife. Listen for them as you cross the campus.

History

Founded 1836

From Wikipedia

Emory University is a private research university in Atlanta, Georgia, United States. It was founded in 1836 as Emory College by the Methodist Episcopal Church and named in honor of Methodist bishop John Emory. Its main campus is in Druid Hills, four miles from downtown Atlanta.
