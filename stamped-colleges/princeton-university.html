Planning the visit

  • Fly intoNewark Liberty International Airport (EWR)

    Newark is about an hour's drive north. NJ Transit trains run from the airport station to Princeton Junction, where the short shuttle train known as the Dinky continues to campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / NJ Transit Dinky

    The campus is compact and green, and best seen on foot. The Dinky shuttle train links Princeton station with Princeton Junction on the main New York–Philadelphia line.

  • Colors and teams

    Nassau Hall is affectionately known as 'Old Nassau', a name that also belongs to the university's alma mater song.

5 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Nassau Hall

    Princeton University, Princeton, NJ 08544

    Princeton's oldest building was the largest in colonial New Jersey when completed in 1756 and briefly served as the US Capitol in 1783. After fires it was rebuilt by Benjamin Latrobe and then John Notman, who added its cupola.

    Built
    1756
    Architect
    Robert Smith; later Benjamin Latrobe and John Notman
    Style
    Georgian, remodelled Italianate
    Today

  2. 02

    Princeton University Chapel

    Princeton University, Princeton, NJ 08544

    Ralph Adams Cram's sandstone and limestone chapel evokes a small English cathedral, with a nave, transepts and four great stained-glass windows. When finished in 1928 its only rival in size among university chapels was King's College Chapel, Cambridge.

    Architect

    Style

    Firestone Library

    1 Washington Rd, Princeton, NJ 08544

    Princeton's main library, the Harvey S. Firestone Memorial Library, opened in 1948 in Collegiate Gothic dress to match the campus. It is the headquarters of a system holding more than seven million books.

    Alexander Hall

    Class of 1975 Walk, Princeton, NJ 08544

    A rugged Richardsonian Romanesque hall finished in 1894, built to host commencement and now home to the 900-seat Richardson Auditorium and the Princeton Symphony Orchestra.

  5. 05

    Prospect House

    Princeton University campus, Princeton, NJ 08544

    John Notman's sandstone villa of 1851, with its three-story tower and bracketed eaves, helped popularize the Italianate style in America. Woodrow Wilson lived here as university president; today it is the faculty club.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • FitzRandolph Gate superstition

    Campus legend says students who walk out through FitzRandolph Gate before graduation will not finish their degree, so undergraduates avoid it until Commencement. Visitors can see the gate on Nassau Street and decide for themselves which way to walk.

  • Arch sings at Blair Arch

    Blair Arch, the largest arch on campus, is where many of Princeton's a cappella 'arch sings' take place. Stop under the arch to hear its echo, or catch a free evening concert.

  • Big Cannon on Cannon Green

    A Revolutionary-era cannon has been buried muzzle-down on Cannon Green behind Nassau Hall since 1840, and it is slowly sinking. Visitors can see the exposed end; the green is also where the bonfire is lit when Princeton beats both Harvard and Yale in football.
