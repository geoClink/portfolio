Johns Hopkins University

Johns Hopkins opened in 1876 on the model of European research universities and is considered the first research university in the United States.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoBaltimore/Washington International Thurgood Marshall Airport (BWI)

    BWI, south of Baltimore, is the closest major airport. MARC and Amtrak trains link it with Baltimore Penn Station, a short ride from campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Baltimore Penn Station

    Homewood's brick paths are easy to walk. Baltimore Penn Station, with Amtrak and MARC trains, is a short drive or bus ride south of campus.

  • Colors and teams

    Students call the Milton S. Eisenhower Library 'MSE' and the old Hutzler Reading Room in Gilman Hall 'the Hut.' The teams are the Blue Jays.

8 buildings to stamp

    Homewood Museum

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    Charles Carroll Jr. began this country house of his own design in 1801 on an estate given by his father, a signer of the Declaration of Independence. Its red brick and white marble Federal style set the look for the whole campus, and it is now a museum.

    Gilman Hall

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    Parker, Thomas & Rice designed Gilman Hall, dedicated in 1915 as the academic heart of Homewood and named for first president Daniel Coit Gilman. A renovation finished in 2010 added a glass-roofed atrium that holds the university's Archaeological Museum.

    Milton S. Eisenhower Library

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    Opened in 1964, the main library has six stories but only two above ground, in keeping with a campus myth that nothing may stand taller than Gilman Hall. Its interior is being rebuilt in a major renovation.

    Shriver Hall

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    Built from 1952 to 1954 with a bequest from lawyer Alfred Jenkins Shriver, this lecture hall follows his will closely: its murals show his class of 1891, Baltimore philanthropists and ten Baltimore beauties he chose himself.

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    This two-story Georgian house was built in 1958 to help bring Milton S. Eisenhower to Hopkins, and he became its first resident. It is the president's home and once hosted Russian president Boris Yeltsin.

  6. 06

    Homewood Field

    3400 N Charles St, Baltimore, MD 21218

    Opened in 1906, Homewood Field is home to Hopkins lacrosse, whose teams represented the United States at the 1928 and 1932 Olympic lacrosse demonstrations. The national lacrosse museum stood beside it until 2016.

  7. 07

    Space Telescope Science Institute

    3700 San Martin Dr, Baltimore, MD 21218

    On the edge of Homewood, this institute runs science operations for the Hubble and James Webb space telescopes. It was founded in 1981, and its original building was completed in 1983.

    Wyman Park Building

    3100 Wyman Park Dr, Baltimore, MD 21211

    Built in 1934 as the U.S. Marine Hospital, it was the second-largest hospital in the system that cared for merchant sailors. Johns Hopkins once planned to tear it down but renovated it from 2021 to 2023 for offices.

6 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • The Gilman Hall Seal

    Legend says a student who steps on the university seal in the Gilman Hall foyer won't graduate, so it's roped off. Have a look, then walk around it.

  • The Painted Blue Jay

    Student groups repaint the Blue Jay statue to mark events and celebrations. Stop by to see what colors it's wearing today.

  • Lacrosse at Homewood Field

    Lacrosse is the signature sport at Hopkins, and home games at Homewood Field draw big crowds. Catch a game and cheer on the Blue Jays.

  • The Beach

    "The Beach" is the big lawn by the Eisenhower Library on Charles Street, where students study, picnic and play. Spread out a blanket and watch the sunset.

  • Spring Fair

    Every spring since 1972, students have run Spring Fair, a weekend festival with vendors, food, rides and music that draws Baltimore residents too. Visit in April to join in.

  • The Art Museum Next Door

    The Baltimore Museum of Art sits just steps from campus, and admission is free. Add it to your campus visit.

History

Founded 1876

From Wikipedia

Johns Hopkins University (JHU), often abbreviated as Hopkins, is a private research university in Baltimore, Maryland, United States. Incorporated in 1867 and opened in 1876 based on the European research institution model, Johns Hopkins is considered to be the first research university in the U.S.
