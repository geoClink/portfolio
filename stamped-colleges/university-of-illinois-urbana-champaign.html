Planning the visit

  • Fly intoUniversity of Illinois Willard Airport (CMI)

    Willard Airport, in Savoy just south of campus, has daily flights to Chicago O'Hare and Dallas/Fort Worth. Many visitors fly into Chicago instead.

  • Getting aroundWalking / MTD buses

    The Champaign-Urbana Mass Transit District runs frequent buses across campus, and students ride with their i-card.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are orange and blue, and the teams are the Fighting Illini.

5 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Altgeld Hall

    1409 W Green St, Urbana, IL 61801

    Built in 1896–97 as the University Library, this Richardsonian Romanesque hall was designed by Nathan Ricker and James M. White of the university's architecture department. Its bell tower holds the University Chime, which marks the hours.

    Built
    1897
    Architect

    Style

  2. 02

    Foellinger Auditorium

    709 S Mathews Ave, Urbana, IL 61801

    Clarence H. Blackall's Beaux-Arts auditorium closes the south end of the Main Quad. The round hall, 120 feet across, is crowned by a copper dome and is the university's largest lecture hall.

    Built
    1907
    Architect
    Clarence H. Blackall
    Style
    Beaux-Arts
    Today
    Concert and Lecture Hall
  3. 03

    Main Library

    1408 W Gregory Dr, Urbana, IL 61801

    Charles A. Platt designed this Georgian Revival library, begun in 1924 to replace Altgeld Hall when the collections outgrew it. It holds the University Archives and the Rare Book and Manuscript Library.

    Built
    1924
    Architect
    Charles A. Platt
    Style

  4. 04

    Smith Memorial Hall

    805 S Mathews Ave, Urbana, IL 61801

    Built in 1917–20 just east of Foellinger Auditorium, this Beaux-Arts music hall was the first building on campus paid for entirely by a private gift. Captain Thomas J. Smith dedicated it to his wife, Tina Weedon Smith.

    Built
    1920
    Architect
    James M. White and George E. Wright
    Style
    Beaux-Arts
    Today

  5. 05

    Krannert Center for the Performing Arts

    500 S Goodwin Ave, Urbana, IL 61801

    Illinois alumnus Max Abramovitz designed this performing arts complex, which opened in 1969 with four indoor venues seating about 4,000 in all. Its main lobby is floored with teak from Thailand.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Alma Mater Statue

    Lorado Taft's 1929 bronze of a robed woman with outstretched arms stands by Altgeld Hall to welcome students. Stop for a photo with her, a classic Illinois picture.

  • Altgeld Chimes

    The chime of 15 bells in Altgeld Hall's tower has rung since 1920, marking the hours and playing short concerts. Listen near the tower for "Hail to the Orange," played at every concert.

  • Morrow Plots

    Planted in 1876, these small fields are the oldest agricultural experiment fields in the United States and a National Historic Landmark. Walk past them beside the Undergraduate Library.
