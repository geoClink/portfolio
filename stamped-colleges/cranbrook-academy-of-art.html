Cranbrook

Cranbrook Academy of Art

Newspaper publisher George Gough Booth and his wife Ellen Scripps Booth built Cranbrook on a farm they bought in 1904, and named it after Cranbrook, England, the birthplace of George's father. Eliel Saarinen designed much of the campus, which became a National Historic Landmark in 1989.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoDetroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW)

    Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport in Romulus, southwest of Detroit, is the main airport for the region. From there, Cranbrook is a drive north to Bloomfield Hills.

  • Getting aroundCar / Walking

    Cranbrook sits on Woodward Avenue in Bloomfield Hills, north of Detroit, and most visitors arrive by car. On campus, walking paths link the Art Museum, Saarinen House, the Institute of Science and the gardens of Cranbrook House.

  • Colors and teams

    Cranbrook's emblem is a crane, and its official logo colors are copper and gold.

7 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Saarinen House

    39221 Woodward Ave, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    Eliel Saarinen designed this house and studio in the late 1920s and lived here with his wife Loja from 1930 to 1950. Its restored rooms hold his furniture and her textiles, and the Art Museum runs tours from spring to fall.

    1930
    Architect
    Eliel Saarinen
    Style

    Today

  2. 02

    Cranbrook Art Museum

    39221 Woodward Ave, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    Completed in 1942, the museum and the Academy library are joined by a tall peristyle, the last major part of Saarinen's plan for the Academy of Art. It is one of America's first museums of contemporary art and holds work by Charles and Ray Eames and Harry Bertoia.

    Built
    1942
    Architect
    Eliel Saarinen
    Today
    Art museum and library
  3. 03

    Cranbrook House

    380 Lone Pine Rd, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    Albert Kahn designed this 1908 Arts and Crafts manor for the Booths, Cranbrook's founders. Volunteers lead tours of the first-floor rooms, and its 40 acres of gardens open to the public from May through October.

    Built
    1908
    Architect
    Albert Kahn
    Style
    English Arts and Crafts
    Today
    House museum, gardens and offices
  4. 04

    Christ Church Cranbrook

    470 Church Rd, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    George Booth paid for this towered stone church, consecrated in 1928 as the focal point of Cranbrook. It was designed by the successor firm of Bertram Goodhue, with Oscar H. Murray as chief designer, and it is still an Episcopal parish.

    Built
    1928
    Architect
    Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue Associates (Oscar H. Murray)
    Style
    Gothic Revival
    Today
    Episcopal church
  5. 05

    Kingswood School Cranbrook

    39221 Woodward Ave, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    Eliel Saarinen designed every detail of this girls' school, from its copper roofs to its furniture, with help from Loja, Eero and Pipsan Saarinen. Classes began here in 1931, and it is often called his masterpiece at Cranbrook.

    Built
    1931
    Architect
    Eliel Saarinen
    Today
    Cranbrook Kingswood Upper School
  6. 06

    Cranbrook School

    39221 Woodward Ave, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    Built between 1926 and 1931 for Cranbrook School for Boys, these buildings are set around a quadrangle and dominated by the octagonal tower of Hoey Hall. Saarinen mixed Collegiate Gothic forms with Arts and Crafts, Scandinavian and Art Deco details.

    Built
    1927
    Architect
    Eliel Saarinen with Henry Scripps Booth and J. Robert F. Swanson
    Style
    Collegiate Gothic
    Today
    Cranbrook Kingswood Upper School
  7. 07

    Cranbrook Institute of Science

    39221 Woodward Ave, Bloomfield Hills, MI 48304

    The science museum moved into Eliel Saarinen's building in 1938. Steven Holl added a new entrance and exhibit wing in 1998, and the museum has a planetarium and a 20-inch telescope for public viewing nights.

    Built
    1938
    Architect
    Eliel Saarinen; Steven Holl (1998 addition)
    Today
    Science museum

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Carl Milles's Orpheus Fountain

    In front of the Art Museum, eight bronze figures by Swedish sculptor Carl Milles lean in as if listening for Orpheus's music, with only the splashing water to hear. Installed in 1938, it is one of many Milles sculptures you can find on a walk around campus.

  • Say Hi to Steggy

    A life-sized stegosaurus named Steggy stands outside the Cranbrook Institute of Science as its mascot. Visitors stop for a selfie with him before heading inside.

  • Summer Carillon Recitals

    Every summer since 1928, carillonneurs have played free recitals on the 50 bells in Christ Church Cranbrook's tower. Bring a lawn chair or blanket and listen from the church grounds.

History

Founded 1904

From Wikipedia

It consists of Cranbrook Schools, Cranbrook Academy of Art, Cranbrook Art Museum, Cranbrook Institute of Science, Cranbrook House and Gardens, and the Cranbrook Center for Collections and Research. The founders also built Christ Church Cranbrook as a focal point for the educational complex, though it is a separate entity operated by the Episcopal Diocese of Michigan. The sprawling 319-acre (1,290,000 m2) campus began as a 174-acre (700,000 m2) farm, purchased in 1904. The organization takes its name from Cranbrook, England, the birthplace of the founder's father.
