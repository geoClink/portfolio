University of Chicago

The campus mixes two big ideas: Gothic quadrangles and some of the boldest modern buildings in America, from Frank Lloyd Wright's Robie House to Helmut Jahn's glass-domed Mansueto Library.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoChicago Midway International Airport (MDW)

    Midway is the closer of Chicago's two airports to Hyde Park, on the city's South Side. O'Hare International (ORD) handles far more long-haul flights but is a longer trip across the city.

  • Getting aroundWalking / UChicago Shuttles / Metra

    The main quadrangles are compact enough to walk. Metra Electric trains run between Hyde Park and downtown Chicago, and university shuttles loop the neighborhood.

  • Colors and teams

    Campus has its own shorthand: the Regenstein Library is simply 'the Reg', and the Institute for the Study of Ancient Cultures was known for a century as the Oriental Institute.

    Rockefeller Chapel

    5850 S Woodlawn Ave, Chicago, IL 60637

    Bertram Goodhue's soaring Collegiate Gothic chapel was built between 1925 and 1928 without structural steel. Around 70 sculptures are carved into its stone, and its carillon tower is, by covenant, the tallest building on campus.

    Robie House

    5757 S Woodlawn Ave, Chicago, IL 60637

    Frank Lloyd Wright's Prairie-style masterpiece hugs the ground with long brick bands and roofs cantilevered far out over its terraces. Completed in 1910, it helped popularize picture windows and attached garages in American homes.

    Regenstein Library

    1100 E 57th St, Chicago, IL 60637

    'The Reg' is the university's main library and one of its great Brutalist statements, designed by Walter Netsch of Skidmore, Owings & Merrill. It opened in 1970 and holds one of the largest collections of books in the world.

4 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Reynolds Club Seal

    Legend says a student who steps on the University seal in the lobby of the Reynolds Club won't graduate in four years. Visitors can see the seal in the student union lobby and decide whether to test the myth.

  • Cobb Gate Gargoyles

    By campus lore, the grotesques on Cobb Gate stand for the admissions officers and the climb of first- to fourth-year students, with the fourth-year at the top. Visitors can walk through the gate and look up to spot each figure.

  • Botany Pond

    Botany Pond in Hull Court is a beloved campus spot where visitors come to see the ducklings and turtles. Stop by the pond for a quiet look at the wildlife and gardens.

  • Rockefeller Chapel Carillon

    The 72-bell carillon at the top of Rockefeller Chapel is played in a daily recital, and student guild members give weekly concerts with bell-tower tours beforehand for anyone interested. Visitors can listen from the quad or join a tour.
