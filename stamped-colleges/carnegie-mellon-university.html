Carnegie Mellon University

Carnegie Mellon was formed in 1967, when the Carnegie Institute of Technology, founded by Andrew Carnegie in 1900, merged with the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoPittsburgh International Airport (PIT)

    Pittsburgh International is about 27 km west of downtown Pittsburgh. The 28X bus runs to the airport from Oakland, the university district next to campus.

  • Getting aroundWalking / PRT buses

    The main campus is compact and easy to walk. Pittsburgh Regional Transit buses connect it with Oakland and Downtown, and the 28X runs from Oakland to the airport.

  • Colors and teams

    Campus has a Scottish theme in honor of Andrew Carnegie: the teams are the Tartans, the mascot is Scotty the Scottish terrier, and the band plays in kilts.

4 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • The Fence

    Painting the Fence in the middle of the Cut has been a tradition since the 1920s, and the original wooden fence once held the Guinness record as the most painted object. Walk by to see the newest coat of paint and message.

  • Buggy Races

    During Spring Carnival, student teams push and free-roll sleek, unpowered buggies around a course in Schenley Park in a race officially called Sweepstakes. Head into the park to watch the heats.

  • Spring Carnival Midway

    Each April, campus groups build multi-story booths with games for the Spring Carnival Midway on the College of Fine Arts parking lot. The carnival is open to the Pittsburgh community, so stop by and explore the booths.

  • The Kiltie Band

    Formed in 1908, the Kiltie Band wears kilts in honor of Andrew Carnegie's Scottish roots and plays at every home football game. Catch a game to hear the band and see King Arthur's quest for the Grail between the third and fourth quarters.

In 1912, it became the Carnegie Institute of Technology and began granting four-year degrees. In 1967, it became Carnegie Mellon University through its merger with the Mellon Institute of Industrial Research, founded in 1913 by Andrew Mellon and Richard B.
