Montreal's Mountainside Campus

McGill University

McGill's oldest building, the Arts Building, was begun in 1839 to a Classical Revival design by John Ostell. Around it stand a Victorian Greek Revival museum, a Richardsonian Romanesque library hall and a Brutalist library from the 1960s.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoMontréal–Trudeau International Airport (YUL)

    Trudeau airport is in Dorval, west of downtown. The 747 express bus runs from the airport into the city centre, a short walk from the Roddick Gates.

  • Getting aroundWalking / Montreal Metro

    The downtown campus climbs the slope of Mount Royal from Sherbrooke Street. McGill metro station on the Green Line is a few blocks south of the main gates.

  • Colors and teams

    McGill is an English-language university in French-speaking Montreal, so you will hear both languages all over campus and on the streets around it.

5 buildings to stamp

  1. 01

    Arts Building

    853 Sherbrooke Street West, Montreal, QC

    The oldest building on campus, begun in 1839, sits at the top of the main avenue with its Classical Revival portico. Wings were added in 1861 and 1925, and Moyse Hall theatre fills the north wing.

    Redpath Museum

    859 Sherbrooke Street West, Montreal, QC

    Sugar baron Peter Redpath gave McGill the oldest building in Canada built specifically as a museum. Its own university calls it an unusual and late example of the Greek Revival in North America.

    1882
    Architect

  3. 03

    Redpath Hall

    3461 McTavish Street, Montreal, QC

    Once the reading room of McGill's first library building, this Richardsonian Romanesque hall is now a concert venue run by the Schulich School of Music. It has a French Classical pipe organ by Hellmuth Wolff.

    Built
    1893
    Architect
    Andrew Taylor
    Style
    Richardsonian Romanesque
    Today
    Concert and events hall
  4. 04

    Macdonald-Harrington Building

    815 Sherbrooke Street West, Montreal, QC

    Built in 1896–97 as the chemistry building, a gift of tobacco magnate Sir William Macdonald. Today it houses all of McGill's architecture studios, so future architects learn inside a Renaissance Revival landmark.

    1897

    Style

    McLennan Library Building

    3459 McTavish Street, Montreal, QC

    A seven-storey reinforced concrete library built in 1967–69 next to the old Redpath Library. Together they form McGill's largest library, the Humanities and Social Sciences Library.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Chimes of the Roddick Gates

    The Roddick Gates, McGill's formal entrance on Sherbrooke Street, carry four clocks whose bells strike the Westminster Quarters. Walk through the gates onto campus and listen for the chimes.

  • James McGill's Tomb

    The remains of founder James McGill rest in a monument in front of the Arts Building, moved there from a Montreal cemetery and consecrated in 1875. Visitors can stop by the founder's tomb on the lower campus.

  • Photo at the Three Bares

    The Three Bares fountain, unveiled at Spring Convocation in 1931 in a tree-lined dell near the Redpath Museum, appears in countless convocation photographs. Visitors can take their own photo at this well-known campus landmark.

History

Founded 1821

From Wikipedia

McGill University is an English-language public research university in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1821 by royal charter, the university bears the name of James McGill, a Scottish merchant, whose bequest in 1813 established the University of McGill College. In 1885, the name of the university was officially changed to McGill University.
