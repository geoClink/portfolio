Wake Forest University

Most Reynolda Campus buildings are modified Georgian in Old Virginia brick trimmed with granite and limestone, planned by Jens Fredrick Larson for the college's 1956 move from the town of Wake Forest.

Planning the visit

  • Fly intoPiedmont Triad International Airport (GSO)

    Piedmont Triad International, east of Winston-Salem, is the nearest major commercial airport.

  • Getting aroundWalking / university shuttles

    The Reynolda Campus is walkable. University shuttles leave from a campus Shuttle Hub, including a weekday route to Wake Downtown.

  • Colors and teams

    The school colors are old gold and black, and the teams are the Demon Deacons.

3 traditions to check off

Stamped! keeps these as a checklist, so you can tick each one off on the visit.

  • Wait Chapel Carillon

    Wait Chapel's tower holds a 12-ton carillon of 48 bronze bells, and the chapel has hosted presidential debates. Visitors see it at the head of the Quad.

  • Stone Arch

    This arch on the Quad is a replica of the one on the Old Campus in Wake Forest, North Carolina, and each graduating class walks through it at Commencement. Visitors can walk through it too.

  • The Magnolias

    The magnolias on Manchester Plaza grew from seeds collected on the Old Campus in 1947. Visitors walk among them on the lower quad.

History

Founded 1834

From Wikipedia

Wake Forest University is a private research university in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, United States. Founded by Baptist Christians in 1834, the institution received its name from its original location in Wake Forest, north of Raleigh, North Carolina. The Reynolda Campus, the university's main campus, has been located north of downtown Winston-Salem since the university moved there in 1956.
